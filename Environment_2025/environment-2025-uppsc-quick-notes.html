Food chains and rolesp. 39–42

  • Food chain: order of feeding; 4–5 links; not within one species.
  • Food chain = flow of carbon and energy.
  • Grass (autotroph) → goat (herbivore) → human (omnivore).
  • Marine chain: diatom → crustaceans → herring.
  • Green plants = primary producers (autotrophs).UPPSC
  • Primary consumer = herbivore; secondary = small carnivore; tertiary = large carnivore.
  • Man = primary and secondary consumer. Deer, ants = primary consumers.UPPSC ×2
  • Phytoplankton (micro-algae): main marine producers.UPPSC

Ozone basicsp. 122–124

  • Ozone (O₃): three oxygen atoms; forms when solar UV acts on O₂.UPPSC
  • Near ground = harmful pollutant; high up = UV shield.
  • Ozone layer absorbs UV, protects life.UPPSC ×7
  • Ozone layer: lower stratosphere, about 15–30 km.UPPSC ×5
  • About 90% of ozone in stratosphere; 10% in troposphere.
  • Natural regulator: nitrogen dioxide (NO₂).UPPSC
  • Unit: Dobson Unit; 1 DU = 0.01 mm pure ozone at 0°C, 1 atm.
  • Order upward: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere.
  • Troposphere: about 10 km; 18–20 km in tropics; thickest at equator.
  • Stratosphere: about 10–50 km.
  • Homosphere (uniform gases): up to 90 km; lowest three layers. Ionosphere not in it.

Ozone depletionp. 125–126

  • Antarctic hole reported: Farman, Gardiner, Shanklin (British Antarctic Survey), Nature, May 1985.UPPSC
  • Ozone hole: very low ozone over Antarctica, not a real hole.
  • Hole needs polar front, polar stratospheric clouds, CFC inflow.
  • CFCs = main cause of depletion.UPPSC ×5
  • Chlorine, bromine atoms destroy ozone; ODS (ozone-depleting substances) rise in 2–5 years.
  • One chlorine atom destroys over 100,000 ozone molecules.
