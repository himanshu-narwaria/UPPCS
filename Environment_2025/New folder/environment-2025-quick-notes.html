Ecology and ecosystemp. 32–34

  • Ecology ('Oekologie'): Ernst Haeckel, 1860s; organisms and their environment.
  • Ecosystem: A.G. Tansley, 1935; community with its environment.UPSC
  • Ecosystem = living parts + non-living parts, linked by nutrient cycles, energy flow.×2
  • Ecosystem = open system: materials and energy enter and leave.×2
  • Functional unit of nature with own productivity.×2
  • Four components: abiotic materials, producers, consumers, decomposers.
  • Parts interdependent: foxes killed → rabbits increase.×2
  • Biosphere = largest ecosystem; air, water, land zone with life; open system.
  • Biosphere absent at extreme poles, highest mountains, deepest oceans.
  • Habitat: actual place where organism lives.
  • Biome: large area of similar climate: forest, grassland, desert, tundra. Human-made: crop land.
  • Order: Population → Community → Ecosystem → Landscape.
  • Population: same species, same area. Community: two or more species together.

Types of ecosystemp. 35–36

  • Artificial: farmland, rice field, orchard, garden, reservoir. Natural: forest, pond, lake.×2
  • Five terrestrial: desert, forest, grassland, taiga, tundra.
  • Marine = biggest type: 71% of Earth's surface, 97% of its water.×2
  • Marine = most stable ecosystem.×4
  • Oceans: cradle of life, highest biodiversity; coral reefs richest.×4

Imbalance and carrying capacityp. 37–38

  • Deforestation = main cause of ecological imbalance in India.
  • Other causes: desertification, floods, famines, rainfall variation.
  • Lumbering disturbs balance; social forestry, Vanamahotsav, afforestation do not.
  • Carrying capacity (K): largest population an environment can sustain indefinitely.
  • Biotic potential (r): maximum reproduction in best conditions.

Food chains and rolesp. 39–42

  • Food chain: order of feeding; 4–5 links; not within one species.UPSC
  • Food chain = flow of carbon and energy.×2
  • Grass (autotroph) → goat (herbivore) → human (omnivore).×2
  • Marine chain: diatom → crustaceans → herring.UPSC
  • Green plants = primary producers (autotrophs).×2
  • Primary consumer = herbivore; secondary = small carnivore; tertiary = large carnivore.
  • Man = primary and secondary consumer. Deer, ants = primary consumers.
  • Phytoplankton (micro-algae): main marine producers.
  • Marine phytoplankton, photosynthetic bacteria make about 50% of world's oxygen.2025
  • Decomposers: bacteria, fungi; dead organic → inorganic matter; virus not one.UPSC
  • Producers and decomposers link biotic and abiotic parts.
  • Butterfly crash: less pollination; fewer spiders, birds; no extra fungal infection.UPSC

Energy flow and pyramidsp. 42–45

  • Solar energy = primary source driving ecosystems.×3
  • Energy flow unidirectional: producers upward, lost as heat.×2
  • Energy falls at each trophic level.×2
  • Ten percent law: Raymond Lindeman, 1940s; about 10% passes on.
  • Levels: producers → herbivores → carnivores → tertiary consumers; decomposers feed on all.

Check Book's level table calls tertiary consumers omnivores; elsewhere it says large carnivores.

  • Pyramid of energy: always upright.
  • Pyramid of biomass: upright in grassland, forest; inverted in pond, marine.

Greenhouse effectp. 91–95

  • Greenhouse effect = natural warming; global warming = rise from extra GHGs (greenhouse gases).
  • GHGs absorb Earth's long-wave heat, re-emit it all ways → surface warms.×4
  • Sun sends short waves; cooler Earth re-emits long waves (infrared).
  • Concept by Joseph Fourier (1820s). No effect → Earth's average ≈ –18 °C.
  • No atmosphere → well below freezing everywhere on surface.2025
  • CO₂ warms air by absorbing infrared.UPSC

Greenhouse gasesp. 91–95

  • Main GHGs: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, water vapour; also CFCs, ozone.×8
  • Natural, most important: CO₂, methane, ozone, water vapour.
  • IPCC direct gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, SF₆, NF₃. Indirect: NOₓ, CO, NMVOC, SO₂.
  • Water vapour = most abundant GHG; about half of natural effect (NASA).
  • Water-vapour feedback: warmer → more vapour → more heat (positive loop).
  • CO₂ = main gas behind global warming (66%).×8
  • CO₂ + CH₄ = main contributors; CF₄, N₂O less.×3
  • Share order: CO₂ 66 > CH₄ 16 > CFCs and others 12 > N₂O 6 (WMO 2024).×3
  • Nitrogen: not a GHG.×3
  • Argon: not a GHG.×2
  • Also not GHGs: oxygen, hydrogen (H₂), propane.

Ozone basicsp. 122–124

  • Ozone (O₃): three oxygen atoms; forms when solar UV acts on O₂.
  • Near ground = harmful pollutant; high up = UV shield.
  • Ozone layer absorbs UV, protects life.×4
  • Ozone layer: lower stratosphere, about 15–30 km.×5
  • About 90% of ozone in stratosphere; 10% in troposphere.×5
  • Natural regulator: nitrogen dioxide (NO₂).
  • Unit: Dobson Unit; 1 DU = 0.01 mm pure ozone at 0°C, 1 atm.
  • Order upward: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere.
  • Troposphere: about 10 km; 18–20 km in tropics; thickest at equator.
  • Stratosphere: about 10–50 km.
  • Homosphere (uniform gases): up to 90 km; lowest three layers. Ionosphere not in it.

Ozone depletionp. 125–126

  • Antarctic hole reported: Farman, Gardiner, Shanklin (British Antarctic Survey), Nature, May 1985.
  • Ozone hole: very low ozone over Antarctica, not a real hole.
  • Hole needs polar front, polar stratospheric clouds, CFC inflow.UPSC
  • CFCs = main cause of depletion.×8
  • Chlorine, bromine atoms destroy ozone; ODS (ozone-depleting substances) rise in 2–5 years.×8
  • One chlorine atom destroys over 100,000 ozone molecules.×8
