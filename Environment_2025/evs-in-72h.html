Environment and Sustainable Development · part 1 of 5

Environment basics; Sustainable development concept

Read every line once, slowly (about 35 minutes). Then answer the PYQs (about 25 minutes).

Environment basics

  • Environment = all biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) things around an organism.UPPSC
ComponentIncludes
Physical (abiotic)Soil, air, water, landforms, climate, rocks, minerals
Biological (biotic)Plants, humans, animals, microorganisms
Cultural (man-made)Economic, social, political elements
  • Only these three; "operational environment" is not one.UPPSC
  • Inexhaustible: solar, wind, tidal energy, rain, atomic energy.
  • Renewable: forest, pasture, water, agriculture, wild animals, soil.
  • Non-renewable: biotic species; petrol, coal, gas, gypsum, metals.
  • Natural capital: nature-made resources (air, water, land, minerals, forests); roads excluded.UPPSC

Sustainable development concept

  • Sustainable development (SD): meets present needs without harming future generations' needs; vital for human well-being.UPPSC ×3
  • SD = inter-generational care in using natural resources.
  • Term from Brundtland Report, Our Common Future, WCED (world development commission), 1987.UPPSC
  • Gro Brundtland: three-term Norway PM, WCED chair.
  • Three parts: economic development, social progress, environmental protection.
  • SD includes biodiversity conservation, pollution control, less poverty.UPPSC
  • SD data tools: remote sensing, statistics, GIS (mapping system), environment information system.
  • Circular economy: less raw material and waste, so lower greenhouse emissions.
  • Order: Limits to Growth (1972) → Brundtland (1987) → Montreal in force (Jan 1989) → Rio (Jun 1992).UPPSC
  • Club of Rome: April 1968, 30 people, Rome; gave 'Limits to Growth'.UPPSC

    Carbon, water, plants, air

    • CO₂ added by respiration, decay, volcanoes, burning fossil fuels.
    • Photosynthesis removes CO₂, releases oxygen; does not add CO₂.UPPSC
    • Carbon allotropes (crystal forms): graphite, fullerene, diamond. Compounds: CO₂, carbonates, hydrogen carbonates.
    • Solar radiation drives water cycle through evaporation.UPPSC ×2
    • Water cycle = closed system; hydrosphere volume constant.
    • Humidity: water vapour in air; measured by hygrometer or psychrometer.
    • Transplanted seedlings often fail: most root hairs lost.
    • Root hairs take water and minerals from soil.
    • Excess fertilizer → water uptake falls → leaves turn brown.
    • Amazon = 'Lungs of the Earth'; nearly one-third of remaining tropical rainforest.

            Ecology · part 1 of 4

            Ecology and ecosystem; Types of ecosystem; Imbalance and carrying capacity

            Read every line once, slowly (about 35 minutes). Then answer the PYQs (about 25 minutes).

            Ecology and ecosystem

            • Ecology ('Oekologie'): Ernst Haeckel, 1860s; organisms and their environment.UPPSC
            • Ecosystem: A.G. Tansley, 1935; community with its environment.UPPSC
            • Ecosystem = living parts + non-living parts, linked by nutrient cycles, energy flow.
            • Ecosystem = open system: materials and energy enter and leave.UPPSC ×2
            • Functional unit of nature with own productivity.
            • Four components: abiotic materials, producers, consumers, decomposers.
            • Parts interdependent: foxes killed → rabbits increase.UPPSC ×2
            • Biosphere = largest ecosystem; air, water, land zone with life; open system.UPPSC ×2
            • Biosphere absent at extreme poles, highest mountains, deepest oceans.
            • Habitat: actual place where organism lives.
            • Biome: large area of similar climate: forest, grassland, desert, tundra. Human-made: crop land.
            • Order: Population → Community → Ecosystem → Landscape.UPPSC
            • Population: same species, same area. Community: two or more species together.

            Types of ecosystem

            • Artificial: farmland, rice field, orchard, garden, reservoir. Natural: forest, pond, lake.UPPSC ×2
            • Five terrestrial: desert, forest, grassland, taiga, tundra.UPPSC
            • Marine = biggest type: 71% of Earth's surface, 97% of its water.UPPSC ×2
            • Marine = most stable ecosystem.UPPSC ×2
            • Oceans: cradle of life, highest biodiversity; coral reefs richest.

            Imbalance and carrying capacity

            • Deforestation = main cause of ecological imbalance in India.
            • Other causes: desertification, floods, famines, rainfall variation.
            • Lumbering disturbs balance; social forestry, Vanamahotsav, afforestation do not.UPPSC ×2
            • Carrying capacity (K): largest population an environment can sustain indefinitely.
            • Biotic potential (r): maximum reproduction in best conditions.

              Ecology · part 2 of 4

              Food chains and roles; Energy flow and pyramids

              Read every line once, slowly (about 35 minutes). Then answer the PYQs (about 25 minutes).

              Food chains and roles

              • Food chain: order of feeding; 4–5 links; not within one species.
              • Food chain = flow of carbon and energy.
              • Grass (autotroph) → goat (herbivore) → human (omnivore).
              • Marine chain: diatom → crustaceans → herring.
              • Green plants = primary producers (autotrophs).UPPSC
              • Primary consumer = herbivore; secondary = small carnivore; tertiary = large carnivore.
              • Man = primary and secondary consumer. Deer, ants = primary consumers.UPPSC ×2
              • Phytoplankton (micro-algae): main marine producers.UPPSC
              • Marine phytoplankton, photosynthetic bacteria make about 50% of world's oxygen.
              • Decomposers: bacteria, fungi; dead organic → inorganic matter; virus not one.
              • Producers and decomposers link biotic and abiotic parts.UPPSCcheck
              • Butterfly crash: less pollination; fewer spiders, birds; no extra fungal infection.

              Energy flow and pyramids

              • Solar energy = primary source driving ecosystems.UPPSC ×3
              • Energy flow unidirectional: producers upward, lost as heat.UPPSC
              • Energy falls at each trophic level.UPPSC ×2
              • Ten percent law: Raymond Lindeman, 1940s; about 10% passes on.
              • Levels: producers → herbivores → carnivores → tertiary consumers; decomposers feed on all.UPPSC

              Check Book's level table calls tertiary consumers omnivores; elsewhere it says large carnivores.

              • Pyramid of energy: always upright.
              • Pyramid of biomass: upright in grassland, forest; inverted in pond, marine.UPPSC

                      Causes of loss

                      • Main cause: habitat destruction (forests, wetlands cleared; dams change rivers).UPPSC ×5

                      • Threats: warming, habitat fragmentation, alien species; not vegetarianism.

                      Conservation and reefs

                      • In-situ (natural home): National Parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, reserved and protected forests, sacred groves.
