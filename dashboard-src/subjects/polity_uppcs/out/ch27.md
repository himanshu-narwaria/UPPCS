## State executive and the office of Governor
- The Constitution gives the States the same pattern of government as the Centre: a **parliamentary system**.
- **Articles 153 to 167** (Part VI) deal with the Government of the State.
- The **Governor** is the executive (constitutional) head of the State. {2}
- All executive work of the State is formally done in the name of the Governor.

## Why a nominated Governor
- The draft Constitution's plan of an elected Governor was dropped: it meant another election (more expense), an elected Governor might think himself superior to the Chief Minister, and the Governor had to work under the parliamentary system. {1}
- In the Constituent Assembly Krishnamachari, B.G. Kher and J.B. Pant favoured election; B.R. Ambedkar and Jawaharlal Nehru favoured a nominated Governor.
- A nominated Governor was accepted by general consensus.
- Reasons given: election would raise election expenditure; nomination avoids a clash of political ambitions of Chief Minister and Governor (an election fought on major issues would lead to different policies); otherwise the Centre would lack effective control over the State.
## Appointment, qualifications and oath
- **Article 157**: a Governor must be a citizen of India and have completed **35 years** of age; the same person may be Governor of two or more States. {6,7}
- Citizenship by birth is not required; eligibility for Lok Sabha membership is not required.
- Article 158: the Governor shall not be a member of either House.
- Generally one person is Governor of one State; the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** added to Article 153 that the same person can be Governor of two or more States. {12}
- **Article 159**: before entering office the Governor takes an oath or affirmation before the **Chief Justice of the High Court**, or in his absence the senior-most judge available. {8,9}
- The Third Schedule does not contain the Governor's oath.
- **Sarkaria Commission** (set up 1983, report 1988): Governor should be an eminent person from outside the State, a detached figure without intense political links, not active in politics in recent years. {56}
- Sarkaria Commission also said: the State Chief Minister should have a say in the appointment; the Governor should not be a member of the ruling party.
## Term and removal
- Article 156(1): the Governor holds office during the **pleasure of the President** (in effect the Union Government); he is responsible to the President. {15,16}
- Article 156(2): the Governor may resign by writing addressed to the President.
- The Governor is appointed by the President, is head of the State's executive power, holds office at the President's pleasure, and normally for **five years** from the date he enters office [Article 156(3)]. {11}
- A Governor continues in office after his term expires until his successor enters office; and he can be removed before five years. {14}
- The President may remove a Governor any time before five years, on the advice of the Council of Ministers; the **State Legislature has no power** to remove him. {13}
- The Constitution lays down **no procedure for removal** of a Governor. {17}
- The Constitution has no provision for **impeachment of a Governor**; "impeachment" is used only for the President. {45}
- President: removed by impeachment under Article 61; a President or Vice-President can be removed for violating the Constitution.
- Vice-President: removed by a Rajya Sabha resolution passed by a majority of all its then members and agreed to by Lok Sabha (Article 67).
- Chief Justice of India: removed by President's order after an address of each House with special majority, on proved misbehaviour or incapacity (Article 124(4)).
- First Governor dismissed from office = **Prabhudas Patwari**, Governor of Tamil Nadu, October 1980.
- **Raghukul Tilak** = first Governor of Rajasthan to be dismissed, August 1981. {54}
## Salary and immunities
- **Article 202(3)(a)**: the Governor's emoluments, allowances and other office expenditure are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of the State**. {24}
- **Article 158(3A)**: when one person is Governor of two or more States, emoluments and allowances are shared among the States in the proportion the **President** decides by order. {25}
- Article 158(4): the Governor's emoluments and allowances cannot be reduced during his term; Article 361: no criminal proceedings against him during his term. {42}
- Article 361(1): the Governor is not answerable to any court for the exercise of the powers and duties of his office.
- Article 361(2): no criminal proceedings can be started or continued against the President or a Governor in any court during the term of office.
- Article 361(4): civil proceedings can be brought against the President or a Governor during the term after **two months'** written notice; so it is false that they cannot be sued for any act. {41}
- The Governor may be dismissed for unconstitutional acts; the President can be impeached (Article 61).
- Article 194(2): no member of a State Legislature is liable to court proceedings for anything said or any vote given in the Legislature or its committees; this immunity is absolute. {43}
- Article 74(2): courts cannot inquire whether any, and what, advice was given by Ministers to the President. {44}
- True statements: President / Governor are immune from legal action for official acts; no court can compel a Governor to perform any duty; two months' written notice is needed for civil proceedings on personal acts.
## Executive powers and appointments
- Article 164(1): the Governor appoints the Chief Minister, and the other Ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- Article 165: the Governor appoints as **Advocate General** a person qualified to be a High Court judge.
- Members of the State Public Service Commission and the Advocate General are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- **High Court judges** are not appointed by the Governor; they are appointed by the President. {38}
- Article 217(1): the President appoints High Court judges by warrant under his hand and seal after consulting the Chief Justice of India and the Governor; for other judges the Chief Justice of the High Court is also consulted.
- The Governor cannot appoint High Court judges, but can nominate some members to the Legislative Council (Article 171(3)(e)). {39}
- The Governor nominates **one-sixth** of the members of the Legislative Council.
- The Governor is an integral part of the State Legislature (Governor + Upper House where bicameral + Lower House); he does have judicial powers.
- He can address and send messages to the Legislature and summon, prorogue and dissolve the Legislative Assembly.
- Earlier the Governor could nominate one Anglo-Indian member to the Assembly if the community was not adequately represented.
- The Constitution (104th Amendment) Act, 2019 did not extend this beyond **25 January 2020**; nomination of an Anglo-Indian member to Assemblies has ended.
- The Governor has no emergency powers.
## Discretionary powers
- **Article 163(1)**: the Governor acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister, except where the Constitution requires him to act in his discretion; so the Constitution explicitly mentions discretion. {28}
- The President cannot on his own (suo motu) reserve a State Bill without the Governor reserving and sending it.
- Discretionary powers: sending a report to the President for President's Rule (Article 356) and reserving Bills for the President (Article 200). {26}
- Not discretionary: appointing Ministers (on Chief Minister's advice, Article 164(1)) and making rules for convenient transaction of government business (Article 166(3)).
- Governor acts in his discretion: asking the Government to prove majority in the Assembly; terminating a Chief Minister who lost the Assembly's confidence; giving or withholding assent or reserving a Bill; returning a Bill for reconsideration. {27}
- He also uses discretion in imposing President's Rule and dissolving the Assembly; he is not authorised to consult the High Court.
## Bills
- **Article 200**: on a Bill passed by the State Legislature the Governor may give assent, withhold assent, return it (if not a Money Bill) for reconsideration, or reserve it for the President. {30,31,32}
- If the Legislature passes the Bill again, the Governor may give assent or reserve it for the President.
- There is **no time limit** for the Governor to return an ordinary Bill; "as soon as possible" is not defined and is left to his discretion. {29}
- Article 201: when a Bill is reserved for the President, the President declares whether he assents or not.
- If a Bill needing the Governor's recommendation was introduced and passed without it, the Act is **not invalid** once the Governor gives assent. {33}
## Ordinances and pardon
- **Article 213**: the Governor can promulgate an ordinance when the State Legislature is not in session and circumstances need immediate action. {34,35,37}
- An ordinance has the same force as an Act of the State Legislature; it must be laid before the Legislature (both Houses where there is a Council), so approval of the **State Legislature** is necessary. {36}
- It ceases to operate six weeks after the Legislature reassembles, or earlier if a disapproving resolution is passed by the Assembly and agreed to by the Council, if any.
- **Article 161**: the Governor can grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions, or suspend, remit or commute sentences for offences against laws within the State's executive power.
- The Governor **cannot pardon a death sentence**. {40}
## Jammu and Kashmir, Union Territories and Rajpramukh
- Jammu and Kashmir had special status under Article 370 (Part XXI: Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions).
- Before 1965 the executive head of Jammu and Kashmir was elected by its Legislative Assembly; later the Governor was appointed by the **President of India**. {18}
- In **1965** the **6th Amendment to the State Constitution of J&K** changed the name of the executive head from Sadar-e-Riyasat to Governor. {19}
- Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019: UT of Jammu & Kashmir with a legislature and UT of Ladakh without a legislature.
- Both UTs are administered by the President through a Lieutenant Governor appointed by him.
- **Article 239(1)**: every Union Territory is administered by the **President**, through an administrator appointed by him with a designation he specifies. {21}
- In a Union Territory with a legislature the Chief Minister is appointed by the President, not the Lt. Governor; the Chief Minister of NCT of Delhi is appointed by the President (Article 239AA).
- First Lieutenant Governor of UT of Jammu & Kashmir = **Girish Chandra Murmu**; Manoj Sinha became the 2nd on 7 August 2020. {22}
- First Lt. Governor of UT of Ladakh = **Radha Krishna Mathur** (assumed charge 31 October 2019). {23}
- At present the Lt. Governor of Ladakh is Kavinder Gupta, since July 2025.
- **Rajpramukh** was an administrative title in India from 1949 until 1956.
- On the recommendation of the **States Reorganisation Commission**, the office of Rajpramukh was abolished by the 7th Constitutional Amendment with effect from 1 November 1956. {20}
- The States Reorganisation Commission was set up by the Central Government in December 1953: Justice Fazal Ali, K.M. Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru.
- **Gurumukh Nihal Singh** was appointed first Governor of Rajasthan on **25 October 1956**, not 1 November 1956. {53}
## Notable Governors
- **Sarojini Naidu** = first woman Governor of a State in India; Governor of Uttar Pradesh (United Provinces) from 15 August 1947 to 2 March 1949; died in office. {46,47}
- National Women's Day of India = **13 February**, birth date of Sarojini Naidu (born 13 February 1879, died 2 March 1949). {48}
- **Padmaja Naidu** (daughter of Sarojini Naidu) = first woman Governor of West Bengal (1956-1967). {49}
- Chakravarti Rajagopalachari was the first Governor of West Bengal.
- **Sarla Grewal** = first woman Governor of Madhya Pradesh (1989-1990), its 9th Governor; second woman IAS officer, joined 1952. {50}
- Droupadi Murmu was sworn in as 15th President of India on 25 July 2022; earlier Governor of Jharkhand 2015-2021.
- **Dr. Bhagwat Dayal Sharma**: first Chief Minister of Haryana (1966-67); Governor of Madhya Pradesh 1980-84; Governor of Odisha 1977-80. {52}
- Madhya Pradesh Governors H.V. Pataskar, K.C. Reddy and Satyanarayan Sinha were Constituent Assembly members; Niranjan Nath Wanchoo was not.
