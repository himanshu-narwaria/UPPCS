@ 30 | The Centre-State Relation
## Basis of Centre-State relations
- The Constitution, being federal in structure, divides all powers (legislative, executive and financial) between the Centre and the States.
- **Part XI** deals with relations between the Union and the States: Legislative Relations (Articles 245–255) and Administrative Relations (Articles 256–263). {6,7}
- Centre-State relations are influenced by: provisions of the Constitution, the planning process, conflict of political interests, and the dominance of desire to dictate. {1}
- Planning is done by the Union; with different parties in power at the Centre and in a State, conflict of interest and a desire to dominate arise, which hampers the country's growth.
- Centre-State relations depend upon constitutional provisions, conventions and practices, judicial interpretations and mechanisms for dialogue. {2}
- A federal polity involves relations between Union and States, relations among States, mechanism for cooperation and mechanism for solving disputes. {3}
- The Centre's control over the States in the **planning process** has been termed 'Municipal Relations'. {4}
- It promotes relationships and strategic partnerships between levels of government and with non-governmental organisations at all levels.
- The Central Government is more empowered and has more resources than the States; States lack sufficient resources and depend on the Centre, hence the growing demand to review Centre-State relations. {42}

## Legislative relations: Articles and lists
- Articles 245 and 246 regulate the distribution of power through the three lists. {8}
- Article 245: extent of laws made by Parliament and State Legislatures; Parliament can make laws for the whole or any part of India, a State Legislature for the whole or any part of the State.
- Article 246: subject matter of laws made by Parliament and by State Legislatures.
- Article 246(1): Parliament has exclusive power to make laws on List I (Union List).
- Article 246(2): Parliament and, subject to clause (3), the State Legislature also can make laws on List III (Concurrent List).
- Article 246(3): the State Legislature has exclusive power to make laws for the State on List II (State List), subject to clauses (1) and (2).
- Article 246(4): Parliament can make laws on any matter for any part of India not included in a State, even if the matter is in the State List.
- The **Seventh Schedule** divides legislative subjects in three lists: List I (Union List), List II (State List), List III (Concurrent List). {9}

- Both the Union and the States can make laws on **Concurrent List** subjects; in a conflict, the Union law prevails. {10}
- 'Economic and social planning' and 'infants and minors' are in the Concurrent List; both Central and State Governments can make laws on them. {11}
- **Article 246A** (inserted by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016): notwithstanding Articles 246 and 254, Parliament and every State Legislature can make laws on goods and services tax imposed by the Union or by that State.
- Article 247: Parliament can provide for the establishment of certain additional courts.
## Residuary powers
- **Article 248**: residuary powers of legislation (matters not in the Concurrent or State List) vest exclusively in **Parliament** / the Union. {12,14}
- Residuary subjects = matters not mentioned in the Union List, State List or Concurrent List of the Seventh Schedule. {13}
- Residuary subjects arise from changing circumstances, advances in technology or new policy needs.
- Examples of residuary subjects: cyber laws, space research, atomic energy, artificial intelligence regulations, foreign trade policies.
- In other federal set-ups, such as the USA, residuary powers are often given to the States.
- The Indian Union is an example of a strong Union, as residuary powers rest with Parliament.
## Parliament's power over State List
- **Article 249**: power of Parliament to legislate on GST under Article 246A or on any State List matter in the national interest. {15}
- If the Rajya Sabha resolves that it is necessary in the national interest, Parliament becomes competent to make laws on that State List matter.
- The resolution must be supported by not less than **two-thirds** of the members present and voting.
- Article 249(2): a Rajya Sabha resolution under Article 249(1) remains in force for a period not exceeding **one year**. {16,17}
- Article 250(1): Parliament can legislate on GST under Article 246A or any State List matter while a proclamation of emergency is in operation.
- Article 251: if laws made by Parliament under Articles 249 and 250 are inconsistent with State laws, the laws made by Parliament prevail.
- Article 252: Parliament can legislate for two or more States by consent; any other State may adopt such legislation.
- Article 253: Parliament can make laws on any State List matter to implement international treaties, agreements and conventions.
- Article 253 enables the Central Government to fulfil its international obligations and commitments.
## Administrative relations
- **Articles 256 to 263** in Part XI deal with administrative relations between the Centre and the States.
- **Article 256** (obligation of States and the Union): State executive power must ensure compliance with laws of Parliament and existing laws applying in the State; the Union's executive power extends to giving necessary directions to a State for that purpose. {43}
- **Article 257** (control of the Union over States in certain cases): State executive power must not impede or prejudice the Union's executive power; the Union may give necessary directions to States. {44}
- Article 258: power of the Union to confer powers on States; the President may, with the State Government's consent, entrust to it functions relating to any matter of Union executive power.
- Article 262: adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-State rivers or river valleys (Parliament's power).
- Inter-State Water Disputes Act = 1956; National Water Policy = 1987.
- The **River Boards Act** was enacted and came into force in **1956**, not 1970. {31}
- Article 365: effect of failure to comply with, or give effect to, directions given by the Union.
- Article 360: provisions as to financial emergency.
- Article 368: provisions for the amendment of the Constitution.
## Inter-State Council
- **Article 263** of the Constitution provides for the Inter-State Council, to investigate and discuss subjects of common interest and co-ordinate relations between the Centre and States and among States. {18,20,22}
- The **President** is empowered to establish the Inter-State Council, by order, whenever he feels it would serve the public interest. {19,21}
- The Inter-State Council was set up by a Presidential order dated **28 May 1990**; the first ISC was established in May 1990 and constituted in December 1990.
- The **Sarkaria Commission** recommended a permanent Inter-State Council under Article 263, to be called the **Inter-Governmental Council**; the Inter-State Council was set up on its recommendation. {37,38,39}
- The name Inter-Governmental Council was suggested to distinguish it from other bodies founded under the same Article 263.
- The Commission wanted it as an independent national forum for consultation with a well-defined mandate under Article 263.
- The Janata Dal Government headed by V.P. Singh established the Inter-State Council in 1990.
- Inter-State Council members: Prime Minister (Chairman); Chief Ministers of all States; Chief Ministers of UTs having Legislative Assemblies; Administrators of UTs without Legislative Assemblies; Governors of States under President's rule; six Central Cabinet Ministers, including the Home Minister, nominated by the Prime Minister.
- The Council is a recommendatory body on inter-State, Centre-State and Centre-Union Territory relations.
- Of the Inter-State Council, the National Security Council and the Zonal Councils, only the **Inter-State Council** was established under the provisions of the Constitution. {40}
- The National Security Council was set up by an executive decision with the Prime Minister's approval, not under any Article.
- The **National Development Council** and the **Governors' Conference** are extra-constitutional and extra-legal devices. {23}
## Zonal Councils and other councils
- **Zonal (regional) Councils** are statutory bodies, not constitutional bodies; set up under the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956**, an Act of Parliament. {24,26}
- They advise on matters of common interest to each of the five zones; they are advisory bodies.
- Originally five Zonal Councils: Northern, Central, Eastern, Western and Southern.
- The **Union Home Minister** is the Chairman of every Zonal Council. {25}
- The sixth regional council, for the North East, was formed in 1971.
- Chandigarh, though not a State, is included in a Zonal Council; so are Delhi, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, and Puducherry.
- The North Eastern States (Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Nagaland) are not in the Zonal Councils; the North Eastern Council looks after their special problems.
- Regional councils act as **'Sub-Federal Links'** of the Indian federal system and pave the way for co-operation and integration among the States. {27}
- They are a platform to resolve Centre-State and inter-State differences through free and fair discussion and consultation.
- They are a regional platform for economically, politically and culturally interconnected States, focusing on regional matters while keeping the national perspective.
- The **North Eastern Council (NEC)** was established by the North Eastern Council Act, 1971, initially as an apex advisory body for balanced and coordinated development of the seven North Eastern States.
- NEC is the nodal agency for economic and social development of the North Eastern Region: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura.
- NEC Act amendment of December 2002: Sikkim was added as the 8th member State; NEC became a statutory Regional Planning Body.
- Amended Section 3(1): NEC comprises the Governors and Chief Ministers of the constituent States and three members nominated by the President. {28}
- Amended Section 3(3): the President nominates the Chairman of the NEC (need not be from the other members) and may nominate a member as Vice-Chairman.
- At present the Union Home Minister is ex-officio Chairman (notification of 7 July 2018) and the Minister of DoNER is ex-officio member and Vice-Chairman (24 August 2021).
- The **Jharkhand Area Autonomous Council** was formed on **8 August 1995**; it comprised 18 districts of Santhal Pargana and Chota Nagpur. {29}
- National integration involves political, economic, social, cultural and psychological dimensions and their inter-relations; the National Integration Council was formed in **1962**, not 1961. {30}
## Commissions on Centre-State relations
- The **Sarkaria Commission** was set up to review relations between the Union and the States and suggest ways to strengthen them. {32,35}
- It was formed on **9 June 1983** as a three-member commission under Justice R.S. Sarkaria, a retired Supreme Court Judge.
- The other two members were **B. Shivaraman** and **Dr. S.R. Sen**. {33}
- The Sarkaria Commission submitted its final report in **January 1988**; it had 247 recommendations in 19 chapters. {34}
! Year of the Sarkaria report: Bihar Public Service Commission gave 1987 as its answer, which is not correct.
- The Sarkaria Commission said President's Rule (Article 356) must be used only in rare or exceptional cases, because Article 356 had been misused several times. {36}
- Often President's Rule was declared without considering other provisions.
- The **Rajamannar Committee** and the **Punchhi Commission** are also associated with Centre-State relations.
- The **Indrajit Gupta Committee** (1998) was on state funding of elections, not Union-State relations. {41}
- It recommended state funds only for national and state parties allotted a symbol, not for independent candidates, and in the form of certain facilities.

## Taxes and their sharing
- Article 270: taxes on income other than agricultural income are levied and collected by the Government of India and distributed between the Union and the States.
- Presently all taxes and duties in the Union List, except those in Articles 268, 269 and 269A, are levied by the Union and divided between the Union and the States.
- Stamp duty on financial transactions is imposed by the Union Government but collected and appropriated by the State Government (Article 268). {49}
- Taxes on transactions in stock exchanges and futures markets belong to the Union List; under Article 270 they are levied and collected by the Union but assigned to the States. {50}
- **Land revenue** is a tax exclusively imposed and collected by the States; it is entry 45 of the State List. {51}
- Entry 45 covers land revenue, assessment and collection of revenue, maintenance of land records, survey for revenue purposes and records of rights, and alienation of revenues.
- Taxes on **agricultural income** are entry 46 of the State List; so the Constitution itself assigns agricultural income tax to the States. {54}
- Article 276(2): total tax on professions, trades, callings and employments payable by a person to the State or local bodies cannot exceed **Rs. 2,500 per annum**; raised from Rs. 250 by the 60th Amendment Act, 1988. {56}
## Finance Commission, grants and borrowing
- Article 280(1): the President constitutes a **Finance Commission** within 2 years of the commencement of the Constitution and thereafter every fifth year, or earlier if he considers necessary.
- The Finance Commission is considered a quasi-judicial body, as it has certain powers of a civil court.
- The Finance Commission recommends to the President the distribution of net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States (tax sharing); this is its core responsibility.
- Article 281: the President lays every recommendation of the Finance Commission before both Houses of Parliament, with an explanatory memorandum on action taken.
- Present means of resource transfer from Centre to States: tax sharing, grants-in-aid and centrally sponsored schemes.
- Transfer for plan implementation under the **Gadgil formula** is not a means of resource allocation at present. {5}
- Article 282: the Union or a State may make grants for any public purpose, even if Parliament or the State Legislature cannot make laws on that purpose.
- Article 293(1): a State can borrow only within the territory of India, on the security of the Consolidated Fund of the State, within limits fixed by its Legislature by law, and can give guarantees within such limits; so a State **cannot** take foreign loans directly. {53}
- The **Supreme Court** is the main agency to resolve fiscal disputes between the Centre and the States, under Article 131. {55}
## Trade and suits
- Article 301: subject to the other provisions of Part XIII, trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India shall be free.
- Article 300: the Government of India may sue or be sued by the name of the Union, and a State Government by the name of the State.
- They may sue or be sued in like cases as the Dominion of India and the corresponding Provinces or Indian States could have, subject to laws of Parliament or the State Legislature.
- Pending legal proceedings at the commencement of the Constitution: the Union of India is substituted for the Dominion of India, and the corresponding State for a Province or Indian State.
