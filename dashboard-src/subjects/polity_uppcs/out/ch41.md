@ 41 | Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes
## Part XVI overview
- **Part XVI** (Articles 330 to 342A) = special provisions relating to certain classes: reservation of seats for SC/ST and representation of the Anglo-Indian community. {1}
- Part XVI also includes Articles **330A, 332A and 334A**, inserted by the Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023.

## Reservation of seats for SC and ST
- Under Article 332, reserved seats are fixed by the proportion of SC or ST population to the State's total population.
- Article 332 thus keeps SC/ST representation in the Assembly roughly proportional to the total number of seats.
- Article 332 also extends to the autonomous districts in Assam's Legislative Assembly.
- SC/ST reservation exists only for Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies; there is **no reservation for any caste in Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Councils**. {13}
- After the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, the Union Territory has no Legislative Council.
- Under the 2019 Act, seats are reserved for SCs and STs in the Legislative Assembly of the UT of Jammu and Kashmir.
- The **104th Amendment Act, 2019** amended Article 334 and extended SC/ST seat reservation in Lok Sabha and Assemblies for 10 more years (80 years from the commencement of the Constitution).
## Anglo-Indian representation
- Article 331: despite Article 81, the President could nominate **two** Anglo-Indian members to Lok Sabha if the community was not adequately represented.
- Under Article 334 this provision was first for 10 years; later it was extended for 10 years each time.
- The **104th Amendment Act, 2019** did not extend Anglo-Indian nomination in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies beyond **25 January 2020**; so the arrangement has ended. {21,24}
- The 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019 was enacted by Parliament in 2020 (as printed).
## Reservation of seats for women (106th Amendment, 2023)
- Article 330A: as nearly as may be, **one-third** of the directly elected seats of the House of the People are reserved for women.
- This one-third includes the seats reserved for women belonging to the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
- Article 332A: as nearly as may be, one-third of the directly elected seats of every State Legislative Assembly are reserved for women (including SC and ST women's seats).
- Article 334A: women's reservation takes effect after a delimitation based on the first Census taken after the commencement of the 106th Amendment Act, 2023.
- This reservation ceases on the expiry of **15 years** from such commencement.
## Lists of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes
- Article 341(1): the **President** specifies, by public notification, the castes, races, tribes or parts/groups to be Scheduled Castes for a State/UT; for a State, after consulting its Governor. {4}
- Article 342(1): the President (not the Governor) specifies tribes, tribal communities or parts/groups as Scheduled Tribes for a State/UT; for a State, after consulting its Governor.
- The ST list is State/UT specific: a community declared a Scheduled Tribe in one State need not be so in another State. {7}
- A tribe can be declared a Scheduled Tribe for only a part of a State.
- The SC and ST lists were made by an order of the President issued in 1950 for each State after consulting its Governor.
- Only **Parliament**, by law, can include in or exclude from the SC list (Article 341) and the ST list [Article 342(2)]; State Governments cannot prepare or amend them. {6}
- Once the President issues a notification under Article 342, it cannot be altered by any subsequent notification.
- Bihar's 2015 notification moved the **Tanti-Tantwa** community from Extremely Backward Class to Scheduled Caste; the Supreme Court quashed it in July 2024. {8}
- The Supreme Court ruled that States cannot change the SC list published under Article 341.
- Scheduled Tribe status is **religiously neutral** under the Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950: a follower of any religion can be included. {9}
- Para 3 of the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order, 1950: only Hindus (including Buddhists and Sikhs) can be included in the Scheduled Castes.
- The National Commission for Religious and Linguistic Minorities, headed by **Rangnath Mishra**, recommended repealing this para.
- Criteria for specifying a Scheduled Tribe: indications of primitive traits, distinctive culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact with the community at large, and backwardness. {10}
- These criteria are not spelt out in the Constitution but have become well established.
- After Parliament's amendment of 2007 (14 August 2007), there were **1206** Scheduled Castes in India. {11}
- As per the Constitution (Scheduled Castes Amendment) Act, 2017, the total number of Scheduled Castes in India is 1261.
- Census 2011: Scheduled Castes form 16.6 percent of the total population.
- **Madhya Pradesh** has the maximum Scheduled Tribe population. {12}
- Descending order of ST population among four States: Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar.
## National Commissions for SC and ST
- Original Article 338 provided a **Special Officer** for SCs and STs to look into constitutional safeguards and report to the President.
- The **65th Amendment Act, 1990** replaced the single Special Officer with a high-level multi-member National Commission for SCs and STs.
- The **89th Amendment Act, 2003** split the combined commission into two: NCSC (Article 338) and NCST (new Article 338A).
- At present, **Kishor Makwana** is the chairman of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes.
- NCST was set up on **19 February 2004**.
- Objective of NCST: oversee the implementation of safeguards for STs under the Constitution or any government order, and evaluate their working.
- The commissions under Articles 338 and 338A each have a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and **three other members**; NCST's three full-time members include one female member. {19}
## Backward Classes Commission
- The **National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)** is a constitutional body under **Article 338B**, inserted by the **102nd Amendment Act, 2018**. {26,27}
- NCBC is a commission for socially and educationally backward classes; it can examine complaints and welfare measures regarding them.
- NCBC has five members: a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and three other Members, appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.
- Hansraj Gangaram Ahir assumed charge as NCBC chairperson on 02-12-2022 and continues till now.
- The first All India Backward Classes Commission was appointed by the President on **29 January 1953** and formally inaugurated on 18 March 1953.
- Its first chairman was **Kaka Saheb Kalelkar**. {28}
- Dattatreya Balkrushna Kalelkar (1 Dec 1885 – 21 Aug 1981), popularly Kaka Kalelkar, was an independence activist, social reformer and journalist.
- Kalelkar was a major follower of Mahatma Gandhi's philosophy and methods; he was born in Satara (Maharashtra).
- Kalelkar was a member of Rajya Sabha from 1952 to 1964.
- He presided over the Gujarati Sahitya Parishad in 1959.
- He established Gandhi Vidyapith, Vedchhi in 1967 and served as its vice-chancellor.
- Mahatma Gandhi called him **Savai Gujarati** (a quarter more than a Gujarati).
## Other bodies and Acts
- National Human Rights Commission = statutory body under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.
- Law Commission of India = executive body; chief function is to recommend legal reforms.
- National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission = set up in 1988 under the Consumer Protection Act, 1986; quasi-judicial and statutory.
