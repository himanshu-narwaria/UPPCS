## Party system
- Party system is a part of the **political system**; government in a democracy is formed and run through the party system. {38}
- In India elections are held on the basis of Universal Adult Franchise.
- Political parties are free to set their own standards, subject to mandatory legal/registration conditions.
- **Inner-party democracy** = periodical elections within a party to elect its office bearers. {41}
- **Rajni Kothari** described India's party system in the early years of Independence as a one-party dominant system. {42}
- Rajni Kothari was a political scientist and writer; works: 'Politics in India', 'Caste in Indian Politics', 'Rethinking Democracy'.
- India has no fixed party system because there are too many political parties. {37}
- Indian democracy is in transition; casteism and regionalism still dominate; regional leaders form parties to meet various interests.
- Other socio-economic reasons also affect the multi-party system in India.
- India has a written Constitution; growth of strong regional parties shows regional aspirations; the two facts are unrelated. {39}
- Regional parties raise the region's problems and demand more facilities for it, so the regional public turns to them.
- **Pressure group** = organised group that does not put up candidates but seeks to influence government policy or legislation; e.g. a trade union. {40}
- Pressure groups are also called interest groups or lobby groups: trade unions, farmers' organisations, women's organisations, business organisations.
## Registration and recognition of parties
- Representation of the People Act, 1951 provides for registration of political parties; the Election Commission registers them. {1}
- Election Commission recognises parties as National or State parties under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968. {2,3,4}
- During the 1999 general elections there were six National and 48 State level recognised parties.
- National party condition 1: 6% of valid votes in four or more States (Lok Sabha or Assembly election) plus at least four Lok Sabha seats from any State or States. {6}
- National party condition 2: 2% of Lok Sabha seats (11 of 543), elected from at least three different States.
- National party condition 3: recognised as a State party in at least **four States**. {7}
- A party needs to fulfil any one of these three conditions to be a National party.
- State party condition: at least 3 seats or 3% of seats in the State Assembly, whichever is greater.
- State party condition: at least 1 Lok Sabha seat for every 25 seats (or fraction) allotted to that State.
- State party condition: 6% of valid votes in the latest Lok Sabha election plus one Lok Sabha seat.
- State party condition: 6% of valid votes in the latest Assembly election plus 2 Assembly seats. {9}
- State party condition: at least **8% of total votes** in the State, even without winning any Lok Sabha or Assembly seat. {8}
- A party needs to fulfil at least one of these conditions to be a State party.

## National and regional parties
- Presently **6 National parties**: INC, BJP, Bahujan Samaj Party, CPI (Marxist), Aam Aadmi Party, National People's Party.
- Muslim League, Revolutionary Socialist Party, All India Forward Bloc, and Peasants and Workers Party of India are not national parties. {12}
- Trinamool Congress (All India Trinamool Congress) got national party status on 2 September 2016; at present it is not a national party. {13}
- **Samajwadi Party** is not a national party; formed on 4 October 1992 in Lucknow under Mulayam Singh Yadav. {15}
- **Akali Dal** (Shiromani Akali Dal) is a regional party of Punjab. {16}
- **Telugu Desam Party** is a regional party of Andhra Pradesh. {17}
- Aam Aadmi Party was earlier a State-level party and also a registered party (all parties are registered); at present it is a national party. {18}
- In Kerala, Congress or CPI(M) (both national parties) have formed the government alternately; no regional party in power. {34}
- Under the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 Jammu and Kashmir is split into two UTs (J&K and Ladakh) and is not a State at present.
## Founding of parties
- **Indian National Congress** was founded in 1885; first president W.C. Banerjee.
- 'National' in its name reflects its all-India character; the word was influenced by the reaction against British rule. {10}
- **Bharatiya Janata Party** was founded in 1980; first president **Atal Bihari Vajpayee**. {14}
- BJP is the successor party of the Bharatiya Jana Sangh.
- Nationalist Congress Party was formed on 10 June 1999 by Sharad Pawar, P.A. Sangma and Tariq Anwar after a split in the Congress. {11}
- They were expelled from the INC after the dispute over Sonia Gandhi's foreign origin.
- Dr. B.R. Ambedkar founded Samata Sainik Dal, Depressed Classes Federation (1930), **Independent Labour Party** (August 1936) and Scheduled Castes Federation (1942). {19,20}
- Peasants and Workers Party of India was founded in 1948 in Maharashtra; a Marxist party, not founded by Ambedkar.
- Workers and Peasants Party was founded on 1 November 1925 by Kazi Nazrul Islam, Hemant Sarkar, Shamsuddin Hussain and Qutubuddin Ahmad.
- Workers and Peasants Party worked under the Indian National Congress from 1925 to 1929.
- Dr. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee (1901-1953) founded the Bharatiya Jana Sangh in 1951 and was its first President; Jagjivan Ram founded Congress for Democracy (CFD) in 1977. {21}
- Socialist Party's prominent leaders: Jai Prakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Acharya Narendra Dev.
- C. Rajagopalachari was the founder leader of the Swatantra Party, not the Socialist Party.
- Communist Party of India split into CPI and CPI(M) in **1964**, when a faction broke away. {24}
- Order of founding: Telugu Desam Party (1982) → Bahujan Samaj Party (1984) → Samajwadi Party (1992) → All India Trinamool Congress (1998). {26}

## Elections and governments
- Bahujan Samaj Party, Samata Party and Haryana Vikas Party were not part of the United Front Government of 1996-97; Asom Gana Parishad was. {28}
- BJP's Lok Sabha vote share: 7.4% (1984), 11.4% (1989), 22.4% (1991). {29}
- 2004 General Election (14th Lok Sabha): Congress 145 seats, BJP 138 seats, CPI(M) 43 seats. {30}
- 13th Lok Sabha seats: CPM 32, TDP 29 (not 30), BSP 14, BJD 10. {31}
- November 1989 election (9th Lok Sabha): Congress was the single largest party with 197 seats but could not form the government. {32}
- Janata Dal formed a coalition government with outside support of BJP and Left parties; V.P. Singh became Prime Minister.
- The 1989 elections marked the end of what political scientists called the 'Congress System'.
- Coalition governments may contain many parties, so the government must respect the feelings of all partner parties.
- BJP-led coalition's National Agenda had some policy guidance, promises and guiding principles; it also discussed many things in detail (both true, not cause and effect). {33}
- In India governments are formed on majority of seats, not majority of votes; winners are decided by relative majority of votes. {35}
- **First-past-the-post system**: candidate with the highest votes wins; percentage of total votes does not matter; causes vote-seat distortion. {52}
- FPTP is used for the State Legislative Assemblies and the Lok Sabha.
- Karnataka Assembly election December 1994: Janata Dal 36% votes, 116 seats; Congress 31%, 35 seats; BJP 20.4%, 40 seats.
- Articles 330 to 334 provide reservation for some special classes in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies; there is no provision for women.
- 33% reservation for women in Parliament and State Legislatures needs a Constitutional Amendment. {36}
- Parties can give 33% of the seats they contest to women candidates without any Constitutional amendment.
- To be recognised as the Opposition Party, a party needs at least 1/10th of Lok Sabha membership (54.3 of 543), i.e. at least 54 seats.
## Anti-defection law
- **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985** gave political parties constitutional status for the first time. {45}
- It added the **Tenth Schedule** (anti-defection law) and amended Articles 101, 102, 190 and 191.
- Anti-Defection Law was passed by Parliament on **15 February 1985** and came into force on 1 March 1985. {43}
- The law covers en-bloc defection in a party, smaller defection in a single spell, and members voting to topple or support a government.
- Large defections in a party in various spells are not covered by the law. {44}
- A merger is recognised when at least **two-thirds** of the members of the legislature party agree to it; members are then not disqualified. {46}
- Defection disqualification is decided by the Lok Sabha Speaker or Rajya Sabha Chairman, not by the President. {47}
- The term 'political party' is explicitly mentioned in the Tenth Schedule.
- A nominated legislator cannot join any political party after six months of being appointed; if he does, he can be disqualified.
- The law gives no time-frame within which the presiding officer must decide a defection case. {48}
- An MP or MLA is disqualified if he voluntarily gives up membership of his party.
- An MP or MLA is also disqualified for being absent from voting in the House on a particular issue.
- A member is not disqualified if there is a merger of political parties.
- A presiding officer who voluntarily leaves his party on election to the office is not disqualified, nor if he rejoins it after leaving office. {50}
- This exemption is given for the dignity and impartiality of the office.
- But the Lok Sabha Speaker can be disqualified if he becomes a member of another political party. {49}
- **Jammu and Kashmir** Assembly passed an Anti-Defection Law as early as 1979. {51}
- Under the J&K law an MLA who left his party ceased to be a member of the Assembly.
## Movements, plans and party base
- **'Bhu Poratam'** (struggle for land) movement was launched in **Andhra Pradesh**. {54}
- It was organised by the Bharatiya Khet Mazdoor Union (BKMU) to demand farm land and house sites for landless agricultural workers.
- The name 'Bhu-Poratam' was given by the Communist parties.
- Business class is not part of the social base of the Communist Party; the party opposes capitalism, which business supports. {55}
- **Kamaraj Plan** was prepared in 1963 by K. Kamaraj, then Congress President, to make the Indian National Congress vibrant. {56}
## Electoral Bond Scheme 2018
- Electoral Bond Scheme was notified by the Government of India on 2 January 2018.
- Aim: bring transparency in the funding of political parties.
- A citizen of India or a body incorporated or established in India may buy bonds; an individual may buy singly or jointly.
- Eligible parties: registered under Section 29A of RPA 1951 and securing not less than **1%** (not 2%) of votes in the last Lok Sabha or Assembly general election. {57}
- Electoral Bond is valid for **15 days** from the date of issue; no payment is made if deposited after validity expires.
- A bond deposited by an eligible party in its account is credited on the same day.
- State Bank of India is authorised to issue and encash Electoral Bonds through its 11 Authorised Branches.
