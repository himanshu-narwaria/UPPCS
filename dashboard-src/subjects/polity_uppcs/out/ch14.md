@ 14 | Directive Principles of State Policy
## Basics and nature
- Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are in **Part IV**, **Articles 36 to 51**.
- Idea of DPSP was borrowed from the **Irish Constitution** (Ireland). {17,18,19}
- **Article 37** deals with the application of the principles in Part IV.
- Article 37: DPSP are **non-justiciable** (not enforceable by any court), yet fundamental in the governance of the country; it is the State's duty to apply them in making laws. {5,7,8,9,21,22}
- Purpose of DPSP = to establish **social and economic democracy**; this helps India grow as a welfare state. {1,3,10}
- A religious State is not an objective of DPSP; the Constitution makers aimed at a secular and democratic state ensuring socio-economic justice. {2}
- DPSP embody the concept of a **Welfare State**; the welfare state concept is in Part IV (Articles 36 to 51). {12,13,14}
- The idea of India as a welfare State is found in both the Preamble and the DPSP. {15}
- Preamble gives importance to constituting India into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic; DPSP guide both the Centre and the states to establish a welfare State.
- Article 38(1): State shall promote people's welfare by securing a social order in which social, economic and political justice informs all institutions of national life.
- DPSP also include clear political and institutional objectives: Panchayati Raj, Uniform Civil Code, separation of judiciary from executive, international peace.
- DPSP are important for economic, social and political functions in a modern democratic State.
- DPSP are directions to the Legislature and the Executive showing how the State should use its legislative and executive power.
- DPSP are in nature an instrument of instruction to the Government to do certain things and achieve certain ends.
- DPSP create no justiciable rights for the individual and are not a limitation on the legislative or executive function of the Government. {6}
- DPSP, being non-enforceable, impose no legal duty on the State; they lay down moral ideals, are merely instructions to guide the legislature and executive, and are not binding on the State. {11}
- The Constitution has no classification of DPSP; by content and direction they fall in three categories: **socialistic, Gandhian, liberal-intellectual**. {16}
- **K.T. Shah**: DPSP is "a cheque which is paid at the Bank's convenience". {66}
## Fundamental Rights versus Directive Principles
| Point | Fundamental Rights | Directive Principles |
| Enforcement | Justiciable: legally enforceable by courts on violation | Non-justiciable: not legally enforceable by courts {4} |
| Nature | Mostly negative | Positive |
| Sanction | Legal sanctions | Moral and political sanctions |
| Aim | Political democracy | Social and economic democracy |
| Welfare | Welfare of the individual | Community welfare |
| Implementation | Automatically enforced | Need legislation for implementation |

## Article 39 and equal pay
- Article 39(a): citizens, men and women equally, have the right to an adequate means of livelihood.
- Article 39(b): ownership and control of the community's material resources distributed so as best to subserve the common good.
- Article 39(c): the economic system must not result in concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment; so concentration of wealth violates DPSP. {23}
- Article 39(d): **equal pay for equal work** for both men and women; it is a Directive Principle, not a Fundamental Right. {24,25,26,34}
- Article 39(e): health and strength of workers and the tender age of children not abused; citizens not forced by economic necessity into avocations unsuited to their age or strength.
- Article 39(f): children get opportunities to develop in a healthy manner, in freedom and dignity; childhood and youth protected against exploitation and moral and material abandonment.
- **Randhir Singh vs Union of India, 1982**: Supreme Court deduced Equal Pay for Equal Work as a fundamental right from Articles 14, 16 and 39(d) with the Preamble.
- Parliament passed the **Equal Remuneration Act, 1976** with respect to Article 39(d).
- Protection in respect of conviction for offences, protection of life and personal liberty, and protection of interests of minorities are not Directive Principles.
## Free legal aid and Legal Services Authorities
- **Article 39A** (equal justice and free legal aid) was added later, by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** (w.e.f. 3/1/1977). {58,59}
- Article 39A gives equal justice and free legal aid to the poor and weaker sections and ensures justice for all.
- **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)** was constituted under the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**.
- NALSA gives free legal services to weaker sections and organises Lok Adalats for amicable settlement of disputes.
- NALSA's objective: free and competent legal services to weaker sections on the basis of equal opportunity; it also instructs State Legal Services Authorities and gives them financial grants. {60}
- State Legal Services Authority is headed by the Chief Justice of the respective High Court, who is its Patron-in-Chief.
- Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act: entitled to free legal service = member of SC or ST; victim of human trafficking or begar (Article 23); woman or child; person with disability (Persons with Disabilities Act, 1995); person with annual income below the prescribed amount; industrial workman.
- The income limit may be any higher amount prescribed by the State government.
- Income ceiling under Section 12(h): Andhra Pradesh 3 lakh; Arunachal Pradesh 1 lakh; Gujarat 1 lakh; Karnataka 1 lakh; Madhya Pradesh 1 lakh.
- Delhi income ceiling: General 1 lakh; Senior Citizen 2 lakh; Transgender 2 lakh.
- Free legal services go to a person with annual income below Rs. 1,00,000 and a transgender below Rs. 2,00,000 (Delhi); not to OBCs as a class, and not to all senior citizens. {61}
- Senior citizens' eligibility for free legal aid depends on rules of the respective state government; in Delhi it is subject to an annual income ceiling.
## Panchayats, work and workers
- Panchayati Raj System is laid down under DPSP (Part IV), Article 40.
- Article 40: State shall organise village Panchayats and give them powers and authority needed to function as units of self-government.
- Article 33 = power of Parliament to modify Part III rights in their application to forces.
- **MGNREGA** (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) = Indian labour law and social security measure that aims to guarantee the "Right to Work".
- MGNREGA gives at least 100 days of employment in a financial year to every household whose adult member volunteers for unskilled manual work, for livelihood security in rural areas.
- MGNREGA aims to follow DPSP; right to employment is not enumerated in Part III. {37}
- Article 43: State shall secure to all workers (agricultural, industrial or otherwise) a living wage, decent conditions of work, full enjoyment of leisure, and social and cultural opportunities.
- MGNREGA is a flagship programme to achieve the goal of **Article 43**. {38}
- **Article 43A**: State shall take steps to secure participation of workers in the management of undertakings, establishments or other organisations engaged in any industry; added by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**. {39,40,48}
- Article 43B: State shall promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of co-operative societies.
- 42nd Amendment added Article 39(f) (protection of children and youth from exploitation), Article 39A and Article 43A; Uniform Civil Code (Article 44) was not added by it. {47}
- Article 44: State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India.
- **National Social Assistance Programme** was launched on 15 August, 1995 to fulfil DPSP on right to work, education and public assistance in certain cases. {67}
- NSAP included from the beginning: National Old Age Pension Scheme (NOAPS), National Family Benefit Scheme (NFBS), National Maternity Benefit Scheme (NMBS).
- On 1 April 2001, NMBS was made a part of the population control scheme.
## Gandhian principles
- Organisation of Village Panchayats and promotion of cottage industries in rural areas follow Gandhian principles; Uniform Civil Code (Liberal-Intellectual) and Right to work (Socialist) do not. {20,53}
- Securing reasonable leisure and cultural opportunities for all workers is a Directive Principle but not a Gandhian one.
- Articles 40, 47 and 48 are based on Gandhian principles.
- Article 48: prevention of slaughter of cows, calves and other milch and draught cattle and improvement of their breeds.
## Health, education, environment
- Article 47: State shall endeavour to prohibit consumption, except for medicinal purposes, of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health. {52,64}
- Prohibition of trafficking in human beings and forced labour (begar) = Article 23(1), a Fundamental Right.
- Prohibition of employment of children in factories or mines = Article 24; abolition of untouchability = Article 17; both are Fundamental Rights, not DPSP. {65}
- The Constitution grants the right against exploitation to children, women and tribals; the word 'Dalit' is not mentioned in the Constitution. {49}
- Free and compulsory education for all children up to age 14 was in Article 45; in 2002 the **86th Amendment** made it a Fundamental Right under **Article 21-A**. {50,51}
- Article 45 now: early childhood care and education to children below the age of six years.
- Adult education is not mentioned in the DPSP. {57}
- **Article 48A**: State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wild life; the same subject is a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A(g). {54,55,56}
- Article 51A(g) (Part IVA): duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures.
- Right to Information is not a Directive Principle; it is a legal right under the **Right to Information Act, 2005**. {62}
- Small family norm and education through mother tongue at primary level are not Directive Principles. {68}
- Article 350A directs every State and local authority to provide instruction in the mother-tongue at the primary stage to children of linguistic minority groups.
## Judiciary and international peace
- **Article 50**: State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State; so it is a Directive Principle. {42}
- **Article 51** deals with promotion of international peace and security; it is a Directive Principle and the Article related to Indian foreign policy. {43,44,45,46}
- Article 51: State shall endeavour to promote international peace and security; maintain just and honourable relations between nations; foster respect for international law and treaty obligations; encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration.
