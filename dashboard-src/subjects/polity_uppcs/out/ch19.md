@ 19 | The Attorney-General and the C.A.G.
## Attorney-General: office, appointment, qualification
- **Article 76** provides for the office of the Attorney-General (AG) for India.
- AG = **highest (first / top) law officer** of the country and of the Government of India. {5,6}
- AG is a part of the **Union Executive**.
- AG is appointed by the **President** [Article 76(1)], on the advice of the government. {3,4}
- Qualification: a person **qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court** [Article 76(1)]. {11,12,14}
- So (Article 124(3)) AG must be a citizen of India and: High Court judge for 5 years, or High Court advocate for 10 years, or an eminent jurist in the President's opinion.
- AG need not be a member of either House of Parliament; appointed by President with SC-judge qualifications. {13}
## Attorney-General: duties and rights
- Duty [Article 76(2)]: to advise the **Government of India** on legal matters referred to him by the President, and do other legal duties referred or assigned by the President. {1,8,23}
- AG = **chief legal adviser** to the Government of India and the top law officer who represents the government in court (its primary lawyer in the Supreme Court); not the Solicitor General, CJI or CAG. {7,10}
- AG has the **right of audience in all courts** in the territory of India in the performance of his duties [Article 76(3)]. {9}
- AG is not a full-time (whole-time) government servant/counsel; with permission he may do private legal practice.
## Attorney-General: tenure, removal, pay
- AG holds office **during the pleasure of the President** [Article 76(4)]; not of the Prime Minister. {2}
- AG has no security of tenure; the President can remove him at any time.
- The Constitution does not state the procedure and grounds for removal of AG; he is not removed like a Supreme Court judge, nor by impeachment by Parliament.
- AG gets such **remuneration as the President may determine** [Article 76(4)], not as Parliament may determine.
- The Constitution does not say that AG resigns when the government that appointed him resigns. {16}
## Attorney-General in Parliament (Article 88)
- **Article 88**: rights of Ministers and the Attorney-General with respect to the Houses of Parliament.
- Every Minister and the AG can speak and take part in proceedings of **either House**, any **joint sitting**, and any **Committee of Parliament** of which he is named a member, but **cannot vote**. {17,18,19,20,22}
- AG can take part in proceedings of both Houses of Parliament though **not a member**. {21,25}
- AG enjoys **all the privileges and immunities** available to a Member of Parliament. {15}
- The Vice-President is also not a member of Parliament, so he too can be an answer for "non-member who takes part in proceedings". {24,26}
- When a motion to remove the Vice-President is under consideration in Rajya Sabha, he does not remain ex-officio Chairman then; he can be part of the House and speak in it.
- The Vice-President participates only in the Rajya Sabha; for proceedings of **both** Houses the answer is the Attorney-General.
- Solicitor General is not given the right to participate in meetings of Parliament.
## Solicitor General of India
- Solicitor General of India = a **Legal Adviser**; second law officer of the country. {27,28}
- Solicitor General is subordinate to the Attorney-General and assists him.
- Solicitor General is himself assisted by several **Additional Solicitor-Generals** of India.
## Advocate-General of a State (Article 165)
- **Advocate-General** = legal adviser (first legal adviser) of the State Government; duty to advise the State Government on legal matters [Article 165(2)]. {29,30,31}
- Advocate-General also does other legal duties referred or assigned by the Governor, and functions conferred by the Constitution or any other law in force [Article 165(2)].
- Advocate-General is appointed by the **Governor** of the State [Article 165(1)], not by the President. {33}
- Qualification: a person qualified to be appointed a **Judge of a High Court** [Article 165(1)].
- Advocate-General holds office **during the pleasure of the Governor** [Article 165(3)], not of the Cabinet. {32}
- The post is created by the Constitution and corresponds to the Attorney-General at the Union level.
- **Article 177**: Advocate-General can speak and take part in proceedings of the State Legislature, but has no right to vote.
- Under the Civil Procedure Code, High Courts do not have original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction at State level.
- **Article 143**: advisory jurisdiction belongs to the Supreme Court.
## CAG: office and appointment
- The office of the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG) is created **by the Constitution**; Article 148 provides an independent office. {38}
- **Article 148(1)**: there shall be a CAG, appointed by the **President** by warrant under his hand and seal. {34,35}
- CAG is a constitutional functionary, independent of Parliament/legislature and the executive.
- CAG = head of the **Indian Audit and Accounts Department**.
- Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: CAG is probably the most important officer under the Constitution of India.
- **Girish Chandra Murmu** = 14th CAG of independent India. {61}
- Currently **K. Sanjay Murthy** = 15th CAG of India, appointed in 2024.

## CAG: tenure, salary, service conditions
- CAG holds office for **6 years or up to age 65 years**, whichever is earlier, under the CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971. {40,41}
- Salary and service conditions of CAG are given in the **Second Schedule** (Part E). {39}
- CAG's salary = salary of a **Judge of the Supreme Court** (Second Schedule and Section 3 of the CAG Act, 1971).
- The power to grant or refuse leave to the CAG vests in the **President**. {55}
- After ceasing to hold office, CAG is **not eligible for any further office** under the Government of India or of any State [Article 148(4)].
- Administrative expenses of the CAG's office (all salaries, allowances and pensions) are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India**, not subject to the vote of Parliament [Article 148(6)]. {53}
## CAG: removal
- CAG is removed only in the **like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court** [Article 148(1)]. {43}
- Grounds: **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**.
- Removal is by the President on a resolution (address) passed by both Houses of Parliament with special majority; the President alone cannot remove him. {42}
- Article 124(4): SC judge is removed by President's order after an address by each House, backed by majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting, presented in the same session.
## CAG: duties and audit
- CAG = **guardian of the public purse** (public expense). {59}
- CAG acts as the **guardian of public finance**: custodian and trustee of public money. {60}
- CAG must see that not a single penny of the Consolidated Fund of India or of any State is spent without proper authorization of the Legislature.
- CAG audits the whole financial system of the country at both levels: Centre and State.
- CAG audits all receipts and expenditures of the Government of India and of the State Governments.
- CAG is the apex authority for external and internal audits of the expenses of the Central and State Governments.
- The accounts of the States, along with those of the Union, are controlled by the CAG (not by Governor, Chief Minister or State Finance Secretary). {58}
- CAG's duty is to ensure not only legality of expenditure but also its propriety, because he upholds the Constitution and laws of Parliament in financial administration. {44}
- CAG audits: Ministries and departments of the Government of India and State Governments.
- CAG audits: Central and State Government Public Sector Undertakings and other autonomous bodies and authorities financed from government funds.
- CAG audits: receipts of the Union or States (all receipts payable into the Consolidated Fund of India); accounts, stores and stock of any Union or State office or department; companies and corporations. {46}
- CAG audits and reports on all expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India, the Contingency Funds and Public Accounts, and on all trading, manufacturing, profit and loss accounts.
- CAG does **not** control the receipt and issue of public money or ensure that public revenue is lodged in the Exchequer. {45}
- Broad aims of audit: legality, regularity, economy, efficiency and effectiveness of financial management and public administration.
- **Financial audit**: whether financial statements are properly prepared, complete and presented with adequate disclosures.
- **Compliance audit**: whether the Constitution, applicable laws, rules, regulations, orders and instructions of competent authority are complied with.
- **Performance audit**: how far an activity, programme or organisation works economically, efficiently and effectively.
- **Public Finance** = study of the Government's role in financial activities; a branch of Economics assessing government revenue and expenditure and their adjustment.
- Public Finance covers public expenditure, public revenue and financial administration; not the functioning of commercial banks. {63}
## CAG: Act of 1971 and 1976 amendment
- The CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act came into effect in **1971**.
- The Act was amended in **1976** to separate accounts from audit; CAG was relieved of preparing accounts and confined to audit. {56,57}
- The CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Amendment Act, 1976 came into force on **1 March 1976**.
- Earlier the CAG did both tasks; at the State level the CAG still does both accounting and auditing.
## CAG: reports and Public Accounts Committee
- CAG submits audit reports on Union accounts to the **President**, who lays them before both Houses of Parliament [Article 151(1)]. {36,37}
- CAG submits audit reports on State accounts to the **Governor**, who places them before the State Legislature [Article 151(2)].
- CAG submits **three audit reports** to the President: on appropriation accounts, on finance accounts, and on public undertakings.
- After the reports are laid, the Public Accounts Committee examines them and reports its findings to Parliament.
- **Parliament** has the ultimate responsibility to take action on CAG's reports; the Public Accounts Committee debates them extensively. {48}
- CAG is the **"friend, philosopher and guide"** of the Public Accounts Committee (PAC). {54}
- CAG attends the meetings (sittings) of the PAC and assists it in its deliberations. {49}
- Representatives of Ministries appear before the PAC when it examines Accounts and Audit Reports of their Ministries.
- CAG reports on execution of projects or programmes by ministries are discussed by the PAC; investigating agencies can press charges based on facts from CAG reports. {47}
- CAG does not exercise exchequer control during emergencies and has no judicial powers to prosecute in government company audits.
