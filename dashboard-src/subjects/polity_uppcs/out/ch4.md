@ 4 | Sources of the Constitution
## Supremacy and the 1935 Act
- The **Constitution** is supreme in the Indian political system; it is the supreme law of India. {1}
- It lays down fundamental political principles; structure, procedures, powers and duties of government institutions; fundamental rights, directive principles and citizens' duties.
- A major portion of the Constitution is borrowed from the **Government of India Act 1935** = "Blue Print of the Indian Constitution".
- The Constitution's framers took inspiration for many aspects from other countries' constitutions.
## Britain
- From Britain: rule of law, parliamentary form of government, single citizenship, bicameral legislature, law-making procedures, parliamentary privileges, prerogative writs.
- **Cabinet system** (Cabinet Government) = from the British Constitution. {19}
- Rule of Law = England.
## USA
- From the USA: Preamble, judicial review, independence of judiciary, Fundamental Rights, post of Vice-President, impeachment of President, removal of Supreme Court and High Court judges.
- **Fundamental Rights** are borrowed from the Constitution of the **USA** (U.S. Bill of Rights). {24,25,26,27}
- Fundamental Rights = essential rights everyone needs to live with honour and integrity; in **Part III, Articles 12–35**.
| Fundamental Right | Articles |
| Right to Equality | 14–18 |
| Right to Freedom | 19–22 |
| Right against Exploitation | 23–24 |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28 |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | 29–30 |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | 32 |
- **Judicial review** in India is adopted from the Constitution of the **USA**. {2}
- The system of judicial review exists in both India and the USA. {3}
- Judicial review = power of courts to examine actions of the legislative, executive and administrative arms for consistency with the Constitution.
- Actions found inconsistent are declared unconstitutional, so null and void.
- The Judiciary has the upper hand in interpreting the Constitution; it can overturn a legislative or executive order that conflicts with the Constitution.
- Common to Indian and American federal systems: a **federal Supreme Court to interpret the Constitution**. {4}
- USA has one judicial system at Union level and another at State level (dual judiciary); it has no three lists.
- India gives single citizenship; the US Constitution gives dual citizenship.
- The **US Constitution** is the world's first written Constitution; the tradition of written constitutions began in America. {29}
- The US Constitution is the world's shortest written Constitution, with only **7 Articles**.
- The Indian Constitution is the longest written Constitution in the world; originally **22 Parts, 395 Articles, 8 Schedules**.
- Fundamental Rights are borrowed from the US model, but this is not the only reason for the Constitution's length. {23}
## Ireland
- **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)** are inspired by the Constitution of **Ireland**. {12,13,14,15}
- DPSP are in **Part IV, Articles 36–51**.
- DPSP are not enforceable in court, but are fundamental in governance; the State has a duty to apply them in making laws for a just society.
- Within its economic capacity and development, the State shall secure adequate means of subsistence for all men and women and equitable wages for equal work.
- The State shall also secure the right to employment, education, and public assistance in unemployment, old age, illness, disablement or other justifiable needs.
- Method of election of the President = inspired by Ireland.
- Nomination of Rajya Sabha members for talent, qualification, experience and service = influenced by the **Irish Republic**. {16}
## Canada
- **Federation with a strong centre** (Union of States with greater powers to the Union; Union–State relations) = from **Canada**. {5,6,21}
- **Residuary powers vested in the centre** = from Canada, not Australia. {18}
- Appointment of Governors by the centre and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court = from Canada.
- Reserving of a bill by the Governor for the President's consideration = from **Canada**. {20}
- The concept of "a Union of States" comes from the **British North America Act 1867** (the Constitution of Canada). {7}
- **Article 1(1)**: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States".
- Constituent Assembly Debates vol. VII: the Drafting Committee followed the language of the British North America Act 1867 in describing India as a Union, though the constitution may be federal in structure.
## Australia
- The **Concurrent List** is borrowed from the Constitution of **Australia**. {8,9,10,11}
- Concurrent List = items on which both Union and States can make law; it is in the **Seventh Schedule**.
- Concurrent List originally had **47 items**; now **52** (items 11A, 17A, 17B, 20A, 33A added by amendments).
- Also from Australia: joint sitting of both Houses of Parliament, language of the Preamble, freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse.
## Other countries
- **Emergency provisions** (except financial emergency) = from **Germany** (Weimar Constitution). {17}
- **Procedure established by law** = from **Japan**.
- Procedure of constitutional amendment and election of Rajya Sabha members = from **South Africa**.
- **Fundamental Duties** = from the **USSR** (Russian / former Soviet Union) Constitution. {22}
- Fundamental Duties were added by the **42nd Amendment Act 1976**, on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**.
- Ideals of justice (social, economic and political) in the Preamble = from the USSR.
- Republican system and the principles of liberty, equality and fraternity in the Preamble = from **France**.
- The **Election Commission** in the Indian Constitution is not inspired by any other constitution of the world. {28}
