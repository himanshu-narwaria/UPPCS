@ 21 | The Parliament (1)
## Parliament: basics
- Parliament = legislative organ of the Union Government.
- **Articles 79 to 122** in **Part V** deal with the Parliament.
- **Article 79**: Parliament = the President + two Houses (House of the People and Council of States).
- Lok Sabha is also called the **"House of the People"**; its members are elected by direct election.
## Lok Sabha: strength
- Maximum strength of Lok Sabha now = **550** (Article 81): 530 from States + 20 from Union Territories. {12,15}
- Earlier maximum could be 552: 530 States + 20 UTs + 2 Anglo-Indians [Articles 81, 331].
- Article 81: not more than **530** members chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies in the States. {18}
- Article 81: not more than **20** members to represent Union Territories, chosen in the manner Parliament provides by law. {17,19}
- Article 81 stands as amended by the 31st Amendment Act, 1973 and the Goa, Daman and Diu Reorganisation Act, 1987.
- At present Lok Sabha has **543** members: States 524 + UTs 19. {16}
- UT seats in Lok Sabha (19): Delhi 7, Jammu & Kashmir 5, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu 2, Andaman and Nicobar 1, Chandigarh 1, Lakshadweep 1, Puducherry 1, Ladakh 1.
- UT seats in Rajya Sabha (8): Delhi 3, Puducherry 1, Jammu and Kashmir 4.
- **7th Amendment Act, 1956** fixed Lok Sabha members at 520; it and the **31st Amendment Act, 1973** raised the number of members elected from States. {13}
- **31st Amendment Act, 1973**: Lok Sabha strength raised from **525 to 545**. {14}
- 31st Amendment: members from States raised from 500 to 525; members from Union Territories cut from 25 to 20.
## Anglo-Indian nomination
- **Article 331**: President could nominate not more than **2** Anglo-Indian members to Lok Sabha if he felt the community was not adequately represented.
- The **104th Amendment Act, 2019** [126th Amendment Bill, 2019] did not extend the Anglo-Indian arrangement beyond **25 January 2020**; so the nomination has ended.
- In January 2020 Anglo-Indian reserved seats in Parliament and State Legislatures were discontinued by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019.
## Seat allocation and delimitation
- Lok Sabha seats are allotted to States on the basis of **population**. {6}
- Article 81: each State is divided into territorial constituencies so that the ratio of population to seats is, as far as practicable, the same throughout the State.
- This allocation procedure does not apply to a State with population up to six million (Article 81).
- State-wise allocation of Lok Sabha seats (Article 82) is based on the **1971 Census**. {7,8}
- **84th Amendment Act, 2001**: allocation frozen till **2026**; next assessment after 2026 (first Census after 2026). {9,10,11}
- 84th Amendment changed the Article 81(3) proviso: year 2000 replaced by 2026; reference to be read as the 1971 Census.
- Division of each State into territorial constituencies for Lok Sabha is done by the Delimitation Commission.

- Article 330(2): reserved seats in a State/UT bear, as nearly as may be, the same proportion to its total seats as SC/ST population bears to its total population.
- In 1996 West Bengal and erstwhile Andhra Pradesh had 42 Lok Sabha seats each; SC reserved seats were 8 and 6.
- After the 2008 delimitation, SC reserved seats in West Bengal and Andhra Pradesh became 10 and 7.
- After the 2008 delimitation: SC seats in Lok Sabha rose from 79 to **84**. {23}
- Delimitation Commission order 2008: 412 general, 84 SC and **47 ST** seats; earlier 79 SC and 41 ST.
- Reserved-seat members are elected by all voters of the constituency; there is no separate electorate.
- **Madhya Pradesh** has the most ST reserved seats in Lok Sabha: **6** (after Delimitation Commission 2008). {25,26,27}
- ST seats: Jharkhand 5, Odisha 5, Gujarat 4, Chhattisgarh 4, Maharashtra 4; Uttar Pradesh and Bihar have none.
- Till 2008 no ST reservation in Lok Sabha in: Arunachal Pradesh, Goa, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Karnataka, Kerala, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Punjab, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh.
- After the 2008 delimitation Karnataka and Meghalaya have 2 ST reserved seats each (both Meghalaya seats are ST).
- At present no ST reservation in Lok Sabha in **Kerala and Tamil Nadu**. {21}
- Till 2008 no SC and ST reservation in: Arunachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Goa, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Sikkim.
- At present no SC and ST reservation for Lok Sabha in **Arunachal Pradesh** and **Jammu & Kashmir** (UT). {22}
- UTs with no seats reserved for SC and ST: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh, Puducherry.
- Rajasthan: 25 Lok Sabha seats; 4 reserved for SCs and 3 for STs.
- **106th Amendment Act, 2023**: reserves one-third of all seats for women in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and Delhi Assembly, including SC and ST reserved seats.
## Membership and elections
- **Article 84(b)**: minimum age for Lok Sabha = **25 years**; for Council of States = 30 years. {1,2,3}
- Lok Sabha member must: be a citizen of India; be at least 25; make and subscribe an oath; hold other qualifications set by Parliament by law.
- **Article 326**: elections to Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies are on the basis of adult suffrage.
- **Article 324**: superintendence, direction and control of elections vest in the Election Commission; electoral rolls are prepared under its direction [324(1)], not the President's. {4}
- The Election Commission conducts Lok Sabha elections; on its request the President makes available the staff it needs.
- Section 4(d), Representation of the People Act, 1951: nomination for Lok Sabha can be filed by any citizen whose name is in the electoral roll of any parliamentary constituency. {5}
- Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections follow the **First Past the Post** system: candidate with highest votes wins; a majority (50%) is not needed. {70}
- A candidate who fails to get more than **one-sixth (1/6)** of valid votes polled loses the security deposit. {61}
- Earlier security deposit for Lok Sabha: general Rs. 10,000, SC/ST Rs. 5,000.
- Since 2010 (raised by the Election Commission): Lok Sabha general Rs. 25,000, SC/ST Rs. 12,500; Legislative Assembly general Rs. 10,000, SC/ST Rs. 5,000.
- January 2022: ECI raised Lok Sabha candidate expenditure limit from Rs. 70 lakh to **Rs. 95 lakh**.
- January 2022: State Assembly expenditure limit raised from Rs. 28 lakh to **Rs. 40 lakh**.
- Small States: Lok Sabha limit raised from Rs. 54 lakh to Rs. 75 lakh; Assembly limit from Rs. 20 lakh to Rs. 28 lakh (40 percent higher).
## Tenure, sessions, dissolution
- Normal tenure of Lok Sabha = **5 years** from the date of its first meeting [Article 83(2)], unless sooner dissolved.
- Article 83(2) proviso: during a Proclamation of Emergency Parliament may by law extend the term by **one year at a time**. {38}
- Such extension cannot continue beyond **6 months** after the Emergency has ceased to operate.
- Lok Sabha can be dissolved before its term [Article 85(2)(b)] by the **President on the recommendation of the Prime Minister or Council of Ministers**. {31,32}
- **Article 85**: sessions, prorogation and dissolution of Parliament.
- **Article 85(1)**: President summons each House at such time and place as he thinks fit; no minimum number of sitting days is fixed. {33}
- Gap between last sitting of one session and first sitting of the next cannot exceed **six months**; so at least **two sessions a year** are mandatory. {39}
- The Constitution fixes the maximum gap between two sessions, but no minimum gap; it does not provide for three sessions.
- Usually **three sessions** a year: Budget (February–May), Monsoon (July–August), Winter (November–December). {34}
- A sitting of Lok Sabha may be terminated by **adjournment, prorogation or dissolution**. {35}
- Adjournment / adjournment sine die is done by the Speaker.
- Prorogation and dissolution are done by the **President** [Article 85(2)], not by the leader of the House. {36,37}
- Summoning of the House means convocation; prorogation ends a session; dissolution terminates a House.
- **Article 100(3)**: quorum of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha = **1/10th** of the total members of the House. {40,41,42}
- Quorum = minimum number of members who must be present before the House can transact business.
- **Leader of the Lok Sabha = Prime Minister**; he is head of the Council of Ministers and leader of the majority party in Lok Sabha. {69}
- Article 75(1): the Prime Minister is appointed by the President.
- If the Prime Minister is not a member of the Lower House, he can nominate another minister as Leader of that House.
- 14th Lok Sabha: PM Manmohan Singh was a Rajya Sabha member; Leader of the Lok Sabha was Pranab Mukherjee.

## Lok Sabha history
- First Lok Sabha elections: **25 October 1951 to 21 February 1952**, for **489 seats**. {62}
- First meeting (first session) of the first Lok Sabha: **13 May 1952**.
- First Lok Sabha: Indian National Congress won 364 of 489 seats (single largest party); Communist Party of India was second with 16 seats.
- **5th Lok Sabha** had a tenure of about 6 years: 15 March 1971 to 18 January 1977. {63}
- 7th Lok Sabha election was held in **1980** (not 1982); 9th in 1989, 11th in 1996, 13th in 1999. {67}
- **9th Lok Sabha**: constituted 2 December 1989, dissolved **13 March 1991**. {64}
- **12th Lok Sabha**: elections in February–March 1998; constituted 10 March 1998; dissolved 26 April 1999. {65}
- **13th Lok Sabha**: constituted 10 October 1999, dissolved 6 February 2004; did not complete its 5-year term. {66}
- Around the 1999 General Elections the country's politics faced conflicts and clashes; in this turmoil the 13th Lok Sabha was dissolved early.
- Fewest women in Lok Sabha: **1977** (only 19). {106}
- Most women reached Lok Sabha in the General Election 2019: 78.
- 17th Lok Sabha: women were more than 14% of members; 78 women, 41 from BJP and 6 from Congress (INC).
- 17th Lok Sabha: most women elected from Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal (11 each).
## Speaker and Deputy Speaker: election and oath
- **Article 93**: Lok Sabha chooses two of its members as Speaker and Deputy Speaker, as soon as may be; and again whenever the office falls vacant. {71,72}
- The Speaker is chosen by all the then members of the Lok Sabha from among themselves.
- The Speaker must be a member of the House at the time of his election; a non-member cannot be chosen Speaker or Deputy Speaker. {84}
- By tradition the Speaker's post goes to the majority party and the Deputy Speaker's to the Opposition; the Constitution has no such provision.
- The Constitution does not require a member elected as Speaker to resign from his political party.
- The Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha **do not take an oath of office**. {73,74}

## Speaker: vacation, resignation, removal
- Speaker or Deputy Speaker vacates office if he ceases to be a member of the House [Article 94(a)].
- **Article 94(b)**: Speaker resigns in writing to the **Deputy Speaker**, and the Deputy Speaker to the Speaker. {75,76,82,83}
- **Article 94(c)**: Speaker/Deputy Speaker is removed by a Lok Sabha resolution passed by a **majority of all the then members**; **14 days' notice** is needed. {77,78,79}
- The Speaker does not hold office during the pleasure of the President.
- Article 94 (second proviso): on dissolution the Speaker does not vacate office until immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha. {81}
- **Article 96(1)**: while a resolution for his removal is under consideration, the Speaker (or Deputy Speaker) shall not preside though present; Article 95(2) applies as if he were absent.
- **Article 96(2)**: he can speak and take part in proceedings then, and can vote only in the first instance, not on equality of votes. {80}
- A Speaker whose position is challenged by a motion stays in office until the motion is disposed of [Article 96(1)].
- It is also stated that during the debate on a no-confidence motion against him the Speaker is not allowed to vote on that motion.
- On other matters the Speaker votes only in a tie (casting vote); he may also delegate this casting vote to other members of the House.
## Speaker: powers and voting
- **Article 100(1)**: questions at any sitting or joint sitting are decided by majority of members present and voting, excluding the Speaker or person acting as Chairman/Speaker.
- **Article 100(1)**: Speaker/Chairman does not vote in the first instance; he has a **casting vote** only in case of a tie (equality of votes). {88,89,90}
- President summons a joint sitting (Article 108); the **Speaker presides** over a joint sitting of both Houses [Article 118(4)]. {87}
- **Article 110(4)**: Speaker's signed certificate that it is a Money Bill is endorsed when the Bill goes to the Council of States (Article 109) and to the President for assent (Article 111).
- **Lok Sabha Secretariat** works under the direct control and direction of the **Speaker**. {102,103}
- The Secretariat has supreme authority over its employees, premises and assets of Parliament, exercised through the Secretary (Article 98 and rules 124 and 124-A).
- **Article 98(3)**: President makes rules on recruitment of secretarial staff of the House after consulting the Speaker. {104}
- Individual appointment orders are issued by the Speaker: Rule 6, Lok Sabha Secretariat (Recruitment and Conditions of Service) Rules, 1955.
## Pro-tem Speaker
- The Speaker of the last Lok Sabha vacates office immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha; so the President appoints a Pro-tem Speaker.
- Pro-tem Speaker = one of the **senior-most members** of the newly elected Lok Sabha. {86}
- Process: Legislative section of Parliament lists senior-most members → Minister of Parliamentary Affairs → note to President for approval.
- Pro-tem Speaker **administers the oath** to newly elected members and conducts proceedings till a regular Speaker is chosen. {85}
## Deputy Speaker
- Rule 8(1), Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha: election of Deputy Speaker is held on a date fixed by the **Speaker**; the Secretary conveys it to all members. {98}
- No mandatory provision that the Deputy Speaker must be from the principal opposition party or the ruling party.
- Article 95(1): in the Speaker's absence the Deputy Speaker chairs the House with the same power as the Speaker; no appeal lies against his rulings.
- Rule 8(2): the motion to appoint the Deputy Speaker is moved by one member and seconded by another (not by Speaker and Prime Minister).
- Article 95: when the Speaker's office is vacant the Deputy Speaker performs its duties; when presiding he can vote only on **equality of votes**. {100}
- M.A. (M. Ananthasayanam) Ayyangar = first Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha.
- P.M. Sayeed was Deputy Speaker in 1999.
- Deputy Speakers: 14th Lok Sabha = Charanjit Singh Atwal; 15th = Karia Munda; 16th = M. Thambidurai.
- The post of Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha is vacant since May 2019.
## Speakers of Lok Sabha
- **G.V. (Ganesh Vasudev) Mavalankar** = first Speaker of Lok Sabha, from 15 May 1952 till his death on 27 February 1956. {91,92,93}

- Speakers: 11th Lok Sabha = P.A. Sangma; 12th = G.M.C. Balayogi; 14th = Somnath Chatterjee; 17th = Om Birla. {97}

## Leader of the Opposition
- A "Leader of the Opposition" was formally recognised in Lok Sabha for the first time in **1969**. {108}
- Before 1969 the leader of the largest opposition party was called leader of the opposition without formal recognition, status or privilege.
- For recognition by the Speaker, the party needs at least **10%** of the total strength of Lok Sabha.
- 10% of 543 is given as 54.3, i.e. 54 members; elsewhere it is given as 55 seats.
- In 2014 and 2019 no opposition party got the minimum number; the post stayed vacant in the 16th and 17th Lok Sabha.
- In the 18th Lok Sabha **Rahul Gandhi** (Indian National Congress) became Leader of the Opposition.
