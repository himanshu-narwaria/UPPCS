@ 18 | The Council of Ministers
## Council of Ministers and Prime Minister: basics
- Article 300: the legal name of the Government of India is **'The Union of India'**. {1}
- Central Council of Ministers = Prime Minister, Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State and Ministers of State (Independent Charge).
- **Article 74(1)**: a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head aids and advises the President, who acts on such advice; so it is a constitutional body.
- President may require the Council to reconsider advice, but must act on the advice given after reconsideration (added by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978).
- Article 74(2): advice tendered by Ministers to the President cannot be inquired into in any court.
- Prime Minister is the real head of the Council of Ministers; real executive power is exercised by the Prime Minister (Article 75). {3,15}
- Prime Minister = Head of the (Central) Government; President = Head of the State. {5}
- Executive power of the Union is vested in the President (Article 53(1)), not the Prime Minister; President is the constitutional head of the Union executive.
- **Article 75(1)**: Prime Minister is appointed by the President; other Ministers are appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice. {2,4,18,22}
- Prime Minister has full discretion in choosing persons to serve as ministers in his Cabinet. {23}
- Article 75(2): Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President (not of the Prime Minister).
- President administers the oath of office and secrecy to the Prime Minister and other ministers.
- Prime Minister is the Chairman of Union Cabinet meetings; he submits his resignation to the President.
- Minimum age to become Prime Minister = 25 years, same as for a Lok Sabha member. {21}
- **Article 78**: duties of the Prime Minister: communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers on Union administration and proposals for legislation. {6,36}
- Article 78: PM furnishes information the President calls for; if President requires, submits to the Council a matter decided by a minister but not considered by the Council.
## Size and ranks of ministers
- **Article 75(1A)**: total Ministers including the Prime Minister cannot exceed **15%** of the total strength of the Lok Sabha; added by the **91st Amendment Act, 2003**. {8,72,73}
- 91st Amendment also added Article 164(1A): State ministers including the Chief Minister cannot exceed 15% of the Legislative Assembly's total members.
- The Constitution has no classification of ministers; the ranking is based on British Parliamentary tradition. {7}
- Ranks in practice: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (Independent Charge), Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers.
- **Cabinet** = Prime Minister and ministers of Cabinet rank only; it is the supreme decision-making body. {9,10}
- Union Council of Ministers exercises executive authority; the Union Cabinet is its senior executive body.
- The Council of Ministers seldom meets as a body; the Cabinet, an inner body, shapes government policy; Cabinet Ministers hold key portfolios.
- The word 'Cabinet' appears only in **Article 352(3)**, inserted by the 44th Amendment Act; it was not in the original Constitution. {11}
- Article 352(3): President shall not issue a Proclamation unless the Union Cabinet communicates in writing that it may be issued; 'Cabinet' appears twice in this Article.
## Membership of Parliament
- **Article 75(5)**: a non-member of Parliament can be a minister (or Prime Minister) but ceases to be one unless he becomes a member of either House within **six months**, by election or nomination. {20,24,25,26,27}
- The Constitution provides for a Parliamentary form of Government; Parliament provides a Cabinet; the Cabinet enjoys majority confidence in the popular chamber.
- Cabinet membership is not restricted to the Lower House; ministers can be from either House.
- Prime Minister is generally a member of Lok Sabha, but can be from Rajya Sabha. {19}
- Indira Gandhi was the first PM from Rajya Sabha at the time of appointment; later she became a Lok Sabha member.
- A minister can speak in both Houses but vote only in the House of which he is a member; a Rajya Sabha PM cannot vote on a no-confidence motion. {17}
## Collective responsibility and motions
- **Article 75(3)**: Council of Ministers (including PM) is collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha** only, not Rajya Sabha or Parliament as a whole; a constitutional obligation. {28,29,32,33,34,35}
- Article 74(3) does not exist in the Constitution.
- Where Lok Sabha is not an option, the nearest answer for responsibility of the Council is Parliament.
- Convention, not stated in the Constitution: Prime Minister resigns if he loses majority in the Lower House. {37}
- In Indian polity the executive is subordinate to the Legislature; Parliament is the supreme legislative body. {38}
- Ministerial responsibility: for every act of bureaucrats the concerned Minister is answerable to Parliament.
- Ministers make policy and civil servants adhere to it; Parliamentary government works on 'Cabinet Collective Responsibility'; both are true, but the second does not explain the first. {65}
- The Council of Ministers can move a **Confidence Motion**, to prove majority support in Lok Sabha. {39}
- No-Confidence Motion can be introduced only in Lok Sabha and is not mentioned in the Constitution; if passed, the Council must resign. {40,44}
- Neither Confidence nor No-Confidence Motion is mentioned in the Constitution; procedure is in Rule 198 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business of Lok Sabha.
- At least **50** members are needed to table a No-Confidence Motion against the Council of Ministers. {42,43}
- Rule 198(1)(b): written notice of the motion is given to the Secretary-General of the Lok Sabha.
- First no-confidence motion: 1963, third Lok Sabha, moved by Acharya J.B. Kripalani against Jawaharlal Nehru's government.
- Till 15 August 2023: 28 no-confidence motions (28th on the Manipur situation) and 11 confidence motions discussed.
- 14 of the 28 motions (50%) were discussed between 1965 and 1975; 12 of these were against Indira Gandhi's governments.
! The Commission deleted the 2023 Rajasthan question on wrong statements about the No-Confidence Motion.
- On resignation of the Council of Ministers, President's Rule is not imposed. {46}
- President asks the outgoing Council to continue till alternative arrangement, i.e. earliest General Election or formation of a new Government.
- A minister who resigns can make a statement in the House when allowed by the Speaker; he cannot be compelled to do so. {41}
## Ministries and allocation of business
- Article 77(1): all executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the name of the President.
- **Article 77(3)**: President makes rules for convenient transaction of government business and its allocation among Ministers. {16}
- Ministries/departments are created by the President on the PM's advice under the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, which the President made under Article 77.
- President assigns each Ministry to a Minister on the Prime Minister's advice; the PM does not create ministries on the Cabinet Secretary's advice. {13,14}
- Ministries at the Centre on 15 August 1947 = **18**; at present = 53. {74}
- **Ministry of Tribal Affairs** was set up in **1999** by bifurcating the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, for focused, integrated socio-economic development of Scheduled Tribes (the most underprivileged section). {75}
- It is the nodal Ministry for policy planning and coordination of programmes for development of Scheduled Tribes.
- Before it, tribal affairs were handled by different Ministries at different times.
- **Ministry of Minority Affairs** was carved out of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment on 29 January, 2006.
- Notified minority communities: Muslim, Christian, Buddhist, Sikhs, Parsis and Jain.
- Smriti Zubin Irani was Minister for Minority Affairs and John Barla the Minister of State (at the question's time). {81}
- Minister for Minority Affairs at present (2025) = Kiren Rijiju.
- After the 18th Lok Sabha elections, **Giriraj Singh** (MP from Begusarai, Bihar) became Union Minister of Textiles. {80}
- **White Paper** = official document of the Government of India or its Ministry explaining policy on a subject and collecting feedback before a bill. {82}
## Cabinet Secretariat and administration
- Cabinet Secretariat is directly under the Prime Minister; its administrative head, the Cabinet Secretary, is ex-officio chairman of the Civil Services Board. {69,84}
- Union Cabinet Secretariat is supervised by the Prime Minister's Office, not the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs. {70}
- Cabinet Secretary is the highest civil services officer of the central government. {85}
- Cabinet Secretariat is in the 1st Schedule of the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961.
- Its subjects: secretariat assistance to the Cabinet and Cabinet Committees; administration of the Rules of Business; inter-ministerial coordination. {86}
- Allocation of financial resources to Ministries is not a function of the Cabinet Secretariat.
- Its assistance: convening Cabinet meetings on PM's orders; preparing and circulating agenda and papers; recording discussions; circulating record after PM's approval; watching implementation.
- Inter-ministerial coordination: removing difficulties and differences, overcoming delays, coordinating administrative actions and policies.
- **Cabinet Committees** are not in the Constitution (extra-constitutional); Rules of Business provide for them; the PM sets them up as needed. {71}
- Cabinet Committees are mostly chaired by the PM; sometimes by other Cabinet Ministers, the Finance Minister or Home Minister.
- If the Prime Minister is a member of a Cabinet Committee, he is necessarily its Chairman.
- Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs is chaired by the Minister of Defence.
- Reorganisation of the Union Cabinet is based on the Report of **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar**. {83}
- Ayyangar's 1950 report 'Reorganization of the Machinery of Central Government' recommended grouping of ministries, better personnel capabilities and better O&M Division working. {12}
- It did not suggest a minister and secretary solely for administrative reform.
- Department of Personnel was created under the Cabinet Secretariat on 1 August 1970.
- 7 February 1973: Administrative Reforms work moved to it; renamed Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms.
- April 1977: this Department shifted from the Cabinet Secretariat to the Ministry of Home Affairs.
- March 1985: elevated to Ministry of Personnel and Training, Administrative Reforms, Public Grievances and Pensions.
- 10 December 1985: renamed Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, under the Prime Minister assisted by a Minister of State.
- Its three departments: Personnel and Training (DOPT); Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances; Pension and Pensioners Welfare.
- Split system in Indian administration relates to policy/implementation; it ensures efficient policy-making and implementation; the Ex-Secretariat system is based on it. {87}
- Prime Minister is the President of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research. {88}
- National Defence Committee is headed by the Prime Minister. {89}
- Office of the Parliamentary Secretary was created in 1951. {90}
! The printed answer option for the Parliamentary Secretary question is 1952, but the book's explanation says 1951.
## Deputy Prime Minister
- Office of Deputy Prime Minister is not in the Constitution: an extra-constitutional (non-constitutional) post created by political necessity. {67}
- By convention a Cabinet Minister can be declared Deputy PM, a political decision; it confers no power of the Prime Minister. {66}
- First Deputy Prime Minister = **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**, 15 August 1947 to 15 December 1950; also Minister of Home Affairs. {68}
- Patel integrated over 500 princely states into the Indian Union.

## Other persons and bodies
- **War Resisters International**: international anti-war association of more than 40 countries; founded 1921 at Bilthoven, Netherlands; headquarters London, U.K.
- Narayan Desai was chairman of War Resisters International from 1988 to 1991. {76}
- Nagendra Singh: President of the International Court of Justice, 1985 to 1988. {77}
- A.N. Roy was appointed Chief Justice of India on 26 April, 1973.
- R.K. Trivedi: Chief Election Commissioner from 18 June 1982 to 31 December 1985.
- Ashok Desai was Attorney-General of India.
- UIDAI was created in June 2009; first chairman = Nandan Nilekani; first Director General = **R.S. (Ram Sewak) Sharma**. {78}
- Since 12 July 2016 the UIDAI chairman's post became chief executive officer (CEO); present CEO = Bhuvnesh Kumar.
- **Sarosh Zaiwalla**: renowned international law expert; former member of the International Court of Arbitration of the ICC Paris (1990-2002). {79}
- Zaiwalla received India's Annual National Law Day Award from the Prime Minister for contribution to International Arbitration Law.
