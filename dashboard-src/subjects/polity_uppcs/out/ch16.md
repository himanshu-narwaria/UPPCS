@ 16 | The President
## Position of the President
- President = head of the Indian State (Republic), the first citizen, and symbol of unity, integrity and solidarity of the nation. {1,2}
- **Article 52**: there shall be a President of India.
- **Article 53(1)**: executive power of the Union is vested in the President; exercised by him directly or through officers subordinate to him, in accordance with the Constitution. {5,6,7,8,9,10,11}
- The President is the constitutional head of the Executive of the Union.
- The President's power is not unlimited: he must exercise it in accordance with the Constitution. {4}
- **Article 53(2)**: supreme command of the Defence Forces is vested in the President, but its exercise is regulated by law (not independent of legislative control). {13}
- President is the supreme commander of the defence forces: Army, Navy and Air Force.
- **Article 77**: all executive action (business) of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the President's name. {12}
- Executive power is concerned with carrying on the business of the Government.
- "He represents the nation but does not lead the nation" applies to the President; real executive power is with the Prime Minister, who leads the nation. {14}
- President of India is indirectly elected, British King is hereditary; U.S. President has more power than the Indian President, so the two offices are not similar. {3}
- President has titular (formal) and de-jure authority; constitutional and nominal authority. {109}
- **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** made the President bound by the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.
- **44th Amendment Act, 1978** (Janta Party Government): President may require the Council of Ministers to reconsider advice, but must act on the advice given after reconsideration.
- Article 74(1): President acts in accordance with the advice of the Union Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.
## Election: electoral college
- President is elected by **indirect election**, not directly by the people, through an electoral college. {15,16}
- **Article 54**: electoral college = elected members of both Houses of Parliament + elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (including Delhi and Puducherry). {18,19,23,24,25}
- **70th Amendment Act, 1992** (effective 01-06-1995) included elected members of the assemblies of NCT of Delhi and UT of Puducherry in the electoral college. {20}
- Elected members of the Legislative Assembly of UT of Jammu and Kashmir are not yet included in the electoral college.
- Members of State Legislative Councils (elected and nominated) do not take part; a Chief Minister who is a member of the Legislative Council cannot vote. {21,34}
- Nominated members of both Houses of Parliament and of State Legislative Assemblies do not participate in the election. {33}
- **Article 55(3)**: election by proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote; voting by secret ballot. {17}
- A dissolved Legislative Assembly cannot take part in the election.
- Election cannot be challenged or postponed on the ground that the electoral college is incomplete (any vacancy among its members): Article 71(4). {27}
- Election of the successor must be held before the term of the incumbent President expires.
- On expiry of his term, the President continues to hold office until his successor enters upon office.
## Election: value of votes
- Article 55: as far as practicable, uniformity in the scale of representation of different States, and parity between the States as a whole and the Union.
- Value of an MLA's vote = State population ÷ total elected MLAs of the State, then ÷ 1000 (multiples of one thousand, not one hundred). {31}
- Value of an MP's vote = total value of votes of all elected MLAs ÷ total number of elected MPs. {29}
- Value of each MLA's vote varies from State to State because population and number of elected members differ. {30}
- Vote value of each Lok Sabha member and each Rajya Sabha member is the same.
- More elective Assembly seats means lower value of each MLA's vote (seats are the denominator).
- MLA vote values: Uttar Pradesh 208, Kerala 152, Madhya Pradesh 131, Puducherry 16, Arunachal Pradesh 8.
- "Population" = **1971 census**, until figures of the first census after 2026 are published (84th Amendment Act, 2001; Explanation to Article 55; Article 81(3)). {35}
- Article 81(3): "population" means the population at the last preceding census of which relevant figures have been published.
- Presidential Election 2022: vote value of each Rajasthan MLA = **129** (1971 population 2,57,65,800 ÷ 200 MLAs = 1,28,829; ÷ 1000 = 128.82). {32}
- 2022 election: Droupadi Murmu (former Jharkhand Governor, NDA candidate) defeated opposition candidate Yashwant Sinha and became the 15th President.
- Droupadi Murmu = first tribal woman President (Santhal tribe) and the youngest President.
- Yashwant Sinha = former Union Finance Minister; the Murmu versus Sinha contest is also called the 16th Presidential election.
- Meera Kumar was the candidate of the United Progressive Alliance (UPA) in the 15th Presidential election. {132}
## Qualifications, nomination, conditions
- **Article 58(1)**: citizen of India; completed 35 years; qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha. {36,37}
- Citizenship by birth is not required; education is not a qualification.
- Article 58(2): must not hold any office of profit.
- Nomination must be subscribed by at least **50 electors as proposers and 50 electors as seconders**. {48}
- Security deposit = Rs. 15000; forfeited if the candidate is not elected and gets valid votes not exceeding one-sixth of the votes needed to win.
- **Article 59(1)**: President cannot be a member of Parliament or a State Legislature; a member elected President is deemed to have vacated the seat on the date he enters office. {38}
- **Article 60**: oath of office to the President is administered by the Chief Justice of India.
- President's salary at present = Rs. 5 lakh per month, paid from the Consolidated Fund of India.
- Article 361(2): no criminal proceedings can be instituted or continued against the President or a Governor in any court during his term.
- Article 361(4): civil proceedings can be instituted against the President or a Governor during his term after two months' written notice.
## Election disputes
- **Article 71(1)**: all doubts and disputes about the election of President or Vice-President are inquired into and decided by the Supreme Court; its decision is final. {41}
- Article 71(2): if the election is declared void by the Supreme Court, acts done before the date of the decision are not invalidated. {42}
- **V.V. Giri** appeared in person before the Supreme Court as incumbent President in a petition challenging his election for corrupt practices. {134}
- The bench headed by Justice S.M. Sikri dismissed the petition and upheld Giri's election.
- V.V. Giri had got the majority on counting of second preference votes.
## Term, re-election, resignation
- **Article 56(1)**: term of five years from the date on which he enters upon his office. {43}
- **Article 57**: a person who holds or has held office as President is eligible for re-election, any number of times. {39,40}
- **Article 56(1)(a)**: President resigns in writing addressed to the **Vice-President**. {44,45}
- Article 56(2): Vice-President immediately communicates the resignation to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
## Impeachment
- Article 56(1)(b): President can be removed before his term by **impeachment** by Parliament; the only ground (Article 61(1)) is **violation of the Constitution**. {46,47,51,57}
- **Article 61** lays down the procedure for impeachment of the President. {50,53,54,55}
- The Constitution does not define "violation of the Constitution".
- The charge can be preferred (initiated) by either House of Parliament. {52,56}
- At least **14 days'** prior written notice is needed, signed by not less than one-fourth of the total members of the House: Article 61(2)(a). {58}
- Article 61(2)(b): resolution must be passed by not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House.
- Article 61(3): the other House investigates the charge; President has the right to appear and be represented.
- If the other House also passes the resolution by two-thirds of total membership, President stands removed from the date the resolution is passed.
- Impeachment is a **quasi-judicial procedure** in Parliament. {61}
- State Legislative Assemblies are part of the electoral college but not of the impeachment process. {59,60}
- No President has so far been impeached.
## Vacancy
- **Article 62(2)**: a vacancy by death, resignation, removal or otherwise must be filled within **six months** of its occurrence; so the Vice-President can act for at most 6 months. {62,63,64,65}
- **Article 65(1)**: on such vacancy the **Vice-President** acts as President until a new President enters office. {66}
- When the President cannot function due to absence, illness or other cause, Vice-President discharges his functions until he resumes.
- If offices of both President and Vice-President are vacant, the **Chief Justice of India** acts as President: President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969. {67,68,69,70}
- In the absence of the CJI, the senior-most Supreme Court judge available performs the President's functions.
## Parliament: summoning, prorogation, dissolution
- **Article 79**: Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; President is a constituent part of Parliament because a Bill cannot become law without his assent. {81}
- President addresses both Houses at a joint meeting every year but does not participate in Parliamentary discussions. {80}
- The Union Cabinet prepares the President's address to Parliament, as real executive power vests in it. {113}
- Article 85: President summons and prorogues either House and can dissolve the Lok Sabha.
- **Article 85(2)(b)**: President may dissolve the Lok Sabha, on the recommendation of the Union Cabinet (Council of Ministers). {72,73}
- Prorogation = termination of a session by President's order under Article 85(2)(a); it needs the advice of the Council of Ministers.
- Prorogation normally follows adjournment sine die, but the President can prorogue a House in session; dissolution is done on Council of Ministers' advice save in exceptional circumstances. {74}
- Time-lag between adjournment sine die and prorogation is generally two to four days; sometimes the same day.
- Article 83(2): Lok Sabha continues five years from the date of its first meeting unless sooner dissolved; expiry operates as dissolution.
- President may at his discretion look for an alternate government if the present government cannot prove majority in Lok Sabha.
- **Article 108**: President summons a joint sitting of both Houses. {75,76,77}
- Article 118(4): the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting.
- Joint sitting cases: Bill rejected by the other House; final disagreement on amendments; more than six months elapse without the other House passing it.
- President notifies his **intention** to summon a joint sitting: by message if Houses are sitting, by public notification if not. {78,79}
- No joint sitting if the Bill has lapsed by dissolution of the Lok Sabha.
- **Article 86(2)**: President may send messages to either House, on a pending Bill or otherwise; the House must consider it with all convenient dispatch.
- Sending messages to Parliament is literally a right of the President, not a power. {103}
## Assent to Bills and veto
- A Bill passed by Parliament becomes an Act only after the President's assent (Article 111). {82}
- **Article 111**: President declares that he assents to the Bill or withholds assent. {85,86}
- President may return a Bill (not a Money Bill) for reconsideration; if passed again, with or without amendments, he must give assent.
- The Constitution prescribes no time-limit for the President to decide on a Bill presented for assent.
- President cannot return a Money Bill or Finance Bill for reconsideration. {111}
- Money Bill is introduced only in Lok Sabha and only with the President's prior recommendation; it cannot be introduced in the Council of States.
- Returning an ordinary Bill, dissolving the Lok Sabha and appointing the Prime Minister are constitutional prerogatives of the President.
- **Pocket veto** = President takes no action on a Bill; used only once, by **Gyani Zail Singh** in 1986 on the Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill. {83,84}
- Dr. Rajendra Prasad applied an absolute veto on the PEPSU Appropriation Bill 1954, on the advice of the Council of Ministers.
- Pocket veto is used at the President's discretion; absolute veto is subject to the discretion of the Union Council of Ministers.
- Discretionary powers of the President: to return a Bill with objections, to withhold a Bill, to send messages to Parliament. {87}
- Discretionary powers also: to detain a bill, to ask the Prime Minister for information, to ask the Council of Ministers to reconsider advice. {90}
- Not discretionary: appointing the majority party leader as Prime Minister, summoning Parliament, appointing ministers, assenting to a bill referred by a Governor.
- Appointing judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts and the Council of Ministers = executive power; summoning a joint session = legislative power.
- It is not essential for the President to take the Council of Ministers' advice when assenting to Bills (answer as per Chhattisgarh PSC). {88}
- Advice is essential for: appointing Ambassadors, assent to Bills referred by Governors, dissolving Lok Sabha, assent to a constitutional amendment bill.
## Ordinances
- **Article 123**: President can promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session (except when both Houses are in session); a legislative power. {89,91,93}
- He must be satisfied that circumstances make immediate action necessary.
- In 2002 the President assented under Article 123 to the Ordinance on electoral reforms when the Union Cabinet sent it back unchanged.
- Article 123(2): an Ordinance has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament.
- Ordinance must be laid before both Houses and approved within **six weeks** of reassembly; otherwise it ceases to operate at the end of six weeks. {94}
- President can withdraw an ordinance at any time. {92}
- Scope of an ordinance = ordinary legislative powers of Parliament: it can amend any Central Act but not the Constitution. {95}
- An ordinance has the same constitutional limits as an Act; it cannot take away or abridge a Fundamental Right in violation of the basic structure.
- Like any Act, an ordinance can be made effective retrospectively (from a back date).
! For the 2025 question on ordinances, the printed answer is "I and II only", but the book's own explanation says only statements I and III are correct.
## Appointments
- President appoints the Chairman of the Finance Commission (Article 280(1)) and the Chief Minister of a Union Territory (Article 239AA(5)). {96}
- Article 239AA(5): other UT Ministers are appointed by the President on the Chief Minister's advice; Ministers hold office during the President's pleasure.
- Vice-Chairman of the Planning Commission was appointed by the Prime Minister; Planning Commission is now replaced by NITI Aayog.
- President appoints the Attorney General (Article 76), the Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148) and the Governor of a State (Article 155). {97}
- Vice-President is not appointed by the President; he is elected under Article 66. {98}
- President appoints the Prime Minister, the Governor and the Chief Election Commissioner.
- **Article 160**: President may make provisions for discharge of a Governor's duties in any contingency. {99}
- Chief Minister of a State is appointed by the Governor (Article 164(1)), not by the President. {100}
- President cannot on his own remove a Supreme Court judge. {104}
- Article 124(4): SC judge removed by President's order after an address by each House, passed by majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting.
- Such address is presented in the same session, on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
## Judicial and other powers
- **Article 143**: President can consult the Supreme Court on a question of law or fact. {101}
- President is not bound to accept the Supreme Court's advice. {102}
- **Article 72**: President can grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment, or suspend, remit or commute sentences. {107}
- Pardoning power is a judicial power of the President. {105}
- Detention and continuation are not part of the pardoning power. {106}
- Article 72 applies to: offence against a Union law; punishment by a court-martial (military court); sentence of death.
- President's clemency power is subject to limited judicial review; he cannot use it without the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers. {108}
- President must lay before Parliament: Finance Commission recommendations (Article 281), CAG report (Article 151(1)), report of National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338(6)). {110}
- Public Accounts Committee is a committee of selected MPs constituted by Parliament; its chairman is appointed by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
- President can declare any area a **Scheduled Area** (Fifth Schedule); he has powers over administration of scheduled and tribal areas. {112}
- President may order that the whole or part of a Scheduled Area ceases to be one.
- President may increase the area of a Scheduled Area after consulting the Governor of that State.
- President may alter a Scheduled Area only by way of rectification of boundaries.
- On alteration of State boundary, admission or creation of a State, President may declare territory not earlier in any State as a Scheduled Area.
- President has emergency powers and the power to extend President's Rule in States.

## Facts about individual Presidents
- First President **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** hailed from Bihar; born 3 December, 1884 in a village of Siwan district. {114,115}
- Dr. Rajendra Prasad completed two successive terms; he served three terms: 1950-1952, 1952-1957, 1957-1962. {116}
- **Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan**, second President (1962-1967), is known as the Philosopher King or Philosopher Ruler. {126}
- **Dr. Zakir Husain**: Governor of Bihar 6 July 1957 – 11 May 1962; Vice-President 13 May 1962 – 12 May 1967; President 13 May 1967 – 3 May 1969 (till death). {133}
- **V.V. Giri** was the 4th President (1969-1974); by tenure the 6th. {117}
- V.V. Giri was associated with the trade union movement; chairman of the All India Trade Union Congress for two terms. {127}
- Former Chief Justice **M. Hidayatullah** was acting President from 20 July 1969 to 24 August 1969. {129}
- **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy** (1977-1982), sixth President, is the only President elected unanimously (unopposed). {118,119}
- Neelam Sanjiva Reddy had not been Vice-President before becoming President. {122,123}
- Neelam Sanjiva Reddy was twice Speaker of Lok Sabha: 17 March 1967 – 19 July 1969 and 26 March 1977 – 13 July 1977. {124,125}
- Radhakrishnan, Zakir Husain, R. Venkatraman and Shankar Dayal Sharma were Vice-Presidents before becoming President.
- **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** (2002-2007), 11th President as an individual, was called the 'Missile Man' for work on Ballistic Missile and Satellite Launch Vehicle technology. {128}
- **Pratibha Devi Singh Patil** took oath on 25 July 2007 as the 12th President and first woman President; 14th by tenure order. {131}
- Jawaharlal Nehru was Prime Minister, not President; Rajendra Prasad, Radhakrishnan and Zakir Husain were Presidents. {130}
- **Rashtrapati Bhavan**, official home of the President, was designed by British architect **Edwin Lutyens**. {135}
- It was called Viceroy House till 1947; on 15 August 1947 it was renamed 'Government House'.
- C. Rajgopalachari became the first Indian to reside in Government House, on 21 June 1948.
