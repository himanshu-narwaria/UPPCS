## Article 370 and Jammu and Kashmir
- **Article 370** was a temporary provision relating to the State of Jammu and Kashmir. {8,9}
- Article 370 gave special status to Jammu and Kashmir; currently no special status is granted to it.
- Jammu and Kashmir had its own separate Constitution; it was the one exception to 'one Constitution for Centre and States'.
- Its administration and provisions were different from those of other States.
- J&K was excluded from the definition of 'State' in Article 152 (Chapter 1 of Part VI).
- Earlier, Article 1 and Article 370 applied on their own to Jammu and Kashmir.
- Article 1 declared Jammu and Kashmir an integral part of Indian territory.
- Article 370 was declared inoperative on **6 August 2019** by a Presidential notification on the recommendation of Parliament.
- Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 split the State into two Union Territories: Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh.
- Jammu and Kashmir came into existence as a Union Territory on 31 October 2019.
- Article 1 = India is a Union of States; Article 360 = Financial Emergency.
- Article 270 = taxes levied and collected by the Union and distributed between Union and States.
## Jammu and Kashmir timeline
- Maharaja Hari Singh signed the Instrument of Accession on 26 October 1947.
- Order of events: Instrument of Accession → Constitution of India enforced → accession ratified by J&K Constituent Assembly → J&K Constitution in force. {13}
- The Constitution of India was enforced on 26 November 1950 (date as printed).
- J&K Constituent Assembly ratified accession to India and adopted the J&K Constitution on 17 November 1956.
- Constitution of Jammu and Kashmir came into force on 26 January 1957.
- J&K Constituent Assembly abolished the Maharaja's hereditary rule and elected Karan Singh as first Sadr-e-Riyasat in 1951.
- 'Sadr-e-Riyasat' was renamed 'Governor' by the Sixth Amendment Act, 1965 of the J&K Constitution.
## Special Category Status
- Special Category Status (SCS) is granted by the Central Government to help States facing geographical and socio-economic challenges.
- The Constitution does not provide for SCS; it began on the recommendation of the **Fifth Finance Commission in 1969**.
- First States to get SCS in 1969: Jammu and Kashmir, Assam, Nagaland.
- Earlier SCS for plan assistance was granted by the National Development Council of the erstwhile Planning Commission.
- Eleven States given SCS: Assam, Nagaland, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Uttarakhand, Telangana.
- Telangana, the newest State, got SCS as it was carved out of Andhra Pradesh.
- 14th Finance Commission discontinued SCS for States, except Northeastern States and three hill States.
- SCS parameters (Gadgil Formula): hilly terrain, low population density or significant tribal population, strategic border location, economic and infrastructural backwardness, weak state finances.
- A sizeable SC and ST population is not an accepted parameter for special status. {6}
- **Bihar** was never granted SCS; Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir were. {14}
