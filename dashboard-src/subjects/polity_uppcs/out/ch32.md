@ 32 | The Finance Commission
## Constitution and composition
- **Article 280** provides for the Finance Commission, constituted by the President by order. {2,3,4}
- Article 280(1): the President constitutes it within two years of the commencement of the Constitution and thereafter at the expiration of every **fifth year**, or earlier if he considers necessary. {9,10}
- The Finance Commission consists of a **Chairman and four other members** (total 5), appointed by the President. {13,14,26}
- Article 280(2): **Parliament by law** determines the qualifications of the members and the manner of their selection; the President only appoints them. {11,12}
- Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951: the Chairman is selected from persons with experience in public affairs.
- The four other members are selected from persons who: (a) are, have been or are qualified to be a High Court Judge; or (b) have specialised knowledge of government finance and accounts; or (c) have wide experience in financial matters and administration; or (d) have special knowledge of economics.
- It is not necessary that at least one member be a Judge of a High Court or the Supreme Court.
- Section 6 of the Act: the Chairman of the Commission can be reappointed.
- The Commission makes its recommendations to the President (Article 280(3)); it does not report to NITI Aayog.
- The recommendations of the Finance Commission are only advisory in nature.
- **Article 281**: the **President** causes every recommendation of the Finance Commission to be laid before each House of Parliament. {27}
## Functions
- The Finance Commission recommends the distribution of the net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States; the federal Finance Commission relates to finances between the Centre and the States. {1,5,6,7,8,19,20}
- It recommends the allocation among the States of their respective shares of tax proceeds. {15}
- It recommends the principles governing **grants-in-aid** of State revenues out of the Consolidated Fund of India (Article 275); with tax sharing, this is its main function. {16,17}
- It recommends measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of Panchayats and Municipalities, on the basis of the State Finance Commission's recommendations.
- It also deals with any other matter referred by the President in the interest of sound finance.
- Allowing withdrawal of money from the Consolidated Fund of India is the function of Parliament, not the Finance Commission.
- Overseeing whether the Union and State Governments collect taxes as per the budget is the task of the Finance Ministry.
- The Finance Commission does not encourage inflow of foreign capital, distribute finances among Public Sector Undertakings, or ensure transparency in financial administration. {18}
- The Inter-State Council is for coordination and cooperation among States.
- The main function of the Planning Commission was to allocate resources between the Centre and the States; it has been replaced by NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India).

- The 15th Finance Commission submitted two reports: the first for the financial year 2020-21 and the final report for 2021-26.
## State Finance Commission
- The **State Finance Commission** is a constitutional body, constituted by the Governor under **Article 243-I**. {28,29,30}
- The Governor constitutes it within one year of the commencement of the 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 and thereafter every fifth year.
- It reviews the financial position of the Panchayats and makes recommendations to the Governor.
- The Union Finance Commission can recommend for Panchayats under Article 280(3)(bb) and for Municipalities under Article 280(3)(c), on the basis of the State Finance Commission's recommendations.
