## Early ideas and background
- Sir **Charles Metcalfe**, acting Governor-General, wrote in 1830 that village communities are "little republics", having nearly everything within themselves and almost independent of foreign relations. {1}
- **Lord Ripon** (appointed Viceroy in 1880) is regarded as the Father of local self-government in India. {2}
- Ripon formulated local self-government and laid the foundations of representative institutions; his scheme developed the municipal institutions.
- The first Municipal Corporation in India was established in **Madras in 1688**.
- **Mahatma Gandhi** suggested the name "Panchayati Raj" for rural local governments. {98}
- Gandhi wanted each village to be a little republic, self-sufficient in its vital wants and free to decide local affairs.
- **Article 40** (a Directive Principle) directs the State to organise village Panchayats and give them powers to function as units of self-government. {3,4}
- The Panchayat system has been an integral part of Indian village life through the ages.
## Community development and the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
- The **Community Development Programme (CDP)** was started in 1952 as a pilot project; first major rural development programme after independence.
- The **National Extension Service (NES)**, started in 1953, was the first programme of community development. {151,222}
- NES was formulated in April 1953 and inaugurated in October 1953.
- NES was formed because CDP (1952) could not be implemented all over India due to shortage of funds.
- Three phases of community development: National Extension Phase, Intensive Community Development Project Phase, Post-Intensive Development Phase.
- The **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** was set up in January 1957 (by the National Development Council) to examine CDP and NES; it reported in November 1957.
- It recommended "democratic decentralisation" (Panchayati Raj) with a **three-tier system**: Gram Panchayat (village), Panchayat Samiti (block), Zila Parishad (district); Nyaya Panchayat is not a tier. {99,100,101,103,104,105,108,109,116,117,118,119,120}
- **Balwant Rai Mehta** is called the Architect of Panchayati Raj in India. {107}
- Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru inaugurated Panchayati Raj on **2 October 1959 at Nagaur, Rajasthan**; Rajasthan was the first State to adopt it. {102,111,112,114,115}
- **Andhra Pradesh** was the second State to establish Panchayati Raj. {110}

## Aims and nature of Panchayati Raj
- Panchayati Raj = local self-governance, a system of **democratic decentralisation** (Balwant Rai Mehta's phrase), three-tier governance with bio-relations. {82,84,85,87}
- Its principal aim is to decentralise power to villagers so they make and implement policies for their own needs.
- Panchayati Raj is called **Grass-Root Democracy** because it empowers local units by decentralising power. {81}
- Its main objective: people's participation in development and democratic decentralisation; bringing development to the grass-root level. {83}
- Its main purpose is to enable people to participate in developmental administration. {90}
- Panchayati Raj represents decentralisation of power, community development and participation of the people. {92}
- It has three tiers, aims at economic development and social justice for villages, and some MPs are representatives in the Zila Parishad. {88}
- Panchayati Raj needs the full co-operation of the **local public** to work properly. {91}
- Attributes of local government: statutory status, participation of the local community, power to raise finances by taxation; **central control** is not one. {86}
- Decentralisation = delegating decision-making to the lowest possible level; features: autonomy, people's participation, empowering local communities (not non-confidence among them). {94}
- Under decentralisation the central unit distributes functions, responsibility and accountability to regional and local units; it extends delegation.
- Decentralisation is the process by which power is divided among many units; local self-government helps manage local affairs.
- Local bodies have representatives elected by local people, who understand grass-root issues better.
- **Judicial review** is a power of the judiciary, not of Panchayati Raj Institutions; implementing land reforms and poverty alleviation programmes are. {38}
- Under judicial review, legislative and executive actions are subject to review by the Supreme Court.
- **Maintenance of public order** is not a concern of local self-government; public health, sanitation, public utility services, roads and bridges, development schemes are. {36,37}
- The State Government controls local bodies in legislative, financial and personnel matters, but has no authority over citizens' complaints. {96,97}
- There are separate provisions for local governance in the Constitution: Article 40, Part IX and Part IX-A. {160}
- The 73rd Amendment rejuvenated rural local governance institutions and reduced political interference in their management. {95}
## The 73rd Amendment
- The **64th Amendment Bill** (prepared 1989, introduced in Lok Sabha in July 1989) was the first attempt to give constitutional status to PRIs. {9}
- The **73rd Amendment Act, 1992** gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions as institutions of self-government for rural India. {5,10,11,12,14,41}
- It came into force on **24 April 1993**, after ratification by the required number of States. {6,7,89}
- It added Part IX "The Panchayats" (**Articles 243 to 243-O**, 16 new Articles) and the 11th Schedule. {13}
- The 73rd and 74th Amendments deal with local governance institutions and added a third tier of governance; they mark a watershed in local self-government. {15}
- The 73rd and 74th Amendments introduced the three-tier governance system in the Constitution; this system is taken as copied from the Constitution of Australia. {8}
! The same Chhattisgarh question also treats "74th Amendment gives constitutional recognition to Panchayats" as true, though the book elsewhere links the 73rd to Panchayats and the 74th to Municipalities.
- Both were made in 1992 when **P.V. Narasimha Rao** was Prime Minister. {16}
- The 73rd Amendment lays the foundation of strong and vibrant Panchayati Raj Institutions; the 74th refers to Municipalities. {79}
- It aims to establish a local self-governance system under Panchayati Raj. {80}
- The 73rd Amendment gave a practical shape to Article 40.
- It provides: regular elections (Article 243K), not less than one-third seats for women (243D), transfer of funds recommended by the State Finance Commission (243I), transfer of 11th Schedule subjects. {18}
- It made Panchayat elections mandatory and set up State Election Commissions, to make them as fair as Lok Sabha and Assembly polls. {159}
- Regular remuneration to Panchayat members is not a provision of the 73rd Amendment. {53}
- Its other new provisions: collective responsibilities in agriculture, rural development, primary education and social forestry; mandatory elections for vacant posts; one-third representation of women.
- After the 73rd Amendment, decision-making powers, administrative powers and the system as a whole are decentralised; judicial power was not. {93}
- The 73rd and 74th Acts give only a basic framework; giving it practical shape rests with the States.
- The 73rd Amendment Act was passed in 1992; it let State governments formalise Gram Panchayats and make them work as self-governing bodies.
- Provisions on Panchayats came by the 73rd Amendment; the 74th Amendment relates to Municipalities. {49}
- 73rd covers Gram Panchayat, Kshetra Panchayat and Zila Parishad; Nagar Panchayat, Nagar Palika Parishad and Nagar Nigam are under the 74th. {141}
- **Madhya Pradesh** was the first State to implement Panchayati Raj in the true spirit of the 73rd Amendment. {50}
- **National Panchayati Raj Day** = 24 April every year; first celebrated in 2010. {51}
- National Panchayati Raj Day 2025 (32nd anniversary) main event: Lohna Uttar Gram Panchayat, **Madhubani** district, Bihar, on 24 April 2025. {52}
- Panchayati Raj is a **State List** subject (entry 5, 7th Schedule). {44,45,46,47}
- The decision to hold Panchayat elections is taken by the **State Government**, which conducts them through the State Election Commission. {42,43}
- The State Legislature decides by law the structure of Panchayats and all matters relating to Panchayat elections.

## Structure and composition of Panchayats
- **Part IX** envisages a three-tier system of Panchayats in every State (Article 243B). {137}
- Three tiers: Gram Panchayat (village), Panchayat Samiti/Block Panchayat (intermediate/block), Zila Parishad (district). {25,26,138}
- Article 243B(1): Panchayats shall be constituted in every State at village, intermediate and district levels. {29}
- Article 243B(2): intermediate-level Panchayats may not be constituted in a State with population not exceeding **20 lakh**; so not every State must have all three. {24,27,140}
- Article 243C(1): the **State Legislature** may by law provide for the composition of Panchayats. {23}
- Article 243C(2): all seats are filled by direct election from territorial constituencies, with the population-to-seat ratio the same throughout the Panchayat area as far as practicable.
- The territory of a Kshetra Panchayat and all other Panchayats is determined by the **State Government**. {142}
- Gram Panchayats are the smallest unit of local self-governance, at the village level.
- Panchayati Raj is a three-tier, organically linked structure.
- A **Gram Panchayat** covers a village or group of villages divided into wards; each ward elects a Panch (ward member). {139}
- Gram Sabha members elect ward members directly; the Sarpanch is elected by ward members as per the State Act; both serve five years.
- **Panchayat Samiti** is constituted at the block (intermediate/tehsil) level. {150,152}
- Members of the Panchayat Samiti are directly elected by the people. {148,149}
- Jharkhand's Panchayat Samiti is constituted under Section 32 of the Jharkhand Panchayati Raj Act, 2001.
- In Uttar Pradesh the Panchayat Samiti is called Kshetra Panchayat.
- The Panchayat Samiti is an **administrative authority**; its functions are co-ordination and execution; it links Gram Panchayats and Zila Panchayat. {153,154}
- It collects plans prepared by Gram Panchayats, evaluates them and processes them for funding and implementation.
- **Zila Parishad** is a supervisory and co-ordinating body, at the apex of the three tiers.
- Zila Parishad includes district department heads, Panchayat Samiti chairpersons, MPs, MLAs and MLCs of the district, and representatives of SC, ST, women and co-operative societies; so not all members are directly elected.

## Gram Sabha
- **Gram Sabha** [Article 243(b)] = body of persons registered in the electoral rolls of a village within the Panchayat area at village level; not all persons living there. {72}
- Gram Sabha is the foundation and primary body of Panchayati Raj, by far the largest, and a permanent body.
- Gram Sabha is a village assembly of all registered voters in the Panchayat area; at village level it has powers like the State Legislature has at State level.
- Article 243A: Gram Sabha exercises such powers at village level as the **State Legislature** (not the Central Government) may by law provide. {73}
- Gram Sabha is the Sabha of the electorate; Gram Panchayat, Block Panchayat and Zilla Parishad consist of elected representatives.
- A Gram Sabha decision cannot be annulled by any other body; only the Gram Sabha itself can annul it.
- A Gram Panchayat can have more than one village; Gram Sabha can meet in each village by rotation; Gram Panchayat meets at its headquarters. {74}
## Powers and the Eleventh Schedule
- **Article 243G**: the State Legislature may by law give Panchayats powers to function as institutions of self-government; it adds to their importance. {17}
- Such law may devolve powers for preparing plans and implementing schemes for economic development and social justice, including Eleventh Schedule matters.
- The **11th Schedule** (added by the 73rd Amendment) lists **29 subjects** for Panchayats. {19,20}
- **Rural Sports** is not among the 29 matters of the 11th Schedule; libraries, fuel and fodder, and technical training are. {21}
## Duration and elections
- Article 243E: every Panchayat continues for **five years from the date appointed for its first meeting**, unless sooner dissolved. {31,32,48}
- If dissolved early, elections must be completed within **six months** of dissolution. {34}
- No election is needed if the remaining period of the dissolved Panchayat is less than six months.
- A Panchayat formed after early dissolution continues only for the **remainder** of the dissolved Panchayat's term. {33,176}
- Example: first meeting 10 February 2014, dissolved 2016; the new Panchayat lasts only up to 10 February 2019.
- The minimum age to be a member of a Panchayat (all three levels) is **21 years** [Article 243F(1)]. {174,175}
- Articles 243K(1) and 243ZA(1): the **State Election Commission** superintends, directs and controls electoral rolls and all elections to Panchayats and Municipalities. {161,163,164,173}
- The State Election Commission consists of a State Election Commissioner; detailed provisions are in Article 243K itself.
- The **State Election Commissioner** is appointed by the Governor. {165}
- The State Election Commission is a constitutional authority for each State/UT; the Election Commission of India has no role in local body elections. {187}
- SECs conduct elections to corporations, municipalities, Zilla Parishads, District Panchayats, Panchayat Samitis, Gram Panchayats and other local bodies.
! MPPCS (Pre) 2021 asked who appoints the "State Chief Election Commissioner"; the Commission deleted the question because of that wording (the State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor).
- Constitutional authorities: State Election Commission (243K), State Finance Commission (243I), District Panchayat (243B); State Electoral Officer/Chief Electoral Officer is not. {188}
- The **Rajasthan State Election Commission** was constituted in July 1994 under Article 243K; single-member body headed by the State Election Commissioner; its secretary is also the State's Chief Electoral Officer. {167,168}
- It prepares electoral rolls and holds elections for Panchayati Raj Institutions and municipal bodies.
- The **Madhya Pradesh State Election Commission** was constituted on 1 February 1994, under Articles 243K and 243ZA. {169}
## Reservation of seats
- Article 243D(2): not less than one-third of the seats reserved for SCs/STs are reserved for SC/ST women (not one-fourth or fifty percent).
- **Article 243D(3)**: not less than **one-third** of directly elected seats in every Panchayat (including SC/ST women's seats) are reserved for women; added by the 73rd Amendment. {55,58,60}
- Such seats may be allotted by rotation to different constituencies in a Panchayat.
- Article 243D(4): one-third of Chairpersons' offices at all levels are reserved for women.
- Article 334 deals with reservation in the House of the People and Legislative Assemblies, not Panchayati Raj.
- Women's reservation in village local bodies was not the result of any demand; it did change the working of Panchayats. {54}
! IAS (Pre) 1997 and UPPCS (Mains) 2016: women's reservation is one-third, not 30 percent; and no two-child disqualification was proposed by the 73rd Amendment, so two options fit / no option fits.
- Earlier, seats were reserved for women only in Panchayati Raj Institutions. {59}
- The 106th Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, also called the Women's Reservation Bill) reserves one-third seats for women in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly, including SC/ST seats.
- It takes effect after the first delimitation based on the first census after the Act's commencement.
- In 2011, 50% seats for women in Panchayats existed in Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Himachal Pradesh.
- At present 50% women's reservation in Panchayats: Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Tripura, Bihar, Uttarakhand, Madhya Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Karnataka, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, West Bengal, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh. {63}
- The **U.P. Panchayat Laws (Amendment) Act, 1994** (in force 22 April 1994) reserves one-third of total seats for women. {64}
- SC reservation in Panchayat elections does not apply to **Arunachal Pradesh**, which has no Scheduled Caste population (Census 2011). {65}
## Finance
- **Article 243I**: the **Governor** constitutes a Finance Commission within one year of the 73rd Amendment and then every fifth year, to review the financial position of Panchayats. {178,179,180,181}
- It makes recommendations to the Governor (printed once as "to the President") for Panchayats at all levels, not only the intermediate level.
- The **State Finance Commission** recommends the principles for distributing net proceeds of taxes between State and Panchayats, and the taxes, duties, tolls and fees assignable to Panchayats. {182,184,185}
- The Union Finance Commission (Article 280) also recommends measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund to supplement Panchayats and Municipalities, based on SFC recommendations. {186}
- Local government remains a State subject in the Seventh Schedule despite the 73rd and 74th Amendments.
## PESA Act
- The **Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act (PESA)** was passed by Parliament in **1996**. {66}
- PESA was enacted on the recommendations of the **Bhuria Committee**. {67}
- PESA applies in ten Fifth Schedule States: Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Telangana; not Assam, Meghalaya, Tamil Nadu. {69}
- PESA aims at self-governance, recognising tribals' traditional rights and freeing them from exploitation; it has no provision for creating autonomous regions. {68}
- Under PESA the Gram Sabha owns minor forest produce and can ban transfer of land. {70}
- Gram Sabha consent is needed for licences/mining leases for minor minerals only; not for other minerals.
- PESA rules: a Scheduled Tribe member is chosen by consensus to chair Gram Sabha meetings for one year. {78}
- If there is no consensus, the oldest Scheduled Tribe lady among the members present is the Chairperson.
## State-specific Panchayat rules
- Chhattisgarh Panchayati Raj Act, 1993: a Gram Panchayat has 10 to 20 wards, each electing one Panch, plus a Sarpanch as head; all are elected, none nominated. {35}
- In Chhattisgarh a tie (equal votes) in the election of a Panch is decided by lot.
- Chhattisgarh (Act as amended in 2004): Gram Sabha meets once every three months; quorum = 1/10th of members, of whom 1/3rd must be women.
- Sarpanch and Panchs are responsible for the quorum; removal of Sarpanch is initiated if quorum is absent in five consecutive meetings.
- Annual working plan, beneficiary selection, annual budget, audit report, annual accounts and administrative report must be passed in a meeting with quorum. {75}
- A Gram Sabha meeting can be adjourned for want of quorum; an adjourned meeting needs no quorum, but the budget and audit report cannot be passed without quorum.
- Chhattisgarh Panchayat Raj Act, 1993 gives Zila Panchayats: planning minor water bodies, control over institutions in all social sectors, control of tribal sub-plans, other functions conferred by the State Government. {71}
- Section 127 of the Chhattisgarh Act: the **Governor** may by notification change a Block's headquarters or alter its limits. {143}
- Section 39(3), Chhattisgarh Act: if a Sarpanch or Janpad/Zila Panchayat President is suspended, members elect one of themselves to hold the office temporarily. {156}
- For this the secretary/Chief Executive Officer calls a special meeting immediately, within fifteen days of receiving the information.
- The officiating Sarpanch or President does all duties and uses all powers of the office while the suspension lasts.
- A woman Sarpanch cannot nominate her husband to attend Gram Panchayat or Janpad Panchayat meetings; MLA/MP ex-officio members can nominate representatives. {158}
- Section 46, Chhattisgarh Panchayat Nirvachan Niyam, 1995: if a candidate dies before polling, poll is postponed only if just one candidate remains; not if more than one remains. {171}
- Chhattisgarh Panchayat election: one district election officer; one or more returning officers, appointed by the State Election Commission or, with its consent, by the District Election Officer. {172}
- A Naib Tehsildar may be returning officer for Janpad Panchayat and Gram Panchayat.
- M.P. Panchayati Raj Avam Gram Swaraj Adhiniyam, 1993: if Sarpanch and Up-Sarpanch are absent, a Panch elected by members present presides over the Gram Sabha. {76}
- Standing Committees of Zila Panchayat in Madhya Pradesh include General Administration, Agriculture and Education Committees. {157}
- Others are Communication and Works, and Co-operation and Industries Committees; Zila Panchayat may create more committees beyond the five.
- **Section 89** of the Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994 was amended to use "Gram Vikas Adhikari" in place of "Gram Sevak". {77}
- Section 19, U.P. Kshetra Panchayats and Zila Panchayats Act, 1961: the Zila Panchayat Chairman is elected by its elected members from among themselves. {155}
- Section 19-A of that Act reserves the Chairman's office for SCs, STs and Backward Classes.
- Bihar Panchayati Raj Acts of 1947 and 1993 let the village court (Nyaya Panchayat) award imprisonment.
- In 1996 the Patna High Court declared Part VI (village court) of the Bihar Act, 1993 illegal.
- Under the Bihar Panchayati Raj Act, 2006 no bench of a village court may sentence any imprisonment.
## The 74th Amendment and Municipalities
- The **74th Amendment Act, 1992** gave constitutional status to urban local self-government (Municipalities). {191,192,193}
- It added **Part IX-A** "The Municipalities" (Articles 243P to 243ZG), relating to urban areas only; in force from 1 June 1993. {189,194}
- It added the 12th Schedule with 18 functional items, restructured Municipalities, reserves seats for women, and applies to all States. {190}
- **Article 243Q**: three types of Municipalities — Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council (smaller urban area), Municipal Corporation (larger urban area). {40}
- Municipal corporations are formed in big cities with definite area and population, because urban administration of big cities is very complex. {200}
- Eight types of urban local government: Municipal Corporation, Municipality, Notified Area Committee, Town Area Committee, Cantonment Board, Township, Port Authority (earlier Port Trust), Special Purpose Agencies.
- All members of a Municipality are directly elected by the people of the area; additional representation may be given under Article 243R(2).
- Article 243P(d): the municipal area is the territorial area notified by the **Governor**. {202}
- A Municipality's term is 5 years from the date of its first meeting; it can be dissolved earlier.
- Article 243T(1) provides reservation of seats for Scheduled Tribes (and SCs) in Municipalities. {196}
- Article 243T(3): not less than one-third of directly elected seats in every Municipality (including SC/ST women) are reserved for women; many States have raised it to 50%. {195}
- Article 243V proviso: a person who has attained **21 years** is not disqualified from Municipality membership for being under 25. {204}
- A person can be elected to a Municipal Council only if his name is in its electoral roll. {177}
- The **Mayor** is the highest-ranking official of a city's municipal government; tenure 5 years (Article 243U). {198}
- In Delhi and Bengaluru the Mayor's tenure is one year; in Mumbai 2.5 years.
- Article 243S: wards committees for one or more wards in Municipalities with population of three lakh or more.
- Wards committee: made of some wards; ward councillors are members; two residents of the area are nominated (by the Mayor). {208}
- Each ward has one seat in the wards committee; members are elected by adult franchise for five years.
- Census of India urban area: municipality/corporation/cantonment/notified town area committee; minimum population 5000; at least **75%** of working males in non-agricultural work (not 60%); density at least 400 per sq km. {199}
## State-specific municipal rules
- Rajasthan: security deposit for Mayor's election = Rs. 30,000 for general candidates. {197}
- It is Rs. 15,000 for women, SC, ST and Backward Class candidates.
- Rajasthan Municipal Act, 2009: State Government may nominate up to **8** persons to a Municipal Council. {203}
- The limit is 6 for a municipal board and 12 for a municipal corporation.
- Section 79(2), Chhattisgarh Municipalities Act, 1961: a Panchayat–Cantonment Board dispute is decided by the State Government, subject to the Central Government's concurrence. {201}
- Section 34, Chhattisgarh Municipalities Act: enrolled voter may contest; President needs age 25, Councillor 21; one may contest President and Councillor together. {205}
- A candidate for one ward cannot be a candidate for another ward.
- A person elected both President and Councillor must resign one office within seven days of being declared elected.
- Chhattisgarh municipal body: 5 years from the first meeting; one formed after dissolution serves only the remainder (dissolved at two years → new one for three years). {206}
- Its election must be completed before expiry of term or within six months of dissolution; not needed if the remainder is under six months.
- Section 47, Chhattisgarh Municipalities Act: recall of Council President needs a proposal signed by 3/4 of elected Councillors, a majority of voters by secret ballot, and can be initiated only once in the term. {207}
- Recall needs more than half of the total voters casting votes; the proposal is presented to the Collector.
- Recall cannot start within two years of the President entering office, or before half the tenure of a President elected in a by-election.
- The Collector verifies the signatures and sends the proposal to the State Government, which refers it to the State Election Commission to arrange voting.
- Chhattisgarh: a Councillor who acts as a legal practitioner for another person against the Municipal Council is removed by the Collector. {209}
- In U.P. the chairman of a Municipality is elected by the voters on the electoral list of the municipal area, including ward electors. {210}
- Ghaziabad has a Nagar Nigam (Municipal Corporation), not a Nagarpalika Parishad. {211}
- Lucknow has a Municipal Corporation and a Cantonment Board; Kanpur has a Nagar Nigam; Kakori is a Nagar Panchayat in Lucknow.
## Planning committees
- 73rd and 74th Amendments created: State Election Commission (243K; 243ZA), State Finance Commission (243I; 243Y), District Planning Committee (243ZD); not the State Property Tax Valuation Board. {212}
- District Planning Committees came by the **74th** Amendment (Part IX-A); the 73rd provided State Election Commissions and State Finance Commissions. {213}
- Article 243ZD(1): the **District Planning Committee** consolidates plans of Panchayats and Municipalities and prepares a draft development plan for the whole district.
- The 74th Amendment provides ward committees, Metropolitan Planning Committee (243ZE) and District Planning Committee (243ZD). {218}
- Article 243ZE: a **Metropolitan Planning Committee** in every metropolitan area prepares a draft development plan for it; it only sends the draft to the State Government. {219}
- Rajasthan District Planning Committee: 20 elected and 5 ex-officio members. {214}
- Other nominated persons there: Collector, ADM and two nominations from MLA, MP or CSO.
- DPC size differs by State: 34 members in Bihar, 20 in Chhattisgarh.
- Section 121(6), Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act, 1994: the **Zila Pramukh** chairs the District Planning Committee. {216}
- The Planning Commission issued its first district planning guidelines in 1969; this led several States to formulate District Plans.
- In U.P. the District Plan includes Nagar Panchayat, Gram Panchayat and Kshetra (Block) Panchayat, along with the District Panchayat. {217}
## MGNREGA
- The National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 was passed in August 2005 and came into force on 2 February 2006.
- It was renamed MNREGA on 2 October 2009; the programme started in Anantpur district, Andhra Pradesh.
- **Adult members of any household** are eligible for at least 100 days of guaranteed employment. {220}
- Under MNREGA the **Gram Panchayat**, after considering Gram Sabha recommendations, prepares the scheme and is responsible for execution of works. {221}
- The **VB-G RAM G Act, 2025** (Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin)) got the President's assent on 20 December 2025.
- It comes into effect on a date notified by the Central Government; it replaces MGNREGA with a new statutory framework aligned with Viksit Bharat 2047.
- It raises the employment guarantee to 125 days per rural household annually and strengthens income security.
