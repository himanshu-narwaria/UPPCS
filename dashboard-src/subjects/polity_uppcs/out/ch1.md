@ 1 | The Constitutional Development of India
## Regulating Act 1773
- Acts for East India Company rule were passed during **1773–1853**.
- **Regulating Act 1773** made the Governor of Bengal, **Warren Hastings**, the Governor General of Bengal.
- It put the Presidencies of **Madras and Bombay** under Bengal's control.
- Governor General got an **executive council of four**; military and non-military decisions were taken by majority of the council.
- Governor General could vote only in a tie = he had a **casting vote**.
- Regulating Act 1773 provided for a **Supreme Court at Fort William, Calcutta**; it was set up in **1774**. {1}
- The Supreme Court had a Chief Justice and three other judges.
- **Sir Elijah Impey** was the first Chief Justice of the Calcutta Supreme Court; Lemaister was not. {2}
- The Act barred Company servants from private trade and from taking gifts or bribes from Indians.
- British Government tightened control: the **Court of Directors** (governing body of the Company) had to report on revenue, civil and military affairs in India.
## Pitt's India Act 1784 and Act of 1786
- **Pitt's India Act 1784** separated the commercial and political functions of the Company.
- It created the **Board of Control** for political matters; the Court of Directors kept commercial affairs.
- The Board of Control had **6 members**.
- **Act of 1786**: Governor General could overrule his council in important matters of safety, peace or interest of the Empire in India.
- Act of 1786 gave the Governor General the powers of **Commander-in-Chief**.
- **Lord Cornwallis** was the first Governor General to enjoy these powers.
## Charter Acts 1793 and 1813
- **Charter Act 1793** extended the Company's commercial monopoly for another 20 years.
- **Charter Act 1813** ended the Company's trade monopoly in India, except trade in **tea** and trade with **China**. {5,6}
- Charter Act 1813 asserted the **sovereignty of the British Crown** over Indian territories held by the Company. {7}
- Charter Act 1813 left territory and revenue with the Company for the next 20 years; it did not give revenue control to British Parliament.
- Indian revenue came under direct control of British Parliament by the **Act of 1858** (Secretary of State, a British Cabinet Minister, helped by the Council of India).
- Charter Act 1813 empowered local governments to impose taxes and punish those who did not pay.
- Charter Act 1813 permitted **English missionaries** to work in India.
- Charter Act 1813 first provided at least **Rs 1 lakh per year** for education, revival and improvement of literature of Indians.
- English education in India came through three things: Charter Act 1813, General Committee of Public Instruction 1823, and the Orientalist–Anglicist controversy. {4}
- **General Committee of Public Instruction** was formed in **1823**; it had the job of granting the 1 lakh rupees.
- East India Company discouraged orientalist education and promoted western education.
- Orientalist–Anglicist controversy = dispute on the kind of education the Company should give to local people.
- **H.T. Prinsep** and **Dr. H.H. Wilson** supported orientalist education.
- Anglicists were led by **Charles Trevelyan**; they wanted western education through English medium.
- On **7 March 1835**, under Governor General **Lord William Bentinck (1828–35)**, **Lord Macaulay's** proposal was accepted: English became the medium of higher education.
## Charter Acts 1833 and 1853
- **Charter Act 1833** made the Governor General of Bengal the **Governor General of India**, with all civil and military powers.
- **Lord William Bentinck** became the first Governor General of India.
- Charter Act 1833 ended the Company's commercial activities; it became a purely administrative (political) body.
- Charter Act 1833 added a **law member (fourth member)** to the Governor General's executive council.
- **Macaulay** was the first law member; the law member could act only for legislative purposes.
- Charter Act 1833 first made it possible for Indians to share in the administration of their country. {8}
- **Section 87** of Charter Act 1833: no person of India or the Crown to be barred from any Company post on grounds of religion, place of birth, descent or colour.
- Section 87 later became the basis of the demand for Indian participation in administration during the political movement.
- **Charter Act 1853** first separated the legislative and executive functions of the Governor General's council.
- Charter Act 1853 set up a separate Indian (Central) Legislative Council for law-making.
- Charter Act 1853 introduced **open competition** for selection and recruitment of civil servants.

## Government of India Act 1858
- Acts under the British Crown were passed during **1858–1947**.
- By the **Government of India Act 1858**, the British Crown took sovereignty over India from the East India Company.
- The Governor General also came to be called the **Viceroy**, the direct representative of the British Crown.
- **Lord Canning** became the first Viceroy of India.
- The Board of Control and the Court of Directors were ended.
- A new post, **Secretary of State for India**, was created; he got the powers of the Court of Directors and Board of Control.
- He was assisted by a **council of 15 members** (Council of India).
- The Council of India was advisory; initiative and final decision mostly stayed with the Secretary of State.
## Indian Councils Act 1861
- **Indian Councils Act 1861** began the participation of Indian representatives in law-making.
- It restored law-making powers to **Madras and Bombay** provinces = start of decentralization.
- It empowered the Viceroy to issue **ordinances** in an emergency, without the Legislative Council's permission; valid for not more than **six months**.
- It turned the Viceroy's Executive Council into a miniature cabinet on the **portfolio system**.
- All **five members** got one department each: Revenue, Finance, Law, Home, Military.
- In **1874**, a sixth member was added to the executive council for the **Public Works Department**.
- For legislative work, additional members = minimum **6**, maximum **12**; nominated by the Viceroy for **2 years**. {9}
- At least half of the additional members were to be non-official.
- Indian Councils Act 1861 made no difference between Central and Provincial subjects.
## Indian Councils Act 1892
- **Indian Councils Act 1892** got royal assent on **20 June 1892** and commenced on **3 February 1893**.
- It increased non-official members in Central and Provincial Legislative Councils but kept the official majority.
- Additional members of the Governor General's Council were raised to minimum **10**, maximum **16**.
- At least half of these had to be non-official; as per rule, **6 official and 10 non-official**.
- Non-official members included both Indians and Europeans.
- Indian Councils Act 1892 gave the Legislative Council the power to **discuss the budget**, but not to vote on it. {11}
- Members could ask questions to the executive with **6 days' prior notice**; supplementary questions were not allowed.
- It introduced the principle of **election** in a limited sense; the word "election" was carefully avoided.
## Indian Councils Act 1909
- **Indian Councils Act 1909** is also called the **Morley-Minto Reforms**.
- It gave **separate electorates to Muslims** = communal representation. {12,13}
- **Lord Minto** came to be known as the father of the communal electorate.
- It sowed the seeds of separatism that later led to the partition of the country.
- It first provided for association of Indians with the executive councils of the Viceroy and Governors.
- **Satyendra Prasad (Prasanna) Sinha** was the first Indian to join the Viceroy's Executive Council, as law member.
- Functions and size of legislatures at the centre and in provinces were enlarged.
- Council members got the right to discussion and to ask **supplementary questions**.
## Government of India Act 1919
- **Government of India Act 1919** is known as the **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms**; passed by the British Parliament.
- It embodied reforms recommended by Secretary of State **Edwin Montagu** and Viceroy **Lord Chelmsford**.
- Its objective was to gradually introduce **responsible government** in India; the term was used for the first time.
- Government of India Act 1919 introduced **dyarchy in the provinces**. {21}
- Principal features of the 1919 Act: dyarchy in the provincial executive and devolution of legislative authority from centre to provinces. {18}
- Separate electorates for Muslims came from the 1909 Act; the 1919 Act continued them.
- The 1919 Act extended separate electorates to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Europeans and Anglo-Indians**.
- **Dyarchy** comes from Greek "di-arche" = double rule.
- Dyarchy = division of provincial subjects into two categories: **reserved** and **transferred**. {19}
- Reserved subjects: governor and his executive council, not responsible to the legislative council.
- Transferred subjects: governor with the aid of ministers responsible to the provincial legislature.
- **Reserved subjects**: judicial administration (justice), police, prisons (jail), land revenue, finance, irrigation, law and order, press, forests, water, labour disputes settlement, agriculture loans. {20}
- **Transferred subjects**: local self-government, public works, health and sanitation, industrial research, education, agriculture.
- The 1919 Act separated subjects into Central (Federal) and Provincial subjects.
- The 1919 Act first introduced provincial autonomy by relaxing central control through this separation of subjects.
- It separated provincial budgets from the central budget and let provincial legislatures enact their budgets.
- The 1919 Act adopted **direct elections** for the first time.
- Government of India Act 1919 made the central legislature **bicameral** for the first time. {22,23}
- The bicameral Central Legislature could make laws for the whole of British India.
- Upper House = **Council of State**; Lower House = **(Central) Legislative Assembly**.
- Council of State: **60 members**, maximum 20 official (section 18 of the 1919 Act).
- Central Legislative Assembly: originally **140 members**, 100 elected and 40 nominated (section 19).
- Under the Rules of the 1919 Act, Council of State = 60 members: 33 elected and 27 nominated.
- Under the Rules, the Assembly was raised first to 143 (102 elected, 41 nominated), then to 145 (104 elected, 41 nominated).
- One member in each house was elected from **Berar** out of the nominated members; so many sources count him as elected.
- In 1918 Britain gave limited suffrage to women property holders; this did not apply to other parts of the Empire.
- The All India Women's Deputation led by **Sarojini Naidu** met Secretary of State Edwin Samuel Montagu; women's demands were ignored in the reforms.
- The 1919 Act gave women no right to vote or to stand in elections.
- The 1919 Act let Provincial Councils decide if women could vote, subject to strict property, income or education levels.
- Merit-based civil services were introduced in **1854**, after Lord Macaulay's Report of the select committee of British Parliament.
- A Civil Services Commission was set up in London; competitive examinations began in **1855** in London.
- Government of India Act 1919 provided a separate Indian Civil Service examination to be held in India; in 1941 Indians were a higher percentage than Europeans in the ICS. {25}
- The ICS examination in India started in **1922**, first in Allahabad and later in Delhi.
- The Act of 1919 created the **Chamber of Princes** with **120 members**. {26}
- Chamber of Princes was set up by King-Emperor George V's Proclamation on **23 December 1919**, after royal assent to the 1919 Act.
- It was a forum for rulers of princely states to voice needs and aspirations to the colonial government.
- The Viceroy presided over its annual meeting; it was an advisory body.
- First chancellor of the Chamber of Princes: **Maharaja Ganga Singh of Bikaner**.
- Central Legislative Assembly elections of **1926** and **1945** were held under the 1919 Act; 1937 was not. {27}
- The last election of the Indian legislature under the 1919 Act was held in **1945**. {28}
- The 1919 Act provided for a commission after 10 years to review its working and report.
- In **November 1927** (2 years before schedule) the British Government announced a seven-member statutory commission under **Simon** = Simon Commission.
## Government of India Act 1935
- **Government of India Act 1935** provided for an **All India Federation** of provinces and princely states = federal polity. {35,36}
- The federation was named the **Federation of India**. {39}
- Its units: British Indian Provinces (Governor's Provinces and Chief Commissioners' Provinces) and Princely States. {38}
- The federation never came into existence.
- Government of India Act 1935 provided **dyarchy at the centre** and abolished dyarchy in the provinces. {16,17,34}
- Dyarchy at the centre never came into force; central subjects were divided into reserved and transferred.
- Section 11(1): Defence, Ecclesiastical Affairs, External Affairs and Tribal Areas were handled by the Governor-General in his discretion, not by the federal legislature.
! The book notes: in Union-State terms, Defence and External Affairs were in the Federal List and the Governor-General was part of the federal legislature; on that view both statements are correct.
- The 1935 Act further extended and established **Provincial Autonomy**. {33}
! The book notes: Jharkhand PSC first keyed the 1935 Act for provincial autonomy, then changed its amended key to the 1919 Act.
- The 1935 Act divided legislative powers between central and provincial legislatures; the Constitution's Centre–State distribution of power is based on it. {31}
- Under the 1935 Act, **residuary powers** were vested in the Viceroy (Governor-General), not in the central or provincial legislature. {32,37}
- The 1935 Act introduced the **Principle of Constitutional Autocracy**. {30}
- It vested executive authority of provinces in the Governors and of the centre in the Governor-General, on behalf of the Crown.
- Supremacy of the British Parliament stayed intact under the 1935 Act.
- The **Federal Court** was set up on **1 October 1937** by the Government of India Act 1935. {14,15}
- **Sir Maurice Gwyer** was the first Chief Justice of the Federal Court.
- The 1935 Act separated **Burma** from India, with effect from April 1937. {44}
- The 1935 Act created two new provinces: **Sindh and Orissa** (now Odisha).
- The 1935 Act abolished the Council of the Secretary of State for India, created in 1858.
- The 1935 Act set up the **Reserve Bank of India** (first called Central Federal Bank) to control currency and credit.
- The 1935 Act extended separate electorates to **Scheduled Castes (depressed classes), women and labourers**; so women got reserved seats in the legislature. {24}
- The 1935 Act introduced bicameralism in **6 of 11 provinces**: Bihar, Bengal, United Province, Bombay, Madras and Assam.
! The book notes: BPSC keyed the 1919 Act for making Bihar's legislature bicameral, which the book calls incorrect; the Bihar Legislative Council website says 1919, the Bihar Legislative Assembly website says 1935.
- The President's ordinance power (**Article 123**) is inspired by **section 42** of the Government of India Act 1935. {29}
- Features of the 1935 Act included the Governor-General's executive council, provincial autonomy and the proposal for a Federation of India; "appraisal of the Act of 1935" was not a feature. {40}
- The primary focus of the 1935 Act was not on establishing the judiciary.
- It laid the base for division of powers between Centre and States, a Central Legislature, and assignment of powers in the States.
- It proposed strengthening the Federal Government's authority over the provinces.
- The 1935 Act did not provide for a **written Constitution** for the country. {41}
- The Government of India Act 1935 is the main source of the Constitution of India; the present structure is substantially based on it. {42}
- Taken from the 1935 Act: federal government, diarchy at the centre, Centre–State distribution of power, Federal Court.
- **August Offer 1940**: British Government accepted in principle that Indians should mainly frame the new constitution.
- In **1942**, **Sir Stafford Cripps**, a British cabinet member, brought a draft proposal for an independent constitution to be adopted after World War II.
## Indian Independence Act 1947
- **Indian Independence Act 1947** declared India an independent dominion on **15 August 1947**.
- It abolished the office of Viceroy; a Governor General was appointed for each dominion.
- It dropped the title **"Emperor of India"** from the royal titles of the King of the United Kingdom.
