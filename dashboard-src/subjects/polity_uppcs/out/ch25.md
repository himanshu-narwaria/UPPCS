## Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955
- The Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 prescribes punishment for the preaching and practice of **untouchability**, for enforcing any disability arising from it, and connected matters.
- Section 1(2): the Act extends to the **whole of India**. {8,9}
- Section 15(1): notwithstanding the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, all offences punishable under the Act are **cognizable and triable summarily**; they are also non-compoundable. {2,4,5}
- The Act provides both imprisonment and fine as punishment.
! For "all offences under the Act are" (cognizable / triable summarily / non-compoundable) and (cognizable / imprisonment and fine both), two options each are given as correct; M.P.P.S.C. first keyed "compoundable" in the second and then gave bonus marks to all.
- Offences under the Act are tried by a **Judicial Magistrate First Class**; in a metropolis by a Metropolitan Magistrate. {3}
- First-class Judicial Magistrates were empowered to hold special courts for trial of offences under the Act.
- In Uttar Pradesh, in each district the Courts of Chief Judicial Magistrates / Judicial Magistrates are designated Special Courts for offences under the Act.
- **Section 5**: refusing admission to a hospital, dispensary or educational institution (meant for the general public or any section of it) on the ground of untouchability is punishable. {6}
- Section 5 punishment: imprisonment not less than 1 month and not more than 6 months, and fine not less than Rs. 100 and not more than Rs. 500.
- Untouchability is not an offence when the accused and the victim belong to the **same social group**. {13}
- If a company commits an offence under the Act, the person in charge of its affairs at the time (Director, Manager or Secretary) is deemed guilty and punished. {11}
- Primary responsibility for implementing the PCR Act / Rules rests in practice with State Governments / Union Territory Administrations.
- At the Centre, under the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, three ministries handle the PCR Act: **Home Affairs, Social Justice and Empowerment, Tribal Affairs**. {16}
- Ministry of Home Affairs: criminal offences against SCs/STs, including offences under the PCR Act.
- Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment: implementation of the PCR Act for Scheduled Castes (excluding criminal justice for offences under the Act).
- Ministry of Tribal Affairs: implementation of the PCR Act for Scheduled Tribes (excluding criminal justice for offences under the Act).

## SC and ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989
- The President gave assent to the SC and ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 on **11 September 1989**.
- The Act of **1989** came into force on **30 January 1990**. {22,23}
- The Act has **23 sections**. {24}
- The Act is based on the **Doctrine of Protective Discrimination**; it implements the system under Article 15(4). {32}
- Article 15 prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth.
- Offences under the Act are committed by a person who is not a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe; prosecution lies only against such a person.
- **Section 23(1)**: the **Central Government** may make rules by notification in the Official Gazette to carry out the purposes of the Act. {25}
- **Section 2(bc)** defines "economic boycott". {26}
- "Exclusive Special Court" (set up under Section 14(1) only to try offences under the Act) is defined in **Section 2(1)(bd)**, not Section 2(d). {44}
- "Victim" is defined in Section 2(ec); "Dependent" in Section 2(bb).
- A public servant committing an offence under Section 3 gets imprisonment of not less than **one year**. {27}
- A non-SC/ST person causing physical harm or mental agony to an SC/ST member on allegation of witchcraft or being a witch: imprisonment not less than **6 months, up to 5 years, with fine**. {43}
- **Section 14** provides for a Special Court and an Exclusive Special Court. {28}
- Under Section 14 the State Government, with the concurrence of the **Chief Justice of the High Court**, by Gazette notification specifies for each district a Court of Session as Special Court. {29}
- The Special Court is specified for the purpose of **speedy trial**. {30}
- Section 15 provides for a Special Public Prosecutor and an Exclusive Public Prosecutor.
- An appeal from any judgment, sentence or order lies to the High Court **both on facts and on law**. {31}
- **Section 16**: power to impose and realise a **collective fine** is vested in the **State Government**. {33}
- A Special Court cannot impose a collective fine; it can remove a person, forfeit property and take measurements. {41}
- A Special Court has no power to authorise a person other than police for extra investigation. {40}
- Special Court powers include: forfeiture of a convict's property, removal of a suspected person from the area, taking measurements and photographs of a suspected person.
- **Section 8**: in a prosecution the court may presume abetment, common intention and common object. {34}
- **Section 6** applies Section 34, Chapters 3, 4, 5, 5-A, Section 149 and Chapter 23 of the Indian Penal Code. {35}
- The Indian Penal Code is replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.
- **Section 18** bars anticipatory bail: Section 438 of the Code does not apply to arrest of a person accused under this Act. {37,38}
- The accused can plead in defence that he belongs to the same caste as the victim.
- The accused cannot plead private defence or that the harm was too slight to be a subject of complaint.
! For "the accused cannot take the plea that", two options (private defence; slight harm) are correct; M.P.P.S.C. gave bonus marks to all in its revised key.

## SC and ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Rules, 1995
- The Central Government framed the rules in 1995 to implement the 1989 Act.
- **Rule 7(1)**: an offence under the Act is investigated by a police officer not below the rank of **Deputy Superintendent of Police**. {39}
- **Rule 17**: the District Magistrate sets up a **District Level Vigilance and Monitoring Committee** in each district to review implementation of the Act. {45}
- **Rule 18**: "Material for Annual Report". {46}
- Rules, 1995 (original): investigating officer to complete investigation on top priority within **30 days** and report to the Superintendent of Police.
- Amendment Rules, 2016: SP forwards the report at once to the DGP or Commissioner of Police; the officer in charge of the police station files the charge-sheet in the Special Court within **60 days** (investigation plus charge-sheet).
! For "in how many days will the investigating officer submit his report", the commission's answer was 30 days, which is wrong as per the Amendment Rules, 2016.
## Right to Information Act, 2005
- The RTI Act was passed by Lok Sabha on **11 May 2005** and by Rajya Sabha on **12 May 2005**; passed in the year **2005**. {48}
- It received the assent of President Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam on **15 June 2005**. {47}
- It came fully into force after 120 days, on **12 October 2005** (12-13 midnight).
- Right to Information is a **legal right**; according to the Supreme Court it is also a **fundamental right**. {49,50}
- In many decisions the Supreme Court said freedom of thought and expression includes the right to information; the right is not clearly mentioned in the Constitution.
- Raj Narain vs State of Uttar Pradesh (1976): Supreme Court ruled the right to information is a fundamental right under Article 19.
- Objective of the Act: citizens secure access to information under the control of **public authorities**. {63}
- Section 2: "right to information" = access to information controlled by public officials; records, documents and works can be inspected and samples of material taken.
- The Act mandates timely response to citizens' requests for government information.
- Objects of RTI: empower citizens, promote transparency and accountability in government working, prevent (tackle) corruption, make democracy work for the people in a real sense.
- "To empower women and weaker sections of society" is **not** an objective of the RTI Act. {52}
- An informed citizen can better keep vigil on the instruments of governance and make government more accountable to the governed.
- The RTI Preamble mentions: transparency of information, revelation of information, informed citizens (citizenry), paramountcy of the democratic ideal. {51}
- Preamble: democracy needs informed citizens and transparency of information to contain corruption and hold governments accountable; conflicting interests are to be harmonised while preserving the democratic ideal.
- Section 6(2), Chapter 2: an applicant need **not give any reason** or personal details for seeking information, except details needed to contact him.
- A **Madras High Court** division bench (Justices N. Paul Vasanthakumar and K. Ravichandrababu) said an RTI applicant must disclose the object of seeking information and show it has legal backing. {64}
- No such obligation to give reasons is mentioned in the RTI Act, 2005.
- **Namit Sharma vs Union of India (2012)** is related to the RTI Act, 2005. {61}
- **Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS)**: Indian social movement and grassroots organisation best known for its demand for the RTI Act. {60}
- MKSS was founded in **1990** by social activists Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey and Shankar Singh.
- The people's movement for right to information is taken to have begun in 1990, when MKSS demanded data on famine relief works and workers' wages.
- The RTI Act has in general increased the sense of responsibility in bureaucracy; it still has miles to go to create the desired impact (both true, second not the explanation of the first). {62}
- The RTI Act, 2005 was amended in 2019.
- After Article 370 special status of Jammu and Kashmir was abolished (President's notification of 6 August 2019), the RTI Act is in force in the UTs of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
! "Which RTI provision has not been provided" (2006): at that time the Act had all the listed provisions except that an applicant must give reasons; the J&K and tenure provisions have since changed.
## Information Commissions
- The **Central Information Commission** was constituted with effect from **12 October 2005** under the RTI Act, 2005. {56}
- Its jurisdiction extends over all Central Public Authorities.
- CIC powers and functions are in **Sections 18, 19, 20 and 25** of the RTI Act.

- The RTI Act provides for a Central Information Commission and a State Information Commission at state level.
- State Information Commission = one State Chief Information Commissioner and not more than **ten** State Information Commissioners.
- Section 15(3): they are appointed by the **Governor** on the recommendation of a committee: Chief Minister (Chairperson), Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly, a Cabinet Minister nominated by the Chief Minister.
- The **Chief Justice of the High Court** is not a member of this committee. {58}
- Commissioners must be persons of eminence in public life with wide knowledge and experience in law, science and technology, social service, management, journalism, mass media or administration and governance.
- They must not be a Member of Parliament or **Member of the Legislature** of any State or Union Territory. {59}
- Original Act: Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners (Centre and State) held office for 5 years (or until age 65, whichever earlier).
- RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 removed the fixed five-year term; the term is as prescribed (notified) by the Central Government.
- Tenure of CIC and Information Commissioners is now given as **3 years or 65 years of age**.
- The tenure prescribed by the Central Government under the 2019 amendment is also given as 5 years from the date of joining (Central CIC, each Information Commissioner, State CIC).
## Citizen Charter
- Nine basic elements of the Citizen Charter: set standards of service; be open and provide full information; consult and involve; encourage access and the promotion of choice; treat all fairly; put things right when they go wrong; use resources effectively; innovate and improve; work with other providers.
- "Provision for inspection of the agency's work" is **not** a basic element of the Citizen Charter. {65}
## Forest Rights Act, 2006 and PESA
- Nodal agency for the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 = the **Ministry of Tribal Affairs** (or an officer or authority authorised by the Central Government). {66}
- Every authority under Chapter IV of that Act is subject to written general or particular directions of the Central Government.
- Section 6(1): the **Gram Sabha** starts the process of determining the nature and extent of individual or community forest rights; then it is enquired at Tehsil and District level. {67}
- **PESA** = Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996: Parliament's law ensuring self-governance through traditional gram sabhas for people in Scheduled Areas.
## National Green Tribunal
- The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 was enacted in line with the right to a healthy environment, read as part of the Right to Life under **Article 21**. {68}
- The NGT was established on **18 October 2010** under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010. {69}
- Purpose: effective and quick disposal of cases on environmental protection and conservation of forests and other natural resources, enforcement of environmental legal rights, relief and compensation for damage to persons and property.
- NGT is a specialised body with expertise for environmental disputes involving multi-disciplinary issues.
- NGT is not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908; it is guided by principles of natural justice.
- NGT can review its own decisions; if this fails, its decision can be challenged in the Supreme Court within **90 days**. {70}
## Disaster and epidemic laws
- The **Disaster Management Act** was passed by Parliament in **2005** for efficient management of disasters. {77,78}
- It has 11 chapters and 79 sections; it received the President's assent (was enacted) on 23 December 2005.
- The Act envisaged the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), headed by the **Prime Minister**. {79}
- National Disaster Management Division = nodal division in the Ministry of Home Affairs for disaster management.
- NDMA may have not more than nine members including a Vice-Chairperson; members' tenure is five years.
- Legal basis of the Government's extraordinary powers during lockdown = **Disaster Management Act, 2005** and **Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897**. {80}
- On 11 March 2020 the Centre advised all States and UTs to invoke Section 2 of the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897 (a 123-year-old law) to enforce Health Ministry and State advisories on COVID-19.
- The Disaster Management Act, 2005 was invoked to enforce the 21-day nationwide lockdown that started at midnight on 23 March 2020.
## Environment and biodiversity laws
- The **Forest Conservation Act** was enacted in **1980**; effective from 25 October 1980. {81}
- The **Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023** was published in the Official Gazette on **3 August 2023**, the same day it got the President's assent. {82}
- It decriminalises violations of the Biological Diversity Act, replacing imprisonment with civil penalties.
- It gives relief to Indian entities in securing intellectual property rights.
- It exempts AYUSH practitioners and traditional knowledge holders from paying Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) fees.
- The Biological Diversity Act, 2002 came into force on 1 October 2003 and 1 July 2004; passed to meet obligations under the UN Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD).
- In a Community Reserve hunting is not allowed and people may collect non-timber forest produce; the Chief Wildlife Warden does not govern it and traditional agriculture is not allowed. {83}
- Section 36C, Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972: the **State Government**, not the Central Government, declares a Community Reserve.
- Section 36D: a Community Reserve is managed by a **Community Reserve Management Committee**, not the Chief Wildlife Warden.
- The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 prohibits hunting in protected areas, including Community Reserves.
- The Act does not explicitly mention non-timber forest produce; sustainable use by local communities is generally allowed under the committee's supervision.
- Traditional agricultural practices that may alter the habitat are typically restricted in Community Reserves.
- Community Reserves aim to involve local communities in conservation; the Act stresses protecting flora, fauna and traditional conservation values, which may limit some land uses.
- The **Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act** was passed in **2001**. {84}
- Its aims: an effective system to protect plant varieties and the rights of farmers and plant breeders, and to encourage new plant varieties.
## Aadhaar, IT Act and RTE Act
- Aadhaar number = 12-digit random number issued by **UIDAI** to residents of India after verification.
- Any resident of India, of any age or gender, may voluntarily enrol; enrolment needs minimal demographic and biometric information and is free of cost.
- Section 9, Aadhaar Act, 2016: Aadhaar is only proof of identity, not proof of citizenship or domicile; Section 23(g): the issuing authority can deactivate or omit an Aadhaar number. {86}
- Supreme Court: Aadhaar authentication data cannot be kept beyond **6 months** (not three months).
- Supreme Court upheld the Aadhaar Act, 2016 except some provisions on disclosure of personal information, cognizance of offences and use of the Aadhaar ecosystem by private corporations; and held Aadhaar valid for welfare schemes funded from the Consolidated Fund of India. {85}
- The Court called Aadhaar for welfare schemes a "legitimate" exercise and defined welfare schemes as only those funded directly from the Consolidated Fund of India.
- Aadhaar is not mandatory for insurance: IRDAI advised insurers not to seek Aadhaar for KYC; it may be accepted only when offered voluntarily.

## Consumer protection and food security
- The **Consumer Protection Act, 1986** was passed to protect consumer rights.
- Under it, consumers may take samples for food testing, and the legal heir of a dead consumer may file a complaint in the Consumer Forum on his/her behalf. {91}
- A consumer filing a complaint in the Consumer Forum (for malfunction or failure of a product) has to pay a small fee.
- The 1986 Act gave a 3-tier structure: National Commission, State Commissions and District Forums, to speed up consumer disputes.
- Under the 1986 Act the State Government sets up a District Forum in each district; District Forum limit = value and compensation up to **Rs. 20 lakh** (not Rs. 50 lakh). {93}
- Under the 1986 Act the State Commission took claims above Rs. 20 lakh and up to Rs. 1 crore.
- A State Government can file a complaint in a District Forum as a representative of consumers' interest in general.
- The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** got the President's assent on 9 August 2019, came into effect on 20 July 2020 and replaced the 1986 Act, to modernise consumer safety.
- Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021: District Commission up to **Rs. 50 lakh**; State Commission above Rs. 50 lakh up to **Rs. 2 crore**; National Commission above Rs. 2 crore. {92}
- Section 28(1): the State Government may set up more than one District Commission in a district if it deems fit.
- A District Forum is headed by a person who is, has been, or is eligible to be a District Judge; it has two other members, one of whom shall be a woman.
- A State Commission is headed by a person who is or has been a High Court Judge.
- **State Food Commission** is set up under Section 16 of the **National Food Security Act, 2013**. {90}
