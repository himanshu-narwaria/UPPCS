@ 17 | The Vice-President
## Office and role
- The post of Vice-President of India is modelled on the American Vice-President.
- Vice-President is next to the President in the official order of precedence; second-highest constitutional office.
- **Article 63**: there shall be a Vice-President of India.
- **Article 64**: Vice-President is the **ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States** (Rajya Sabha) and shall not hold any other office of profit. {10,11}
- Rajya Sabha is presided over by the Vice-President, who is not its member. {19}
- Vice-President: second highest dignitary; no formal function attached to the office; discharges President's functions in his absence; acts as President if President resigns, is removed or dies. {15}
- Vice-President acts as President during casual vacancies caused by death, resignation, impeachment or other situations.
- While acting as President or discharging his functions, Vice-President does not perform duties of Rajya Sabha Chairman (Article 64, proviso).
- In that period he gets the President's salary and allowances (Article 65(3)), not the Chairman's.
- If the Vice-President resigns while discharging President's duties, the Chief Justice of India performs them: President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969. {16}
- Vice-President draws salary (4 lakh per month) as ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; the Constitution fixes no emoluments for him as Vice-President.
## Election and qualifications
- **Article 66(1)**: Vice-President is elected by an electoral college of all members (elected and nominated) of both Houses of Parliament. {1,2,3,4,5,9}
- Members of State Legislative Assemblies and Legislative Councils do not elect the Vice-President.
- Election is by proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote; voting by secret ballot.
- Election of the Vice-President, like that of the President, is indirect; State Legislatures play no role in the Vice-President's election. {7}
- Vice-President is elected, not appointed by the President. {12}
- 16th Vice-Presidential Election (2022): electoral college = **788** members: 233 elected Rajya Sabha + 12 nominated Rajya Sabha + 543 elected Lok Sabha. {6}
- **Article 66(3)**: citizen of India; completed 35 years; qualified for election as a member of the **Rajya Sabha** (not Lok Sabha), since he chairs it. {8}
- A member of a State Legislature can be a candidate for Vice-President.
- Minimum age to hold the office of President is 35 years; the first President was Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
- Article 71(2): if the Vice-President's election is declared void by the Supreme Court, acts done on or before the date of the decision are not invalidated.
## Term, resignation, removal
- **Article 67**: term of five years, the same as the President's.
- Article 67(a): Vice-President resigns by writing addressed to the President.
- **Article 67(b)**: removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all its then members (absolute majority) and agreed to by the Lok Sabha; not by formal impeachment. {13,17,18}
- The removal resolution can be moved only in Rajya Sabha, and only after at least 14 days' notice.

## Facts about Vice-Presidents
- Dr. S. Radhakrishnan was the first Vice-President (1952 to 1962); Dr. Zakir Hussain was the second, from 13 May 1962 to 12 May 1967. {22}
- Mohammad Hamid Ansari entered office in 2007 and was re-elected in July 2012.
- Mohammad Hidayatullah (sixth, 1979-84) and Shankar Dayal Sharma (1987-92) were Vice-Presidents; Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed and Neelam Sanjiva Reddy were only Presidents. {21}
- Dr. S. Radhakrishnan (Ambassador to U.S.S.R., 1949-1952) and V.V. Giri (High Commissioner to Sri Lanka, 1947-1951) held diplomatic posts before becoming Vice-President. {20}
- K.R. Narayanan was ambassador to China from 1976 to 1978.
## Other facts in this chapter
- A Constitutional Amendment Bill must be passed by both Houses of Parliament separately with special majority.
- Constitutional cases in the Supreme Court are heard by a minimum of five judges.
- Freedom of Press is included in the fundamental right of Freedom of Speech and Expression.
