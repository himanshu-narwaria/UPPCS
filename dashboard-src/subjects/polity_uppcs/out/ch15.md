@ 15 | Fundamental Duties
## Origin and place in the Constitution
- Fundamental Duties are inspired by the Constitution of the erstwhile **USSR**.
- Originally the Constitution of India did not contain Fundamental Duties.
- They were added on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**, which recommended them in 1976. {2,3,4}
- They were added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** (in force 3 January 1977), as ten duties, in a new chapter. {5,7,9,10,14,17,18,19,20}
- The 42nd Amendment added a new part, **Part IV-A**, where Fundamental Duties are enumerated. {11,12,15}
- Part IV-A has only one Article: **Article 51A**, which lists the duties of every citizen of India. {8,16}
- Present number of Fundamental Duties = **11**; originally 10; the 11th, Article 51A(k), was added by the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (in force 1 April 2010); so duties came by the 42nd and 86th Amendment Acts. {23,40}
- Fundamental Duties are not a part of Fundamental Rights. {32}
- Part V pertains to the Union Executive, not Fundamental Duties; Part II = Citizenship, Part III = Fundamental Rights, Part IV = Directive Principles. {37}
## Swaran Singh Committee
- In 1976 the Congress Party set up the Sardar Swaran Singh Committee to make recommendations about fundamental duties.
- The need for duties was felt during the internal emergency (1975-77).
- The Committee stressed that citizens, besides enjoying rights, also have certain duties to perform.
- The Government accepted the report and added Part IV-A through the 42nd Amendment.
- The Committee had 12 members: Swaran Singh (Chairman), A.R. Antulay, S.S. Ray, Rajni Patel, H.R. Gokhale, V.S. Sayed Muhammed, V.N. Gadgil, C.M. Stephen, D.P. Singh, Dinesh Goswami, Vasanth Sathe, B.N. Banerjee. {13}
- Haridev Joshi was not a member; he was the 11th, 16th and 18th Chief Minister of Rajasthan.
- Haridev Joshi fought 10 state assembly elections from 1952 until his death in 1995 and was undefeated in all.
## Nature of Fundamental Duties
- Fundamental Duties are non-justiciable: no legal sanction behind them, not enforceable in court, no legislative process to enforce them; they are not correlative to legal duties. {1}
- Fundamental Duties cannot be enforced by writs. {21}
- Fundamental Duties can be promoted only by constitutional methods and can be used for interpreting ambiguous statutes.
- Performance of any particular duty comes within the sphere of constitutional law, which the court has to decide.
- They are moral obligations of all citizens to help promote a spirit of patriotism and uphold the unity of India.
- Citizens are morally obligated by the Constitution to perform these duties.
## The eleven duties of Article 51A
| Clause | Duty of every citizen of India |
| 51A(a) | To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem {24} |
| 51A(b) | To cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle for freedom |
| 51A(c) | To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India (number three) {6,39} |
| 51A(d) | To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so |
| 51A(e) | To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India; to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women |
| 51A(f) | To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture {25,38} |
| 51A(g) | To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures {30,31} |
| 51A(h) | To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform {41} |
| 51A(i) | To safeguard public property and to abjure violence |
| 51A(j) | To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity |
| 51A(k) | Parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years (86th Amendment Act, 2002) |
## What is not a Fundamental Duty
- To vote in a general election or public elections is not a Fundamental Duty. {22,36}
- To strive towards abolition of untouchability is not a Fundamental Duty; abolition of untouchability = Article 17 (Fundamental Rights, Part III). {26}
- To help in organising Village Panchayats is not a Fundamental Duty; it is a Directive Principle (Article 40). {27}
- Article 40: State shall organise village panchayats and give them powers and authority to function as units of self-government.
- To pay income tax is not a Fundamental Duty. {28}
- To protect the weaker sections from social injustice is not a Fundamental Duty. {29}
- To protect monuments and places of national (public) importance is not a Fundamental Duty; it is a Directive Principle under Article 49. {33,34}
- To respect our parents and teachers is not among the eleven Fundamental Duties. {35}
- Separation of judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State = Article 50, Part IV (Directive Principle).
- Prohibition of employment of children below 14 years in factories = Article 24, Part III (Fundamental Right).
- To foster respect for international law = Article 51(c), a Directive Principle.
