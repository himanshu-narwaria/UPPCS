@ 22 | The Parliament (2)
## Rajya Sabha: composition
- Rajya Sabha = Upper House of Parliament, also called the **Council of States**.
- **Article 79**: Parliament = the President + House of the People + Council of States (not the two Houses only). {5}
- **Article 80**: maximum strength of Rajya Sabha = **250**: not more than 238 representatives of States and UTs + 12 nominated. {1,3}
- The President nominates **12 members** with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service. {18,19}
- Rajya Sabha has members nominated by the President and members indirectly elected by citizens through their representatives; none is directly elected. {2}
- Present strength = **245**: 233 from States and UTs (225 from State Assemblies + 8 from UT Assemblies) + 12 nominated.
- **Article 80(4)**: representatives of each State are elected by the **elected members of its Legislative Assembly**, by proportional representation by single transferable vote. {6}
- Representation of States in Rajya Sabha is in proportion (ratio) to their **population**. {4}
- The **Fourth Schedule** allocates Rajya Sabha seats to States and Union Territories. {39}
- 7th Schedule: allocation of powers and functions between the Union and the States.
- 8th Schedule: list of 22 languages recognised by the Constitution.
- 5th and 6th Schedules: administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Tribes.
- Union Territories are represented in **both Houses** of Parliament. {51}
- Only 3 of 8 UTs have Rajya Sabha seats (8 members): Delhi 3, Puducherry 1, Jammu and Kashmir 4.
- Election disputes are decided by the Judiciary through election petitions, not by the Chief Election Commissioner.
- The Rajya Sabha was first constituted in **1952** (formed on 3 April 1952). {62}
- The idea of a Second Chamber traces to the Montague-Chelmsford Reform of 1919.
- The First Amendment Act was enacted in 1951, when the Rajya Sabha did not exist; so its ratification was not taken.
## Rajya Sabha: permanent House and tenure
- Rajya Sabha is a **Permanent House**; it is **not subject to dissolution** [Article 83(1)]; nobody can dissolve it. {12,13,14,15,16,17}
- As nearly as possible **one-third** of its members retire at the end of every **second year**, as Parliament provides by law; new members replace them. {7,8,10}
- Term of a Rajya Sabha member = **6 years**. {9,11}
## Rajya Sabha: qualifications and members
- Minimum age for Rajya Sabha = **30 years**; member must be a citizen of India. {21}
- A Rajya Sabha member should not hold an office of profit under the State.
- He must be an elector for a parliamentary constituency anywhere in India.
- Before 6 April 2003 a candidate had to be a voter of the State he was elected from; a 2003 amendment to the Representation of the People Act, 1951 removed this. {20}
- Now a person can contest Rajya Sabha from anywhere, even if resident of another State.
- **Nargis Dutt** = first actress nominated to Rajya Sabha, in 1980; tenure 3 April 1980 to 3 May 1981. {22}
- Vaijayanthimala Bali: nominated to Rajya Sabha from 27 August 1993 to 26 August 1999.
- Jayalalitha: Rajya Sabha member from 3 April 1984 to 28 January 1989.
- Devika Rani Chaudhari: first Indian actress to get the Dada Saheb Phalke Award, in 1969.
- 2024: President Droupadi Murmu nominated Satnam Singh Sandhu and Sudha Murty to Rajya Sabha [Article 80(1)(a) and clause (3)].
- Satnam Singh Sandhu (educationist, founder of Chandigarh University): nominated on 30 January 2024.
- **Sudha Murty** (philanthropist, former chairperson of Infosys Foundation): nominated on 8 March 2024; became the 12th nominated member. {63}
- A Minister who is a Rajya Sabha member can take part in the deliberations of the Lok Sabha. {64}
- The senior-most minister who is a Rajya Sabha member is appointed Leader of the House by the Prime Minister.
- The Attorney General of India has the right to speak in both Houses of Parliament.
## Chairman and Deputy Chairman
- The **Vice-President** is the **ex-officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha [Articles 64 and 89(1)]. {53,56,58,59}
- The Chairman (Vice-President) is **not a member** of the Rajya Sabha or of either House; Rajya Sabha is presided over by a non-member. {54,55,57}
- If an MP is elected Vice-President, his seat in that House is deemed vacant on assuming office.
- Article 66(1): Vice-President is elected by an electoral college of the members of both Houses of Parliament.
- Presidential electoral college = elected members of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies; Vice-Presidential college = all MPs, elected or nominated. {52}
- When the Vice-President acts as President he gets the President's salary and allowances, not those of the Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
- Rajya Sabha elects a **Deputy Chairman** from among its members; the Deputy Chairman is a member of the House.
- There is also a panel of "Vice Chairmen" in the Rajya Sabha.
- The Deputy Chairman resigns in writing to the Chairman.
- Deputy Chairman is removed by a resolution passed by absolute majority of the total members of Rajya Sabha, after at least **14 days' notice**.
- When the Chairman's post is vacant, the Deputy Chairman performs the Chairman's duties.
- While presiding, the Deputy Chairman cannot vote in general; he has only a casting vote in a tie.
- The Vice-President does not preside as Chairman while a resolution for his removal is under consideration; likewise the Deputy Chairman.
- Quorum of Rajya Sabha = **one-tenth** of its total members.
- Dr. S. Radhakrishnan (first Vice-President) was Chairman for two consecutive terms, 13 May 1952 to 12 May 1962, but is **not the only one**. {61}
- Mohammad Hamid Ansari was also Vice-President/Chairman for two consecutive terms (2007-2017); he was the Chairman in 2010.
- Presently Vice-President **C.P. Radhakrishnan** is the Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
- Joint sitting (Article 108) has been called for only 3 bills: Dowry Prohibition Bill 1962, Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill 1978, Prevention of Terrorism Bill 2002.
## Special powers of Rajya Sabha
- Special (exclusive) powers of Rajya Sabha: resolution letting Parliament legislate on the State List (Article 249) and creation of All India Services (Article 312). {33}
- **Article 249**: if Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by **2/3 of members present and voting**, Parliament can make laws on State List subjects in the national interest. {23,24,25,26}
- Under Article 249 Parliament (not Rajya Sabha alone) makes the law; it may apply to the whole or any part of India.
- The Article 249 resolution is valid for **1 year**; it can be extended by passing another resolution.
- **Article 312**: if Rajya Sabha resolves by **2/3 of members present and voting** (not of total membership), Parliament may by law create one or more **All India Services**. {34,35,36}
- All India Services are common to the Union and the States, and include an All-India Judicial Service.
- **Article 250**: Parliament can legislate on any State List matter while a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation; such law expires six months after the Emergency ends.
- **Article 252**: Parliament can legislate for two or more States by consent; any other State may adopt such legislation.
- **Article 253**: Parliament can make law for the whole or part of India to implement international agreements, conventions or decisions of international conferences.
## Money Bills and Lok Sabha's exclusive powers
- **Article 109**: a Money Bill cannot be introduced in Rajya Sabha; only in Lok Sabha, with prior recommendation of the President. {29}
- A Bill other than a Money Bill may be introduced in either House.
- Article 109(2): Rajya Sabha returns a Money Bill with recommendations within **14 days**; Lok Sabha may accept or reject all or any of them. {28,30}
- Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill, and cannot vote on Demands for Grants. {27}
- Article 113(2): Demands for Grants go to the Lok Sabha; only it can assent or refuse.
- Rajya Sabha can discuss the Annual Financial Statement but cannot vote on it.
- Exclusive powers of Lok Sabha: introduction of Money Bill, its rejection and amendment (Article 109), and accountability of the Council of Ministers. {31}
- **Article 75(3)**: Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha; only Lok Sabha passes a no-confidence motion against it. {32}
- So if the Government fails in Rajya Sabha, the Prime Minister need not resign, Parliament is not dissolved and there is no President's Rule. {38}
- Article 352: President can declare National Emergency when security of India or a part is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion.
- Proclamation of Emergency must be approved by both Houses within one month of issue; not an exclusive power of Lok Sabha.
- Article 61(1): the charge to impeach the President may be preferred by either House.
- Rajya Sabha has **equal powers** with Lok Sabha in **amending the Constitution**. {37}
- Article 368(2): amendment Bill may be introduced in either House; passed in each by majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting; then President assents.
