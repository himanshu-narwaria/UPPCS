@ 28 | The State Legislature
## State Legislature: Articles and make-up
- **Articles 168 to 212** in **Part VI** of the Constitution deal with the State Legislature.
- **Article 168**: State Legislature = Governor + Legislative Assembly + Legislative Council (where it exists); in other States = Governor + Legislative Assembly. {1}
- Article 168(2): where there are two Houses, one is the Legislative Council and the other the Legislative Assembly; where there is one House, it is the Legislative Assembly.
- With two Houses, **Legislative Council = Upper House** and Legislative Assembly = Lower House. {2}

## Legislative Council: creation and abolition
- **Article 169**: Parliament may by law abolish or create a Legislative Council in a State, if the State Legislative Assembly passes a resolution to that effect. {4,6,7,8,9}
- The resolution needs a majority of the total membership of the Assembly and a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting.
- Article 169 applies notwithstanding anything in Article 168.
- The Assembly's resolution under Article 169 does **not** impose an obligation on the Union Government to initiate legislation in Parliament. {10}
- State Legislative Councils cannot be dissolved but can be **abolished** (Article 169). {5,17}
- Rajya Sabha can neither be dissolved nor abolished.
- Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies cannot be abolished but can be dissolved.
## States with Legislative Councils
- At present only **6 States** have Legislative Councils (bicameral legislatures): Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Uttar Pradesh. {23}
- **Madhya Pradesh** has no Legislative Council, even though the Constitution (Seventh Amendment) Act, 1956 provides for it. {19,22}
- **Tamil Nadu** has a unicameral legislature (no Legislative Council). {20}
- **Rajasthan** has no Legislative Council. {21}
- Kerala, Himachal Pradesh and Delhi do not have Legislative Councils.
- After the 1956 reorganisation, **7 out of the total 14 States** had a bicameral legislature: Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Bombay (Maharashtra), Madras (Tamil Nadu), Mysore (Karnataka), Punjab, West Bengal.
- Legislative Council abolished: West Bengal in **1969**, Punjab in **1970**, Tamil Nadu in **1986**.
- The Legislative Council in Jammu and Kashmir was established under the constitution adopted by Jammu and Kashmir in 1957.
- The Legislative Council of the Union Territory of Jammu & Kashmir stands abolished since **31 October 2019**.
## Legislative Council: size and composition
- Article 171(1): Council strength must not exceed **one-third** of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly (so it cannot be larger than half of the Assembly), and must not be less than **40**. {11,12}
- **Uttar Pradesh** has the largest Legislative Council, with 100 members.
- Article 171(3): nearly **5/6** of the Council members are elected and the remaining are nominated by the Governor.
- One-third of the Council members are elected by the Legislative Assembly from persons who are not members of the Assembly.
- One-third are elected by local bodies: Municipalities, District Boards or any other local authority specified by law of Parliament.
- One-twelfth are elected by **graduates** of at least three years' standing residing in the State.
- One-twelfth are elected by **teachers** of secondary schools with at least three years' teaching experience.
- Industrialists are not a category that chooses Council members. {16}
- The remainder are nominated by the Governor from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art, the co-operative movement and social service.
- Article 171(4): elected Council members are chosen by proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, and by secret ballot.
- Article 171(2): until Parliament by law otherwise provides, the composition of the Council is as provided in clause (3).
- Presently the composition of Legislative Councils is under Section 10 and Schedule 3 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 (as amended), made by Parliament under Article 171(2).
- Under this Act, only **10 of the 100** members of the Uttar Pradesh Legislative Council (1/10) are nominated by the Governor. {13}
- Governor's nominees: nearly 1/7 in Andhra Pradesh, nearly 1/6 in Bihar, nearly 1/7 in Karnataka, nearly 1/7 in Telangana.
- So there is no single fixed fraction of nominated members (not 1/3, 1/4 or 1/6 of the total strength). {14}
## Legislative Council: tenure and presiding officers
- The Legislative Council, like the Council of States, is a **permanent House**; it is not subject to dissolution; one-third of members retire every second year, like the Rajya Sabha. {15}
- The tenure of a Council member is **6 years**. {18}
- Minimum age for a member of the Legislative Council = **30 years**.
- Article 182: the Legislative Council chooses its Chairman and Deputy Chairman from amongst its members; the Governor does not nominate the Chairman.
- The Council can remove its Chairman or Deputy Chairman by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the Council.
## Legislative Assembly: size and seats
- The Legislative Assembly is composed of members elected by **direct election** on the basis of Universal Adult Suffrage from territorial constituencies.
- Article 170(1): subject to Article 333, an Assembly has not more than **500** and not less than **60** members (generally), chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies. {25,28}
- **Sikkim** is an exception to the minimum of 60: its Assembly has 32 members, the smallest among States. {26}
- Smallest Assembly among Union Territories = **Puducherry (33)**.
- **Uttar Pradesh** has the largest Legislative Assembly, with **403** members. {32}
- Article 170(2): each State is divided into territorial constituencies so that the ratio of population to seats is, as far as practicable, the same throughout the State.
- 84th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2001: the number of members in State Assemblies stays the same until the figures of the first census after 2026 are published.
- Article 332: seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the Legislative Assembly in proportion to their population.
- Article 333 (earlier): the Governor could nominate one member of the Anglo-Indian community to the Assembly if he felt the community was not adequately represented.
- The Constitution (104th Amendment) Act, 2019 did not extend Anglo-Indian nomination to State Assemblies beyond 25 January 2020.

## Union Territories with Assemblies
- Only three Union Territories have Legislative Assemblies: **Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir**.
- Section 14(3) of the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019: J&K UT Assembly has 90 elected and 5 nominated members, plus 24 vacant constituencies in areas occupied by Pakistan (POK).
- On 5 August 2019, Jammu and Kashmir became a Union Territory with a legislature and **Ladakh** a Union Territory without a legislature. {35}
- J&K and Ladakh have separate Lieutenant Governors.
- The Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 fixed the tenure of the J&K Legislative Assembly at 5 years.
- The tenure of the Chief Minister of Jammu and Kashmir was 6 years earlier; in a way it is not fixed but is deemed to be the tenure of the Legislative Assembly.
- The leader of the majority party becomes the Chief Minister and holds office as long as he has the majority.
## Elections, qualifications and disqualification
- Article 324: the **Election Commission of India** conducts the elections to State Assemblies / State Legislatures. {36}
- Article 173: a member of the State Legislature must be (a) a citizen of India, (b) not less than **25 years** for the Assembly and 30 years for the Council, (c) have other qualifications prescribed by or under a law of Parliament. {27,30}
- Being eligible to vote does not by itself qualify a person to be made a minister for six months; Article 173 qualifications apply. {70}
- Representation of the People Act, 1951: a person convicted of any offence and sentenced to imprisonment of not less than two years is disqualified from the date of conviction and for a further six years after release; this is not permanent.
- Article 191 deals with the disqualification of members of the State Legislature.
- **Article 192**: a question on disqualification under Article 191(1) is referred to the **Governor**, whose decision is final; he must first obtain the Election Commission's opinion and act according to it. {37,38,39}
- Disqualification under the Tenth Schedule (defection) is decided by the Speaker in the Assembly and by the Chairman in the Council.
- The State Legislative Assembly takes part in electing the President of India, members of Rajya Sabha and members of the Legislative Council; it does not take part in electing the Vice-President. {52}
## Speaker of the Legislative Assembly
- The Speaker is elected by the Assembly itself from amongst its members; usually he remains in office during the life of the Assembly.
- The Speaker vacates office earlier if he ceases to be a member of the Assembly, resigns in writing to the Deputy Speaker, or is removed by a resolution passed by a majority. {41}
- Article 179(b): the Speaker gives his resignation to the **Deputy Speaker**. {40}
- Article 179: when the Assembly is dissolved, the Speaker does not vacate office until immediately before the first meeting of the Assembly after the dissolution. {42}
- The Speaker exercises a **casting vote** in case of equality of votes.
- The Speaker establishes the contact between the House and the Governor.
- In the absence of the Speaker, the Deputy Speaker works as the Speaker.
- The rights and functions of the Assembly Speaker are the same as those of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
- **Ramkishore Shukla** held the offices of Protem Speaker, Speaker and Deputy Speaker in the Madhya Pradesh Legislative Assembly. {44}

## Sessions and procedure
- Article 174(2): the **Governor** prorogues the Vidhan Sabha. {45}
- The Governor addresses the Assembly (both Houses assembled, where there is a Council) at the first session after each general election and the first session of each year, and informs the legislature of the causes of its summons. {46}
- When a State Legislature has no rule on a particular matter, it follows the Lok Sabha rule.
- Article 189(3): until the State Legislature by law otherwise provides, quorum = **10 members or one-tenth** of the total members of the House, whichever is greater. {50}
- Article 210(1): business in the State Legislature is transacted in the official language(s) of the State, or in Hindi, or in English. {53}
- The Advocate-General has no right to vote in the proceedings of the State Legislature.
- The State Legislature cannot discuss the conduct of a Judge of the Supreme Court or a High Court in the discharge of his duties.
- The validity of proceedings of the State Legislature cannot be questioned on the ground of any alleged irregularity.
- Order of proceedings in the Madhya Pradesh Legislative Assembly: oath or affirmation → Governor's address → introduction of ministers → mention of death. {47}
- Rajasthan Assembly adjournment motion on a matter of public importance: needs the Speaker's consent, must be restricted to a specific matter of recent occurrence, and not more than one such motion at the same sitting. {48}
- An adjournment motion cannot raise a question of privilege and should not be framed in general terms.
- Rajasthan Assembly: the Speaker does not refer Bills containing delegation of legislative powers to the Committee on Subordinate Legislation. {49}
- Rule 241 (duties of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation): the Committee selects for scrutiny such orders as it considers appropriate.
- The Committee on Subordinate Legislation examines all "orders" framed under the Constitution or a statute delegating power to a subordinate authority; it may examine provisions of Bills that seek to delegate powers to make orders; this is done by Rule 240 of the Rajasthan Assembly Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business.
- Rajasthan Assembly rules: the **Home Department's** estimates do not come under Estimates Committee 'A'. {51}
## Bills and finance
- Article 197: a Bill passed by the Assembly is transmitted to the Legislative Council; the Council can withhold it for **3 months** the first time. {58}
- When the Assembly passes the Bill a second time, the Council can hold it for up to 1 month; so the Council can detain an ordinary Bill for **4 months** in total. {59}
- The **Speaker of the Vidhan Sabha** decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill in the State. {60}
- Money Bills are introduced only in the **Legislative Assembly**. {61}
- Article 198(2): the Council must return a Money Bill with its recommendations within **14 days**; the Assembly may accept or reject all or any of the recommendations.
- Article 207(1): a Bill or amendment on matters in Article 199(1)(a) to (f) cannot be introduced or moved except on the **Governor's** recommendation, and cannot be introduced in a Legislative Council. {63}
- The budget is presented by the Finance Minister and officials of the concerned Ministry.
- If the budget is disclosed before being introduced in the Assembly, the **Finance Minister** must take individual responsibility and resign; punishment is provided in the Official Secrecy Act, 1923. {62}
- The **State Legislature** may by law establish the Contingency Fund of the State, like an imprest, into which sums fixed by such law are paid. {55}
- The State Contingency Fund is placed at the disposal of the **Governor**, to make advances for unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation by the State Legislature under Article 205 or 206. {54}
- The contingency fund is an account to meet urgent or unforeseen expenditure of the government.
- Article 162: the State's executive power extends to matters on which the State Legislature can make laws; on matters where both the State Legislature and Parliament can legislate, it is subject to and limited by the executive power expressly given to the Union by the Constitution or a law of Parliament.
## Chief Minister and Council of Ministers
- Articles **153 to 167** in Part VI deal with the State executive.
- The State executive consists of the Governor, the Chief Minister, the Council of Ministers and the Advocate General.
- **Article 164(1)**: the Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor; other Ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister; Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor. {64}
- The Chief Minister and other Ministers are not selected by the Governor; the Chief Minister is chosen by the members of the majority party in the Assembly and formally appointed by the Governor. {68}
- The Chief Minister generally presides over the Cabinet meetings.
- Article 163(1): a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister aids and advises the Governor, except where the Constitution requires him to act in his discretion; so not all his functions are on the Chief Minister's advice. {65}
- Article 163(3): whether any, and what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the Governor cannot be inquired into in any court.
- Article 164(2): the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the **Legislative Assembly** of the State. {75}
- Article 164(3): before a Minister enters office, the Governor administers the oaths of office and secrecy as per the forms in the Third Schedule.
- Article 164(1A), added by the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003**: total Ministers including the Chief Minister must not exceed **15%** of the total members of the Legislative Assembly. {66}
- The number of Ministers including the Chief Minister must not be less than **12** in any State. {67}
- Article 164(4): a Minister who is not a member of the State Legislature for **six consecutive months** ceases to be a Minister at the end of that period. {69}
- Article 164(5): salaries and allowances of Ministers are fixed by the **State Legislature** by law; until then they are as specified in the Second Schedule. {71}
- **Article 167** (duties of the Chief Minister): communicate to the Governor all decisions of the Council of Ministers on State administration and proposals for legislation; furnish such information as the Governor calls for; and, if the Governor requires, submit to the Council a matter decided by a Minister but not considered by the Council. {72,73}
- The Chief Minister's participation in National Development Council meetings is an administrative duty, not a constitutional duty.
## Chief Ministers: firsts and names
- **Sucheta Kriplani** was the first woman Chief Minister of any Indian State; she became Chief Minister of **Uttar Pradesh** in 1963. {77,78}
- The first Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh was **Pt. Ravishankar Shukla**; he served from 1 November 1956 to 31 December 1956. {79}
- Dr. Mohan Yadav is the 19th and current Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh, since 13 December 2023.
- **Govind Narayan Singh**, the fifth Chief Minister of Madhya Pradesh, did not hold the office of Leader of the House in the MP Legislative Assembly during his tenure. {80}
## State Secretariat and Chief Secretary
- The **State Secretariat** is the apex wing of State administration and the centre of the State's political and administrative functions; it is the main force forming the principles of procedure for the State Government's work. {81}
- The Chief Secretary is chosen by the Chief Minister and appointed by the Governor; there is no fixed tenure of the Chief Secretary. {82}
- The Chief Secretary is the head of the State's administrative machinery and controls the administrative body of the State.
- In the State Secretariat, every file is sent to the Cabinet through the **Chief Secretary**. {83}
## Other State facts
- After the Ayodhya incident in 1992, the Vidhan Sabhas of the BJP-ruled States Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh and Rajasthan were dissolved; Bihar's was not. {84}
- Bihar came under President's rule in 1995.
- **Andhra Pradesh** gave reservation to Muslims in jobs and education in 2004; the Andhra Pradesh High Court cancelled it. {85}
- In 2007 Andhra Pradesh limited the Muslim reservation to 4 per cent within the 50% limit; the High Court cancelled it again.
- The Supreme Court, by an interim decision, accepted the Andhra Pradesh reservation; the final decision is awaited, pending before a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court.
- **Goa** was the only State to have the Common Civil Code. {86}
- The Goa Civil Code is based on the Portuguese Civil Code (Codigo Civil Portugues) of 1867, introduced in Goa in 1870 with some modifications.
- Uttarakhand enforced the Uniform Civil Code on 27 January 2025.
