@ 36 | Election Commission
## Constitutional basis
- Articles 324 to 329 in **Part XV** deal with elections and the electoral system.
- **Article 324** provides for an Election Commission, a permanent and independent constitutional body. {1,2}
- Election Commission of India was established in accordance with the Constitution on 25 January 1950 (not 25 January 1952).
- Article 324(1): superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and conduct of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President vest in the Election Commission.
## Composition and appointment
- Article 324(2): Election Commission = Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners; their number is fixed by the President from time to time.
- CEC and other Election Commissioners are appointed by the **President**, under the law made by Parliament. {3}
- Till 15 October 1989 there was only a Chief Election Commissioner.
- On **16 October 1989** two additional Election Commissioners were appointed for the first time; the Commission became a three-member body. {58}
- Those two posts lasted only up to 1 January 1990.
- On 1 October 1993 two additional Election Commissioners were appointed again; since then the Commission has three members.
- So the multi-member Commission dates from 1 October 1993, not from 1995.
- At present: one Chief Election Commissioner and two Election Commissioners (three-member body, not five-member).
## Powers, salary, tenure, removal
- CEC and Election Commissioners have equal powers; their salary and perks equal those of a **Supreme Court judge**. {6}
- CEC acts as head, but all issues are decided by majority; differences are not decided by the Law Commission. {4}
- Tenure of CEC and Election Commissioners = **6 years or age 65**, whichever is earlier. {9}
- CEC or an Election Commissioner resigns by writing addressed to the **President** (not to the CEC). {7,8}
- Article 324(5): CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a **Supreme Court judge**. {12}
- Other Election Commissioners or a Regional Commissioner can be removed by the President only on the **recommendation of the CEC**. {13}
- Article 324 vests the conduct of free and fair elections to Parliament and State Legislatures in an independent Election Commission; the power to remove Election Commissioners lies with the executive (President), but that is not the reason for its independence. {11}
- Table of precedence (mha.nic.in): CEC ranks at 9(A), with the UPSC Chairperson and the Auditor-General of India.
- Table of precedence: Governor of a State = 4th; Chief Justice of India and Lok Sabha Speaker = 6th; Speaker of a State Assembly = 14th.
## Functions of the Election Commission
- Functions: supervise, direct and control preparation of electoral rolls; conduct elections to Parliament and State Legislatures; conduct elections of President and Vice-President.
- Election Commission does not recommend President's Rule when a State cannot hold free and fair elections. {14}
- Making provision with respect to elections to Legislatures is not a function of the Election Commission. {15}
- Elections to local bodies (Panchayats and Municipalities) are conducted by the **State Election Commission** under Articles 243K and 243ZA. {16}
- Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha are elected by the members of the House from among themselves.
- Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
- Election Commission does not conduct House presiding-officer elections or municipal elections, and does not decide election disputes; the Judiciary resolves election doubts and disputes. {17,18}
- Election Commission decides the schedule of general elections and bye-elections (not the Home Ministry).
- Election Commission resolves disputes relating to splits and mergers of recognised political parties. {19}
- Election Commission distributes election symbols and conducts elections peacefully.
- Election notification is not issued by the Election Commission: President issues it for Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, Governor for State Assemblies and Councils. {20,21}
- The President or Governor issues the notification on the recommendation of the Election Commission; then the Commission issues date and time.
- Elections to President, Vice-President, Rajya Sabha and Legislative Councils: indirect, by proportional representation through single transferable vote.
- Elections to Lok Sabha and State Assemblies: direct, on the basis of adult suffrage. {22}
- List system and cumulative voting system are not adopted in India.
## Disqualification and Election Commission's advice
- Election Commission has advisory jurisdiction in post-election disqualification of sitting members: Article 103 (MPs) and Article 192 (State legislators). {5}
- Article 103: question of an MP's disqualification under Article 102(1) is decided by the President; his decision is final.
- Before deciding, the President must obtain the opinion of the **Election Commission** and act according to it. {23,24,25}
- **Parliament** made the decision to disqualify convicted persons from contesting elections. {27}
- Section 8(3), Representation of the People Act, 1951: conviction with imprisonment of not less than 2 years = disqualified from conviction date and for 6 more years after release.
- A defeated candidate who fails to get more than **one-sixth** of valid votes polled loses his security deposit.
- A winner losing his deposit means a very large number of candidates contested and votes got divided. {26}
## Model Code of Conduct
- Model Code of Conduct = guidelines for parties and candidates on speeches, polling booths, processions, manifestos and general conduct.
- It is framed by the Election Commission, not enacted by Parliament; it applies as soon as the election schedule is announced. {28}
## Right to vote and voting age
- Right to vote and right to be elected in India are **legal (statutory) rights**, under the Representation of the People Act, 1951. {29,30,31}
- Kuldip Nayar vs Union of India (2009): right to vote is neither a constitutional nor a fundamental right; merely statutory/legal.
- Legal right = offshoot of ordinary law made by the Legislature; fundamental rights are of transcendental character and protected against any authority.
- A fundamental right cannot be waived; an ordinary legal right can be taken away by the State.
- Fundamental rights are beyond the reach of the State, except as provided by the Constitution.
- **Article 326**: elections to Lok Sabha and State Assemblies are based on adult suffrage. {36,37}
- **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988** reduced voting age from 21 to 18 by amending Article 326; in force from 28 March 1989. {33,34,35,38}
- Aim: give unrepresented youth a chance to express their feelings and join the political process; the Representation of the People Act, 1951 was also amended.
- Youth aged 18 first voted in the Lok Sabha General Election of November **1989**. {32}
- Principles of State Legislature elections: adult suffrage; one electoral roll for every territorial constituency; no distinction of religion, race, sex; parties free to set their own norms. {40}
- On 1 March 2024 the Ministry of Law and Justice, after consulting the Election Commission, amended Rule 27A of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961.
- Age for senior citizens to vote by postal ballot from home was raised from 80 to **85 years**. {39}
- In the 2024 Lok Sabha elections, citizens aged 85+ and persons with 40% or more certified disability could vote from home by postal ballot for the first time nationally.
- Voting rights are statutory; there is no legal duty to vote, so non-voters cannot be punished by taking away their voting rights.
- Lower turnout than the previous election does not bind the Election Commission to order re-election. {55}
- Awareness campaigns and advertisements are used as tools to increase voter turnout.

- For 'One Nation, One Election', amendment may be needed in Articles 83, 85, 172, 174 and 356. {41}
- High-Level Committee on Simultaneous Elections was set up on 2 September 2023, chaired by former President **Ram Nath Kovind**.
- Members: Amit Shah, Ghulam Nabi Azad, N.K. Singh (former Chairman, 15th Finance Commission), Dr. Subhash C. Kashyap (former Lok Sabha Secretary General), Harish Salve, Sanjay Kothari (former Chief Vigilance Commissioner).
- Arjun Ram Meghwal (MoS Independent Charge, Law and Justice) was Special Invitee; Dr. Niten Chandra was Secretary of the committee.
- Adhir Ranjan Chowdhury was named a member but declined to participate.
- Tushar Mehta was not a member of the High-Level Committee on Simultaneous Elections. {42}
- **Exit Poll** = post-election survey of voters about the candidates they voted for. {43}
- Opinion Poll = survey done by agencies before elections to know public views.
- Total electors in the 2009 Lok Sabha elections = **714 million**. {44}
## Representation of the People Act and electoral reforms
- Section 33, RPA 1951: a person cannot be a candidate from more than two Parliamentary constituencies in a Lok Sabha general election.
- Same limit for Assembly general elections: not more than two Assembly constituencies in that State.
- In the 1991 Lok Sabha election **Devi Lal** contested from three constituencies; in response the RPA was amended in 1996. {45}
- A leader who wins more than one seat keeps only one, forcing bypolls in the rest.
- No rule makes a party bear the cost of by-elections for seats vacated by its candidate.
- Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1996 was based on the **Dinesh Goswami Committee** recommendations.
- RPA Amendment 1996: conviction for insulting the National Flag or Constitution = disqualified for six years; security deposit for Lok Sabha increased; no countermanding on a candidate's death. {46}
- After 1996, on a candidate's death a new candidate of that party contests in his place.
- Dinesh Goswami Committee was concerned with **electoral reforms**. {48}
- Dinesh Goswami Committee recommended government funding of parliamentary elections. {47}
- Dinesh Goswami was Law Minister in the V.P. Singh Government in 1989.

## Delimitation Commission
- Delimitation = fixing the number of seats and boundaries of territorial constituencies in each State/UT for Lok Sabha and Assemblies.
- It also fixes the seats reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
- Delimitation Commission is constituted by the Central Government under a law made by Parliament; not appointed by the Lok Sabha Speaker.
- Its task: redraw boundaries of Assembly and Lok Sabha constituencies on the basis of the latest census.
- Its purpose: rationalise constituencies on the principle of "One Vote and One Value".
- The Delimitation Commission decides which constituency is to be reserved. {49}
- As per the 2008 Delimitation order: of 543 elected Lok Sabha seats, 84 are reserved for SCs and 47 for STs (not 44).
- Orders of the Delimitation Commission cannot be challenged in a court of law. {50}
- Its orders are laid before Lok Sabha or the State Assembly, but they cannot modify them.
- Delimitation Commissions under the Delimitation Acts were constituted **four times**. {51}

- Chairman of the 2002 Delimitation Commission = Justice Kuldip Singh.
- Delimitation of Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland and Manipur was deferred for security risks by four presidential orders of 8 February 2008.
- Those deferment orders were rescinded on 28 February 2020.
- On 6 March 2020 a Delimitation Commission was set up under Section 3 of the Delimitation Act, 2002 for those four States and UT of Jammu and Kashmir.
- Its chairperson was Justice **Ranjana Prakash Desai**.
- In March 2021 the four north-eastern States were removed from the purview of the reconstituted Commission.
## Proportional representation
- Proportional representation ensures representation of minorities by giving due representation to all sections of society. {52,53}
- It gives due representation to all groups based on ethnicity, gender, interests and ideologies.
- Proportional representation is not necessary where a **two-party system** has developed. {54}
