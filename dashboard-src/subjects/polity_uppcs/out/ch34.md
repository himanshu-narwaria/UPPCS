## Ombudsman and origin of Lokpal idea
- **Ombudsman** (Scandinavian institution) = earliest democratic institution in the world for redressal of citizens' grievances.
- Ombudsman first created in **Sweden in 1809**, as an independent supervisory agency.
- Indian model of the Ombudsman = **Lokpal**. {8}
- To deal with corruption complaints against certain public functionaries, India proposed Lokpal at the Centre and Lokayukta at State level.
- First Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC, 1966-1970) recommended setting up **Lokpal and Lokayukta** for redressal of citizens' grievances. {6,7}
- First ARC gave this recommendation in its interim report dated 20 October 1966.
- First ARC was set up on 5 January 1966 under the chairmanship of **Morarji Desai**. {10}
- In 1967 K. Hanumanthaiah became Chairman of the first ARC; its term ended in 1970.
- Creation of a **Local Body Ombudsman** was recommended by the First Administrative Reforms Commission. {9}
- ARC proposal: Lokpal chairperson and members appointed by President on recommendation of a selection committee (PM as chairperson, Lok Sabha Speaker, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, CJI or SC judge nominated by him, one eminent jurist).
## Lokpal Bills
- First Lokpal Bill was introduced in Parliament in **1968**, in the 4th Lok Sabha. {11}
- It was passed by the Fourth Lok Sabha on 20 August 1969 (first Jan Lokpal Bill).
- Before Rajya Sabha could pass it, the Lok Sabha was dissolved and the bill lapsed.
- Lokpal Bill, 2011 was introduced in Lok Sabha on **4 August 2011** by V. Narayanaswami, then Minister of Parliamentary Affairs (not by Home Minister P. Chidambaram). {12}
- Lokpal Bill, 2011 was sent to the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Public Grievance, Law and Justice.
- Civil Society members termed the Lokpal Bill, 2011 'weak'.
- Five civil society members in the Lokpal Bill drafting panel: Anna Hazare, Arvind Kejriwal, Shanti Bhushan, N. Santosh Hegde, Prashant Bhushan. {13}
- Kiran Bedi and Prashant Joshi were not civil society members of the Lokpal Bill drafting panel.
- The Bill relating to Lokpal and Lokayukta took the form of an Act from 1 January 2014.
- Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (Lokpal at Centre, Lokayukta in States) came into force on **16 January 2014**.
## Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013
- Institution of Lokpal was established for the first time in India by the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013.
- Lokpal = a chairperson and not more than **eight members**.
- Section 4(1): Lokpal chairperson and members are appointed by the President on the recommendation of a selection committee.
- Lokpal selection committee: PM (Chairperson), Lok Sabha Speaker, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, CJI or SC judge nominated by him, one eminent jurist. {1}
- The eminent jurist is recommended by the other four members and nominated by the President.
- Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not in the Lokpal selection committee.
- Section 1(3): the Act applies to public servants both within India and outside India. {2}
- Minimum age for Lokpal Chairperson or Member = **45 years** on the date of assuming office. {3}
- Lokpal Chairperson or Member cannot be an MP or a member of the Legislature of any State or Union Territory.
- Lokpal Chairperson need not be only a CJI: other Supreme Court judges or a qualified 'eminent person' can also be Chairperson.
- The Prime Minister is within the jurisdiction of the Lokpal.
- **Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose** (retired Supreme Court judge) was the first Lokpal of India. {4}
- **Justice Ajay Manikrao Khanwilkar** (former Supreme Court judge) was appointed Lokpal Chairperson in 2024; assumed office on 10 March 2024. {5}
## Lokayukta in States
- **Maharashtra** was the first State to establish the Lokayukta, in 1972. {14,15,16}
- Maharashtra Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayuktas Act was enacted on 30 November 1971 and came into effect on 25 October 1972.
- **Odisha** was the first State to pass a Lokayukta Act (1970), but it came into force only in 1983.
- Odisha was also the first State to abolish the Lokayukta, in 1993.

- Rajasthan Lokayukta and Deputy Lokayukta Act, 1973: Section 7 = matters which may be investigated; Section 8 = matters not subject to investigation.
- Rajasthan Lokayukta and Up-Lokayukta mainly investigate corruption and misconduct by ministers and public servants acting in official capacity.
- Sexual harassment of women, caste discrimination and violence against children are not investigated by the Rajasthan Lokayukta. {21}
- Those cases fall under criminal laws, the SC/ST Act and the POCSO Act and are investigated by police or other authorities.
- Rajasthan Lokayukta can investigate: unreasonable harm by public servants, abuse of official position for illegal benefit, corruption or lack of transparency.
- In 2014 Rajasthan set up a high-level committee under Advocate General **Narpat Mal Lodha** to review the existing Lokayukta Act. {22}
## Minorities
- The term 'Minority' is not defined in the Constitution.
- Minority Commission is a statutory body, not a constitutional body, set up under the National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992; this does not explain why 'Minority' is undefined. {23}
- National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992 came into force on 17 May 1993; the commission became statutory and was renamed National Commission for Minorities. {24}
- First statutory National Commission for Minorities was set up on 17 May 1993.
- Gazette notification of 23 October 1993 (Ministry of Welfare) notified five minority communities: Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Zoroastrians (Parsis).
- **Jains** were notified as a minority community on 27 January 2014; so six communities are notified minorities at present.
- Zoroastrians are not the smallest religious minority; smaller minorities exist that are not notified.
- The Constitution recognises and protects religious and linguistic minorities.
- Section 21 of the **7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956** added Articles 350A and 350B (not the 9th Amendment). {25}
- Article 350A = facilities for instruction in mother tongue at primary stage.
- Article 350B = Special Officer for linguistic minorities, appointed by the President.
- The Special Officer is for the Union (all India), not one for each State.
- This officer is named Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities in India.

- National Commission for Women Act, 1990 was enforced on 30 August 1990.
- **Section 10** of the National Commission for Women Act, 1990 defines the functions of the Commission. {26}
- Secondary Education Commission (1952-53) was appointed in 1952 under **Dr. A. Laxman Swami Mudaliar**, Vice-Chancellor of Madras University. {27}
- It was also called the Mudaliar Commission; aim: improve the quality of secondary education.
- Mudaliar Commission stressed science, agriculture and technical education to match national development needs.

- **NITI Aayog** (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015.
- NITI Aayog is the main think-tank giving policy inputs and directions; the Prime Minister is its Chairman.
- NITI Aayog's Administrative Board: Chief Ministers of all States (including Delhi and Puducherry) and Lt. Governors of Union Territories.
- Ex-officio members of NITI Aayog: Home Minister, Agriculture Minister, Defence Minister, Finance Minister.
- **Vohra Committee** (1993) studied criminalisation of politics and the nexus among criminals, politicians and bureaucrats. {29,30}
- **Rajamannar** Committee/Commission recommended abolition of the Indian Administrative Service and Indian Police Service. {31}
- First Law Commission of independent India was constituted in 1955; Chairman = **M.C. Setalvad**, the then Attorney General. {78}
- National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution was set up by the NDA Government on 22 February 2000 under Justice **M.N. Venkatachaliah**. {79,80}
- Its appointment was not approved by Parliament; its report was recommendatory.
- Its function: review the working of the Constitution over the last 50 years and assess its present effectiveness.
- It submitted its last report on 31 March 2002.
- Central Pollution Control Board Chairman is nominated by the Central Government (not President, PM, Vice-President or Environment Minister).
- CPCB Chairman = a person with special knowledge or practical experience in environmental protection or in administering such an institution.
- At present Amandeep Garg (IAS) is the acting chairman of the Central Pollution Control Board.

- It was called the 'Constitutional Amendment Committee'; the 42nd Amendment resulted from its recommendations.
- Swaran Singh Committee said the Presidential system is inappropriate for India.
- Swaran Singh Committee (1976, Congress Party) also made recommendations about fundamental duties, felt necessary during the internal emergency.
- Kelkar Committee studied and evaluated the public-private partnership (PPP) model in India.
- Bakshi Tek Chand Committee (1948) enquired into the patent system and recommended reforms.

## Central Vigilance Commission
- CVC is the main agency for preventing corruption in the Central Government.
- CVC is an apex body promoting integrity, transparency and accountability in public administration.
- CVC was set up in February 1964 on the recommendation of the **Santhanam Committee** on Prevention of Corruption (1962-64). {83,84,85}
- Committee on Prevention of Corruption was appointed in 1962: K. Santhanam (parliamentarian) as Chairman, four other MPs and two senior officers.
- CVC was set up to advise and guide central government agencies in the field of vigilance.
- Originally CVC was neither a constitutional nor a statutory body; no Article of the Constitution describes it. {86}
- Parliament gave CVC statutory status by a law passed in September 2003.
- Article 268 = duties levied by the Union but collected and appropriated by the States.
- Article 276 = taxes on professions, trades, callings and employment.
## Mandal Commission and SC/ST report
- **Mandal Commission** was set up in 1979 by the Janata Party Government under Prime Minister Morarji Desai. {88}
- Its chairman was Bindeshwari Prasad Mandal (B.P. Mandal), a parliamentarian.
- Its task: identify socially and educationally backward classes and recommend steps for their advancement.
- Mandal Commission submitted its report in **1980**. {89}
- 1990 report of the National Commission for SCs and STs: main causes of atrocities = land disputes, alienation of tribal lands, bonded labour, indebtedness, non-payment of minimum wages.
- Religious causes are not among the causes of atrocities on SCs and STs in that report. {90}
- Article 340 = appointment of a commission to investigate the conditions of backward classes.
## Universal Declaration of Human Rights
- UDHR was adopted by the UN General Assembly on **10 December 1948**. {33}
- UDHR has **30 Articles**. {34}
- **10 December** is observed every year as Human Rights Day. {35}
- In 1950 the General Assembly passed resolution 423(V) inviting all states to observe 10 December as Human Rights Day.
- World Health Day = 7 April; UN Day = 24 October; Labour Day = 1 May.
- Concept of human rights mainly stresses the **dignity** of man as a human being; UDHR Preamble stresses dignity and justice for every human. {36}
- UDHR includes freedom and equality, right to life and liberty, right to education; it does not include the **right to privacy**. {37}
- UDHR is a milestone document but not a legally binding treaty; it first set out fundamental human rights to be universally protected.
- UDHR Article 3: everyone has the right to life, liberty and security of person.
- UDHR Article 25: right to a standard of living adequate for health and well-being.
- Right to **Universal Basic Income** is not included in the UDHR. {38}
- UDHR principles are reflected in the Preamble, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties, besides Fundamental Rights. {32}
- UDHR Article 3 is reflected in the Preamble and Fundamental Rights of the Indian Constitution.
- UDHR Article 26 (right to education; free and compulsory elementary education) is reflected in Article 45 of the Directive Principles.
- UDHR Article 26 also says technical and professional education be generally available and higher education equally accessible on merit.
- UDHR Article 29 (everyone has duties to the community) is reflected in the Fundamental Duties.
- UDHR Articles 3 to 21 = first generation human rights; Articles 22 to 27 = second generation human rights.
- Right to work (Article 23) and right to education (Article 26) are both second generation human rights.
- First generation rights deal with liberty and participation in political life; they are civil and political in nature.
- Eradication of all types of social discrimination comes under the **second generation** of human rights. {40}
- Third generation of human rights = community and collective rights.
! For the social discrimination question, the Jharkhand PSC held 'third generation' as the correct answer.
## Right to the City
- 'Right to the City' was proposed by sociologist Henri Lefebvre in 1968; David Harvey developed it in the new millennium.
- Habitat III (UN-Habitat) policy theme = "Right to the City and Cities for All"; but it is not an agreed agenda.
- Right to the City gives every occupant the right to reclaim public spaces and public participation in the city; the State cannot deny any public service or facility to unauthorised colonies in the city. {41}
! Right to the City and unauthorised colonies: the answer accepts 'the State cannot deny any public service or facility to unauthorised colonies' as correct, yet the same explanation says the right does not mean a guarantee of public services or facilities to unauthorised colonies.
- Definition: right of all inhabitants, present and future, to occupy, use and produce just, inclusive and sustainable cities as a common good.
- It implies responsibilities on governments and people to claim, defend and promote this right.
- Right to the city = spatially just resource distribution + political agency + social, economic and cultural diversity.
## Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993
- On 28 September 1993 the President promulgated the Protection of Human Rights Ordinance, 1993 under **Article 123**. {42}
- The Ordinance provided for a National Human Rights Commission, State Human Rights Commissions and Human Rights Courts.
- Protection of Human Rights Act came into force on **28 September 1993**; it extends to the whole of India. {43,44}
- Objectives of the Act: constitute National and State Human Rights Commissions and Human Rights Courts for better protection of human rights. {45}
- The Act defines human rights as rights relating to life, liberty, equality and dignity of the individual.
- These rights are those guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in International Conventions and enforceable by courts in India.
! The question on which right is outside NHRC purview was deleted from evaluation by MPPSC.
- **Section 2(M)** of the Act defines 'Public Servant'; same meaning as in Section 21 of the Indian Penal Code. {47}
- 'Armed forces' in the Act = naval, military and air forces and other armed forces of the Union; armed forces of the State are not included. {48}
- Every member and authorised officer of the Commission or State Commission is deemed a **public servant** (Section 21 IPC). {71}
- Section 31: for every Human Rights Court the State Government specifies a public prosecutor or appoints an advocate as Special Public Prosecutor.
- The advocate appointed as Special Public Prosecutor must have at least **7 years** of practice. {72}
## National Human Rights Commission
- NHRC is a statutory (non-constitutional) body, set up on **12 October 1993** under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. {75,76}
- Constitutional bodies: State Finance Commission (Article 243-I), Secretariat of Parliament (Article 98), Finance Commission (Article 280), Election Commission (Article 324).
- Article 338B = National Commission for Backward Classes; Article 340 = commission to investigate condition of backward classes.
- NHRC headquarters are in Delhi; it can set up offices at other places in India.
- NHRC is a multi-member body: a chairman and four other members.
- NHRC also has 6 ex-officio members and one special invitee to the statutory full commission.
- Chairperson of the Law Commission of India is not an ex-officio member of NHRC. {51}
- Chairpersons of the National Commissions for Minorities, for SCs and STs, and for Women are ex-officio members of NHRC.
- Section 4: NHRC Chairperson and members are appointed by the President on the recommendation of a 6-member committee.
- NHRC selection committee: PM (head), Lok Sabha Speaker, Home Minister, Leaders of Opposition in Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
- Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) is not a member of the NHRC selection committee. {49,50}
- Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Act, 2019 amended the 1993 Act.
- After 2019: NHRC Chairperson = a former Chief Justice of India or a judge of the Supreme Court; so it need not be headed only by a former CJI, and the term is 3 years, not 5. {56}
- 2019 Amendment: NHRC members with human rights knowledge raised from two to three, of whom at least one must be a woman.
- 2019 Amendment: term of NHRC and SHRC chairperson and members = **3 years or age 70**, whichever is earlier. {53}
- After their tenure, NHRC Chairman and members cannot take further employment under the Central or State Government.
- NHRC's function and powers are mainly recommendatory in nature.
- State Human Rights Commissions are constituted by the States and are not part of NHRC.
- Section 12 of the Act gives the functions of NHRC.
- NHRC functions include undertaking and promoting research in human rights and encouraging NGOs and institutions working in the field. {68}
- NHRC can regulate its own procedure; it has all the powers of a **civil court** and its proceedings have a judicial character. {73}
- For human rights violations by armed forces, NHRC's role is limited: it may **seek a report from the Central Government** and make recommendations. {74}
- **Justice Ranganath Misra** was the first Chairman of NHRC (12 October 1993 to 24 November 1996). {58}
- Justice H.L. Dattu was NHRC Chairman from 29.02.2016 to 02.12.2020. {59}
- Justice V. Ramasubramanian is NHRC Chairman since 30/12/2024.
## State Human Rights Commission
- The Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 provides for State Human Rights Commissions; currently 25 States have constituted them.
- After the 2019 Amendment, a Chief Justice or a Judge of a High Court is eligible to be SHRC Chairperson. {60}
- Section 22: SHRC Chairperson and members are appointed by the **Governor** on a committee's recommendation. {61,62}
- SHRC selection committee is headed by the **Chief Minister**. {63}
- Other members: Speaker of Legislative Assembly, State Home Minister, Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly. {65}
! Members of the SHRC selection committee: two answers are accepted, Speaker of the Legislative Assembly and Leader of Opposition.
- Where there is a Legislative Council, its Chairman and its Leader of Opposition are also members.
- The Governor is not a member of the SHRC selection committee. {64}
- A sitting High Court judge or sitting District Judge can be appointed to SHRC only after consulting the **Chief Justice of the High Court** of that State. {66}
- Governor can appoint a member as acting SHRC chairman when the office is vacant or the chairman cannot function due to absence or other reason. {67}
- Functions of a Human Rights Commission: suo motu inquiry into violations, visiting any jail, reviewing protection of human rights.
- The Commission cannot **punish** for violation of human rights. {69}
- SHRC submits its annual report to the **State Government**. {70}
- The report is laid before the State Legislature with a memorandum of action taken and reasons for not accepting any recommendation.
## State Finance and Information Commissions
- Chairperson and members of the State Finance Commission are appointed by the Governor.
- Central Information Commission was set up by Gazette notification under the Right to Information Act, 2005.
- Under the RTI Act, 2005 the Chief Information Commissioner's term was up to 5 years or 65 years of age.
- RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019: tenure of Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners is decided by the Central Government.
- After 2019, salary, allowances and facilities of Central and State Chief Information Commissioners and Information Commissioners are decided by the Central Government.
## Public Service Commissions: setup
- Articles 315 to 323 in **Part XIV** deal with composition, appointment, removal, independence, powers and functions of UPSC and SPSCs.
- **Article 315**: a Public Service Commission for the Union and one for each State. {91}
- UPSC is a **constitutional** body formed under Article 315; it is the central recruiting agency. {93}
- UPSC has nothing to do with the State Public Service Commission. {92}
- Article 315(2): two or more States may agree to have one Joint Public Service Commission.
- If the State legislatures pass a resolution to that effect, Parliament may by law provide for a Joint State Public Service Commission. {96,97}
- Article 315(4): UPSC may serve the needs of a State on request of the **Governor**, with approval of the **President**. {109,110}
- Article 316(1): Chairman and members of UPSC or a Joint Commission are appointed by the **President**. {98}
- Chairman and members of a State Public Service Commission are appointed by the **Governor**. {99,100,101}
- Government of India Act, 1919 first provided for a Public Service Commission in India. {115}
- First Public Service Commission was set up on 1 October 1926, with a Chairman and four members.
- It was named Federal Public Service Commission under the Government of India Act, 1935.
- Madhya Pradesh PSC was constituted by the President under **Section 118(3)** of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956. {116}
- Madhya Pradesh PSC has a maximum of **five members**. {117}
- **Rose Millian Bathew** was the first woman Chairman of UPSC (1992 to 1996). {118}
## Public Service Commissions: removal
- **Article 317**: removal of the Chairman or a member of a Public Service Commission. {102}
- Article 317(1): President removes by order for misbehaviour, only after the Supreme Court inquires on his reference and reports for removal. {103,107}
- The Supreme Court inquiry follows the procedure prescribed under Article 145.
- SPSC chairman and members are appointed by the Governor but can be removed only by the President. {104}
- Article 317(3): President may also remove for insolvency, paid employment outside office duties, or infirmity of mind or body.
- Parliament plays no role in removal of the UPSC Chairman, unlike High Court judges, Supreme Court judges and CAG. {106}
## Public Service Commissions: functions, expenses, reports
- Article 320 = functions of Public Service Commissions (Union and State).
- UPSC gets functions from the Constitution, parliamentary laws, executive rules and orders, and conventions. {94}
- **Article 320(3)(c)**: PSC must be consulted on all disciplinary matters affecting a person serving the government in a civil capacity. {105}
- This consultation includes memorials or petitions relating to such matters.
- SPSC is consulted on methods of recruitment, principles of appointment, promotions and transfers from one service to another.
- SPSC is not consulted on making transfers of civil servants. {108}
- **Article 321**: an Act of Parliament or of a State Legislature may give additional functions to UPSC or the SPSC. {111}
- Such functions may also cover services of a local authority, body corporate constituted by law or public institution.
- Article 318 = power to make regulations on conditions of service of members and staff of the Commission.
- **Article 322** = expenses of Public Service Commissions. {112}
- UPSC expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India; SPSC expenses on the Consolidated Fund of the State. {113}
- **Article 323**: UPSC presents its annual report to the President. {95}
- President lays it before each House of Parliament with a memorandum explaining cases where the Commission's advice was not accepted.
- Article 323(2): each State PSC submits its annual report to the Governor. {114}
- Article 325 relates to elections and has no clauses.
## Civil services
- In India, bureaucracy is the agency for the **implementation of public policy**. {119}
- Policy implementation by bureaucracy builds the credibility of the political executive among common people.
- **Article 310** = Doctrine of Pleasure. {120}
- Members of defence services, Union civil services and all-India services hold office during the pleasure of the President.
- Members of a State civil service hold office during the pleasure of the Governor.
- UPSC constituted a committee in July 2000 to review the civil services examination, under the chairmanship of Professor **Sukhadeo Thorat**. {121}
- **P.C. Hota** was appointed Chairman of the Committee on Civil Services Reforms in February 2004; report given within six months. {122}
- **Civil Services Day** = 21 April every year. {123}
- First Civil Services Day function was held at Vigyan Bhawan on 21 April 2006.
- Reason for the date: on 21 April 1947 Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel addressed the first batch of civil servants at Metcalf House.
