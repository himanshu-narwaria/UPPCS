@ 8 | The Preamble
## Origin of the Preamble
- The **Objectives Resolution** was moved in the Constituent Assembly by Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru on **13 December 1946**.
- The Assembly unanimously adopted it on **22 January 1947**; it later became the foundation of the Preamble.
- The idea of the Preamble was adopted from the Constitution of the **USA**.
- A preamble = an introductory statement that makes clear the objectives and philosophy of a document.
- The Constitution starts with the Preamble, which describes the nature, objectives and ideals of the Indian political system.
- The **Preamble** reflects the mind of the makers of the Constitution. {4,5}
- The objectives of the Constitution are reflected in the Preamble. {45}
- The Preamble holds the basic philosophy and fundamental values on which the Constitution is based.
## Text of the Preamble
- The Preamble begins with **"We, the People of India"**; these words show the sovereignty of the people. {29,30}
- People's sovereignty means Indians are not governed by any external power but are directed by the Constitution.
- The Preamble declares India a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic**, in that order. {7,9,10,11}
- It secures to all citizens: Justice, Liberty, Equality; and promotes Fraternity assuring dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the Nation. {27}
- **Justice** in the Preamble is of three types: **social, economic and political**. {46,47}
- "Religious justice" is not mentioned in the Preamble. {36}
- **Liberty** in the Preamble: of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; **economic liberty** is not included. {35}
- Equality in the Preamble: of status and of opportunity.
- The Preamble mentions **3 types of justice, 5 types of liberty, 2 types of equality and 1 kind of fraternity**. {6}
- The Preamble gives the date of adoption: "this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**, do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution". {1}
- The word **"Federal"** is not mentioned in the Preamble. {33}
- The words **"Public Welfare"** are not in the Preamble. {34}
- The word "People's" is not in the Indian Preamble; it is in the Constitution of China.
- The ideals and objectives of the Preamble are elaborated in the chapters on **Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties**. {44}
- The people's advancement should not hinder the nation's advancement, so the Constitution also aims at promoting fraternity.
- **Economic Justice** as an objective is provided in the Preamble and the Directive Principles of State Policy. {48}
- **Article 38(1)**: the State shall promote people's welfare by securing a social order in which justice, social, economic and political, informs all institutions of national life.
- **Social equality** is guaranteed in the Constitution under Right to Equality, **Articles 14 to 18**; the Preamble also aims at it. {51}
## 42nd Amendment and the Preamble
- On 26 January 1950, India's exact constitutional status was **"A Sovereign Democratic Republic"**. {2,3}
- The Preamble adopted on 26 November 1949 had the words Sovereign, Democratic and Republic; "Reliable" and "Non-religious" were never part of it. {22}
- The **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act 1976** added three words to the Preamble: **Socialist, Secular and Integrity**; they were absent in 1950. {8,12,13,14,15,17,18,21,23,24,26,37}
- "Sovereign Democratic Republic" became "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"; "Unity of the Nation" became "Unity and Integrity of the Nation". {28}
- The Preamble has been amended only once so far, in 1976; this amendment was held valid.
- The 42nd Amendment was based on the recommendations of the **Swaran Singh Committee**.
- "Sovereign" and "Republic" were already in the original Preamble, not added by the 42nd Amendment; "Gram Swarajya" was not added either.
- The **Preamble** describes India as a Secular State, through the word "Secular". {16}
- **Secular** in India = there is no official religion of the State; the State treats all religions equally. {32}
- The Indian Constitution embodies the positive concept of secularism.
- **Universalism** = the principle that "all individuals are fully and equally human". {31}
- Under universalism every person is entitled to human rights without any discrimination.
## Legal status and cases
- The Preamble is **non-justiciable**: its provisions cannot be enforced in a court of law. {20}
- The Preamble is a part of the Constitution but has no legal effect independently of other parts. {49}
- The Preamble can be amended; it cannot override the specific provisions of the Constitution. {19}
- The Preamble is very helpful in legal interpretation of the Constitution; it has utility, describes the aims of governance and helps give judicial meaning.
- **Berubari case (1960)**: Supreme Court first held the Preamble is not a part of the Constitution. {41}
- In the Berubari case the Supreme Court also called the Preamble the key to the minds of the Constitution makers.
- **Golaknath vs State of Punjab (1967)**: the Preamble was considered the core soul of the Constitution.
- **Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala (1973)**: Supreme Court accepted the Preamble as part of the Constitution and of its basic structure. {43}
- In Kesavananda Bharati a **13-judge bench** outlined the Basic Structure doctrine.
- In Kesavananda Bharati the Court said the Preamble can be used to explain ambiguous areas of the Constitution.
- **S.R. Bommai vs Union of India (1994)**: Supreme Court again held the Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution. {42}
## Sayings about the Preamble and Constitution
- The **Preamble** is called the **"Soul of the Constitution"**. {38}
- **Pt. Thakur Das Bhargav** said the Preamble is the soul of the Constitution.
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** called the Right to Constitutional Remedies (**Article 32**) the heart and soul of the Constitution.
! The book notes: if the "soul of the Constitution" is asked with respect to Dr. Ambedkar, the answer is the Right to Constitutional Remedies.
- **K.M. Munshi**, a Drafting Committee member, called the Preamble the political horoscope: "horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic". {39}
- **Subhash Kashyap**: if the Constitution is the body, the Preamble is the soul; if the Constitution is the building, the Preamble is the foundation stone.
- **Sir Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar**: "The Preamble to our Constitution expresses what we had thought or dreamt so long".
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** called the Constitution a pious paper, a basic and sacred document. {40}
- Ambedkar became Chairman of the Drafting Committee in 1947; he was a staunch constitutionalist who held that government must be constitutional.
