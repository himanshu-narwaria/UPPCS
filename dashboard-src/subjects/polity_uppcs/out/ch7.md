@ 7 | Schedules
## Number of Schedules
- The Constitution had **8 Schedules** at adoption; there are **12 Schedules** at present. {1,2}
- The Ninth, Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules were added by Constitution Amendment Acts. {3}
- The Constitution has 22 Parts and 395 Articles (not 20 Parts or 390 Articles).
| Schedule | Subject | Added by |
| First | States and Union Territories with their territories | Original {4,5} |
| Second | Salary, emoluments and allowances of Union office bearers (public officials, Judges, CAG) | Original |
| Third | Forms of oaths and affirmations | Original {8} |
| Fourth | Allocation of seats in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) to States and Union Territories | Original {6,13,27} |
| Fifth | Administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes | Original {9,52} |
| Sixth | Administration of tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram | Original |
| Seventh | Division of powers between Union and States: Union, State and Concurrent Lists | Original {11,12,18} |
| Eighth | Languages recognised by the Constitution | Original {10} |
| Ninth | Validation of certain Acts and Regulations (land reforms) | 1st Amendment Act 1951 |
| Tenth | Disqualification of members of Parliament and State Legislatures for defection (Anti-Defection Law) | 52nd Amendment Act 1985 {34,35,47,48} |
| Eleventh | Powers, authority and responsibilities of Panchayats (Panchayati Raj) | 73rd Amendment Act 1992 {62,63} |
| Twelfth | Powers, authority and responsibilities of Municipalities | 74th Amendment Act 1992 |
## First to Fourth Schedules
- The First Schedule must be amended if a new State or Union Territory is created.
- Third Schedule oaths cover: Union Minister, candidate for Parliament, Member of Parliament, Supreme Court Judges, Comptroller and Auditor-General.
- Third Schedule also covers: State Ministers, candidate for State Legislature, member of State Legislature, High Court Judges.
- The **President's oath** is in **Article 60**, not in the Third Schedule. {7}
## Fifth Schedule
- The Fifth Schedule covers Scheduled Areas and **Scheduled Tribes** in any State other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. {51}
- The Fifth and Sixth Schedules are made to protect the interests of Scheduled Tribes. {53}
- Scheduled Areas = areas the **President** declares so by order (Article 244(1); Part C of the Fifth Schedule).
- **Para 4** of the Fifth Schedule: a **Tribal Advisory Council** in any State having Scheduled Areas. {58}
- Largest administrative unit of a Scheduled Area = district; lowest = cluster of villages in the block. {59}
- At present **10 States** have Scheduled Areas: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan.
- The **Governor** (not the Chief Minister) reports to the President on administration of Scheduled Areas, annually or whenever the President requires.
- The President, in consultation with the Governor, can alter, add to or diminish the boundary of a Scheduled Area.
- The Governor can repeal or amend regulations for a State having Scheduled Areas, only with the President's assent.
- **Para 5** of the Fifth Schedule gives the Governor special power to prevent transfer of land from tribal people to non-tribal people. {50}
- **Samatha vs State of Andhra Pradesh (1997)**: Supreme Court held transfer of tribal land to private parties for mining unconstitutional, void and inoperative, under the Fifth Schedule. {49}
- The State's executive power continues in Scheduled Areas, subject to the Fifth Schedule; the Union cannot take over their total administration on the Governor's recommendation. {60}
- Neither the Fifth Schedule nor Article 339 has any such take-over provision.
- Part X, **Article 244** gives a special administrative structure for "scheduled areas" and "tribal areas".
## Sixth Schedule
- The Sixth Schedule applies to tribal areas of **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram**; not to Manipur, Nagaland, Sikkim or Arunachal Pradesh. {54,55,56,57}
- Its main objective is to save and maintain tribal culture.
- Sixth Schedule provisions come under **Articles 244(2) and 275(1)**.
- Tribal areas are administered as **Autonomous Districts** and Autonomous Regions.
- Each autonomous district has a **District Council** of not more than **30 members**: not more than 4 nominated by the Governor, the rest elected by adult suffrage.
- The Governor has the right to direct how Acts of Parliament apply in the Autonomous Districts of Assam. {61}
## Seventh Schedule: three lists
| List | Original subjects | Present subjects | Who makes laws |
| Union List (List I) | 97 | 98 | Only the Centre |
| State List (List II) | 66 | 59 | Primarily the States |
| Concurrent List (List III) | 47 | 52 | Both Centre and States {19} |
- If Union and State laws conflict on a Concurrent subject, the law made by Parliament prevails.
- **Article 246** distributes legislative powers, including taxation, between Parliament and State Legislatures.
- The right of Union and States to impose taxes and fees is given in the **Seventh Schedule**. {44}
- The 52 Concurrent subjects are the joint domain of State Governments, Union Territories and the Central Government.
## Union List subjects
- Union List: Radio and Television, stock exchanges and futures market, share market, Post Office, Banking, Census, Insurance, Defence, Railways, corporation taxes, Foreign Affairs.
- **Stock Exchange** and Post Office Savings Bank are Union List subjects; Stock Exchange is not Concurrent. {31}
- Inter-State trade and commerce = Union List, **Entry 42**.
- **Inter-State migration and inter-State quarantine** = Union List, **Entry 81**. {45}
- Corporation tax = Union List, **Entry 85**.
- Union List **Entry 72**: elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and offices of President and Vice-President. {43}
- State List **Entry 37**: elections to the State Legislature, subject to any law made by Parliament.
! The book notes: for the list and entry on State legislature elections, both List I Entry 72 and List II Entry 37 are correct; MPPSC's official key accepted both.
- **Space Research** = residuary subject, vested in the Centre. {32}
## State List subjects
- State List: Police, public order, public health and sanitation, gas, agriculture, railway police, prisons, Panchayati Raj or local government, land reforms.
- **Gas** is a State List subject; Banking, Insurance and Census are Union List. {36}
- **Agriculture** is a State List subject; Defence, Foreign Affairs and Railways are Union List. {37}
- **Railway Police** is in the State List, though Railways is in the Union List. {38,39}
- **Panchayati Raj** (Local Government) = State List, **Entry 5**. {40}
- **Land Reforms** = State List. {46}
- Public Health and Sanitation = State List Entry 6; Treasure Trove = State List Entry 44; per-person tax = State List.
- Tax on agricultural income = State List Entry 46; tax on consumption or sale of electricity = State List Entry 53.
- Trade and commerce within the State = State List Entry 26, subject to Entry 33 of the Concurrent List.
## Concurrent List subjects
- Concurrent List: economic and social planning, criminal procedure, population control and family planning, education, forests, marriage, divorce, adoption, electricity.
- **Economic and Social Planning** = Concurrent List, **Entry 20**; the Planning Commission was set up under this entry. {20,21}
- **Prevention of cruelty to animals** = Concurrent List, Entry 17. {22}
- **Criminal law**, including all matters in the Indian Penal Code (at present BNS 2023) = Concurrent List. {23}
- **Criminal Procedure** = Concurrent List; public order, prisons and police = State List. {41}
- **Population control and family planning** = Concurrent List, Entry 20-A, added by the 42nd Amendment Act 1976. {24}
- **Marriage, divorce and adoption** = Concurrent List, Entry 5. {42}
- **Adulteration of foodstuffs and other goods** (Entry 18) and **Succession** (Entry 5) are in the Concurrent List. {25}
- Concurrent Entry 33: trade, commerce, production, supply and distribution of products of Union-controlled industries and like imported goods; foodstuffs including edible oilseeds and oils; cattle fodder including oilcakes and other concentrates; raw cotton (ginned or unginned) and cotton seeds; raw jute.
! The book notes: trade and commerce in Entry 33 goods is also in the Concurrent List, but UPPSC's key left "Trade and Commerce" out because inter-State and intra-State trade are in the Union and State Lists.
- **Protection of wild animals and birds** (Entry 17B) and **price control** (Entry 34) are in the Concurrent List. {26}
- **Education** was first in the State List (Entry 11); the **42nd Amendment 1976** moved it to the Concurrent List. {28,29}
- The **42nd Amendment Act 1976** moved Forests, Education, and Protection of Wild Animals and Birds from the State List to the Concurrent List. {30}
- Provisions bearing on education: Directive Principles, rural and urban local bodies, and the Fifth, Sixth and Seventh Schedules. {33}
- Article 45 (after the 86th Amendment Act 2002): early childhood care and education for all children till age six.
- Articles 243G (Panchayats) and 243W (Municipalities) give powers that include management of education; the Fifth Schedule covers education indirectly.
## Eighth Schedule
- The Eighth Schedule originally had **14 languages**; at present **22 languages**.
- **Sindhi** was added by the 21st Constitutional Amendment Act 1967.
- **Konkani, Nepali and Manipuri** were added by the 71st Amendment Act 1992.
- **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali** were added by the 92nd Amendment Act 2003.
- Oriya was renamed **Odia** by the 96th Amendment Act 2011.
## Ninth Schedule
- The Ninth Schedule was introduced during the prime ministership of **Jawaharlal Nehru**, by the First Amendment Act 1951 (June 1951). {14}
- State land reform and Zamindari abolition laws are placed in the **Ninth Schedule** for constitutional protection. {15}
- It holds Acts and Regulations: originally **13**, presently **284**; State laws on land reforms and Zamindari abolition, and Parliament's laws on other matters.
- It was added to protect its laws from judicial scrutiny on the ground of violation of Fundamental Rights.
- Main aim of the First Amendment: fully secure the constitutional validity of Zamindari abolition laws in general and certain specified State Acts.
- Parliament can place a law in the Ninth Schedule; but in **2007** the Supreme Court ruled laws added after **24 April 1973** are open to judicial review. {16}
- The **Coelho case** (Ninth Schedule case) upheld judicial review of laws, including Ninth Schedule laws, against violation of the basic structure. {17}
## Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules
- The Tenth Schedule lays down the process by which legislators may be disqualified on grounds of defection.
- The Eleventh Schedule has **29 subjects** (entries); it is mentioned in **Article 243G**. {64}
- The Twelfth Schedule has **18 subjects**.
- The Eleventh Schedule gives Panchayats administrative control; the Twelfth gives Municipalities administrative control.
