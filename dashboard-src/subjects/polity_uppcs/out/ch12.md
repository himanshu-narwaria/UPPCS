@ 12 | The Citizenship
## Constitutional provisions
- Citizenship is dealt with in **Part II** of the Constitution. {1,2}
- Citizenship Articles = **Articles 5 to 11**. {3}
| Article | Subject |
| 6 | Rights of citizenship of certain persons who migrated to India from Pakistan |
| 7 | Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to Pakistan |
| 8 | Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside India |
| 9 | Persons voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State are not to be citizens |
| 11 | Parliament has power to regulate the right of citizenship by law {12} |
- Only Parliament has the exclusive power of determining the issue of citizenship in India (Article 11: acquisition and termination of citizenship).
! Question on who has exclusive power to determine citizenship (options: Court, President, Lok Sabha, Central Government, State Government): none of the options is correct, since the answer is Parliament.
## Single citizenship
- India, like Britain, adopted **single citizenship** for the whole country; every citizen has citizenship of India only. {4,5}
- Single citizenship concept was adopted from the British Constitution of the United Kingdom.
- In India a person can have only one citizenship and, at a particular time, only one domicile. {8}
- Any citizen (by birth or naturalised) can become the Head of State, i.e. the President, in India.
- The **USA** has dual citizenship: one of the U.S.A. (federation) and one of the state where the citizen resides. {11}
- Articles 371-A to 371-I were inserted to meet regional demands of Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa. {10}
## Citizenship Act, 1955: acquiring and losing citizenship
- Using Article 11, Parliament passed the Indian Citizenship Act in **1955**; it provides for acquisition and termination of citizenship.
- Five ways of acquiring citizenship: Birth, Descent, Registration, Naturalisation, Incorporation of territory; depositing money in Indian banks is a commercial action, not a way. {6}
- Three ways of losing citizenship (whether acquired under the Act or earlier under the Constitution): Renunciation, Termination, Deprivation; a citizen loses it by renouncing it or by voluntarily acquiring another country's citizenship; marrying a foreigner or criticizing the government is not a way. {7}
- Citizenship by Registration = Section 5; Citizenship by Naturalization = Section 6.
- Section 5(1)(a): a person of Indian origin can get citizenship by registration after being ordinarily resident in India for **7 years** before applying. {13}
- A naturalized citizen may be deprived of citizenship under Section 10 of the Citizenship Act, 1955.
- Registration or naturalization can be cancelled if obtained by fraud, false representation or concealment of a material fact.
- It can also be cancelled if the citizen shows disloyalty or disaffection to the Constitution by act or speech.
- It can also be cancelled if the citizen unlawfully traded or communicated with an enemy during a war India is engaged in.
- It can also be cancelled if the citizen has been ordinarily resident out of India for a continuous period of seven years.
- Central Government made the Citizenship Rules, 2009 under Section 18 of the Citizenship Act, 1955.
- The Citizenship Act was amended in 1957, 1985, 1986, 1992, 2003, 2005, 2015 and 2019.
## Overseas Citizen of India cardholder
- Section 7A of the Citizenship Act, 1955 (inserted by the Citizenship Amendment Act, 2015): Central Government may register a person as an Overseas Citizen of India cardholder on application, subject to prescribed conditions.
- Eligible: person of full age and capacity who is a citizen of another country but was a citizen of India at or any time after the commencement of the Constitution.
- Eligible: citizen of another country who was eligible to become a citizen of India at the commencement of the Constitution.
- Eligible: citizen of another country who belonged to a territory that became part of India after 15 August, 1947.
- Eligible: a child, grandchild or great-grandchild of such a citizen; also a minor child of any such person.
- Eligible: a minor child whose both parents are citizens of India or one parent is a citizen of India.
- Eligible: spouse of foreign origin of a citizen of India or of an OCI cardholder, whose marriage is registered and has subsisted for at least two continuous years before the application.
- Such a spouse must get prior security clearance from a competent authority in India.
- Not eligible: a person who, or whose parent, grandparent or great-grandparent, is or has been a citizen of Pakistan, Bangladesh or any other country notified by the Central Government; so Indians who migrated to Pakistan after partition are not eligible. {9}
## Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019
- Citizenship (Amendment) Bill 2019: passed by Lok Sabha on 9 December 2019, by Rajya Sabha (Parliament) on **11 December 2019**; President's assent on 12 December 2019. {15,16}
- The Act inserted Section 6B in the Citizenship Act, 1955; it came into force on 10 January, 2020.
- It grants citizenship to Hindu, Sikh, Jain, Parsi, Buddhist and Christian minorities from Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan who came due to religious persecution on or before 31 December, 2014; they are not treated as illegal migrants. {17}
- Citizenship is granted through certificates of registration or naturalisation, subject to conditions; Muslims are not included in this Act.
- For the first time it provides citizenship based on religion, to non-Muslim communities from the three countries.
- Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2024 were notified on 11 March 2024 to implement Section 6B.
- The Act does not apply to tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Tripura included in the Sixth Schedule.
- The Act does not apply to areas under the inner line under the Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation, 1873.
