@ 24 | Parliamentary Committees
## Why committees and their kinds
- Parliament is assisted by a number of **committees** in the discharge of its duties.
- Reason: Parliament's work is varied, complex and voluminous, its time is limited and it is too unwieldy to deliberate closely on every matter; so a good deal of business is done by committees.
- Parliament exercises control over the administration through **Parliamentary Committees** (Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Standing Committees). {1}
- Committees are appointed or elected by the Houses, or nominated by the Speaker / Chairman.
- They work under the direction of the Speaker / Chairman and report to the House or to the Speaker / Chairman.
- Two main kinds: **Standing committees** and **Ad-hoc committees**.
- Standing committees = permanent (constituted every year or periodically), work on a continuous basis.
- Ad-hoc committees = temporary; cease to exist when the assigned task is completed.
- Six classes of Standing Committees: (i) Financial Committees, (ii) Department-related Standing Committees (total 24), (iii) Committees of Enquiry, (iv) Committees to Scrutinise and Control, (v) Committees relating to day-to-day business of the House, (vi) Housekeeping or Service Committees.
- **Financial Committees** (three) = Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings. {2,25,26}
- These three are also called the three main Standing Committees.
- Joint Committee on Salaries and Allowances of Members of Parliament is a **Housekeeping Committee**, not a Financial Committee.
- Committees of Enquiry include: Committee on Petitions, Committee on Privileges, Ethics Committee.
- Most standing (joint) committees, leaving some exceptions, take members from Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha in the ratio **2:1**. {7}
- The Speaker of Lok Sabha appoints the chairpersons of all parliamentary committees.

## Public Accounts Committee (PAC)
- PAC was first set up in **1921** under the Government of India Act, 1919; it has existed since then.
- PAC is one of the most important committees; it is Parliament's tool for a financial check on the executive and controls public expenditure.
- PAC has not more than **22 members: 15 from Lok Sabha and (not more than) 7 from Rajya Sabha**. {9,18}
- PAC has no eminent persons of industry and trade; only members of the two Houses.
- The 15 Lok Sabha members are elected every year from its members by proportional representation by single transferable vote; the Rajya Sabha members are nominated by that House and associated with the committee.
- PAC is constituted every year; its tenure is one year.
- A minister cannot be elected a member of the PAC.
- PAC Chairman is appointed by the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** from amongst its members. {8,20}
- Till 1966-67 the PAC Chairman belonged to the ruling party.
- In **1967-68** the Speaker for the first time appointed an opposition member as PAC Chairman; the practice continues since then (Chairman from the opposition in Lok Sabha).
- Main function of PAC: examine the annual audit reports of the **Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)** and the financial accounts of the government. {10,11,13}
- PAC scrutinises the appropriation and finance accounts of the Government and examines the CAG report (which includes those accounts). {19}
- Basis of PAC deliberation: Appropriation Accounts of Railways, Defence Services, P&T Department and other Civil Ministries, the CAG reports on them, and CAG reports on revenue receipts.
- PAC examines various expenditure reports and accounts under the purview of Parliament.
- CAG reports are laid before Parliament by the President.
- PAC is assisted by the CAG.
- PAC ensures public money is spent as Parliament decided; it calls attention to waste, extravagance, loss or nugatory expenditure.
- PAC highlights misuse and misappropriation of public expenditure before each House and recommends action against public authorities responsible.
- PAC counterchecks public expenditure on the basis of the CAG report and lays the report before each House of Parliament.
- PAC submits its report to the **Speaker of Lok Sabha**, who gets it presented in the **Lok Sabha** (Parliament); a copy is laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha. {14,15,16,17}
- Ordinarily PAC presents reports to the House; only when the House is not in session it may present to the Speaker and later to the House.
## Estimates Committee
- Estimates Committee was constituted for the first time in **1950**.
- It has **30 members**, all from Lok Sabha, elected every year by Lok Sabha from among its members; no Rajya Sabha member. {22,23,24}
- Rajya Sabha members are associated with the PAC and the Committee on Public Undertakings, but not with the Estimates Committee.
- Estimates Committee is the **largest committee** of Parliament (among PAC, Public Undertakings and Petitions committees). {3,4}
- Tenure of Estimates Committee members = **one year**. {5}
- Its chairman is appointed by the Speaker from amongst its members.
- Most important function: suggest alternative policies to bring efficiency and economy in administration.
- It also reports what improvements in organisation, efficiency or administrative reform, consistent with the policy underlying the estimates, may be made.
- It monitors the examination of estimates, austerity and the expenditure of funds.
## Committee on Public Undertakings
- Committee on Public Undertakings has **22 members: 15 elected by Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha**.
- A minister is not eligible for election to it; its term is one year.
- It analyses the accounts and working of state-owned PSUs and checks the disinvestment policies of PSUs.
- Function (a): examine reports and accounts of public undertakings listed in the Fourth Schedule to the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha.
- Function (b): examine the reports, if any, of the CAG on public undertakings.
- Function (c): examine whether public undertakings are managed on sound business principles and prudent commercial practices, in the context of their autonomy and efficiency.
- Function (d): exercise other functions of the PAC and Estimates Committee about public undertakings not covered above, as allotted by the Speaker.
- The CAG audit report is examined by the **Public Accounts Committee**; the Committee on Public Undertakings also examines CAG reports. {27}
! For the question "CAG audit report is examined by", both Public Undertakings Committee and Public Accounts Committee are given as correct answers.
- The CAG (Article 148) is **not** a tool of parliamentary control over public expenditure; PAC, Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings are. {6}
- CAG has powers in relation to the accounts of any other authority as prescribed by law made by Parliament.
## Committee on Petitions
- Committee on Petitions: **15 members in Lok Sabha**, **10 members in Rajya Sabha**.
- A minister is not nominated to the Committee on Petitions.
- Function: consider and report on petitions presented to the House.
- It also considers representations from individuals and associations on subjects not covered by the petition rules and gives directions for their disposal.
## Department-related Standing Committees
- In **1993**, on the recommendation of the Rules Committee of Lok Sabha, **17** Department-related Standing Committees were set up.
- In **2004** their number was increased from 17 to **24**.
- Of the 24 DRSCs, **8 are under Rajya Sabha and 16 under Lok Sabha**; they have members from both Houses.
- Committee on Welfare of SCs and STs is given as one of the Departmental Standing Committees.
! For "which is not a Standing Committee" (Public Accounts, Estimates, Welfare of SCs and STs, Public Undertakings), no given answer is correct.
- Under Lok Sabha: Committee on Social Justice and Empowerment, Committee on Urban Development, Committee on Labour.
- **Committee on Health and Family Welfare** is under the jurisdiction of Rajya Sabha. {31}
- DRSCs and ad hoc committees set up by Parliament may review the working of **independent regulators** (telecom, insurance, electricity). {38}
- Example: the Joint Parliamentary Committee on 2G spectrum allocation reviewed the policy on spectrum pricing and grant of telecom licences.
- Finance Commission, NITI Aayog and Financial Sector Legislative Reforms Commission have no role in reviewing independent regulators.
## Ethics Committee
- Origin: a resolution at the Presiding Officers Conference, New Delhi, **October 1996**, asked legislatures to explore constituting an Ethics Committee.
- Ethics Committee of **Rajya Sabha** = first such committee of any legislature in India; constituted by the Chairman, Rajya Sabha in **1997**.
- In Lok Sabha the Committee of Privileges recommended an Ethics Committee during the 13th Lok Sabha.
- First Ethics Committee in Lok Sabha was constituted on **16 May 2000** by Speaker G.M.C. Balayogi (13th Lok Sabha).
- Lok Sabha Ethics Committee was at first **ad-hoc**; it became permanent only in **2015**, when its rules were put in the Rules of Procedure; it cannot take up a sub-judice matter. {29}
- Rule 233A(1), Chapter XXA: any person or member may complain about unethical conduct of a Lok Sabha member; a complaint by a person must be forwarded by a member.
- Rule 233A(10), Chapter XXA: the committee shall not take up a sub-judice matter; its decision on whether a matter is sub-judice is final.
## Other committees
- **Committee on Subordinate Legislation**: examines and reports whether the executive properly uses powers to make regulations, rules, sub-rules and bye-laws delegated by Parliament or conferred by the Constitution. {36,37}
- Committee on Subordinate Legislation exists in both Houses; 15 members in each; nominated by Speaker (Lok Sabha) or Chairman (Rajya Sabha); ministers cannot be members.
- **Committee on Government Assurances** (each House): examines assurances, promises and undertakings given by ministers on the floor of the House and reports how far they are implemented.
- **Rules Committee**: considers matters of procedure and conduct of business in the House and recommends amendments or additions to the Rules.
- **Business Advisory Committee**: regulates the programme and time-table of the House.
- No parliamentary committee exists for **welfare of minorities**; instead a Minority Commission has been set up. {30}
- Parliamentary committees do exist on Public Undertakings, Government Assurances and Estimates.
## Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs
- The Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs works under the overall direction of the **Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs**. {12}
- The Minister of Parliamentary Affairs nominates MPs on committees, councils, boards and commissions set up by the Government of India in various ministries.
- **Consultative Committees of MPs for Railway Zones** are constituted by the Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs; MPs of the respective zones are members. {32}
## 2G spectrum Joint Parliamentary Committee
- The proposal for a JPC on the 2G spectrum scam was passed by Lok Sabha on **24 February 2011** and by Rajya Sabha on **1 March 2011**.
- The 2G JPC had **30 members: 20 from Lok Sabha and 10 from Rajya Sabha**. {34}
- **P.C. Chacko** was Chairman of the 2G JPC; he submitted the report to the then Lok Sabha Speaker Meira Kumar. {35}
