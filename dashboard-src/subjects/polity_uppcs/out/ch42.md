## United Nations: founding and membership
- The **United Nations** is an international organization founded in **1945**. {1}
- The draft UN Charter was finalized at the San Francisco conference held from April to June 1945.
- On 26 June 1945, 50 nations signed the Charter.
- Poland's representative was absent at San Francisco; Poland ratified the Charter on 16 October 1945 as the 51st member.
- The UN came into being on **24 October 1945**; this date is observed as **United Nations Day**. {2}
- Switzerland became a UN member on 10 September 2002; it was not a member in 1996.
- UN membership years: Iraq 1945, Libya 1955, Namibia 1990.
- **South Sudan** was created on 9 July 2011 as the newest and 193rd country of the world; its current capital is Juba. {26}
- General Assembly, International Court of Justice and Trusteeship Council are three of the six principal organs of the UN.
- **Antonio Guterres** (a Portuguese politician and diplomat) is the present UN Secretary-General. {14}
## UN Security Council
- UNSC is one of the six principal organs of the UN; it is charged with maintaining peace and security.
- UNSC has 15 members: **5 permanent** (USA, France, China, UK, Russia) and 10 non-permanent; Germany is not a permanent member. {3,4}
- The 10 non-permanent members are elected on a regional basis for a term of 2 years.
- Only the five permanent members have **veto power** to stop a resolution being passed with a majority; not every member has it. {7}
- The veto rests on Article 27 of the UN Charter: a Council decision needs 9 affirmative votes including the votes of the five permanent members, so any permanent member can withhold a decision.
- The demand for democratisation of the UN mainly relates to the **Security Council**, as it has only five permanent members. {8}
- India, Brazil and South Africa are among the countries demanding democratisation of the Security Council.
- India began a two-year term as non-permanent UNSC member on 1 January 2021 (2021-22 term).
- Others elected for the 2021-22 term: Ireland, Kenya, Mexico and Norway; Canada was not elected. {5}
- April 2024: UNSC rejected the proposal for Palestine's full UN membership; USA vetoed it, Switzerland and UK abstained = **three** members. {6}
## UN peacekeeping, committees and observers
- UN peacekeepers can use force in self-defence and also in defence of the mandate.
- Peacekeeping operations can be withdrawn only by a UN Security Council resolution; USA is the largest budget contributor (around 28% in 2020-21). {9}
- The **UN Credentials Committee** is appointed at the beginning of each regular session of the General Assembly (not set up by the Security Council).
- It has nine members, appointed by the General Assembly on the proposal of the President; it usually meets in November.
- It assesses the credentials of all UN members before submitting a report to the General Assembly for approval. {10}
- The General Assembly can grant observer status to non-member States; inter-governmental organisations can seek it; Permanent Observers can maintain missions at UN headquarters. {11}
- Permanent observer status is based purely on practice; the UN Charter has no provision for it.
- The practice dates from 1946, when the Secretary-General accepted the Swiss Government as a permanent observer.
## Good governance (UN)
- UN lists **8 characteristics** of good governance: Participation, Rule of law, Transparency, Responsiveness, Consensus Oriented, Equity and Inclusiveness, Effectiveness and Efficiency, Accountability. {12,13}
- "Government legitimacy" and "religiosity and emotionality" are not among them.

## UN agencies and conventions
- The **International Court of Justice** was established in June 1945; headquarters at **The Hague** (the Netherlands). {27}
- Indian judges elected to the ICJ: Benegal Narsingh Rau (1952-1953), Nagendra Singh (1973-1988), Raghunandan Pathak (1989-1991), Dalveer Bhandari (2012-2018, at present). {28}
- On 20 November 2017 Dalveer Bhandari was re-elected by the UN General Assembly and Security Council for a 9-year term (2018-2027).
- The **International Labour Organisation** was established in **1919** as part of the Treaty of Versailles. {29}
- ILO is a specialised agency of the UN; headquarters in Geneva.
- The Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air comes under UNTOC (UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime), not UNCAC.
- UNCAC (UN Convention against Corruption) is the only legally binding universal anti-corruption instrument; UNODC is mandated by member States to assist in implementing both UNCAC and UNTOC. {30}
- Returning assets to rightful owners from whom they were taken illicitly is covered under UNCAC, not UNTOC.
- The UN observed **1975** as the International Year for Women. {137}
- International Day of Persons with Disabilities = **3 December**, celebrated annually since 1992. {138}
- International Day of Non-violence = **2 October**, the birthday of Mahatma Gandhi. {139}
- The UN General Assembly fixed this day by a resolution on 15 June 2007.
- Sweden was the first European Union country to officially recognise the State of Palestine, in October 2014. {136}
- On 30 September 2015 the Palestinian flag was raised outside the UN Headquarters for the first time.

## USA
- The **White House** is the official residence and workplace of the US President; John Adams became the first President to live in it on 1 November 1800; it is a symbol of American democracy. {37}
- Its foundation stone was laid in 1792; construction was almost complete in 1800.
- Its official address is **1600** Pennsylvania Avenue, Washington D.C. (not 1800).
- The US President and Vice President are not directly elected; they are chosen by "electors" through the **Electoral College**, not by members of Congress, the House or the Senate. {38}
- The Electoral College is the mechanism set by the US Constitution for the indirect election of the President.
- The first primary in the US presidential election takes place in **New Hampshire** and Iowa. {39}
- The first primaries are held about a year ahead of the presidential election; conventionally the election process starts from here.
- The concept of a **written Constitution** first came into existence in the United States of America. {40}
- **Larry Pressler**, a US Senator, brought the Pressler Amendment in 1986 to link US assistance with nuclear non-proliferation.
- He criticized the Clinton administration's assistance to Pakistan because of Pakistan's involvement in nuclear proliferation. {41}
- Pressler Amendment = linked American aid to development of nuclear capacity; Chakma refugees = Bangladeshi refugees in India; Perestroika = Gorbachev; 13 September 1993 = Israel–PLO accord. {42}
- Perestroika was the economic policy of Mikhail Gorbachev, the last President of the Soviet Union.
- The State of Israel was proclaimed in May 1948; five Arab nations attacked in support of the Palestinian Arabs.
- **Pivot to Asia** is the foreign policy strategy of the USA. {43}
- In July 2023 the USA reiterated that Arunachal Pradesh is an integral part of India. {65}
- In March 2024 the US foreign ministry again affirmed Arunachal Pradesh as part of India and contradicted China's claim.

## India's foreign policy
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** was the first Indian Prime Minister to visit Afghanistan, in September 1959. {60}
- Indira Gandhi visited Afghanistan in 1976; Narendra Modi visited on 4 June 2016.
- Nehru, after taking over as interim Prime Minister, announced a foreign policy based on **non-alignment**. {61}
- Non-alignment = staying away from international power groups and developing an independent policy.
- **Panchsheel** = five rules of conduct: mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty; mutual non-aggression; non-interference in internal matters; mutual benefit and equality; peaceful co-existence.
- Non-alignment is not a principle of Panchsheel; the policy of non-alignment is itself based on Panchsheel. {62}
- **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** was Prime Minister when India's nuclear doctrine of **No First Use** was adopted. {63}
- India enunciated No First Use after the 1998 nuclear test; the doctrine was formally adopted in January 2003.
- India's Six Points Formula proposal for the South Asian region was presented by Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee. {64}
- Tashkent Agreement (Pakistan) 10 January 1966 → Indo-Soviet Treaty of friendship and cooperation 9 August 1971 → Shimla Agreement (Pakistan) 2 July 1972 → Farakka Accord (Bangladesh) 1977. {93}
- The Farakka Accord was signed temporarily in 1977 and finally on 12 December 1996.
- On 3 December 1971 India intervened on behalf of Bangladeshi refugees who had fled oppression in East Pakistan after Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's arrest.
- On 16 December 1971 the Pakistani Army surrendered unconditionally; East Pakistan became **Bangladesh** (December 1971). {94}
- Sheikh Mujibur Rahman became the first Prime Minister of Bangladesh.
- India–Sri Lanka Accord, 1987: keeping the integrity of Sri Lanka was part of it; complete freedom for a Tamil State was not mentioned. {134}
- The first Festival of India outside India was organised in **Britain in 1982**. {123}

## Awards
- **Param Vir Chakra** = India's highest military decoration; instituted on 26 January 1950.
- Four replicas of "Indra Vajra" are embossed on the Param Vir Chakra medal. {78}
- It can be given to a person once or more than once in a lifetime.
- Till 31 December 2025, only 21 people have been awarded the Param Vir Chakra.
- Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles barred by Article 18; they only denote State recognition of good work by citizens.
- Padma Awards (instituted 1954) are announced every Republic Day, except breaks during 1978 to 1979 and 1993 to 1997 (two interruptions).
- The number of Bharat Ratna awards is restricted to a maximum of **three** in a year (not five). {79}
- **Bharat Ratna** is the highest civilian award of India; instituted in 1954 along with Padma Vibhushan. {83}
- Bharat Ratna is given for exceptional service/performance of the highest order in any field of human endeavour; it is treated differently from Padma Awards.
- The Prime Minister recommends Bharat Ratna to the President; no formal recommendations are necessary.
- First Bharat Ratna awardees (1954): **C. Rajagopalachari, Dr. S. Radhakrishnan and C.V. Raman**. {81,82}
- Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Pakistan), 1987 = first non-Indian Bharat Ratna awardee; Nelson Mandela (South Africa), 1990 = second of two non-Indians.
- Pranab Mukherjee got Bharat Ratna in 2019; he was India's 13th President.
- Bharat Ratna 2024: five people — Karpuri Thakur, L.K. Advani, P.V. Narasimha Rao, Chaudhary Charan Singh and M.S. Swaminathan; four posthumous (all except Advani). {80}

- Its jury has five members: Prime Minister (Chairperson, ex officio), Chief Justice of India, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, and two other eminent persons. {84}
- If the Leader of Opposition post is vacant, the leader of the single largest opposition party in Lok Sabha is the member.
- One of the two nominated eminent persons may be the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
- The President is not a jury member; the President confers the award as constitutional head.
- Gandhi Peace Prize 2021 was conferred on **Gita Press, Gorakhpur**. {85}
- The prize is open to all persons regardless of nationality, race, language, caste, creed or gender.
- It carries Rs. 1 crore, a citation, a plaque and an exquisite traditional handicraft/handloom item.

## Important days and campaigns
- **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas** (NRI Day) = **9 January**, marking Mahatma Gandhi's return from South Africa to India in 1915. {124}
- The first PBD Convention was held on 9 January **2003**, to mark the overseas Indian community's contribution to India's development. {125}
- On 8 January 2002 PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee declared the day on the report of the Lakshmimal Singhvi Committee.
- PBD was held every year from 2003 to 2015; since 2015 it is held once every 2 years.
- 17th PBD 2023 (at Indore): theme "Diaspora: Reliable Partners of India's Progress in Amrit Kaal".
- 18th PBD, 8-10 January 2025: theme "Diaspora's Contribution to a Viksit Bharat".
- National Voters' Day = 25 January; Constitution Day = 26 November; World Human Rights Day = 10 December. {127}
- **Good Governance Day** = 25 December, birthday of former PM Atal Bihari Vajpayee; announced in 2014. {128}
- **Har Ghar Tiranga Abhiyan** was launched by the Central Government under Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav, for the 75th anniversary of independence. {129}
- Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav began on 12 March 2021 (75-week countdown) and ended on 15 August 2023.
## Countries and regions in the news
- Argentina: worst economic crisis; Sudan: war between regular army and paramilitary forces; Turkey has not left NATO. {130}
- Argentina: among the world's highest inflation rates, over a decade of stagnation and rising poverty; cause = persistent public overspending financed by money creation.
- Javier Milei assumed office as President of Argentina on 10 December 2023, with a mandate to end inflation and revive growth.
- Sudan civil war began on 15 April 2023 (during Ramadan): Sudanese Armed Forces under Abdel Fattah al-Burhan vs Rapid Support Forces under Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo "Hemedti".
- RSF and its allies are together called the Janjaweed coalition; thousands of civilians were killed and millions displaced.
- The conflict traces to April 2019, when Sudan's President Omar al-Bashir was overthrown by military generals after a nationwide uprising.
- NATO was established in 1949; Turkey joined in 1952; all NATO members must approve new applicants.
- Turkey objected when Sweden and Finland asked to join in 2022; in January 2024 it finally backed Sweden's bid.
- **Chechnya** is in the North Caucasus of the Russian Federation, in the most southern part of Eastern Europe, 100 km from the Caspian Sea. {131}
- Darfur is a region in Western Sudan (not Mali).
- Swat is a river valley and district in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan (not Iraq); the Swat River rises in the Hindu Kush.
- The **Berber**-speaking community (Amazigh) lives in North African countries like Morocco and **Algeria**. {132}
- Berbers speak a language completely different from Arabic; they are the original inhabitants of the region.
- **Arctic Council** was established by the Ottawa Declaration in 1996; it is the pre-eminent intergovernmental forum on Arctic issues.
- Its eight members: Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, the Russian Federation and the United States; Japan and the United Kingdom are not members. {133}
- It promotes cooperation among Arctic States, with Arctic indigenous communities, on sustainable development and environmental protection.
- India and Japan are among its 12 permanent observers.
- Its chairmanship changes every two years: Iceland 2019–2021; Kingdom of Denmark 2025–2027.
