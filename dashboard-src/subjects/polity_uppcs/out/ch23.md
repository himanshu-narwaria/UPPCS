@ 23 | The Parliament (3): Conduct of Business and Legislative Procedure
## Parliament: composition
- The Parliament of India is the supreme legislative body.
- India has a parliamentary system of government based on the **Westminster model**.
- **Article 79** (Part V): Parliament = the **President + Council of States + House of the People**; these three are its integral parts. {5,6,7,8,9,10,11,12,13}
- **Article 59(1)**: the President shall not be a member of either House of Parliament. {4}
- The President does not sit in Parliament or attend its meetings, yet is an integral part of it.
- The **Vice-President** is not a part of Parliament. {14}
- Lok Sabha [Article 81(1)(a)]: not more than 530 representatives of States, chosen by direct election from territorial constituencies.
- Lok Sabha [Article 81(1)(b)]: not more than **20 representatives of Union Territories**. {1}
- Council of States: not more than 250 members = 12 nominated + not more than 238 representatives of States and Union Territories.
- The President could nominate not more than two Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha if the community was not adequately represented.
- 104th Amendment Act, 2019 (amending Article 334): Anglo-Indian nomination in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies not extended beyond 25 January 2020; so it has ended.
- Earlier both Houses had nominated members: 12 in Rajya Sabha and 2 in Lok Sabha (Anglo-Indian); now only the Rajya Sabha has nominated members. {32}
- No constitutional bar on a nominated member becoming a Minister; a Minister must be an MP or become one within six months.
- **Article 54**: President is elected by an Electoral College of elected members of both Houses and of State Legislative Assemblies; nominated members cannot vote.
- Elected members of the State Legislative Assembly (Lower House) vote in elections to both Lok Sabha (as citizens) and Rajya Sabha; also for the President. {30}
- Representation of the People Act, 1951 was amended in 2003: a Rajya Sabha candidate need not be a voter of that State.
- The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of this amendment in 2006.
- The **Golden Jubilee** of the Indian Parliament was celebrated on **13 May 2002**; Parliament was constituted on 13 May 1952. {139}
- The new Parliament building was designed by architect **Bimal Patel**; Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated it on 28 May 2023. {140}
- The previous Parliament building was designed by British architects Sir Edwin Lutyens and Sir Herbert Baker.
- In 1925 **Vitthalbhai Patel** was elected the first Speaker of the Central Legislative Assembly; tenure 24 August 1925 to 28 April 1930. {142}
## President's address and sessions
- **Article 87**: the President addresses **both Houses of Parliament assembled together** and informs Parliament of the causes of its summons. {2}
- Article 87(1), two occasions: start of the first session after each Lok Sabha general election, and start of the first session of each year. {3}
- The President's speech highlights the government's policy priorities and plans for the coming year; it gives a broad framework of its agenda.
- All executive power is vested in the President; the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister aids and advises him.
- **Article 85(1)**: the gap between two sessions cannot exceed **six months**; so Parliament must meet at least **twice a year**. {15,16}
- Usually three sessions a year: Budget session, Monsoon session and Winter session.
- **Officers (officials) of Parliament**: Speaker, Deputy Speaker and Secretary-General of Lok Sabha; Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha. {17}
- **Article 120(1)**: business in Parliament is transacted in Hindi or English (subject to Article 348, notwithstanding Part XVII).
- Article 120(1) proviso: Chairman or Speaker may permit a member who cannot express himself in Hindi or English to speak in his **mother tongue**. {40}
## Membership: vacation and disqualification
- **Article 101** deals with vacation of seats.
- **Article 101(4)**: if a member is absent from all meetings for **60 days** without informing (permission of) the House, the House may declare his seat vacant. {18,19,20}
- In counting the 60 days, any period when the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days is excluded.
- First member disqualified for 60 days' absence: **Barjinder Singh Hamdard**, Independent member of the **Council of States** from Punjab, in 2000. {21}
- **Article 101(2)** (Rule 2, Prohibition of Simultaneous Membership Rules, 1950): one cannot be member of Parliament and a State Legislature together.
- If so elected, his seat in Parliament falls vacant unless he resigns the State Legislature seat within **14 days**. {22}
- **Article 102(1)(a)**: a person holding an **office of profit** under the Government of India or a State is disqualified from being an MP. {24}
- Exception: an office declared by Parliament by law not to disqualify its holder.
- "Office of profit" is not defined in the Constitution or in the Representation of the People Act, 1951.
- The **Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959** lists offices exempted from disqualification; it was amended five times. {25}
- Office of profit is decided by Parliament for the Union [Article 102(1)(a)] and by State Legislatures for States [Article 191(1)(a)]. {26}
- Whether a member is disqualified: President decides on Election Commission's advice [Article 103(1),(2)]; in States the Governor decides on Election Commission's advice [Article 192(1),(2)].
! "Who will decide the Office of Profit?" has two keyed answers: Union Parliament (who defines it) and President and Governor (who decide disqualification).
## Privileges
- **Article 105**: powers, privileges and immunities of the Houses of Parliament, their members and committees. {27}
- **Article 122**: courts shall not inquire into the proceedings of Parliament. {29}
- Parliamentary privileges = special rights of each House collectively and of each member individually, needed to do their work efficiently.
- Collective privileges: prohibit publication of debates and proceedings; exclude strangers from the House; regulate own procedure and conduct of business.
- Collective privileges: punish members and outsiders for breach of privilege or contempt of the House.
- Collective privileges: no arrest or service of legal process within the precincts of the House without permission of Chairman/Speaker.
- Collective privileges: right to get immediate information of arrest, detention, conviction, imprisonment or release of a member.
- **Freedom from attendance as a witness** is an individual privilege, not a collective one. {28}
- A **Privilege Motion** can be raised against a Minister who withholds facts of a case or gives a distorted version. {23}
## Legislative powers of Parliament
- The right to amend the Constitution is vested in Parliament under **Article 368**.
- Parliament can legislate for the Union of India, the territory of India, and Indian subjects/residents and their property anywhere in the world. {33}
- **Article 248**: residuary legislative power; Parliament alone makes law on matters not in the State or Concurrent List. {39}
- Parliament can legislate on State List subjects in four cases: Article 253, Article 252, Article 249 and Proclamation of Emergency (given as Article 357). {35,37}
- **Article 252**: Parliament legislates for two or more States by their consent (resolutions passed by the States). {34}
- **Article 249**: in the national interest, after a Council of States resolution passed by 2/3 majority.
- **Article 253**: Parliament can make law on State List matters, for the whole or any part of India, to implement international treaties, agreements or conventions, **without the consent of any State**. {36,38}
- Article 253 also covers decisions made at any international conference, association or other body.
- The sovereignty of Parliament is restricted by **judicial review**; the Supreme Court can check the validity of laws made by Parliament. {138}
- Laws made by the legislature must not infringe the Basic Structure of the Constitution.
- **Article 123**: the President may promulgate ordinances at any time except when both Houses are in session, if immediate action is needed. {134}
- National Development Council is presided over by the Prime Minister; it cannot be constituted without him.
- **44th Amendment Act, 1978**: Right to Property ceased to be a Fundamental Right and became a legal right (Article 300-A).
- **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**: Preamble changed from "Sovereign Democratic Republic" to "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"; "Unity of the Nation" became "Unity and Integrity of the Nation".
- No provision lets the Speaker of Lok Sabha resolve a dispute of Parliament; disagreement between Houses goes to a Joint Session.
## Conduct in the House, Zero Hour
- **Yielding the floor**: Speaker/Chairman stops a member from speaking and lets another member speak. {41}
- **Crossing the floor**: a member changes party allegiance, leaving one party and joining another or becoming independent.
- **Interpellation**: parliamentary procedure of demanding that a government official explain some act or policy.
- Decorum: every member should enter and leave the chamber so as not to disturb the proceedings.
- Members should keep a standard of conduct inside and outside the House that enhances the dignity of Parliament.
- **Zero Hour** is an **Indian innovation** in parliamentary procedure; a unique feature of India's Parliament. {44}
- Zero Hour began in the early sixties, when members raised urgent public matters right after Question Hour, with or without the Chairman's prior permission.
- Zero Hour can be a maximum of **one hour**; its duration is not fixed in the Rules. {45}
- Zero Hour timing: Lok Sabha 12 noon to 1 pm (after Question Hour); Rajya Sabha 11 am to 12 noon (before Question Hour). {42}
- Question Hour timing: Lok Sabha 11 am to 12 noon; Rajya Sabha (from 2014) 12 noon to 1 pm.
- In Zero Hour members raise important matters that cannot be delayed; it is described as a question-and-answer session. {46}
## Questions and motions
- Parliament controls the Council of Ministers through Adjournment Motion, Question Hour and Supplementary Questions. {128}
- **Question Hour** = first hour of every sitting, generally kept for asking and answering questions.
- **Starred Question**: member wants an oral answer; marked with an asterisk (*); supplementary questions can be asked. {129}
- Only 20 questions can be listed for oral answer on a day.
- **Unstarred Question**: not called for oral answer; no supplementary questions.
- **Short Notice Question**: on a matter of urgent public importance; asked with shorter notice than an ordinary question; supplementaries follow.
- **Adjournment Motion**: allows discussion on a definite matter of urgent public importance. {130}
- Adjournment motion draws the government's attention where a starred question, short duration discussion or resolution with notice would be too late.
- **Calling Attention**: with the Speaker's prior permission a member calls a Minister's attention to an urgent public matter; the Minister makes a brief statement or asks for time.
- "Calling Attention" is a concept given by India; it is like an Adjournment Motion but without condemnation, and has restricted its scope. {127}
- Calling Attention is a new concept in modern parliamentary procedure; questions and supplementary questions are asked during it.
- **Censure Motion**: expression of strong disapproval or harsh criticism, generally against government policies.
- Censure Motion differs from a No-confidence Motion in purpose and procedure; reasons must be stated; separate leave of the House is not required. {122}
! Censure Motion question: two options were factually correct, so the Bihar Public Service Commission deleted it from evaluation.
- **Rule 184** (Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha): discussion is followed by voting; a ruling party getting fewer votes may lose power. {131}
- **Rule 377**: matters that are not points of order and cannot be raised under rules on Questions, Short Notice Questions, Calling Attention or Motions.
- Rule 377 notice is given in writing to the Secretary-General with the text of the matter; the Speaker's consent is needed.
- In 2015 the number of matters under Rule 377 was raised from 20 to 30 per day (decision of Speaker's meeting with Leaders of Parties).
- **Closure Motion**: moved by a member to cut short debate; there are four kinds; Guillotine is one.
- **Guillotine**: undiscussed clauses of a bill or resolution are put to vote with the discussed ones when allotted time is over; it stops the debate. {133}
## Bills: stages and lapse
- An ordinary (legislative) Bill can be introduced in **either House** of Parliament. {65}
- A Bill on creation of All-India Services can only be introduced in the Rajya Sabha.
- Article 107: provisions on introduction and passing of Bills.
- A Bill passes three stages (readings) in each House.
- First stage: leave is asked to introduce the Bill; if granted, it is introduced.
- **Second reading**: general discussion on the Bill as a whole, then clause-by-clause consideration (as introduced or as reported by Select/Joint Committee). {47}
- Third reading: debate is confined to arguments; then the member in charge moves that the Bill be passed.
- **Private member's Bill** = Bill presented by any member other than a Minister; about a dozen have been passed since independence. {113}
- A private member must give notice and ask leave of the House to introduce a Bill.
- If a Bill was published in the official gazette before introduction, no motion for leave is needed.
- **Article 107(5)**: a Bill pending in Lok Sabha, or passed by Lok Sabha and pending in Rajya Sabha, lapses on dissolution of Lok Sabha (subject to Article 108). {66}
- **Article 107(4)**: a Bill pending in Rajya Sabha and not passed by Lok Sabha does not lapse on dissolution of Lok Sabha. {67}
- **Article 107(3)**: a Bill pending in Parliament does not lapse by prorogation.
- Prior sanction of the President is needed for Bills on: formation of a new State, altering State boundaries, taxation in which States are interested, and Money Bills. {121}
## Joint sitting
- **Article 108**: the President summons both Houses to a joint sitting to resolve a deadlock over a Bill; Article 109 is not the joint-sitting Article. {50,51,58}
- **Article 118** relates to Rules of Procedure; under **Article 118(4)** the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** presides over a joint sitting. {52,53,54,55,56}
- If the Speaker is absent: Deputy Speaker → Deputy Chairman of the Council of States → a person chosen by the members present.
- Joint sitting is only for **ordinary bills** (or non-money financial bills) on which the Houses disagree; not for Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills. {59,62,63,64}
- No joint sitting for a Money Bill: Rajya Sabha cannot block it and must return it within 14 days.
- A Constitution Amendment Bill (Article 368) must be passed by each House separately by special majority.
- Quorum for a joint sitting = **1/10th** of the total members of both Houses (Lok Sabha Rules of Procedure). {61}
- **Article 108(4)**: at a joint sitting the Bill is passed by a **simple majority** of members of both Houses present and voting. {48}
- No joint sitting can be summoned if the Bill has already lapsed by dissolution of Lok Sabha.
- **Article 108(5)**: if Lok Sabha is dissolved after the President notified his intention to summon it, the joint sitting is still held; the Bill does not lapse. {49}
- Only three joint sittings since 26 January 1950: Dowry Prohibition Bill (first, 1961); Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill 1977 (second, 1978); Prevention of Terrorism Bill/POTA (third, March 2002). {57,60}

- **Article 110**: a Bill is a Money Bill if it contains **only** provisions on all or any of these matters: {83,84}
- (a) imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any tax.
- (b) regulation of borrowing or giving of guarantee by the Government of India, or amending the law on its financial obligations.
- (c) custody of the Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund of India; payment into or withdrawal from them.
- (d) appropriation of money out of the Consolidated Fund of India.
- (e) declaring any expenditure as charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, or increasing such expenditure.
- (f) receipt of money on account of the Consolidated Fund or Public Account of India, its custody or issue, or audit of accounts of the Union or a State.
- (g) any matter incidental to matters in (a) to (f).
- Provisions for imposition of **fines or penalties** are not Money Bill provisions. {85}
- **Article 110(3)**: the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill; his decision is final. {89,90,91}
- The Speaker's decision cannot be questioned in any court, in either House or even by the President; he need not consult anyone.
- The Speaker endorses his certificate that it is a Money Bill when it goes to Rajya Sabha and to the President for assent.
- **Article 109(1)**: a Money Bill is introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, on the President's recommendation; never in the Rajya Sabha. {87,92,95,96,97,98,99,100}
- Article 109(2): Rajya Sabha must return a Money Bill, with or without recommendations, within **14 days** of receipt. {101,102,103,104}
- Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a Money Bill; it only recommends, and Lok Sabha may accept or reject all or any recommendations. {94,105}
- Article 109(3): if Lok Sabha accepts any recommendation, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses with those amendments.
- Article 109(4): if Lok Sabha accepts none, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses in the form passed by Lok Sabha.
- Article 109(5): if not returned within 14 days, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses in the form passed by Lok Sabha.
- A Money Bill cannot be referred to a joint session; the President cannot return a Money Bill for reconsideration.
- An ordinary Bill has no time limit in the Rajya Sabha.
! Two U.P.P.C.S. questions say "Finance Bill" where Money Bill is meant (presented in Lok Sabha; Rajya Sabha delay of 14 days); they are answered as Money Bill.
## Financial Bills
- Financial bills deal with fiscal matters (revenue or expenditure); three kinds: Money Bills (Article 110), Financial Bills (I) [Article 117(1)], Financial Bills (II) [Article 117(3)].
- Every Money Bill is a Finance Bill, but every Finance Bill is not a Money Bill.
- **Article 117(1)**: a Bill with Article 110(1)(a) to (f) matters needs the President's recommendation and cannot be introduced in the Council of States (only in the Lower House). {93}
- Financial Bill (I) is like a Money Bill in two ways: only in Lok Sabha, only on President's recommendation.
- Otherwise Financial Bill (I) follows ordinary bill procedure: Rajya Sabha can reject or amend it. {88}
- For Financial Bills (I) and (II) the President may (not necessarily) summon a joint sitting to resolve a deadlock.
- The President can return (refuse to sign) a Finance Bill for reconsideration; Rajya Sabha is not limited to 14 days on a Finance Bill that is not a Money Bill. {86}
- **Article 117(3)**: Bills involving expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India (Financial Bill II) can be introduced in **either House**. {106}
- For Article 117(3) Bills the President's recommendation is essential for consideration of the Bill.
- If a Finance Bill is not a Money Bill, Rajya Sabha can hold it like an ordinary Bill; if it is a Money Bill, only for 14 days. {135}
## Funds
- **Article 265**: no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law. {68}
- **Article 266**: Consolidated Funds and Public Accounts of India and of the States.
- Article 300: suits and proceedings. Article 368: procedure for amendment of the Constitution.
- **Consolidated Fund of India** (Article 266): all revenues received by the Government of India (taxes and other receipts) are credited to it. {70}
- It also holds all loans raised by issue of treasury bills, loans or ways and means advances, and all money received in repayment of loans.
- The Consolidated Fund is under Parliament's control; no money can be withdrawn without **Parliament's authorization** by appropriation [Article 114(3)]. {69,71,77}
- To withdraw funds from the Consolidated Fund of India the government must pass Appropriation Bills.
- Disbursements from the **Public Account** need no authorization from Parliament; the Executive controls this account.
- **Article 267**: Parliament may by law establish a **Contingency Fund of India**, in the nature of an imprest. {75}
- The Contingency Fund [Article 267(1)] is at the President's disposal; he makes advances for unforeseen expenditure **before authorisation by Parliament** (pending it under Article 115 or 116). {76}
## Charged expenditure
- Expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India [Article 112(3)] is not put to the vote of Parliament; it can only be discussed in either House. {110}
- Parliament has no role in creating the budget; it cannot impose or increase any tax without the recommendation of the President.
- Charged: emoluments and allowances of the President and other expenditure of his office.
- Charged: salaries and allowances of the Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha and Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha.
- Charged: debt charges of the Government of India, with interest, sinking fund charges, redemption charges and other expenditure on raising loans and servicing debt.
- Charged: salaries, allowances and pensions of Supreme Court Judges; pensions of Federal Court Judges; pensions of High Court Judges.
- Charged: salary, allowances and pension of the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.
- Charged: sums needed to satisfy any judgment, decree or award of any court or arbitral tribunal.
- Charged: any other expenditure so declared by the Constitution or by Parliament by law.
- **Not** charged: salary and pension of members of the Election Commission of India. {72}
- **Not** charged: salary and allowances of the **Vice-President** as such. {73}
- The Vice-President gets salary and allowances as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, which are charged on the Consolidated Fund.
- **Not** charged: salary and allowances of the **Prime Minister**. {74}
## Budget
- **Article 112**: the President causes the **Annual Financial Statement** to be laid before both Houses for every financial year. {107,108}
- Annual Financial Statement = statement of estimated receipts and expenditure of the Government of India for the year; commonly called the Union Budget.
- Financial year: 1 April to 31 March of the following year.
- The word **"Budget"** does not appear in the Constitution; Article 112 uses "Annual Financial Statement". {109}
- The Union Finance Minister lays the Annual Financial Statement on behalf of the **President** (not the Prime Minister).
- The Ministry of Finance prepares the Union Budget; within it the **Department of Economic Affairs** prepares and presents it (not the Department of Revenue). {78}
- Six stages of the budget: presentation; general discussion; scrutiny by departmental committees; voting on demands for grants; passing of Appropriation Bill; passing of Finance Bill.
- Stages of normal financial legislation: presentation of Budget, discussion on Budget, passing of Appropriation Bill, passing of Finance Bill. {120}
- Vote on account, vote of credit and exceptional grants (Article 116) are not part of normal financial legislation.
- **Article 113(2)**: estimates of expenditure are submitted to the Lok Sabha as **Demands for Grants**; it may pass, reject or reduce a grant. {119}
- **Article 113(3)**: no demand for a grant can be made except on the **recommendation of the President**. {79}
- Usually one demand per ministry or department; large ministries have more than one.
- Demands for Grants: 102 in Budget 2025-26; 101 in Budget 2021-22. {80}
- Parliamentary control over public finance: Annual Financial Statement; Appropriation Bill before withdrawal (Article 114); supplementary grants and vote on account (Articles 115, 116); Finance Bill. {111}
- If the Lok Sabha does not pass the annual Union Budget, the Prime Minister submits the resignation of the Council of Ministers (majority lost). {112}
- The **Economic Survey** is presented in Parliament every year **before** the Budget for the coming year. {114}
- Economic Survey gives the progress, activities and fiscal details of various sectors of the economy for the current year.
## Vote on Account and Cut Motions
- **Article 116**: if the Appropriation Bill is not passed, **Vote on Account** lets the government withdraw money from the Consolidated Fund of India for a fixed period. {115,116,117}
- Vote on Account [Article 116(1)(a)] is used when there is no time to pass the full Budget before the new financial year, or elections are near.
- Vote on Account = the incumbent government draws money from the Consolidated Fund to meet expenses until elections are done; not sanction of all demands for the year. {126}
- Vote on Account covers only expenditure for the next few months; an **Interim Budget** gives both expenditure and receipts. {118}
- A vote-on-account is sometimes referred to as an Interim Budget.
- **Cut Motion**: Lok Sabha motion to reduce the amount of a demand for grant; it is related to the Union Budget. {123,124,125}
- The object of a cut motion is also to draw the House's attention to the matter specified in it.
- If a cut motion is adopted and the government lacks the numbers, it is obliged to resign.
| Cut motion | Meaning |
| Policy cut (Disapproval of Policy Cut) | Budget demand be reduced to Re. one |
| Economy cut (Economic / frugality cut) | Budget demand be reduced by a specified amount |
| Token cut | Budget demand be reduced by rupees one hundred |
## Committees, Secretariat, Indian Parliamentary Group
- **Estimates Committee** = largest financial committee of Parliament: 30 members, all from Lok Sabha; no Rajya Sabha member.
- Parliament controls the administration through parliamentary committees.
- The Secretariat of Parliament is **independent of the Government**. {136}
- The idea of a separate Secretariat independent of the Executive was mooted in January 1926 by Vithalbhai J. Patel, President of the Central Legislative Assembly.
- **Article 98(1)**: each House has a separate secretarial staff; posts common to both Houses may be created.
- **Article 98(2)**: Parliament may by law regulate recruitment and service conditions of the secretarial staff of either House. {137}
- **Indian Parliamentary Group**: autonomous body formed in 1949, after a motion adopted by the Constituent Assembly (Legislative) on 16 August 1948.
- Its membership is open to all MPs and ex-MPs; an MP can become a Life Member by paying a life subscription.
- A life member who ceases to be an MP is called an "Associate Life Member".
- Its affairs are managed by the Executive Committee; the Speaker of Lok Sabha is its ex-officio President.
- The Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha and the Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha are its ex-officio Vice-Presidents.
- Aims: promote personal contacts between MPs; spread information through seminars on issues likely to come before Parliament.
- It arranges lectures on political, defence, economic, social and educational problems, and visits to foreign countries.
- It links the Parliament of India with other Parliaments by exchanging delegations, goodwill missions, correspondence and documents.
- It is the National Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union and the Main Branch of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Association in India.
