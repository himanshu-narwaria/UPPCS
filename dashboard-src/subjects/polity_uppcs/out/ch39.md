@ 39 | Official Language
## Part XVII and language of the Union
- Official language is dealt with in **Part XVII**, Articles 343 to 351.
- Part XVII has four heads: Language of the Union; Regional Languages; Language of the Judiciary; Texts of Laws and special directives.
- The term 'Official Language of India' is nowhere mentioned in the Constitution.
- Article 343(1): official language of the Union = **Hindi in Devanagari script**; numerals = international form of Indian numerals.
- Hindi is the official language of the Union, not the national language of India.
- Article 343(2): English to continue for all official purposes of the Union for **15 years** from the commencement of the Constitution. {1}
- Article 343(3): Parliament may by law make an exception to Article 343(1); under Article 100(1) such a law needs a simple majority.
- Using this power Parliament enacted the Official Language Act, 1963.
- Section 3 of that Act: English also to be used, in addition to Hindi, as official language of the Union from 26 January 1965.
- For some purposes of the Union Government, English is mandatory.
- The Official Language Act can be amended by Parliament with a simple majority.
- Articles 343, 344, 346 and 348(1) can be amended only by the special procedure of Article 368(2); no ratification by State legislatures is needed.
! The question on how Parliament can amend the official language provision has two answers: 2/3rd majority as per its Hindi version, simple majority as per its English version.
- Constituent Assembly accepted Hindi as the official language on **14 September 1949**; 14 September is Hindi Diwas.
## Official Language Commission and Committee
- Article 344: President constitutes an Official Language Commission after 5 years and again after 10 years from the commencement of the Constitution.
- Article 344 deals with the commission and committee of Parliament on official language.
- First Official Language Commission: appointed by Dr. Rajendra Prasad on 7 June **1955**, chairman **B.G. Kher**. {6}
- Kher Commission gave its report in 1956; recommended steps to replace English with Hindi eventually.
- Parliamentary Committee on Official Language under Govind Ballabh Pant was set up in 1957 to review the Kher Commission's report.
- Article 344(4): 30-member Joint Parliamentary Committee on official language: 20 from Lok Sabha, 10 from Rajya Sabha.
## Languages of States, courts and minorities
- **Article 345**: a State Legislature may by law adopt one or more languages in use in the State, or Hindi, for its official purposes. {2}
- Until the State Legislature so provides, English continues for the official purposes it was used for before the Constitution.
- Article 345 is subject to Articles 346 and 347.
- Article 346: official language for communication between two States or between a State and the Union.
- Article 347: special provision for a language spoken by a section of a State's population.
- Article 348(1)(a): all proceedings in the Supreme Court and every High Court are in English.
- Article 348(2): Governor, with previous consent of the President, may authorise Hindi or another official language of the State in proceedings of the High Court having its principal seat in that State.
- **Article 350A**: every State and local authority shall try to provide instruction in the mother tongue at primary stage to children of linguistic minorities. {5}
- Under Article 350A the President may issue necessary directions to any State; added by the 7th Amendment Act, 1956.
- A State cannot compel linguistic minorities to take their mother tongue as medium of instruction in primary schools.
- Article 350B: Special Officer for linguistic minorities, appointed by the President.
- The Special Officer investigates all matters on safeguards for linguistic minorities and reports directly to the President.
## Eighth Schedule languages
- Languages recognised by the Constitution are listed in the Eighth Schedule; any language can be added to it.
- Originally the Eighth Schedule had 14 languages; at present it has **22 languages**. {18}
| Amendment | Languages added to Eighth Schedule |
| 21st Amendment, 1967 | Sindhi (15th language) {11} |
| 71st Amendment, 1992 (August 1992) | Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali {14,15} |
| 92nd Amendment Act, 2003 | Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali {7,9} |
! The book prints the four languages of the 92nd Amendment Act, 2003 as added in 2003 in one place and in 2004 in another.
- Before the 92nd Amendment there were 18 languages; after it, 22.
- The 22 languages: Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santhali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu.
- **Bodo** is a recognised language in the Eighth Schedule. {4}
- **Bhojpuri** is not included in the Eighth Schedule. {10,13}
- Rajasthani is not included in the Eighth Schedule. {12}
- English is not one of the 22 languages of the Eighth Schedule. {16}
- 'Angika' is not included in the Eighth Schedule.
- **Uttarakhand** gave Sanskrit the status of second official language on 1 January 2010. {19}
- Later former Chief Minister Ramesh Pokhriyal 'Nishank' declared Rishikesh a Sanskrit city.
## Speakers of languages
- Census 1991 and 2001: among Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi and Telugu, Bengali had the most speakers; Telugu was second. {20}
- Census 2001 order: Bengali (8.11%) > Telugu (7.19%) > Marathi (6.99%) > Tamil (5.91%). {21}
- Census 2011 order: Hindi (43.63%) > Bengali (8.03%) > Marathi (6.86%) > Telugu (6.70%) > Tamil (5.70%).
- Hindi speakers: 41.03% in Census 2001 and 43.63% in Census 2011; Hindi is the most spoken language.
- **Brahui** is a language of Baluchistan but belongs to the Dravidian family. {23}
## Classical languages
- On 12 October 2004 the Government created the category 'Classical Languages'; **Tamil** was the first classical language.
- Classical status is given to preserve and advance old literary traditions.
| Classical language | Year of status |
| Tamil | 2004 |
| Sanskrit | 2005 |
| Telugu | 2008 {26} |
| Kannada | 2008 |
| Malayalam | 2013 |
| Odia | 2014 |
| Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, Bengali | 3 October 2024 (Union Cabinet approval) |
- Till 2014 there were 6 classical languages; Gujarati is not a classical language.
- Total classical languages at present = **11**; Telugu, Marathi and Bengali are all classical languages. {24,25}
