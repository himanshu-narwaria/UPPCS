@ 33 | The Planning Commission / NITI Aayog
## Planning Commission
- The Planning Commission was established on **15 March 1950**. {7}
- It was formed by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet (Government of India), to make planning effective in India. {8}
- It was set up by the first Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru to optimise scarce resources in the newly-born nation.
- It was established on the recommendation of an advisory planning board chaired by **K.C. Neogy**.
- The Planning Commission was neither a constitutional body nor a statutory body; it is not mentioned in the Constitution; it was an extra-constitutional body. {11,12,13,14}
- UPSC and State Public Service Commissions (Article 315) and the Finance Commission (Article 280) are provided in the Constitution.
- The **Prime Minister** was the ex-officio Chairman of the Planning Commission. {17,18}
- **Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru** was the first Chairman of the Planning Commission. {16}
- **Gulzarilal Nanda** was the first Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission.
- The Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission was equivalent in rank to a **Cabinet Minister** of the Government of India. {21,22}
- Deputy Chairmen: Dr. Manmohan Singh (1985-1987), Pranab Mukherjee (1991-1996), Montek Singh Ahluwalia (2004-2014); **P. Chidambaram** was never Deputy Chairman. {23}
- Main function: assess the country's resources and prepare five-year plans for the Central Government for balanced use of those resources; also give suggestions on the annual plans of the States.
- Almost 70 percent of Central grants to the States were given on the Commission's recommendation.
- It was called the **'Economic Cabinet'** of the Union and the States, because of its extra-constitutional discretionary powers and the comprehensive nature of its terms of reference; it also formulates five-year plans for balanced use of natural resources, but that is not the reason for the name. {24}
- **M.V. Mathur** suggested merging the Planning Commission and the Finance Commission; he was a member of the 3rd Finance Commission, formed in 1960. {25}
## NITI Aayog
- Prime Minister **Narendra Modi** announced from the Red Fort on 15 August 2014 the abolition of the Planning Commission. {1}
- **NITI Aayog** replaced the Planning Commission on **1 January 2015**. {3}
- NITI = National Institution for Transforming India.
- NITI Aayog was formed by a resolution of the Union Cabinet (an executive resolution of the Government of India). {2}
- NITI Aayog is a non-constitutional (extra-constitutional) and non-statutory body. {9,10}
- NITI Aayog is a policy think-tank of the Government of India; it gives critical directional and strategic input into the development process.
- NITI Aayog works on the principle of **co-operative federalism**.
- The Prime Minister is the ex-officio Chairperson of NITI Aayog; so it has no full-time Chairman, and the Chairperson changes with the Prime Minister's term. {19}
- The current Chairperson of NITI Aayog is Shri Narendra Modi. {20}
- The Vice-Chairperson of NITI Aayog is appointed by the **Prime Minister**; his rank and status equal a Cabinet Minister. {4}
- **Dr. Arvind Panagariya** was the first Vice-Chairman of NITI Aayog, from January 2015 to August 2017. {5}
- The current Vice-Chairman is Dr. Suman K. Bery, a former World Bank economist.
- The Chief Executive Officer of NITI Aayog is B.V.R. Subrahmanyam.
- Governing Council of NITI Aayog: Prime Minister (Chairperson), Chief Ministers of all States, Chief Ministers of UTs with legislatures (Delhi, Puducherry) and Lieutenant Governors of other UTs; Governors of States are not included. {6}
## National Development Council (NDC)
- The NDC was set up on **6 August 1952** by an executive order of the Government. {26}
- It was set up to ensure the participation of States in the formation of five-year plans.
- The National Development Council, the Planning Commission and the Zonal Councils are not mentioned in the Constitution. {15}
- NDC (1952) and Planning Commission (1950) were set up by executive order; Zonal Councils are statutory bodies under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.
- The NDC is a non-constitutional and **non-statutory** body; it should meet at least twice a year. {35}
- The NDC, not the Planning Commission, is the apex body for decision-making and deliberation on development matters in India. {27}
- The **Prime Minister** is the Chairman of the NDC and presides over its meetings. {34}
- NDC members: Prime Minister (Chairman), Ministers of the Union Cabinet, Chief Ministers of all States, Chief Ministers / Administrators of Union Territories, members of the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog); the Finance Commission Chairman is not a member. {30}
- The Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) is also part of the NDC.
- The **Secretary of the Planning Commission** (now NITI Aayog) is the ex-officio Secretary of the NDC. {32}
- The NDC finally **approves the five-year plans**. {31}
- The NDC acts as a coordinator between the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) and the State Governments. {33}
- NDC functions: prescribe guidelines for formulating the National Plan (including assessment of resources); consider the Plan made by the Planning Commission; review the working of the Plan and recommend measures to achieve its aims and targets. {28}
- The NDC also considers important questions of social and economic policy affecting national development.
- Suggesting allocation of money for the Plan was the Planning Commission's work, not the NDC's.
- Main functions of the NDC: activate the means and efforts of States for implementing the plan, develop general policies in all important areas, and ensure balanced development of all areas of the country. {29}
