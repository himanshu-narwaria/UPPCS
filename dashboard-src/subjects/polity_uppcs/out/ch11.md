@ 11 | States and the Union Territories
## Integration of princely states and the 1950 classification
- Indian Independence Act of 1947 created two independent and separate Dominions: India and Pakistan.
- Princely States had three options: join India, join Pakistan, or remain independent.
- More than 560 princely States lay within Indian territory; most joined India; three refused: Hyderabad, Junagadh and Kashmir.
- Hyderabad was included through military action, Junagadh through a referendum.
- Kashmir was included through the Instrument of Accession (a legal document) executed by Maharaja Hari Singh, ruler of Jammu & Kashmir.
- The Constitution in force from 1950 recognised four categories of States and territories in the First Schedule.
| Category | Made up of | Governed by |
| Part A | Former British India provinces | Governor and a Legislature |
| Part B | Former Princely States | 'Raj pramukh' |
| Part C | Princely States and chief commissioner's provinces | Chief commissioner |
| Part D | Andaman and Nicobar Islands only | President, through a Chief Commissioner or other authority appointed by him |
- Part C states and Part D territories became the basis for the creation of Union Territories.
## Union and its territory: Article 1
- **Article 1(1)**: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States"; the Constitution names the country India and Bharat. {2}
- India is a **Union of States**, not a Federation of States. {3}
- States and their territories are as specified in the **First Schedule**; it lists the names of States and Union Territories.
- Territory of India comprises: (1) States, (2) Union Territories, (3) such other territories as may be acquired.
- India, a Union of States, is a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic with a Parliamentary system of Government.
- The President is the constitutional head of the executive of the Union.
- At present India has **28 States and 8 Union Territories**. {1,23,24,25}
- In 2002 there were 28 States and 7 Union Territories.
- Telangana became the 29th State on 2 June, 2014.
- With the formation of the UTs of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, the count became 28 States and 9 UTs.
- With the merger of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, the count became 28 States and 8 UTs.
- States have no right to secede from the Union; as per Dr. Ambedkar, that is why the term 'Union of States' is used. {18,19}
- States in India have no right to territorial integrity; Parliament can by unilateral action change the area, boundaries or name of any state by simple majority.
- **Article 19(1)** guarantees six rights to all citizens: speech and expression; assemble peaceably without arms; form associations, unions or co-operative societies; move freely throughout India; reside and settle in any part of India; practise any profession, occupation, trade or business.
- The State can impose 'reasonable' restrictions on these six rights only on grounds mentioned in Article 19(2) itself; even mere advocacy of secession is subject to reasonable restriction.
## Articles 2, 3 and 4: new states and alteration
- **Article 2**: Parliament may by law admit into the Union or establish new States; admission of new States is not under Article 3. {7,8,17}
- **Article 3**: Parliament may form a new State by separating territory from any State, by uniting two or more States or parts of States, or by uniting any territory to a part of any State. {4,5,40}
- Article 3 also lets Parliament increase the area, diminish the area, alter the boundaries and alter the name of any State. {6}
- Altering the name of a State is a power of Parliament, not of a State Legislature. {15}
- No State has the authority to include any territory by itself; "a State may include a Union territory" is not a provision. {16}
- Explanation 1 of Article 3: in clauses (a) to (e) "State" includes a Union Territory, but in the proviso "State" does not include a Union Territory.
- Parliament forms new States or alters them by a **simple majority** and the ordinary legislative process; no special procedure is needed. {10,11}
- **Article 4(2)**: such a law is not deemed an amendment of the Constitution for the purpose of Article 368; changes under Articles 2 and 3 are outside Article 368. {9}
- Such a law contains provisions for amending the First Schedule and the Fourth Schedule.
- Parliament can alter the area, boundaries or name of a State without the State's consent. {12,13}
- A Bill for these changes can be introduced in Parliament only with the prior recommendation of the President.
- Before recommending, the President must refer the Bill to the concerned State Legislature for its views within a specified period; the President (or Parliament) is not bound by those views. {14}
- If the State Legislature gives no views within the period fixed by the President, the Bill can still be presented in Parliament.
- **Article 14**: equality before law for all persons, citizens and non-citizens.
- **Article 16**: equality of opportunity in public employment for Indian citizens only.
## Reorganisation of states on linguistic basis
- In 1920 the Congress session was held at Nagpur under C. Vijayaraghavachariar; after it, provincial Congress committees were formed on a linguistic basis. {67}
- **S.K. Dhar Commission**, 1948: examined feasibility of linguistic states; rejected it and recommended reorganisation on administrative convenience (viability), not language.
- **JVP Committee**: formed by Congress in December 1948 to scrutinize the Dhar Commission's recommendations; members Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel and Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
- JVP Committee report, presented in April 1949, officially rejected reorganisation of States on a linguistic basis.
- Death of Potti Sriramulu after a 56-day hunger strike forced the Government to form Andhra Pradesh on a linguistic basis.
- **Andhra Pradesh** = first State formed on a linguistic basis, on 1 October, 1953, by separating Telugu-speaking areas from Madras state. {61,62,63}
- **States Reorganisation Commission**: chairman Justice Fazal Ali; members K.M. Panikkar and H.N. (Hridaynath) Kunzru; K.T. Shah and Pattabhi Sitaramayya were not members. {56,57,58}
- The Commission was set up on 29 December, 1953 and submitted its report on 30 September, 1955.
! Question asking when the Commission for linguistic reorganisation was established (options 1856, 1956, 1957, 1960): no option is correct, since it was established on 29 December, 1953.
- The Commission broadly accepted language as the basis of reorganisation but rejected the theory of 'one language-one state'.
- The Commission suggested abolishing the four-fold classification of states and territories.
- The Commission did not support the splitting of Bombay and Punjab. {64}
- The Government accepted the recommendations with some changes and enacted the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 and the 7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956.
- The Act abolished the distinctions among Part A, Part B and Part C states.
- **14 States and 6 Union Territories** were created on 1 November, 1956. {20,21,22}
- Large-scale reorganisation of States on a linguistic basis took place in **1956**. {59,60,65}
## Union Territories
- **Delhi** is a Union Territory under the First Schedule; officially also called the National Capital Region. {27}
- **69th Amendment Act, 1991** gave Delhi special status among the Union Territories. {28}
- Goa is a State, not a Union Territory; Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Daman and Diu, and Puducherry are Union Territories. {26}
| Union Territory | Year of creation |
| Lakshadweep | 1956 {29} |
| Dadra and Nagar Haveli | 1961 |
| Daman and Diu | 1962 |
| Chandigarh | 1966 |
- Since 26 January, 2020, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu have been integrated into one Union Territory.
- Dadra and Nagar Haveli were under Portuguese (not French) rule until 1954, when supporters of the Indian Union occupied them. {32}
- Diu is an island in the Gulf of Khambhat.
- Daman and Diu were separated from Goa by the 56th Amendment; Goa attained full statehood in 1987.
- **Article 240**: President may make regulations for the peace, progress and good government of certain Union Territories: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and Puducherry. {31}
| Article 240 clause | Union Territory |
| 240(1)(a) | Andaman and Nicobar Islands |
| 240(1)(b) | Lakshadweep |
| 240(1)(c) | Dadra and Nagar Haveli (not Puducherry) {30} |

| 240(1)(e) | Puducherry |
- At present 'Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu' is a single UT under Article 240(1)(c), through the Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu (Merger of Union Territories) Act, 2019; Article 240(1)(d) has been repealed.
## Sikkim, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh
- In 1974 the Sikkim Assembly passed the Government of Sikkim Act, 1974: paved the way for the first responsible Government in Sikkim and sought its representation in India's political institutions.
- **35th Amendment Act, 1974** inserted new Article 2A making Sikkim an Associate State; it set the terms and conditions of Sikkim's association with the Union. {33}
! Question "Sikkim became a state under": the 36th Amendment is not among the options, so the 35th Amendment is taken as the answer.
- **36th Amendment Act, 1975** made Sikkim a full-fledged State, the 22nd State, and included it in the First Schedule; Sikkim became an integral part of India in 1975. {34,35,36,37}
- Sikkim became a State on 16 May, 1975 (State Foundation Day), after a referendum; the 36th Amendment came into force on 26 April, 1975.
- 36th Amendment Act: Legislative Assembly of Sikkim shall have not less than 32 members; the Assembly formed by the April 1974 elections with 32 members is deemed the Legislative Assembly of the State.
- Sikkim is called the Paradise of Botanists because of its biodiversity; it falls under the Himalayan hotspot.
- Natives of Sikkim are Lepchas, Bhutias and Nepalis.
- Memorandum of Settlement on Mizoram was signed by the Government of India and the Government of Mizoram on 30 June, 1986.
- Mizoram got statehood by the **53rd Amendment Act**; the State was formed on 20 February, 1987. {38}
- Capital of Arunachal Pradesh, Itanagar, is named after Ita Fort, a historic fort whose name means "fort of bricks".
- By the latest official data, Arunachal Pradesh has only two national parks: Namdapha National Park and Mouling National Park.
- Nagaland came into existence by an ordinary Act of Parliament, the State of Nagaland Act, 1962, not by a Constitution Amendment Act. {39}
- Tripura was a Part 'C' State after its initial merger, and became a Union Territory after the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.
- Tripura got full statehood from 21 January, 1972 under the North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act, 1971.
