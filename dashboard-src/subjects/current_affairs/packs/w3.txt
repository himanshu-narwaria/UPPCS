[u0330] Ch3 · India-UK CETA and DCC
  context: India – Europe · PDF p. 58
  The India–United Kingdom **Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA)** and **Double Contribution Convention (DCC)** became operational on **July 15, 2026.** They aim to expand trade and investment, facilitate temporary professional mobility and eliminate duplicate social-security contributions for eligible workers.
  - The Enhanced Trade Partnership and Roadmap 2030, launched in May 2021, laid their foundation.
  - After 14 negotiation rounds, CETA was concluded on 6 May 2025 and signed in London on 24 July 2025. DCC was signed on 10 February 2026.
  - CETA is a next-generation free trade agreement with 30 chapters covering goods, services, investment, digital trade, intellectual property, government procurement, competition, innovation, sustainability and MSMEs.
  - The UK provides zero-duty access to nearly 99 per cent of Indian tariff lines, covering almost the entire value of Indian exports to the UK.
  - Major beneficiary sectors include textiles, leather, footwear, gems, jewellery, marine products, engineering goods, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, processed food and automobile components.
  - India excludes dairy products, cereals, millets, apples, edible oils and oilseeds from major tariff concessions.
  - This protects small producers, domestic prices and rural livelihoods while balancing liberalisation with policy space for vulnerable sectors.
  - UK commitments cover 12 service sectors and 137 sub-sectors, including IT, finance, engineering, healthcare, education and telecommunications.
  - CETA facilitates temporary movement of business visitors, intra-corporate transferees, contractual service suppliers, independent professionals and investors.
  - It also creates opportunities for Indian chefs, yoga instructors and classical musicians.
  - DCC prevents dual social-security contributions for eligible temporarily posted or detached workers.
  - Ordinary overseas employees generally contribute in their country of employment.
  - It concerns contribution liability and does not automatically grant host-country pensions or other contributory benefits.

  On **October 19, 2026**, the **European Commission**, on behalf of the **European Union (EU)**, signed an **Association Agreement with Andorra and San Marino in Brussels, Belgium**. It aims to integrate these European microstates into the **EU Single Market** without granting EU membership.
  **Key Highlights**
  - **Nature and Scope:** The **Mixed Agreement** involves the EU, its member states, Andorra and San Marino. It provides for the free movement of **goods, services, capital and persons**, subject to agreed conditions.
  - **Financial Services:** Market access will be phased in after regulatory assessments by the **European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs)**. Cooperation also covers research, education, environment and consumer protection.
  - **Negotiation Timeline:** Talks began in **2015** under December 2014 Council directives. Negotiations with **Monaco were suspended in September 2023**, while those with Andorra and San Marino concluded in **December 2023**.
  - **Approval Process:** The European Commission submitted proposals on **26 April 2024**. The **Council of the EU authorised signing and provisional application on 16 July 2026**.
  - **Legal Status:** Under **Article 112(3)**, provisional application may begin separately with either country after prescribed procedures.
