[u0403] Ch4 · Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 2026
  context: A. Fiscal & Monetary Affairs · PDF p. 89, 90
  The Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 2026 came into force on October 1, 2026. The Act replaces the Bankers’ Books Evidence Act, 1891 with a technology-neutral framework recognising physical, electronic and digital banking records as evidence. The definition of bankers’ books includes records at off site, virtual, cloud, backup and disaster recovery locations. Banks and bankers also include post office savings bank and money order offices.
  **Legislative Background**
  - The Lok Sabha passed the Bill on 5 August 2026 and the Rajya Sabha on 10 August. Presidential assent followed on 13 August 2026.
  **Major Provisions**
  - The first schedule applies to copies of books maintained in written or physical form. The second schedule applies to copies of books maintained electronically, digitally or through other data storage mechanism.
  - **Certification of Banking Records –** Certificates must confirm accuracy and be authenticated manually, digitally or electronically by an authorised bank officer.
  - **Conditions for Digital Records –** Digital records are admissible if created and stored in the ordinary course of banking through regularly used, properly functioning systems.
  - **Evidentiary Value –** Certified copies are prima facie, not conclusive, evidence where original entries are legally admissible.
  - **Extension to Financial-Sector Entities –** The Central Government may extend coverage by notification. NBFCs, payment aggregators and fintech firms are covered only if notified or under another law.
  - **Legal Proceedings and Production of Records –** Proceedings include court cases, arbitration, investigations and inquiries.

  - The **Economic Survey 2025–26** urges a shift to **entrepreneurial policymaking under uncertainty**, where the State acts early, structures risk, learns through experimentation, and adapts without paralysis.
  - The Survey says governance is moving from **compliance-driven regulation** to **capability-driven state capacity**, signalling an emerging **entrepreneurial State**.
  - Despite post-COVID shocks, including global trade disruption and **US tariffs in April 2025**, the **Indian economy** has shown strong **macroeconomic resilience**.
  - The Survey anticipates **real GDP growth above** **7% in FY 2025–26** and expects growth to stay near this level in the following year.
  - It highlights a paradox in which India’s best macro performance in decades comes amid a global system that no longer reliably rewards discipline with currency stability, capital inflows, or insulation.
  - The Survey argues that India’s scale population of 145 crore, and democracy make it unrealistic to copy external development templates in a geopolitically uncertain world.
  - It states that global headwinds can become tailwinds if the **State, private sector, and households** align and sustain a national effort.
  - The Survey outlines **three global scenarios for 2026**: managed disorder, disorderly multipolar breakdown, and a low-probability, high-impact shock cascade worse that of **2008**.
  - Across scenarios, it says India is relatively better placed due to a large domestic market, a less financialised growth model, strong reserves, and credible strategic autonomy, though insulation is not complete.
  - It warns of a common risk of disrupted capital flows and sustained **rupee pressure** that could last beyond one year.
  - The Survey says India must strengthen **foreign currency earnings** through exports and investor confidence to finance a rising import bill with rising incomes.
  - It recommends supply stability, strategic buffers, diversified trade routes, and alternative payment systems, calling for strategic sobriety over defensive pessimism.
  - It stresses that India must balance long-term growth with short-term shock absorption, captured in its “**marathon and sprint**” framing.
  - The Survey emphasises **process reforms** alongside policy reforms because administrative processes shape citizen-state interaction and reform outcomes.
  - It notes that recent **deregulation and smart regulation** by States show the State can shift from controller to enabler of growth.
  - It links **State capacity, society, and deregulation** as key pillars for **Viksit Bharat** and greater global influence.
  - It urges the State to upskill and mentally prepare for a harsher global environment where rules are changing.
  - It concludes that long-term success requires resilience, innovation, and delayed gratification rather than quick fixes.
  - The Survey has been reorganised into **17 chapters** based on depth and time-relevance, making it broader and longer than earlier editions.

  **Key Highlights of Economic Survey 2025–26**

  - **Private Final Consumption Expenditure** rises by **7.0%** in **FY26** and reaches **61.5% of GDP**, the highest share since **2012** (also seen in **FY23**), supported by **low inflation**, **stable employment**, and rising real purchasing power.

[u0634] Ch7 · Revenue Surplus, Fiscal Deficit, and Primary Deficit
  context: Uttar Pradesh Budget 2026–27 · PDF p. 165
  - The budget estimates a **revenue surplus** of **₹64,458 crore**, which is **1.6% of GSDP**, in **2026–27**. This means the state expects its revenue receipts to exceed its revenue expenditure.
  - However, this revenue surplus is lower than the revised estimate for **2025–26**, when revenue surplus was estimated at **₹74,348 crore**, or **2.4% of GSDP**. This suggests that while the state remains in surplus on the revenue account, the cushion is becoming smaller.
  - The **fiscal deficit** for **2026–27** is budgeted at **₹1,18,481 crore**, which is **3% of GSDP**. This is the same ratio as the revised estimate for 2025–26 and aligns with the **16th Finance Commission’s recommended annual limit of 3% of GSDP for states for 2026–31**.
  - The **primary deficit** is estimated at **₹49,560 crore**, or **1.2% of GSDP**, in **2026–27**. This is significantly higher than the revised estimate for 2025–26, indicating a larger gap even before interest payments are considered.

  - According to the research report **‘Ecowrap’ by State Bank of India**, India is moving towards becoming the **third-largest economy in the world by 2027**, surpassing Japan and Germany, and Uttar Pradesh is expected to play a **decisive role** in this achievement.
  - Uttar Pradesh’s GSDP at current prices increased from ₹**13.30 lakh crore in 2016–17** to ₹**30.25 lakh crore in 2024–25 (quick estimates)**, registering a **compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 10.8% over 8 years**.
  - In **2024–25**, the state contributes about **9.1% to India’s GDP**.

[u0645] Ch7 · Agriculture and Allied Sector
  context: Uttar Pradesh Economic Survey 2025–26 · PDF p. 167
  - The agriculture and allied sector has shown continuous progress.
  - Its Gross Value Added increased from ₹**2.96 lakh crore in 2016–17** to ₹**6.95 lakh crore in 2024–25**, growing at a **CAGR of 11.2%**, which is nearly a **doubling of value**.
  - Advance estimates placed it at ₹**6.86 lakh crore**, while revised estimates placed it at ₹**7.12 lakh crore**.
  - Crops remain the **main component**, contributing the highest share of **67.4%** in the Gross Value Added (GVA) of the agriculture and allied sector.
  - Livestock contributes **24.7%**, forestry contributes **4.5%**, and fisheries and aquaculture contribute **3.5%**.
  - In **2024–25**, the total foodgrain production in the state increased by **9.9% over the previous year**, reaching **737.4 lakh metric tonnes**, and the state continues to hold a leading position in the country.
  - Within this, oilseed production increased by **57.9%**, total cereals increase by **11%**, including a **23.6% increase in paddy** and a **6.3% increase in wheat production**.
  - Milk production increased by **33.6%**, placing the state at the **first position in the country**.
  - Egg production increased by **167%**.
  - In **2025–26**, fish production is estimated to increase further by **15.7%**, reflecting efforts towards **sustainable agriculture and diversification**.

[u0646] Ch7 · Forestry and Land Use
  context: Uttar Pradesh Economic Survey 2025–26 · PDF p. 167
  - It increased from **23,437.53 sq. km in 2021** to **23,996.72 sq. km in 2023**, registering an increase of **559.19 sq. km (2.39%)**.
  - Uttar Pradesh ranks **second in the country** in terms of increase in forest and tree cover.

[u0647] Ch7 · Secondary Sector (Industry and Infrastructure)
  context: Uttar Pradesh Economic Survey 2025–26 · PDF p. 167
  - The Gross Value Added (GVA) of the secondary sector increased from ₹**3.34 lakh crore in 2016–17** to ₹**7.60 lakh crore in 2024–25**, growing at a **CAGR of 10.8%**.
  - Within the secondary sector:
    · The **construction sector** leads with a **47.4% share**, reflecting rapid expansion of infrastructure and urban development.
    · The **manufacturing sector** contributes **42.9%**, indicating strong industrial capacity and investment.
    · The **electricity sector** contributes **9.6%**, ensuring energy availability and continuity of industrial activities.
  - According to the **Annual Survey of Industries 2023–24**, the Gross Value Added of industries in the state increased by **25% over the previous year**, which is the **highest among major states** and more than double the **national growth rate of 11.9%**.

[u0648] Ch7 · Infrastructure Development
  context: Uttar Pradesh Economic Survey 2025–26 · PDF p. 167

    · 12 proposed
  - At present, the state has **12 domestic and 4 international airports** (Lucknow, Varanasi, Kushinagar, and Ayodhya).
  - With the start of operation at Noida International Airport at Jewar (Gautam Buddha Nagar), the state will become the first in India with 5 international airports.
  - The total road network includes 12,123 km of National Highways, and 90 National Highways pass through the state.
