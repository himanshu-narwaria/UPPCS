  - It received Presidential assent on **15 June 2005** and became fully operational on **12 October 2005**.

[u0258] Ch2 · 100 Years of the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)
  context: Political / National Affairs · PDF p. 20, 21
  The **Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) completed 100 years on 1 October 2026**. Its institutional journey began on **1 October 1926**, when India’s first Public Service Commission was established.
  - At that time, it was an advisory body whose Chairman and members were appointed by the Secretary of State for India in London.
  - Various programmes and activities were organised throughout the year to mark the UPSC’s centenary. The year-long celebrations began on **1 October 2025** and continued until **1 October 2026**.
  **Key Facts**
  - The UPSC organised a two-day centenary event titled **‘Manthan’** at **Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, from 30 September to 1 October 2026**.
  **The Journey**
  - The **Indian Civil Service (ICS) examination** began in London in 1855, replacing the East India Company’s nomination system with open competition.
  - In **1862**, **21-year-old Satyendranath Tagore**, the elder brother of Rabindranath Tagore, passed the examination in **1863** and joined the service in **1864**.
  - He thus became the **first Indian to join the ICS**. The British referred to it as the **‘heaven-born service’**.
  - The **Government of India Act, 1919**, enacted following the **Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms**, provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission.
  - In **1922**, the ICS examination was held in India for the first time, alongside London. Its Indian examination centre was **Allahabad, now Prayagraj**.
  - In **1924**, the **Lee Commission**, chaired by **Arthur Hamilton Lee**, recommended the immediate establishment of a statutory Public Service Commission.
  - The Commission was established under the **Public Service Commission (Functions) Rules, 1926**, framed under **Sections 96B and 96C(2) of the Government of India Act, 1919**.
  - **Sir Ross Barker**, a member of the United Kingdom’s Home Civil Service, was its first Chairman.
  - The Commission’s office was located at **Metcalfe House in Delhi**.
  - The Indian examination centre was shifted to **Delhi in 1928**.
  **Major Milestones**
  | **Year** | **Major milestone** |
  | 1937 | On **1 April**, the Commission was renamed the **Federal Public Service Commission**. |
  | 1938 | Combined competitive examinations were introduced for several services, marking the beginning of integrated recruitment to multiple services. |
  | 1947 | **Shri H. K. Kripalani** became the first Indian Chairman following independence; the first examination was held in December and the IAS replaced the ICS. |
  | 1950 | On **26 January**, the Federal Public Service Commission was renamed the **Union Public Service Commission**. **Shri R.N. Banerjee** assumed charge on January 14, 1949, and continued as Chairman following the Institution’s Constitutional transition on January 26, 1950. |
  | 1951 | The **All India Services Act** was enacted. |
  | 1979 | The three-stage Civil Services Examination— **Preliminary Examination, Main Examination and Interview**—was introduced. |
  | 2011 | The **Civil Services Aptitude Test (CSAT)** was introduced. A **Single Window System** was launched on **1 September**. |

[u0285] Ch2 · Protection for Women in Live-in Relationships
  context: Political / National Affairs · PDF p. 32
  In **Dr. Lokesh B.H. & Others v. State of Karnataka & Another**, decided on **3 August 2026**, the Supreme Court held that protection against domestic cruelty under **Section 498A of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)** is not confined to women in legally valid marriages.
  - The protection may extend to a woman in a live-in relationship when the relationship is **“in the nature of marriage”** and the parties have the **intention to marry**.
  **What Is Section 498A of the IPC?**
  - Section 498A of the IPC criminalised cruelty committed against a woman by her husband or a relative of her husband.
  - The provision prescribed imprisonment for up to **three years** and a fine.
  - Following the replacement of the IPC by the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**, the corresponding offence is contained in **Section 85 of the BNS**.
  **Supreme Court’s Ruling**
  - The Supreme Court held that Section 498A is not restricted exclusively to relationships based on a legally valid marriage.
  - It can apply to a live-in relationship that legally qualifies as a **relationship in the nature of marriage**.
  - However, the woman must also establish that the relationship involved a genuine **intention to marry**.
  - The relationship must be between consenting adults and must satisfy the legal characteristics of a marriage-like domestic partnership.
  **Essential Conditions for Invoking Section 498A**
  - A woman in a live-in relationship must initially establish two essential requirements:
    · The live-in arrangement must constitute a **relationship in the nature of marriage**.
    · The relationship must involve an **intention to marry**.
  - The initial burden rests on the woman seeking protection under Section 498A.
  - In **Indra Sarma v. V.K.V. Sarma (2013)**, the Supreme Court recognised that a live-in relationship between consenting adults is not an offence.
