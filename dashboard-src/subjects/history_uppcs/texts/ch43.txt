CHAPTER 43: Regional States : Punjab and Mysore

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Ranjit Singh was born to the chief of Sukerchakia Misl on 13th November, 1780 A.D. Treaty of Amritsar was signed between Maharaja Ranjit Singh and British East India Company on 25th April, 1809 A.D. Ranjit Singh captured Multan in 1818 A.D., Kashmir in 1819 and Peshawar and North-West frontier (Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa) in 1834 A.D..
* In 1798 AD, Zaman Shah attacked Punjab. While returning, his cannons accidentally fell into the Jhelam River (some sources refer for Chenab River but Jhelam River is authenticated by many historians). These cannons were sent back to Jaman Shah by Ranjit Singh. Happy with this gesture of Ranjit Singh, Jaman Shah allowed Ranjit Singh to rule Lahore. As a result, in 1799 A.D., Ranjit Singh took control of Lahore and made it his Capital. Zaman Shah gave him the title of Raja (in return of his service and providing tanks and artileries) and appointed him as the Subedar of Lahore. He also captured Amritsar from Bhangi Misl in 1802. Now, both the political capital ‘Lahore’ and religious capital ‘Amritsar’ were under the control of Ranjit Singh.
* Ranjit Singh was an able ruler. He once said, “God wants me to see all the religions as one, so he took the light ofmy other eye”.
* Shahshuja, descendant ofAhmed Shah Abdali gifted Kohinoor diamond to Ranjit Singh.
* His son Kharak Singh succeeded Ranjit Singh after the laters death in 1839. He was an opium addict. During his reign two opposite groups, one consisting of Dogra Brothers - Dhyan Singh, Ghulab Singh and Suchet Singh other included Sandhawaliya Sardar (an aid of Chait Singh) Atar Singh, Lahna Singh and his nephew Ajeet Singh, and Dogras (Dhyan Singh, Gulab Singh and Suchet Singh) were the main reason of growing chaos in Punjab.
* Battle of Chillianwala was fought on 13th January 1849 A.D. between English forces led by Lord Gough and Sikh forces led by Sher Singh. The war ended undecided. Lord Dalhousie was the Governor-General of India during this war.
* Maharaja Duleep Singh was the last ruler of Sikh Empire. He ruled from 1843 to 1849 AD. He converted to Christianity and travelled to Russia. Later in 1886 A.D. he again accepted Sikhism. He died in Paris in 1893 AD. His funeral took place in England. In 1849 AD, after the Anglo-Sikh war, Punjab was annexed by Britishers and Duleep Singh was sent to Britain on a pension in 1854 A.D. After the annexation ofPunjab, in 1849 A.D. Lord Dalhousie made a three-member body consisting of Sir Henry Lawrence as Board’s President and, John Lawrence and Charles Grenville Mansel as members to administer Punjab.
* The First Anglo-Mysore war in 1767-69 A.D. made Haidar Ali victorious. The war ended in April, 1769 AD with the Treaty of Madras. During this the Governor ofBengal was Harry Verelst (1734-85).
* The Second Anglo Mysore War (1780-84 AD) - During this war, Haidar Ali made an alliance with Nizam and Marathas against Britishers. In July 1780 A.D. Haidar attacked Carnatic and on 10 September 1780 AD, colonel Baillie was defeated by the combined forces of Haidar Ali and Tipu in the battle ofPollilur (other name Perambakam). In 1781 A.D. Haidar Ali faced Sir Eyre Coote who defeated Haidar Ali at Porto Novo (July, 1781 A.D.), Pollilur (In this war, Haider Ali was out rightly defeated but his army faced heavy loss during this) and Sholingur.
* Tipu Sultan made Srirangapattnam his Capital where he established Jacobin Club and became its member. He planted the tree of liberty in his capital as a sign of friendship between Mysore and France.
* Tipu sent ambassadors to contemporary foreign states and also established their embassies to maintain friendly relations and to gather help from them against the Britishers. These foreign states included - Iran, Istanbul, Afghanistan, France.
* The Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790-92 A.D.) ended with the Treaty of Srirangapattna (March 1792 A.D.). The treaty fo Seringapatam (Srirangapatna) was signed in March 1792 A.D. between Tipu Sultan ofMysore and Lord Cornwallis on behalf of British East India Company. The agreement also included representatives of the Nizam of Hyderabad and Maratha Empire, requiring Tipu to cede halfofhis Kingdom. Tipu Sultan died fighting in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799 A.D.). All the members of his family were imprisoned in Vellore. British Commanders like Arthur Wellesley (brother ofGovernor General of Bengal Lord Wellesley), Stuart and General Harris led this war from the English side. In the happiness of this victory, the Lord Society of Ireland conferred the title of Marquess to Wellesley. After the war, a minor Krishnaraja Wodeyar III (Mummadi Krishnaraja Wodeyar) from Wodeyar dynasty was placed on the Mysore throne and the British took possession of Kanara, Coimbatore, and Srirangapattnam.
* Begum Samru (1750-1836 A.D.) constructed a very famous church at Sardhana near Meerut (Some sources mention the birth year of Begum Samru is 1753 A.D.). Walter Reinhardt Sombre, a European, was her husband who served many kingdoms. Sombre was granted Jagir of Sardhana as a gift for his service to the Mughal king Shah Alam II in defeating Zabita Khan (induced by NajafKhan). Begum Samru became ruler of Sardhana after the death of her husband.

14. Regarding Punjab Merger, which of the following statement is false?

(a) On 29th March, 1849, the Governor General announced that the state of Punjab had come to an end.
(b) All the territories of Maharaja Duleep SIngh became part of the Indian British Empire.
(c) Duleep Singh was given an annual pension of Rs. 2,50,000.
(d) None of the above
U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2023
Ans. (c)
By Lord Dalhousie's proclamation of March 29, 1849 A.D., the whole of Punjab was merged into the British Empire. All the territories of Maharaja Duleep Singh became part of the Indian British Empire and he was exiled to England in 1854 for study. For his relatives and also the maintenance of states’ employees, his pension was decided not less than four lakh rupees and not more than five lakh rupees (minimum £40,000 and maximum £50,000) per annum.
