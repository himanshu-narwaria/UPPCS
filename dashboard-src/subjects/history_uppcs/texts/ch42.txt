CHAPTER 42: East India Company and Nawabs of Bengal

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Murshid Quli Khan was the last Governor ofBengal who was independently appointed by Mughal King.
* Murshid Quli Khan shifted his Capital to Maksudabad from Dhaka and named it Murshidabad. He started Izara System in the land settlement.
* Alivardi Khan (1740-1756 A.D.) comparing Europeans with the Honeybees, said that ifthey are left undisturbed, they will give you honey, and if try to disturb them they will bite you till death.
* After the death ofAlivardi Khan, his grandson Siraj-ud-Daula succeeded to the throne of Bengal. The Black Hole tragedy took place during his reign on 20th June 1756 A.D. According to one of the 23 survivors, J.Z. Holwell, 146 English prisoners were put in a cell with a dimension of18 feet in length and 14 feet 10 inches in breadth. 123 of them died due to suffocation and only 23 survived. This incident is known as Black Hole tragedy. Contemporary Muslim historian Ghulam Hussain in his book ‘Siyar-ul Mutakherin’ has not mentioned this incident.
* Robert Clive is known as the founder of the English Empire in India who defeated the forces of Bengal’s Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula in the Battle ofPlassey (presently known as Palasi) on 23rd June, 1757 A.D. and sown the seeds ofBritish rule in India. The Battlefield ofPlassey is situated on the Banks ofBhagirathi River in Nadia district ofWest Bengal. Robert Clive was the Governor in 1758- 60 A.D. and 1765-67 A.D. in India. During this period he signed Allahabad Treaty with Nawab ofAwadh Shuja-ud-Daula and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II During his governorship White Mutiny broke out among English soldiers.
* Plundering of resources ofBengal by the British Company was started during Clive’s governorship.
* Mir Qasim (1760-63 A.D.) was the ablest among the successors of Alivardi Khan. As a ‘Faujdar’ of Poornia and Rangpur, Mir Qasim had already proven his ability. To save his courtiers from repeated conspiracies by Britishers and to avoid interference from Englishmen, Mir Qasim shifted his Capital to Munger from Murshidabad so as to stay away from Calcutta. He consolidated his army using European methods. He made arrangements in Munger for the manufacturing of Matchlock guns. Mir Qasim also took some brave steps so as to improve the economic conditions in Bengal. The officers involved in embezzlement were fined heavily, some new taxes were introduced
* The Combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula (Nawab of Awadh), and Mughal King Shah Alam II were defeated by Britishers in the Battle of Buxar on 22/23 October, 1764 AD. English army was led by Hector Munro. Henry Vansittart was the then Governor of Bengal. This victory of Britishers made the English a great power in northern India and contenders for supremacy over the whole country. The area up to Allahabad was now under the British and the door ofDelhi was now open for British. Battle of Buxar changed the course ofIndian History. During this battle, Mir Jafar was Nawab ofBengal and Delhi was ruled by Shah Alam II. By the treaties of Allahabad (August, 1765) Shah Alam II agreed to reside at Allahabad, under the company’s protection. Nawab Shuja-ud-Daula surrendered Allahabad and Kora to Emperor Shah Alam II.
* Shah Alam II issued a decree granting Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the East India Company.
* Robert Clive became the governor of Bengal and started Dual government system in Bengal.
* The Company appointed two deputy Diwans, Mohammad Raza Khan for Bengal and Raja Shitab Roy for Bihar to exercise Diwani functions. Rohilla Sardar Ghulam Qadir annexed Delhi in July 1788, and August 1788, he ousted Shah Alam II and made him blind. During the time of Shah Alam II in 1803, Delhi fell completely to East India Company. Shah Alam II and his two successors Akbar II (1806-37 AD) and Bahadur Shah II (1837-57 AD) were mere pensioners of the East India Company.
* The British came to Sylhet (then Sylhet was part of Bengal Presidency) in 1765 AD after acquiring the Diwani right ofBengal. After the War ofBurma, David Scott tried to connect Assam (Brahmaputra Valley) with Sylhet through roads to Jaintia and Garo Hill areas, but faced a very tough resistance from the Khasi tribes who lived in these areas under the leadership of Tirot Sing.
* K.M. Panikar said that from 1757 to 1773 AD the Company made Bengal a ‘State of Robbers’.

British supremacy was established in India from the Battle of Plassey. British defeated Nawab Sirajuddaula in the battle of Plassey on 23 June, 1757 AD under the command of Robert Clive. A part of Nawab’s army was under the command of three traitors-Mir Jafar, Yar Latif Khan and Rai Durlabh. Siraj fled from the battlefield to Murshidabad due to adverse circumstances of war. It finally resulted in his defeat. This victory provided instant military and commercial benefits to Britishers. Battle of Plassey set the ground for British political dominion over three leading states of agriculture and Handcrafting: Bihar, Bengal, and Orissa. Battle ofPlassey founded the British empire in India, however, it was the Battle of Buxar (1764 AD) that actually established British superiority in India.

11. The most decisive battle that led to the establishment of supremacy of the British in India was:

(a) The Battle of Buxar
(b) The Battle of Plassey
(c) The Battle of Wandiwash
(d) The Third Battle of Panipat
U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 2003; U.P. U.D.A./ L.D.A. (Pre) 2002; U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 1990
Ans. (a)
On 22/23 October, 1764 A.D. British Army defeated allied forces of Mir Qasim, Nawab of Oudh Shuja-ud-Daula and Mughal emperor Shah Alam II. British Army’s command was in the hands of Major Hector Munro in the Battle of Buxar. The battle of Buxar assured the result of Plassey battle and established British supremacy on Banaras and Allahabad (Prayagraj) across the Ganges. The battle ofBuxar made way for British and proved that there was no strong competitor before the British. The battle ofBuxar completed what Plassey had begun. During the battle of Buxar Nawab of Bengal was Mir Jafar. According to Sir James Stephen, ‘’The battle of Buxar deserves more credit than the battle of Plassey as the origin of British power in India”. If the results of both significant battles are analyzed, it can be said that the battle of Plassey was won by treachery, but the battle ofBuxar showed the supremacy of English armies and military skills. Plassey made the English the only controllers of Bengal while Buxar raised the political prestige ofthe Company throughout India.

21. Who was appointed Deputy Diwan of Murshidabad by Robert Clive after the Allahabad Treaty?

(a) Mohd. Raza Khan
(b) Shitab Rai
(c) Rai Durlabh
(d) Syed Ghulam Hussain
45th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2001
Ans. (a)
According to the Treaty of Allahabad (August, 1765 A.D.), Mughal Emperor ShahAlam II was taken under the Company’s protection and had to reside at Allahabad. Shah Alam II conferred Diwani (fiscal revenue collection authority) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the Company by issuing ‘Farman’ on 12 August, 1765 A.D. Nazm-ud-duala (son of Mir Jafar) was accepted as Nawab of Bengal. In return, the company had to pay the emperor an annual sum of Rs 26 Lakhs as pension. At that time, the Company was neither interested in taking responsibility for tax revenue collection nor was it capable of doing so. Therefore, the Company appointed two Diwans- Mohammad Raza Khan for Bengal and Raja Shitab Rai for Bihar. Thus, all business of Diwani and Nizamat was conducted by Indians but authorized by the British Company.

24. Which one of the following is the correct chronological order ofthe battles fought in India in the 18th Century?

(a) Battle of Wandiwash - Battle of Buxar - Battle of Ambur- Battle of Plassey
(b) Battle of Ambur - Battle of Plassey - Battle of Wandiwash - Battle of Buxar
(c) Battle of Wandiwash - Battle of Plassey - Battle of Ambur-Battle of Buxar.
(d) Battle of Ambur - Battle of Buxar - Battle of Wandiwash-Battle of Plassey.
I.A.S. (Pre) 2005
Ans. (b)
Battle of Ambur (August, 1749 A.D.):- The combined armies of Muzaffar Jung, Chanda Sahib and the French defeated and Killed Anwar-ud-din at the Battle ofAmbur near Bellore in August, 1749. Muzaffar Jung became the Subedar ofDeccan. Duplex was appointed Governor ofall the Mughal territories south ofthe river Krishna. The Nizam surrendered some districts in the Northern Circars to the French. A French army under Bussy was stationed in Hyderabad.
Battle of Plassey (June, 1757 A.D.):- Fought between Nawab ofBengal Sirajuddaula and the British Army. Bengal was in control of the British after Plassey. Mir Jafar was placed on the throne by Robert Clive. In exchange, Mir Jafar ceded an area of the south of Calcutta known as 24 Pargana to the British for their services and gifted 2,34,000 pounds to Clive as personal gift reward.
Battle of Wandiwash (January, 1760 A.D.) :- It occurred between French and British. French were defeated by the British. Sir Eyre Coote was the leader of the British Army while French troops were led by Count de Lally.
Battle of Buxar (October, 1764 A.D.) :- Fought between allied forces of Mir Qasim, Shujauddaula, Shah Alam II and the British. British led by Hector Munro defeated the allied forces badly. Thus, option (b) will be the correct answer.
