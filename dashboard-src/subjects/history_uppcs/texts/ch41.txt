CHAPTER 41: The Advent of European Companies

EXPLANATION SECTION
* According to one theory, Vasco da Gama reached India with the help of an Arab navigator Ahmad Ibn Majid, but according to historical & authentic evidence, the person who helped Vasco da Gama reach Indian port of Calicut was a Gujarati sailor. Vasco da Gama sailed to the Western Coast ofIndia, at the port of Calicut on May 20, 1498. He had successfully discovered a new and alternative sea route to India from Europe. Zamorin (Zamorin was the ancestral title of the rulers of Calicut), then the ruler of Calicut, welcomed Vasco da Gama. The arrival of Vasco da Gama in India started a new era of trade between Portuguese and Indians. Vasco da Gama returned with the cargo having 60 times more profit than the expedition cost, in the business of Black Pepper which was enough to lure other merchants to India.
* In 1505 A.D., Francisco de Almeida arrived in India as first Portuguese Viceroy. In the light of Portuguese trade interests and to establish Portuguese authority in the Indian Ocean, Almeida rendered the Blue Water Policy.
* Afonso de Albuquerque came as the Governor of the Portuguese region in India 1509 A.D. after Almeida. In 1510 A.D., he snatched Goa from Adil-Shahi Sultanate, the rulers of Bijapur, and established his authority there. He is known as the real founder of the Portuguese power in India.
* Albuquerque in 1511 A.D. captured Malacca, an important South-East Asian Market and, in 1515, captured Hormuz situated at the mouth of Persian Gulf.
* Nuno da Cunha assumed the Governor office after Lopo Vaz de Sampaio in 1529 A.D. and remained the Governor until the end of 1538 A.D.
* A new sea route to India was discovered by Portuguese traveller Vasco da Gama whereas Columbus, a traveller originally from Italy and financed by Spain discovered America, and Tasman of Holland discovered Van Diemen's Land (currently Tasmania) and New Zealand. The first European to reach Australia was English Navigator Willem Janszoon (1712-1780), while the British Navigator Captain Cook arrived at Australia in 1770 A.D. (about 164 years later, when Willem Janszoon reached to Australia). Captain Cook is credited with surveying Australia’s east coast and laying foundation ofBritish rule there.
* In 1503, at Cochin, first Portuguese Fort (first European Fort in India) was constructed by Afonso de Albuquerque (during his first visit to India in 1503 A.D.). The fort was named as ‘Emmanuel Fort’.
* The sequence ofArrival of European Companies to India is:
* Portuguese, Dutch, English, Danish and French.
* Portuguese were the first to arrive in 1498 A.D. and were last to leave India in 1961 (de Jure transfer took place in August 1962).
* In 1961 the Portuguese Governor-General ofGoa was Manuel Antonio Vassalo e Silva (1958-61 A.D.).
* Jose Ferreira Bossa was the Governor- General of Goa when India gained Independence in 1947.
* Among all European powers, Portuguese were the first to establish Sea trading centers in India.
* Portuguese in 1500 A.D. established their first factory at Calicut.
* In 1535 A.D. Ghiyasuddin Mahmud Shah, the ruler of Bengal, promised the Portuguese to establish the forts in Chittagong and Satgaon, in return for their assistance of the ruler during the Sher Shah Suri’s invasion of Bengal.
* Hooghly was used as a base by Portuguese for plundering activities in the Bay of Bengal.
* In 1632 A.D., Mughal king Shahjahan, ordered Bengal Subedar Qasim Khan to destroy the establishments of Portuguese in Hooghly and imprison thousands ofPortuguese residents.
* Job Charnock, head ofthe factory ofQasim Bazar, selected Sutanuti instead at Hooghly for English Trading Center. This is how he established Calcutta as a Base of Englishmen.
* In 1602 A.D., Dutch (Holland) States General Chartered the establishment of a United East India Company. It had a Capital of6,500,000 Guilders. This company had a monopoly to do trade and to attack and win territories in India and eastern countries. Its eastern centre was situated in Batavia (currently Jakarta). Eastern Empire of Dutch was operated and administered by this company. Its partners worked as a private group. Dutch constructed William Fort at Modern Kochi in 1663 A.D. after defeating Portuguese. Kochi in 1814 AD was included in British settlements under the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814.
* In 1600 A.D., a trading company English East India Company or 'The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies' was founded in England.
* Akbar was the King of India during establishment.
* The Levant Company (1592) was granted a monopoly to trade with Ottoman Empire (The Levant) and its eastern areas (including India). They traded in Silk, spices etc. After the ‘Royal Charter’ of Queen Elizabeth I, the East India company was provided with exclusive 15-year monopoly on English trade with all countries east of Cape of Good Hope, which included India.
* During the Reign of Jahangir, in 1611 A.D., East India Company established its first temporary factory at Masulipatnam. In 1613 A.D., English established their first permanent factory at Surat.
* Dutch established their factory at Surat in 1616 A.D. followed by French in 1668 A.D.
* Thomas Best was the English officer who defeated Portuguese at a place named Swally.
* In 1662 A.D., King of Britain Charles II married Portuguese Princess Catherine and received Mumbai as a gift which was leased to East India Company on payment of 10 Pound annually (under the Marriage Treaty of 1961).
* A British official John Child, in 1688 A.D., surrounded Mughal ports in Bombay and other Mughal ports on the western coast and tried to imprison Hajj travellers going to Mecca as a result of which Aurangzeb expelled him from India.
* First Carnatic War (1746-48 A.D.) was just an extension of the War of Succession in Austria which started in 1740.
* In 1746 English and French started war without the consent of their home nations in India. English Navy under the leadership of Commodore Bernett seized French Ships, this was the immediate reason for war. During the first Carnatic War, the Nawab of Carnatic, Anwaruddin, sent an Army of 10,000 men under the leadership of Mahfooz Khan to attack French. Under the leadership of Captain Paradise, the French defeated the Force of Nawab at a place called St. Thome on the bank ofAdyar River. With the end of the war in Europe, the war in India also ended with the Treaty of Aix - la - Chapelle (1748 A.D.). Austrian Succession war ended and Madras was again granted to English.
* Dupleix for the first time used tactics that guided the future campaigns of English men. It was Dupleix who appointed European forces into Indian Courts on Indian expense and interfered in the internal conflicts of kingdoms in the light ofEuropean interests and sown the seeds ofEuropean rule in India.
* Colbert, a minister of Louis XIV, founded French East India Company in 1664 A.D. which is also known as Compagnie Francaise Pour le Commerce des Indes Orientales (or Compagnie francaise des Indes orientales) in 1667 A.D. Francois Caron headed an expedition to India setting up a factory in Surat in 1668 A.D. Marcara, a Persian who accompanied Caron founded another French factory in Masulipatnam in 1669 A.D. after obtaining permission from the Sultan ofGolconda.
* Dutch established their first factory at Masulipatnam. In Bengal, they established their first factory at Pipali. In 1676 A.D. Dutch left the factory at Pipali and established new factory at Balasore. The trade with Bengal started properly when Dutch in 1653 A.D. established their establishment in Chinsurah. In Chinsurah, the Dutch fort was called Gustavus Fort.
* Europeans obtained high-grade opium and Saltpetre from Bihar. Bihar was famous for opium production during British rule.
The chronology of arrivals of European powers in India is as follows – Portuguese, Dutch, English, Danes and French respectively.

57. What was the chief economic foundation of the triangular commerce between India, China and England?

(a) Silk
(b) Opium
(c) Black pepper
(d) More than one of the above
70th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2024
Ans. (b)
The triangular trade between India, China, and England in the 18th and 19th centuries was centered on opium. The British East India Company established their monopoly in the cultivation of opium in the region of Bengal and Bihar etc., they forced the farmers to cultivate opium and exported it to China, where it was in high demand despite government bans. Profits from the opium trade were used to purchase Chinese goods like tea, silk, and porcelain for England, while manufactured goods from England were exported to India, completing the trade cycle. This exploitative system had severe social and economic consequences, including the Opium Wars between Britain and China. While commodities like silk and black pepper were also traded, opium was the chief economic foundation of this triangular commerce.
