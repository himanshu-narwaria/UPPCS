CHAPTER 61: Lucknow Session of Congress (The Lucknow Pact)

EXPLANATION SECTION
* In December 1916 AD, both Indian National Congress and Muslim League held their sessions at Lucknow.
* Both parties passed separate resolutions on demands for constitutional reforms and agreed on a pact to work together in the political field on a common programme. This is known as Lucknow Pact or Congress-League Scheme.
* Ambika Charan Majumdar was president ofCongress in this session. As a result, they together released 19 bulletins to give a positive direction to the contemporary political scenario.
* Lucknow session was very significant due to two reasons, first, the Extremists who were separated from Congress nine years ago (in 1907 A.D.) were again merged to Congress and, secondly there was a pact between Congress and League. Annie Besant and Tilak played a significant role in the return of Extremists to Congress. On the other hand, the efforts of Jinnah and Tilak were responsible for Congress-League Pact. The period from 1916 A.D. to 1922 A.D. was the time of consensus between Congress and League (although the rupture in their relation stated in 1920, as Jinnah resigned from Congress in 1920 due to his disagreements with Mahatma Gandhi's approach). Lucknow Pact wasjust a temporary arrangement. Muslim League, besides this pact had its own identity and it advocated for the interests of Muslims where ever possible. Both League and Congress were together until 1922 A.D.

QUESTIONS
1. In December, 1916 both Indian National Congress and Indian Muslim League held their session at:

(a) Aligarh
(b) Allahabad
(c) Lucknow
(d) Lahore
U.P P.C.S. (Spl) (Pre) 2004
Ans. (c)
An important step towards achieving Hindu-Muslim unity was the Lucknow Pact, 1916 A.D. Anti-British feelings were generated among the Muslims following a war between Britain and Turkey, which opened the way for Congress and Muslim League unity. Both the Congress and the Muslim League held sessions at Lucknow in 1916 A.D. This meeting settled the details of an agreement on the composition of the legislatures and the quantum ofrepresentation to be allowed to the two communities. The agreement was confirmed by the annual sessions of the Congress, and the League held at Lucknow on 29 and 31 December, 1916 A.D. respectively. The Congress accepted the separate electorates and both organizations jointly demanded dominion status for the country. Hindu-Muslim unity weakened the British attitude. In 1916 A.D., the British Government announced a policy whereby the involvement of Indians in the Government was to be increased, and there was to be a gradual development of local self-Government institutions. Sarojini Naidu described Jinnah, the chiefarchitect ofthe Lucknow Pact, with the title of the ‘Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity.’
