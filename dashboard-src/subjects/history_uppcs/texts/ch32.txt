CHAPTER 32: Shah Jahan

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Shahzada Khurram was born in 1592 A.D. in Lahore. His mother was Jagat Gosain, the daughter of the ruler of Marwar, Mota Raja Uday Singh. Shahzada Khurram married to Arjumand Banu Begum, the daughter ofAsaf Khan in 1612 A.D. She was famous in history as Mumtaz Mahal.
* Shah Jahan adopting the title of ‘Abul Muzaffer Shihabuddin Muhammad Sahib Kiran-i-Sani’ sat on the throne. His coronation took place in Agra. He shifted the capital from Agra to Shahjahanabad (Delhi).
* Ahmadnagar was included in the Mughal Empire in 1600 AD. Hussain Nizam Shah III, the ruler ofAhmadnagar was imprisoned in the fort of Gwalior. Nizam Shahi Sardar Shahaji Bhonsle continued the struggle with the Mughals in the name of one child (Murtaza Nizamshah III). Finally, in 1636 AD, Shahaji got surrounded by the Mughals in the Chunar Fort. Shahaji handed over several forts and Murtaza Nizamshah III to the Mughals. Murtaza Nizamshah III was imprisoned in the fort of Gwalior and Shahaji accepted the service of Bijapur State.
* Aurangzeb was the governor ofDeccan first time during 1636 - 44 A.D. in the reign of Shah Jahan and he was the governor of Deccan second time during 1653-1658 A.D. He remained at this designation till the victory in the war of succession and becoming the Mughal emperor.
* Qandahar was the cause of dispute between Shah of Iran and the Mughal rulers because of strategic and economic grounds and a prestigious issue for Persians and Mughals. The loss ofQandahar was strategically a big blow to the Mughal Empire. The loss ofQandahar exposed the Mughal Kingdom to outside attacks. Finally, Qandahar slipped out from the hands of Mughals during the period of Shah Jahan, weakening Mughals on the North-West frontier.
* The motive behind Shah Jahan’s Balkh and Badakhshan campaign was to secure a friendly ruler in Balkh and Badakshan which shares its border with Kabul. So, it became a buffer state between the Mughals and the Persians.
* Shah Jahan's period was considered to be the Golden age of the Mughal period because there had been a substantial development in the field of art, literature and education. In this period, two styles in the Persian language were prevalent, first was Indo-Persian (dominance of India style) and second was Iranian-Persian (native persian). Abul Fazl (Akbar's courtier) was the best promoter ofthe Indo- Persian style. The scholars of this style were Abdul Hamid Lahori, Muhammad Waris and Chandrabhan Brahmin, etc. Iranian-Persian-style scholars were Ameeni Qazwini and Jalaluddin Tabatabai. Iranian poetry style was quite dominating during that time. Shah Jahan appointed the Persian Poetry style poet Kaleem as court poet. In addition to Kaleem, Persian poets Qudsi, Mir Muhammad Yahya (Kashi), Salim, Masih, Rafi, Farrukh, Munir, Shoda. Chandrabhan Brahmin, Hajiq, Dileri, etc. were among others.
* Kavindracharya was a dependent poet ofShah Jahan and had the perfect combination of Awadhi and Braj in his language.
* 'Kavindra Kalplata' was written in praise of Shah Jahan. He requested the emperor for the abolition of pilgrimage tax on pilgrims visiting Benaras (Kashi) and Allahabad and got it abolished.
* Upanishads were translated into Persian language during the reign of Shah Jahan by his son Dara Shikoh titled as Sirr-i-Akbar. Around 52 Upanishad were translated in this book. Lane pool compares Dara's religions/political posture with Akbar's path. Shah Jahan gave him the title of “Shah Buland Iqbal”. Majma-ul-Bahrain is a masterpiece of Dara Shukoh.
* Shah Jahan collected designers, engineers, architects, from India, Persia and Central Asia for the construction ofTaj Mahal which is the best example of Mughal architecture, blending India, Persian and Islamic styles.
* Jama Masjid of Delhi was built by Shah Jahan. Buildings built by Shahjahan are Diwan-e-Aam, Diwan-e-Khas, Sheesh Mahal, Moti Masjid (Agra), Khas Mahal, Musamman Burj, Nagina Masjid, Jama Masjid (Delhi), Taj Mahal and Red Fort.
* Like Akbar’s Fatehpur Sikri’, Shah Jahan established a city named Shahjahanabad in his name in Delhi, and tried to equip it by building many beautiful and magnificent buildings. Among the buildings ofShahjahanabad, Red Fort is the main building. The name ofthe western entrance ofthis fort is Lahori Darwaza and the southern gate is Delhi Darwaza. It is unique in beauty and splendour.
* Among the four sons of Shah Jahan, the eldest, Dara Shikoh was the most educated scholar and writer. He studied many Hindu texts and translated the Hindu texts like Upanishads, Yoga Vashisht, Bhagwat Gita etc into Persian. The Mughal emperor Shah Jahan abolished the Iranian Court tradition ‘Sijda’ started by Balban. Elphinstone has written about the period ofShah Jahan that the period ofShah Jahan was the most prosperous period in Indian history. Dr. A.L. Srivastava wrote in his book "Mughalkaleen Bharat", that the regime of Shah Jahan was the golden period in the medieval history of India.

25. The famous diamond ‘Kohinoor’ was presented to Shah Jahan by –

(a) Aurangzeb
(b) Murad
(c) Mir Jumla
(d) Abul Hasan Qutb Shah
U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2015
Ans. (c)
The Kohinoor Diamond, one of the famous diamonds in the world, was discovered in the mines of Golconda. Mir Jumla was a minister of Sultan of Golconda, Abdullah Qutub Shah (1626-1672 A.D.) who got angry over Mir Jumla for accepting the Mansab of King Shah Jahan and seized all his property. Later, Shah Jahan sent Aurangzeb to attack Golconda, and the matter was resolved, and the treaty was signed between them. Later, Mir Jumla was called back to Agra and given the title of “Muazzam Khan.” Mir Jumla, the Persian noble and a reputed diamond dealer, visited the court of Shah Jahan and presented the Kohinoor diamond to the Mughal emperor.
