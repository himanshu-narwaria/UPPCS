CHAPTER 80: Indian Princely States

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Butler Committe kings selected Sir Leslie Scott as representative ofIndian princes (he was retained as counsel by standing) before the committee to represent their point ofview.
* In December 1927A.D., All India States People’s Conference was organized in which more than 1500 political workers from various states participated. Balwant Rai Mehta, Manilal Kothari and G.R. Abhayankar played an important role in its organization.
* Jawahar Lal Nehru was chosen the President of All India States People Conference in 1939 A.D.
* Most of the Princely States were acceded to the Indian Dominion by 1947 (barring few) while territorial mergers/integration continued later. By 15th August, 1947 A.D., except Junagarh, J&K and Hyderabad all princely states joined the Indian Union and the remaining three too joined by the end of 1948 A.D.
* British Government by the order set up - two independent Dominions; and suzerainty over Indian States lapsed (princely states had to decide their accession). which complicated the scenario ofa merger ofstates to form one Union. This problem was handled wisely by Sardar Vallabh Bhai Patel and political integration of India was largely achieved (but territorial integration continued later).
* On 26th October, 1947 A.D., Maharaja ofKashmir, Hari Singh signed the ‘instrument of accession’. On March 5, 1948 Sheikh Abdullah was appointed Prime Minister.
* Standstill agreement was signed between Government of India (Lord Mountbatten Governor-General and Viceroy of India) and Nizam of Hyderabad, Mir Osman Ali Khan on 29th November, 1947 A.D.

QUESTIONS
1. The purpose of the Butler Committee, 1927:

(a) Modernization of Indian Army
(b) Innovative modernization of Indian Agriculture.
(c) Implementation ofcensorship over National Newspapers.
(d) Improvement of relationships between Indian Provinces and the Crown.
46th B.P.C.S. (Pre) 2004
Ans. (d)
The Indian States Committee appointed a committee under the Chairmanship ofSir Harcourt Butler, which was popularly known as ‘The Butler Committee’ to investigate and clarify the relationship between paramount power and the princes of Princely States in 1927 AD.
The recommendations of committee were -
The relationship of the paramount power of the state was not merely a contractual relationship but a living, growing relationship shaped by the circumstances and policy. British paramount preserved the princely State.
The state should not be transferred without their agreement to a relationship with a new government in British India responsible to an Indian legislature.

4. Most of the integration of princely states in the Union of India had taken place in which year?

(a) 1948
(b) 1947
(c) 1950
(d) 1961
U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 1990
Ans. (b)
Integration of most of the princely states happened in 1947 A.D. Most of the Princely States acceded in the Dominion ofIndia in 1947 AD except Junagarh, Jammu & Kashmir and Hyderabad and these three States were compelled to join the Union of India till the end of 1948 A.D. Sardar Patel, who took charge of the state department in 1947 A.D., tackled the situation with great statesmanship. Patel appealed to the patriotic and nationalist sentiments of the Princes and requested the States to be handed over to the authority of External Affairs, Defence and Communications to the Indian Dominion. The integration of Princely states took proper shape in his leadership.

10. During the freedom struggle, a parallel movement launched in the Indian States (in the States ruled by the Indian rulers such as Kashmir, Nizam's Hyderabad, Travancore etc.) was

(a) Swaraj Movement
(b) State People's Movement
(c) Praja Mandal Movement
(d) More than one of the above
(e) None of the above
68th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2022
Ans. (c)
In 1920s during freedom struggle various people's movements were launched in the princely states of British Raj. These movements were called State Peoples Conference or Praja Mandals. These movements were spearheaded by people living in princely states who were subject to the rule of local aristocrats rather than the British Raj. They campaigned against those feudatory rulers, and sometimes also the British administrators, in attempts to improve their civil rights.
