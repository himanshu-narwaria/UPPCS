CHAPTER 68: Non-Cooperation Movement

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Congress held a special session in September 1920 A.D. at Calcutta and passed the resolution of Gandhiji on Non Cooperation. The resolution was opposed by several senior leaders like C.R. Das and Madan Mohan Malviya. In December 1920 A.D. this resolution was widely supported and C.R. Das moved the resolution for Non-Cooperation in the Nagpur session.
* Gandhiji launched Non-Cooperation movement on 1st August, 1920 A.D. A special session of the Congress was held under the leadership of Lala Lajpat Rai in Calcutta in September, 1920 AD to decide upon the momentous issue of Non Cooperation. Discussions were held on the Khilafat question, the Government’s failure to protect the innocent people of the Punjab and punish the British officers. The leaders decided that the only way to vindicate the national honour was to establish Swaraj through the policy of Non-Cooperation. The resolution ofnon-cooperation formally adopted in this session. The resolution of the Calcutta Congress was ratified at the regular session held in Nagpur in December, 1920 AD. The movement got widespread success in western India, Bengal and Northern India. During this movement, Motilal Nehru, Lala Lajpat Rai, Sardar Vallabh Bhai Patel, Jawahar Lal Nehru and Rajendra Prasad boycotted court and jumped into the movement. Gandhiji promised Swaraj within one year of the launching ofNon-Cooperation Movement ifeffectively carried out, at All India Congress Committee. The plan was prepared for a total boycott of Schools, Colleges, Government titles, Government institutions, Foreign goods, etc. This Movement introduced the people with modern techniques of politics and made them hungry for freedom. Communal Unity and massive participation ofMuslims was a significant achievements ofthis movement.
* The movement destroyed the fear ofBritish power from the hearts of people.
* At the time when Gandhiji arrived in India, Britain was at World War I. He supported Britain in the war. Gandhiji was given the title of ‘Kaiser-e-Hind’ for his humanitarian work in South Africa, which he returned during the Non-Cooperation Movement.
* Jamnalal Bajaj returned the title of Rai Bahadur.
* Tilak supported the Non-Cooperation movement but his death on the first day of the movement (1st August, 1920 A.D.) made him unable to see the fruits of the movement. Tilak agreed with the policy of non-cooperation with the British (as in June 1920), but Tilak opposed his method of boycott (especially boycott of legislative councils elutions established by the 1919 Montagu- Chelmsford Reforms).
* Chauri Chaura incident took place on 4th February, 1922. At a place called Chauri Chaura in Gorakhpur district in United Provinces, police openly fired at a group of protestors participating in non-cooperation movement, after this, the angered mob burnt down the police station and killed all 23 policemen (3 civilians were also died). This incident is popularly known as the Chauri Chaura incident. After this incident, Gandhiji withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement seeing it becoming violent. On 11th and 12th February, 1922 A.D. Congress Working Committee at the Bardoli meeting suspended Non-Cooperation Movement. After the events at Chauri Chaura near Gorakhpur, the Working Committee ofthe Congress resolves that mass civil disobedience contemplated at Bardoli and elsewhere be suspended and instruct the local Congress Committees forth-with to advise the cultivators to pay the revenue and other dues to the Government and whose payment might have been suspended in anticipation of mass civil disobedience, and instructs them to suspend every other preparatory activity of an offensive nature. The suspension of mass civil disobedience shall be continued till the atmosphere is so nonviolent as to ensure the non-repetition of popular atrocities such as at Gorakhpur or hooliganism such as at Bombay and Madras. On February 24th, 1922 All India Congress Committee organized a meeting in Delhi and a limitation was imposed on all activities that amount to a violation oflaw. In this session, Dr. Moonje introduced a vote ofcensure condemning Gandhiji’s decision to call off the movement.
* Rabindranath Tagore emphasized creative programmes rather than movements and protests by masses. He was against the burning of foreign clothes and wrote a letter to Gandhiji to adopt creative activities instead of mass movements. Rabindranath Tagore called the burning of clothes during the Non-Cooperation as unwise and he said he was unable to obey the same. Kashi Vidyapith ofVaranasi in 1921 A.D., Gujarat Vidyapith at Ahmedabad in 1920 AD and Jamia Millia Islamia at Aligarh in 1920 A.D. which was later shifted to Delhi, were established during the Non-cooperation Movement. Madan Mohan Malviya established Banaras Hindu University in 1916 A.D. and was its Vice-Chancellor till 1939 from 1919 A.D.

* During the years 1919-1922 A.D., two mass movements emerged in India as a means to oppose British rule: the Khilafat movement and the Non-Cooperation Movement. The non-cooperation Movement marked the beginning of the Gandhian Movement against British colonialism.

36. Indicate true sequence of events concerning the postponement of Non-Cooperation Movement: (1) Police firing in Chauri-Chaura (2) Police station set fired by violent crowd (3) Postponement of Movement by Gandhiji (4) Arresting of Gandhiji Select your answer of the following code –

(a) 1, 2, 3 and 4
(b) 2, 1, 3 and 4
(c) 4, 1, 2 and 3
(d) 2, 1, 4 and 3
40th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 1995
Ans. (a)
On 4th February, 1922 A.D., the angry residents of Chauri Chaura village gathered in front of the police station to protest against the arrest of their leader, while campaigning against the sale of liquor. However the police opened fire on the villagers, which angered them to such an extent that they set the police station on fire killing twenty-three policemen and three civilians. Gandhi, worried about the increasing use of violence, immediately withdrew the Movement.
