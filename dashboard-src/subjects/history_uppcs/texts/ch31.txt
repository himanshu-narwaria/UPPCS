CHAPTER 31: Jahangir

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Jahangir was born on 30 August, 1569 A.D. His first marriage was with the daughter of Bhagwandas who was the king of Amber and the sister of king Man Singh, Man Bai in 1584 AD. Khusrau was the son of Man Bai. The second marriage ofSalim was with Jagat Gosain, the daughter ofUday Singh (the Mota Raja). Shahzada Khurram was the son of Jagat Gosain. Jahangir was coronated in the fort of Agra in 1605 A.D. and adopted the title of Nuruddin Muhammad Jahangir Badshah Ghazi. Continuing the tradition of Akbar, Jahangir started his rule generously and when he sat on the throne, he issued various populist orders.
* “Do-aspa and Sih-aspa” system was introduced by Jahangir. Under this system, Mansabdars had to maintain an excess number of soldiers without an elevation of rank (Zat.)
* Do-aspa: Mansabdars had to maintain double the number of horses as required for their “Sawar” rank.
* Sih-aspa: Mansabdars had to maintain three times the number of horses as required for their “Sawar’ rank.
* Treaty of Chittor was signed between Mughal Emperor Jahangir (authorised Prince Khurram) and Rana Amar Singh of Mewar in 1615 A.D. Rana accepted the suzerainty of Mughal emperor and Jahangir returned Chittor which was annexed by Mughals during the reign ofAkbar.
* Nur Jahan was a cultured, educated, intelligent dominating lady and ambitious wife of Mughal emperor Jahangir. Nur Jahan’s Junta consist of a group of five- herself, her mother Asmat Begum, her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg or Itmad-ud Daula, her brother Asaf Khan and Prince Khurram.
* Prince Khurram (Shah Jahan) had been allied with Nur Jahan from 1611 to 1622 but when she swung her support to her son-in-law Shaharyar, he left her junta and rebelled.
* The reign ofJahangir, influenced by Nur Jahan, can be divided into two parts 1611-1622 A.D. and 1622-1627 A.D. Khurram was the member of this group in the first period but not the part of this group in the second period.
* William Hawkins was the first English by East India Company to visit the court ofJahangir in 1608-1611 A.D.. Jahangir gave Hawkins the title of ''English Khan'' and also pressed him to marry a lady from Armenia.
* Sir Thomas Roe led the second delegation which visited the court of Jahangir. Sir Thomas Roe arrived in Surat on September, 1615 AD as an ambassador of British King James-I. He attended the court of Jahangir in Ajmer in January 1616 A.D. He got the opportunity to visit Mandu, Ahmedabad and Ajmer with the emperor. He also went for hunting with the emperor. He stayed for one year in Agra.
* Peter Mundy was a English East India Company factor and traveller who came during the reign of Shah Jahan. Francisco Pelsaert was a Dutch merchant who came to India during Jahangir’s period and left a unique account of the Mughal empire in his text “Jahangir's India: The Remonstrantie of Francisco Pelsaert”.
* The Mughal painting reached its zenith under the reign of Jahangir. Earlier, the paintings were concerned with the contents of manuscripts, but Jahangir freed it from this bondage.
* Ustad Mansur and Abul Hasan were excellent painters of Jahangir’s reign. Emperor Jahangir bestowed both Ustad Mansur (Nadir-Ul-Asra) and Abul Hasan (Nadir-Ul-Zaman). Ustad Mansur was specialized in depicting plants and animals while Abul Hasan was a portrait artist.
* Jahangir was an outstanding author as well as a critic. He wrote his autobiography in Persian language and titled it Tuzuk-i Jahangiri.
* Jahangir’s eldest son Khusrau revolted against Jahangir just after the coronation but the revolt was suppressed in 1606 A.D.
* Shahzada Khurram revolted in 1622 A.D. and thereafter suffered defeat at Bilochpur in March 1623 under Mahabat Khan. The conflict ended with submission by 1625 A.D.
* Mahabat Khan revolted against Jahangir in 1626 A.D.
* The Mausoleums of Mughal emperors Babur and Jahangir are located in Kabul and Shahdara (Lahore) respectively. Both of them are located in Afghanistan and Pakistan, respectively.
* The tomb of Itmad-ud-Daula was built by Nur Jahan at Agra between 1622-1628 A.D. after the death of his father. This is the first tomb in India that is entirely made up of white marble. In spite of marble and inlay work, it was for the first time when a new style of ornamentation, Pietra dura, was used.
* Govind Palace (Bir Singh Palace) is a seven-storey building located in Datia, Madhya Pradesh. It was constructed by Raja Bir Singh Deo with bricks and stones.

QUESTIONS
1. Who introduced ‘Do-aspa and Sih-aspa’ system?

(a) Akbar
(b) Jahangir
(c) Shah Jahan
(d) Aurangzeb
46th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2004
Ans. (b)
‘Do-aspa and Sih-aspa’ system was introduced by Jahangir. Under the provision of this system, Mansabdars had to maintain an excess number of soldiers without increment in rank (zat).
Do-aspa: In this system, Mansabdars had to maintain double horses in comparison to their ‘Sawar’ rank.
Sih-aspa: In this system, Mansabdars had to maintain a triple number of horses in comparison to their ‘Sawar’ rank.

30. Itmad-ud-Daula’s tomb at Agra was built by

(a) Akbar
(b) Jahangir
(c) Nur Jahan
(d) Shah Jahan
48th to 52nd B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2008
Ans. (c)
Tomb of Itmad-ud-Daula was built by Nur Jahan at Agra between 1622-1628 A.D. after the death of her father. This elegant monument is located on the left bank of river Yamuna. The main mausoleum is built with white marble. Itmad-ud-Daula has a special place in the chronicles of both history as well as architecture. The monument is priceless because this is the first tomb in India that is entirely made up of white marble. In spite of marble and inlay work, it was for the first time when a new style of ornamentation, Pietradura, was used.
