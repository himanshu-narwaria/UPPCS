* In 19th century the fear of Russian expansion towards India became the basis of Anglo-Afghan relationships. The Treaty of Turkmenchay (1828 AD) created fear among the British due to the increasing influence ofRussians. Experts in England called this ‘The Crisis in India.”
* North Western passes were the key to entering India. Then it was thought that Afghanistan should be under a friendly government or a government which is in congruence with Britishers. The British "Forward Policy" in 19th century Afghanistan was a proactive, aggressive strategy designed to establish a buffer state against Russian expansion towards India, particularly during the first Anglo-Afghan War (1839- 42). It was advocated by Lord Auckland. This became the basis of Anglo-Afghan relations.
* In 19th century Odisha was hit by a severe famine in 1866-67 AD. An estimated one million deaths took place in Odisha. This famine was described as ‘Sea of Calamity’. Odisha famine emerged decisively in the history of Indian famines because after this a Famine Commission under George Campbell was constituted to look into Famines.

First Anglo-Maratha War (1775-82 AD) - The internal problems of the Marathas and the growing ambition of the English brought the beginning of the Anglo-Maratha struggle. The primary cause of the first Maratha war was the interference of the English Government at Bombay in the internal affairs ofthe Marathas. The First Anglo-Maratha War ended with the Treaty ofSalbai signed between the Mahadaji Scindia and the Warren Hastings in 1782 AD. The Burmese were also forced to pay an indemnity of one million pounds, and sign a commercial treaty ‘Treaty of Yandabo’.
Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848-49 AD)-The Second Anglo Sikh War took place in 1848 and 1849 between the Sikh Empire and the British East India Company.
