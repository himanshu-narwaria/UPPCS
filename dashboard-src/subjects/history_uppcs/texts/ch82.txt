CHAPTER 82: Demand for Pakistan

EXPLANATION SECTION
* The idea ofa separate homeland for Muslims (an autonomous muslim majority territory) was first proposed by Iqbal in Allahabad Session (1930) ofMuslim League in his presidential address. Whereas the word ‘PAKSTAN’ was first used by a student named Chaudhary Rahmat Ali in 1933 in his pamphlet “Now or Never; Are we to Live or Perish for ever?” (which later became Pakistan).
* This name was derived from the five Northern Units of British India. These units were - Punjab, North-West Frontier Province (Afghan Province) Kashmir, Sindh and Balochistan. From 22 to 24 March 1940 A.D., the Muslim League held its session in Lahore which was chaired by Md. Ali Jinnah. In this session a separate Muslim Independent States was demanded (North-Western and Eastern Zones of India). In this session, Jinnah said that he would not accept anything less than a separate Muslim Country.
* Sarojini Naidu termed Jinnah as an ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity. Jawahar Lal Nehru in his book The Discovery ofIndia, mentioned that Iqbal in a meeting said that "Jinnah is a politician, you are a patriot".
* Two Nation theory ofJinnah was accepted in the Lahore Session ofMuslim League which was held in March 1940. This proposal was drafted by Sikandar Hayat Khan and was presented by Fazlul Haq on 23rd March 1940. To commemorate this day 23rd March of 1941 was celebrated as (first) Pakistan Day.

QUESTIONS
1. Who among the following had primarily used the word ‘Pakistan’ for separate State for Indian Muslims?

(a) Sir Mohammad Iqbal
(b) Sir Aga Khan
(c) M. A. Jinnah
(d) Chaudhary Rehmat Ali and his friends
R.A.S./R.T.S. (Pre) 1996
Ans. (d)
The word PAKSTAN (later Pakistan) was primarily used by the student of Cambridge University Chaudhary Rahmat Ali in his letter entitled “Now or Never; Are we to Live or Perish for Ever?”

2. Who among the following first coined the word 'Pakistan'?

(a) Mohammad Ali Jinnah
(b) Choudhary Rehmat Ali
(c) Syed Ahmad Khan
(d) Mohammad Ashfaq Khan
M.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2023
Ans. (b)
The term "Pakistan" or Pakstan (later became Pakistan) was first coined by Chaudhary Rahmat Ali, a Pakistani nationalist in a pamphlet published in 1933 A.D. The word was formed by combining the names ofthe regions that were to constitute the proposed nation: Punjab, Afghan Province (North-West Frontier Province), Kashmir, Sindh and Balochistan. The idea ofPakistan gained further momentum, leading to the creation of the independent state of Pakistan in 1947.

3. The idea of a separate Nation for Muslims was given by:

(a) Mohammad Ali Jinnah
(b) Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
(c) Sir Mohammad Iqbal
(d) Liaqat Ali Khan
Uttarakhand P.C.S. (Mains) 2006; U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2003
Ans. (c)
The poet and political philosopher Muhammad Iqbal is considered as the originator ofthe idea ofa separate Muslim nation. Inspired by the spirit of Pan-Islam in All India Muslim League at Allahabad Session in 1930. Muhammad Iqbal said “ I would like to see Punjab, the North-West Frontier Province, Sindh and Balochistan amalgamated into a single state. Self-government within the British Empire, or without the British Empire, formation of a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim state appears to me to be the final destiny of the Muslims, at least of North-West India.

19. Who raised the cry "Divide and Quit"?

(a) M.A. Ansari
(b) M.A. Jinnah
(c) Jawaharlal Nehru
(d) Muhammad Iqbal
71st B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2025
Ans. (b)
The slogan “Divide and Quit” was raised by Muslim League leader Muhammad Ali Jinnah during the Second World War. Jinnah and the Muslim League demanded that the British first divide India into two parts–Hindustan and Pakistan–and then leave India. This idea was expressed as the political slogan “Divide and Quit”.
