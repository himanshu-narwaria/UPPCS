CHAPTER 48: Development of Education in Modern India

EXPLANATION SECTION
* The Calcutta Madarsa was established by Warren Hastings in 1780 A.D. for the study of Muslim law and related subjects. Mulla Majduddin was the first principal (Najin) ofthis Madarsa. Farsi, Arabic and Muslim law were taught in this Madarsa and the graduates from here worked as interpreters in the British Raj.
* The Sanskrit College was established in 1791 by Jonathan Duncan, (the resident at Benaras), for the study of Hindu law, philosophy and literature.
* Raja Rammohan Roy was granted the membership in the Asiatic Society of Paris (Societe Asiatique).
* Halhed, in 1778, published Bengali grammars namely – ‘A grammer of the Bengal Language’.
* Sir William Jones was appointed the Judge of the Supreme Court in Calcutta during the tenure of Warren Hastings. On his initiative, in 1784, Asiatic Society was established and he himself became its first President. This Society, through publication named Asiatick Researches tried to shed light on India’s past. In this order, in 1789, he translated Abhigyan (Abhijnan) Shakuntalam of Kalidas into English.
* To analyse the educational development after 1854 in India, in 1882, W.W. Hunter led Hunter Commission was established. The report of this commission emphasized on reform and development of the primary education in India.
* On 8th November, 1905, Rangpur National School was established. It was the first milestone in the National Education Sector.
* On 16th November 1905, a session was organized in Calcutta and decision was taken to establish National Council of Education, Bengal to provide education related to Science, national literature and technology. Satish Chandra Mukherjee and others constituted the National Council of Education, Bengal on 11 March, 1906; the Bengal National College was inaugurated later on 14 August, 1906.
* By Charter Act of 1813 an arrangement was made to spend not less than one lakh rupees per year on Education.
* Sadler Commission was related to education. Government constituted a commission to study the possibilities of Calcutta University in 1917. Dr. M.E. Sadler who was the Vice-Chancellor ofLeeds University, was appointed the chiefof the commission. Dr. Ashutosh Mukherjee and Dr. Ziauddin Ahmad were two Indian members of the commission. The commission was asked to inquire into the problems of Calcutta University and to make wider recommendations on secondary, intermediate and university education. The commission suggested that if an improvement is desired in the education system of the university, then the standards of secondary education are required to be listed first.
* William Bentinck appointed Macaulay (the law member ofGovernor General Executive Council) as head of Committee of Public Instruction to present a description ofthe dispute regarding the medium (language) of the Indian education system. Macaulay advocated English as the language of Indian education.
* The theory of downward filtration is related to education sector in colonial times. According to this theory, education should be firstly granted to a higher section of society which later on percolates slowly downward in the Society.
* The seed of the modern education system in India was sown by the Lord Macaulay Minute (1835). Lord Macaulay held the view that Indian learning was inferior to European learning, He said. “A single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole literature of India and Arabia”. The proposal of Macaulay was accepted by Lord William Bentinck on 7th March, 1835. During the tenure of William Bentinck, on 7th March, 1835, English was officially accepted as the medium of Indian Education.
* Considered as the “Magna Carta of English Education in India,” Charles Wood’s Despatch (1854 A.D.) was the first comprehensive plan for the spread of education In India. It proposed three Universities at Calcutta, Madras and Bombay to be established on the patterns of London University. By the efforts of D. K. Karve first Women’s university was established at Pune (1916). ‘Karve’ was a Social worker and reformer in Maharashtra. In 1896 A.D., he established 'Home for Widows' (Now-Maharshi Karve Stree Shikshan Samstha) in Poona. He himself married a Brahmani widow. In 1958 A.D., he was awarded the Bharat Ratna Award.
* New English school was started by V.K. Chiplumkar, Bal Gangadhar Tilak & G.G. Agarkar in 1880 A.D. in Pune. Later Deccan Education Society was established and Vishnushastri Chiplunkar, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Mahadeo Ballal Namjoshi, Gopal Ganesh Agarkar and Vaman Shivram Apte were the main founders of the Society.
* Raja Ram Mohan Roy was among the earliest propagators of Modern Education in India. He along with David Hare established famous “Hindu College” in 1817 A.D.
* Mayo College was established in Ajmer in 1875 A.D. Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College was established in Aligarh in 1875 A.D. Delhi College was established in 1824 A.D.
* Madan Mohan Malaviya advocated religious teachings in the Indian Universities.
* In 1916, he established Banaras Hindu University and was the vice-chancellor of BHU from 1919 to 1939 A.D.. He published several magazines and Newspapers in English and Hindi such as Hindustan, India Union, Abhyudaya, Maryada, etc.

24. The foundation of the modern educational system in India was laid by:

(a) The Charter Act of 1813
(b) Macaulay Minutes of 1835
(c) The Hunter Commission of 1882
(d) Woods Despatch of 1854
I.A.S. (Pre) 1993
Ans. (b)
The foundation of the modern educational system in India was laid by Macaulay’s Minutes of 1835 A.D. (Issued on 2 February, 1835) In that minute, he commented on Sanskrit or Arabic by saying that “a single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole literature of India and Arabia.”. Macaulay thus suggested making English the language of higher education in India with Western literature as the subject of study. Bentinck accepted his suggestion. Accordingly, on 7th March, 1835 A.D., a resolution passed by the Government declared: “His Lordship is ofthe opinion that the great object of the British Government ought to be the promotion of European literature and science amongst the natives of India and that all the funds appropriated for the purpose of education would be best employed on English education alone.” Bentinck’s Resolution opened a new chapter in the history of Indian education. It was at that time that Western education began to spread rapidly in India.

27. Which of the following led to the introduction of English Education in India?

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

I.A.S. (Pre) 2018
Ans. (d)
In 1823 A.D. the governor-general in council appointed a General Committee of Public Instructions, which had the responsibility to grant one lakh rupees for education. The committee consisted of 10 members, with Lord Macaulay as the President. Interestingly, during that time, there was a rapid change in attitude towards the composition of English education mainly due to the missionaries and the political influence of the English language. Thus, for the council of East India Company, the decision to grant money faced a greater problem. The controversy went on for 12 years. Even the General Committee of Public Instruction was also not able to decide the medium of instruction by vote, because out of 10 members, five were supporters of English language or Anglicists as the medium of instruction and the rest were supporters ofthe Classic language or Orientalists as a medium of instruction. This is the famous Anglicist and Orientalist controversy.
As the President of the General Committee of Public Instruction, Lord Macaulay wrote a minute (detailed) where he made the conclusion regarding the controversy. By introducing the English language for the education ofmasses, Macaulay’s opinion was that the public mind ofIndia might expand under the English system and through the English language it may educate the people into a capacity for better government.
