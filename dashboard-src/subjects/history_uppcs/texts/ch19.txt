CHAPTER 19: Tughluq Dynasty

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Among the generals of Alauddin, Ghiyasuddin Tughluq or Ghazi Malik became the first ruler ofthe Tughluq dynasty.
* He established the Tughluq dynasty.
* His mother was a Hindu Jat woman and his father was a Qaraunah Turk who was a slave of Balban.
* GhiyasuddinTughluq was the chief of several important campaigns during Alauddin Khalji’s reign and he was appointed as the governor of Dipalpur.
* He earned the epithet 'Ghazi Malik' for repeated frontier fighting against the Mongols; Barani credits him with 20 routs while Ibn Battuta claims 29 victories.
* He took control of Delhi’s throne by defeating Khusrau Shah and in 1320 AD, he became Sultan.
* He was also known as Ghazi Beg Tughluq or Ghazi Tughluq. Because of this, his successors were known as ‘Tughluq’ and his lineage was called Tughluq Dynasty.
* During the time of GhiyasuddinTughluq, tax from farmers

* land tax was collected with regard to capacity to pay. Tax-free jagirs were granted to the Government employees without giving any share in the revenue.
* The harsh punishments of the Alauddin's time were abolished, but those who did not pay taxes, the dishonest ones with the government money and the thieves were severely punished.
* Among all the sultans of Delhi Sultanate, Muhammad-Bin Tughluq (1325-1351 A.D.) was the most learned and educated ruler.
* He was well versed in many disciplines including astronomy, mathematics, medicine etc.
* Muhammad-Bin Tughluq established a new department, Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi, for the advancement of agriculture.
* The main work of this department was to provide more support to the farmers and bring more land under agricultural work.
* A long piece of land measuring about 30 karohs × 30 karohs was chosen for it.
* One of the most important experiments of Muhammad Tughluq was to move the capital to Daulatabad (Devgiri) from Delhi.
* According to Dr. K.A. Nizami, Sultan Qutbuddin Mubarak Khalji named Devgiri as Qutbabad and Muhammad Tughluq named it Daulatabad.
* Muhammad-Bin Tughluq had inscribed a sentence on his coins, ‘Al Sultan Zilli Allah’ (Sultan is the shadow of God), ‘God is the supporter of Sultan’.
* Golden coins issued by Muhammad-Bin Tughluq were called ‘Dinar’ by Ibn Battuta.
* Barani describes five major policies of Muhammad Tughluq-
(1) Tax increase in Doab,
(2) Making Devgiri (Daulatabad) new capital,
(3) Issuing token currency,
(4) Planned expedition to Khurasan and
(5) Qarachil Expedition.
* Ibn Battuta (1304-77 AD) was an African traveller of Moroccan origin.
* He came to India during 1333-42 AD. in the reign of Muhammad-Bin Tughluq (1325-51 A.D.).
* Muhammad-Bin Tughluq appointed him as the Qazi ofDelhi. Later in 1342 AD, he was sent to China as an ambassador of the Sultan.
* Ibn Battuta presented the details of his journey in his book Kitab-ul-Rihla.
* Details of the postal system were known from Ibn Batuta’s travelogue.
* Muhammad-Bin Tughluq is accussed by Isami that he was the first sultan of Delhi who used to participate in the festival ofHindus, mainly on Holi.
* He also appointed non-Turks and Indian Muslims to government posts. Due to this, Barani harshly criticized him for promoting low-born people/low-occupation persons like gardener, weaver, barber, cook, etc.
* Muhammad-Bin Tughluq died on March 20, 1351. On his death, Badayuni wrote, “The king was freed from his people, and the people from their king”
* Firuz Shah Tughluq started some welfare programmes for the benefit of common people.
* After opening an office (employment office) for the appointment of the individual through investigating the qualifications and merit of each individual and giving appointments to as many people as possible - he tried to solve the problem of unemployment.
* Firuz Shah Tughluq used to donate property to the saints and religious people. He had established a department ‘Diwan-i-Khairat’, which provided financial assistance to the poor Muslims, orphaned women and widows, and arranged marriages for poor Muslim girls.
* Firuz Tughluq was the first Indian ruler to arrange Hajj at state expenditure. He also established a Khairati hospital called ‘Dar-ul-Shafa/Shifa’ where skilled Haqim were employed.
* Firuz Shah Tughluq was very fond of the slaves.
* There were approximately one lakh eighty thousand slaves working under Firuz Tughluq.
* A separate department (‘Diwan-i-Bandagan’) was formed for caring of them.
* He established a Public Work Department.
* It is said that Firuz built 200 new cities. Fatehabad, Hisar, Firozpur, Jaunpur, Firozabad etc. were prominent towns established by him. According to the Firista, Firuz had constructed 40 mosques, 30 schools, 20 palaces, 100 Inns, 200 towns, 100 hospitals, 5 Makabara, 100 public bathrooms, 10 pillars and 150 bridges.
* His chiefarchitect was Malik Ghazi Shahana.
* The plan and proposal ofeach building were presented before ‘Diwan-i-Wajarat’ with its expenditure estimate, and only then the required money could be released.
* Firuz Tughluq’s reign became famous for constructing the largest network ofcanals in India.
* To facilitate irrigation, he constructed five major canals.
* He was the first Sultan of Delhi who imposed irrigation tax named ‘Haqq-i-Sharb’ after seeking the opinion from the Ulemas.
* The farmers who used to use the royal canals for irrigation had to give 1/10 portion of their produce to the government.
* Jizya was imposed on Brahmins by Firuz Tughluq.
* It is noteworthy that by that time Brahmins were kept free from this tax.
* Firuz Shah Tughluq planted 1200 new gardens near Delhi due to his interest in horticulture and rehabilitated thirty old orchards of Alauddin period. He also made measures to improve the quality of fruits in his orchards.
* Two Pillars (Stambh) ofAshoka were brought to Delhi from Meerut and Topra (now in Yamuna Nagar district ofHaryana) by Firuz Shah Tughluq.
* The Topara Pillar (Stambh) was re established near the palace and the mosque ofFirozabad.
* The Pillar (Stambh) of Meerut was re-installed near the current Bara Hindu Rao Hospital of Delhi.
* Sultan Firuz Tughluq of Delhi had commissioned Sanskrit scholars at his court to translate some sanskrit works into persian, often associated with translation of 300 sanskrit manuscripts collected from Jawalamukhi temple into persian.
* Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah (1394 - 1412 AD) was the last ruler ofthe Tughluq dynasty. During his reign, Khwaja Jahan established an independent state ofJaunpur.
* Another grandson of Firuz, Nusrat Shah challenged Nasiruddin.
* As a result, Tughluq dynasty was divided into two parts, and both claimants ruled the shrunk kingdom ofDelhi simultaneously.
* Nasiruddin stayed in Delhi and Nusrat Shah in Firozabad. During the reign of Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah, Timur, the Great Turk (Turco-Mongol) General of Central Asia, invaded India (1398 AD).
* This statement was famous with respect to Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah - ‘The sultanate of Shahanshah extends from Delhi to Palam'.
* Timur’s invasion (1398 AD) destroyed the Delhi and started the fall of the Tughluq dynasty.
* With the death ofNasiruddin Mahmud Shah, the Tughluq dynasty ended in 1412 AD. The warlords of Delhi elected Daulat Khan as Sultan of Delhi in 1413 AD.
* But he was defeated by Khizr Khan.
* Khizr Khan was the Subedar of Multan, appointed by Timur and later granted him control over Lahore and Dipalpur.
* In 1414 AD, he took control of Delhi after the invasion of Timur and laid the foundations ofa new lineage Sayyid Dynasty started. He did not assume the title of Sultan.
