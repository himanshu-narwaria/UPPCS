CHAPTER 47: Other Civil Uprisings

EXPLANATION SECTION
* The revolt of 1857 causes other civil uprising because it gave confidence to fight against British. The immediate revolt was Indigo revolt. The Indigo Revolt (1859-60 A.D.) took place in Bengal immediately after the revolt of1857. Sanyasi Rebellion occurred in 1763-1800 A.D., Santhal Rebellion occurred in 1855-56 A.D., and Pabna uprising took place in 1873-76 A.D.
* Indigo revolt was started in Govindpur village of Nadia district in West Bengal. Farmers there stopped the Indigo cultivation under the leadership of two workers Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas who had once worked for an Indigo cultivator. By 1860, Indigo movement had spread to Nadia, Pabna, Khulna, Dhaka, Malda, Dinajpur etc. The intelligentsia of Bengal through public meetings, journals and articles in newspaper popularized the movement. Harishchandra Mukherjee, the editor of ‘Hindu Patriot’, played a major role in this. Through ‘Neel Darpan’, Dinabandhu Mitra openly visualised the atrocities of Indigo plantation owners on the farmers there. ‘Vande Mataram’ song is derived from the famous Novel Anandamath written by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee. The plot of this novel is based on Sanyasi Revolt. ‘Vande Mataram’ was sung for the first time in Congress session of Calcutta in 1896.
* Wahabi Movement which continued from the 4th to 7th decade of the 19th century provided an organized and stiff resistance to Britishers. Syed Ahmad Barelvi of Rae Bareli was the proponent ofthis movement. He was inspired by Abdul Wahab of Arabia but, in India, the movement was majorly inspired by Shah Waliullah of Delhi. By the efforts of Syed Ahmad Barelvi, this ideology soon spread to Northwest frontier provinces, Bengal, Bihar, Kabul and Central provinces. He captured Peshawar for some time in 1830 but he was killed in the battle of Balakot (1831). He introduced coins with his name in Peshawar. After the death of Syed Ahmad Barelvi, Patna became centre of this movement. During this period the leadership was provided by Maullavi Qasim, Vilayat Ali, Inayat Ali, Ahmadullah etc. After Patna, the branches of this movement were established in Hyderabad, Madras, Bengal, U.P. and Bombay.
The Namdhar Movement, popularly known as the Kuka Movement, was a socio-religious reform movement among Sikhs in Punjab in the mid 19th century. The movement was initiated by Bhagat Jawahar Mal (Sai Sahib) and Baba Balak Singh, who emphasised devotion to one God, spiritual purity and revival of Sikh religious values. It later developed into a powerful reform movement under Baba Ram Singh (1816- 1885), who organised the Namdhari sect and expanded its influence among Sikh masses. The movement was called “Kuka” because followers used to shout ecstatic cries called “Kuk” during religious devotion. Baba Ram Singh preached monotheism, prayer, meditation (Naam Simran) and strict moral discipline among followers. He opposed social evils such as caste system, female infanticide, early marriage, and the practice of exchanging daughters in marriage. He also popularised simple and inexpensive Anand marriages, promoting social reform and equality. The movement gradually acquired political overtones and resistance against British rule, which made British authorities suspicious of its growing popularity. The 1872 Kuka uprising in Punjab against cow slaughter and British authority was severely suppressed by the British administration. He died in 1885 while in exile as a state prisoner in Mergui, Burma (now Myanmar), where he had been held by the British since 1872.
* Pagal Panth was a semi-religious group founded by Karim/ Karam Shah. Tribal peasants organised themselves under Karim/Karam Shah’s son, Tipu Shah, to fight the oppression of Zamindars. Tipu Shah (1852) was the leader of the Pagal Panthi movement after the death of his father Karim Shah in 1813. Under him, the movement changed from a religious group into a peasant rebellion against oppressive zamindars and indirectly against the English East India Company. The main cause of the revolt was the heavy oppression and extra taxes imposed on raiyats (peasants). After the division of the Sherpur zamindari in 1820, litigation and Company financial pressures led zamindars to increase tax burdens further. Tipu Shah declared that land belonged equally to all human beings as God’s creation and that an oppressive ruler had no moral right to rule. Under Tipu Shah and his mother Chandi Bibi (Pir-Mata), the rebels fought several clashes and established an independent administration in the Sherpur area for over two years. Gar-jaripa, a mud fort north of Sherpur, became his capital. Tipu Shah was arrested multiple times between December 1824 and January 1825, then tried and imprisoned. Even after his arrest, leaders like Janku Pathor and Dubraj Pathor continued the movement for some time.
* The Faraizis were the followers of a Muslim sect founded by Haji Shariatullah of Faridpur in Eastern Bengal. They advocated radical religious, social and political changes. Shariatullah’s son Dudu Miyan organised his followers with the aim oforganising the oppressed peasantry against oppressive landlords and Indigo planters. This revolt continued from 1818 to 1862 A.D. and further, at the end, the followers ofthis sect got merged into Wahabi Movement.
* Wellesley in 1805 compelled Maharaja ofTravancore (Kerala) to accept the subsidiary alliance. Maharaja was very unhappy with this alliance and was hesitating towards completing some ofits terms. The behaviour ofthe English resident was rude and was full of hatred as a result of this Diwan Velu Thampi with the support ofNair Battalion revolted against them.
* Vasudev Balwant Phadke (1845-83 A.D.) was one ofthe leading earliest Indian revolutionaries who organized the Ramoshi tribes and trained them into a fighting force. He founded Ramoshi farmers group.
* Gadakaris were the hereditary workers on the Maratha forts. Gadakaris were hereditary fort-servants/garrison men/fort-keepers attached to Maratha forts, especially in the Kolhapur region. They were dismissed/disbanded from their traditional fort service under British-controlled administrative changes, which caused resentment. Their lands were brought under the control/supervision of the Mamlatdar (mamlatdar/ mamlatdars). These situations made them to revolt in 1844-45 A.D. at Kolhapur.
* The Khonds tribes spread in the hills of Orissa/Odisha and neighbouring districts, they were virtually independent. They rebelled against the British from 1846 to 1855 AD. Chakra Bisoi, a young king, led the Khonds who were joined by the Ghumsar, Kalahandi and other tribals to oppose the abolition of human sacrifices (Mariah), the introduction ofnew taxes and the entry ofZamindars into their areas. Khonds opposed the establishment of Mariah Agency.
* Kol Revolt was led by Buddho Bhagat in Chhotanagpur area in 1831-32 A.D. This movement continued intermittently later and was finally suppressed.
* Waghera Revolt in Okha mandal region of Gujarat was against Britishers. It took place in 1818-1819.
The Chronological order of the main revolts in India before 1857 is –
1. Bengal Sepoy Mutiny (1764), in which a group of sepoys in the Army of Hector Munro revolted and went to join the side of Mir Qasim during the battle of Buxar.
2. Vellore Sepoy Mutiny (1806 A.D.)
3. Revolt of Kutch (1816-32 A.D.)
4. Kol Revolt (1831-32 A.D.)
5. Santhal Revolt (1855-56 A.D.)
* In 1855-56 A.D., Santhal was an important tribal revolt in which we see a lot of tribal anger and hatred against the Britishers. The Revolt took place in the Damin-e-Koh area between Bhagalpur and Rajmahal hills. Moneylenders and Britishers were exploiting them together. Interest ranging from 50 to 500% was charged from the Santhal borrowers. Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand and Bhairav were four brothers who led this revolt. Sidhu once said to officials, “God said to me that this country is not of bosses. God will fight from our side and in
this way, these bosses and soldiers will fight with God.”
The revolt continued till 1856 A.D., at last, Sido (Sidhu) was arrested and hanged and Kanu was killed in action and the revolt was suppressed. In 1855 A.D., Santhals revolted in Bhognadih near Barhait of Sahibganj district. Santhals defeated the Army of Britishers led by Major Burroughs. Finally Commissioner ofBhagalpur Brown and Major General Lloyd ruthlessly suppressed the revolt.
Bhil revolts - Bhils community which lived on the upland basin in the Deccan table land of Khandesh revolted against English in 1818-31 A.D. Officials blamed Peshwa Bajirao II and his representative Trimbakji Dengle for inciting the Bhils to revolt. In reality, the agricultural hardship and the fear of the new English government were the main reasons for this revolt. In 1825 A.D., under the leadership of Sevaram, Bhils revolted again. Britishers ruthlessly suppressed the revolt for the second time with some efforts.
Rampa Rebellion - Rampa hilly area lies north of the Godavari river in Andhra Pradesh. The Tribal revolt was against the exploitation by Moneylenders and the Forest Acts.
* The second half of 19th Century, Surji Bhagat and Govind Guru named Social reformers tried to bring some reforms in the tribal communities residing in Mewar of Rajasthan, Dungarpur, Banswada, Pratapgarh, Sirohi, Pali, etc. Govind Guru (also called as Lasodhiya) established ‘Samp Sabha’ in 1883 A.D. with an aim to organize Bhils. He is also called as the propounder of Lasodhiya Movement.
* Birsa Munda led the Revolt of Munda tribes in 1895-1900 A.D. Collective farming prevailed in the Munda community but zamindars, contractors, moneylenders, etc. attacked this tradition to carve out their own interests. Mundas leaders fought for about 30 years to preserve their traditional farming. Sardari fight (Sardari Larai) was the earlier Munda agrarian agitation, while Birsa Munda’s later rebellion is also known as Munda Rebellion or Ulgulan (Great Tumult). In 1895, Birsa declared himself the Messenger of God. The revolt was most famous among the revolts of this time. The revolt started against the attempt to replace the traditional collective farming practices with the Zamindari or Individual ownership practices.
* Khunt Katti or Mundari was the Customary land tenure/common ownership (Khunt-Katti system) practice ofMundas. But with the passage of time, Birsa transformed this revolt into a religious and political movement. He is called ‘Jagat Pita’ or ‘Dharti Aaba’. He said ‘we will fight with non-tribals and will colour this land red with their blood like a Red Flag’. In February, 1900 A.D., Birsa was arrested and died in prison in June 1900 A.D. The area between (Ranchi Khunt) and Singhbhum side areas (e.g- Chakradharpur) was under his influence. He called for the worship of one god ‘Sing Bonga’ renouncing the worship of other gods.
* Thakkar Bapa used word ‘Adivasi’ for the tribal population. He was the General Secretary of ‘Harijan Sevak Sangh’.
* Hauz(Ho) Revolt of 1820-21 A.D. took place in then Santhal subdivision of Bihar (Now Singhbhum district Jharkhand).
* Khairwar tribal movement in 1874 A.D. was led by Bhagirath Manjhi.
* Surendra Sai the claimant to throne of Sambalpur led the movement against British. In 1862, he surrendered.
* Moplah Revolt took place in 1921 A.D. in Malabar area (Tirurangadi) Here the Muslims were the major sharecropper tenants and Hindus were Zamindars. The revolt took place due to exploitation by Zamindars resistance against British Raj and Khilafat movement.
* Ahom Revolt (1828 A.D.) was led by Gomdhar Kunwar.
* Tana Bhagat Movement was started in 1914 at Chhotanagpur by Oraon tribes. Jatra Bhagat, Balram Bhagat and Devmenia Bhagat led this revolt.
* Jadonang was the first tribal leader inspired by Mahatma Gandhi and his philosophy. He was a pioneer freedom fighter for Naga community in Manipur.

1. Which one of the following upheavals took place in Bengal immediately after Revolt of 1857?

(a) Sanyasi
(b) Santhal Rebellion
(c) Indigo revolt
(d) Pabna Disturbances
I.A.S. (Pre) 1994
Ans. (c)
The Indigo Revolt (1859-60 A.D.) took place in Bengal immediately after the revolt of 1857. Sanyasi Rebellion occurred in 1763-1800 A.D., Santhal Rebellion occurred in 1855-56 A.D., and Pabna uprising took place in 1873-76 A.D. Indigo Revolt began in Govindpur village of Nadia district in West Bengal led by two former employees of indigo producers. Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas organized the peasants into a counterforce and refused to sow indigo. This indigo revolt of Bengal was directed against British planters who forced peasants to take advances and sign fraudulent contracts which forced the peasants to grow Indigo under terms that were least profitable to them. In September, 1859 AD all the cultivators revolted against the landlords. The agitation spread to regions like Nadia, Pabana, Khulna, Dhaka, Malda, Dinajpur, etc. All indigo factories were shut down due to this strike. The intellectual class of Bengal expressed support for the Movement through their articles and rallies. The editor of “Hindu Patriot” Harishchandra Mukherjee played a significant role in this agitation. This revolt was vividly portrayed by Dinbandhu Mitra in his play Neel Darpan.

52. Ulgulan Revolt was related to –

(a) Santhal
(b) Kachha Nagh
(c) Kol
(d) Birsa Munda
39th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 1994
Ans. (d)
Munda Rebellion was one ofthe most prominent 19th-Century tribal rebellions in the subcontinent. Birsa Munda led this movement in the region in 1899-1900 A.D. The Ulgulan, meaning ‘Great Tumult,’ sought to establish an independent Munda Raj. The Mundas traditionally enjoyed a preferential rent rate as the Khuntkatti or the original clearer ofthe forest. But in the course of the 19th century, they had seen this Khuntkatti land system being eroded by the jaghirdars and thikedars coming as merchants and moneylenders. In 1895 A.D., Birsa claiming to have seen a vision ofGod, proclaimed himselfa prophet with miraculous healing powers. Thousands flocked to hear the ‘new word’ of Birsa with its prophecy of an imminent deluge. The new prophet became a critic of the traditional tribal customs, religious beliefs, and practices. He called upon the Mundas to fight against superstition, give up animal sacrifice, stop taking intoxicants, wear the sacred thread and retain the tribal tradition of worship in the Sarna or the sacred grove. It was essentially a revivalist movement, which sought to purge Munda society ofall foreign elements and restore its pristine character. Christianity influenced the movement as well and it used both Hindu-Christian idioms to create the Munda ideology and worldview. On Christmas Eve 1899 A.D., the Mundas shot arrows and tried to burn down churches over an area covering six police stations in the districts of Ranchi and Singhbhum. Next, in January 1900, the police stations were targeted and there were rumours that Birsa’s followers would attack Ranchi on 8th January, leading to panic there. However, on 9th January, the rebels were defeated. Birsa was captured and died in jail. Nearly 350 Mundas were put on trial, and three ofthem were hanged and 44 transported for life.
