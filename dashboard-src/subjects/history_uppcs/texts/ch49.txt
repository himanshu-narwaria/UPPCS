CHAPTER 49: Development of Press in Modern India

EXPLANATION SECTION
* James Augustus Hicky in 1780 A.D. started the Bengal Gazette, the first newspaper in India. Lord Wellesley enacted Censorship of Press Regulations, 1799. It imposed almost wartime press restrictions including pre-censorship. In 1807 this Act included pamphlets, books and magazines too.
* Lord Hastings in 1818 abolished the strict pre-censorship ofthe press in India and dispensed with their submitting their papers to an officer of government previous to publications. Vernacular Press Act, 1878 was passed during the tenure of Lord Lytton (1876-1880 A.D.). The Act empowered the district magistrate to call upon the printer and publisher of any vernacular newspaper to enter into a bond with the government not to cause disaffection against the government or antipathy between persons of different religion, caste, race, through published material.
* The press and publisher could also be required to deposit security which could be forfeited ifthe regulation were violated, and press equipment could be seized if the offence reoccurred.
* Under this Act, proceedings were instituted against Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash and Samachar.
* B. G. Tilak was imprisoned for anti-British journalism in 1882. He was the first Indian to be punished for the performance of his duty in this field.
* Tilak was arrested after the murder of W.C. Rand and officer Charles Ayerst on the grounds of the publication ofa poem, “Shivaji’s utterances” in Kesari, and a speech that Tilak had delivered at the Shivaji festival, justifying Afzal’s Khan murder by Shivaji. Tilak’s defence of Shivaji’s killing of Afzal Khan was portrayed by the prosecution as an incitement to kill British officials. In 1897, he was imprisoned for 18 months for inciting Chapekar brothers for killing W.C. Rand and Charles Ayerst through his articles published in Kesari (he was charged for sedition for writing such articles).
* 'The Free Hindustan' newspaper was started by Tarak Nath Das in North America (Vancouver).
* Raja Ram Mohan Roy used the press as a medium to express and publish his views. In 1821 he published ‘Sambad Kaumudi’ Bengali weekly newspaper. After one year he published a newspaper ‘Mirat ul-Akhbar’ in the Persian language. ‘Indian Mirror’ was published from Calcutta (Bengal). Credit for establishment of Indian Mirror (1861) goes to Debendranath Tagore and Manmohan Ghosh.
* Lala Hardayal, Sohan Singh Bhakana and Kartar Singh Sarabha, etc. founded Hindustan Ghadar Party (Ghadar Party) in USA and Canada to free India from British Rule. ‘Ghadar’ a weekly publication, was a manifesto of the party which was first published on 1st November, 1913 at San Francisco in Urdu. From 9th December, 1913 A.D. its printing in Gurmukhi script also started. It was later on published in Marathi, Hindi, English and Gujarati. Its one edition was also published in Pashto language.
* Amrit Bazar Patrika, a Bengali (later English) weekly was launched February 20, 1868, in the village of Palua Magura in the Jessore district ofPresent day Bangladesh, through the efforts ofSishir Kumar and Motilal Ghosh. But later it started being published from Kolkata (West Bengal), but to escape the Vernacular Press Act, it got converted to English format overnight in 1878 A.D.
* Girish Chandra Ghosh started ‘The Bengalee’ in 1862 A.D. which was transferred to S.N. Banerjee in 1879 A.D. Girish Chandra Ghosh started the Hindoo Patriot. Later on, Harish Chandra Mukherjee became its editor.
* In 1881 A.D., Tilak published two newspapers Kesari and Mahratta in Poona (now Pune). Kesari in Marathi was the main newspaper of Tilak’s Home Rule Movement. Earlier Gopal Ganesh Agarkar was the first editor of Kesari. The first editor of the Maratta/ Maratha was Bal Gangadhar Tilak. Mahratta was published in English.
* Bangwasi, Kaal, Kesari etc. Newspaper supported the revolutionary movements and criticized the moderate policies ofCongress. Sandhya, Yugantar and Kaal, advocated revolutionary extremism during the freedom struggle.
* Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar in 1858 started publishing ‘Som Prakash’ newspaper, it supported the interests of the Indigo revolutionaries. Vidyasagar mooted the original idea of the newspaper, it was first edited by Dwarkanath Vidyabhushan. The Free Press Journal is an English-language daily newspaper that was established by Swaminathan Sadanand.
* Indian opinion was started by Mahatma Gandhi in 1903 A.D. in South Africa. Mansukh Lal Hiralal Nazar was its first editor. It was published in Gujarati, Hindi, Tamil and English.
* Young India was published bi-weekly from Bombay from 7 May 1919 A.D. under supervision of Gandhiji. Jamanadas Dwarakadas and Shankarlal Banker were its foundational member Shankar Lal Banker was its publisher. On 8th October, 1919 A.D. Mahatma Gandhi became the editor of Young India and it was started publishing weekly from Ahmedabad. Jamanadas Dwarakadas and Shankar lal Banker were members of Home Rule Movement ofAnnie Besant.
* Indigo Revolt of Bengal was a direct fight against the exploitation of farmers. Harishchandra Mukherjee editor of Hindoo Patriot put a lot of effort in this respect. Girish Chandra Ghosh was the editor of Hindoo Patriot in the first two years (1853-55 A.D.). In 1855 A.D., Harish Chandra Mukherjee became the editor of Hindoo Patriot. He boldly spoke about the exploitation of Indigo farmers by the plantation owners. He helped these farmers in all possible ways. Kristodas Pal in 1861 became the editor of Hindoo Patriot.
* Aurobindo Ghosh was the editor of English weekly Bande Matram.
* In 1931 A.D., Kameshwar Singh of Darbhanga started The Indian Nation. It was published in Patna.
* Ram Krishna Pillai was the editor of Swadeshabhamini.
* Gandhiji started Harijan in English, Harijan Bandhu in Gujarati and Harijan Sewak in Hindi. These were weekly newspapers. First edition of Harijan was published on 11th February, 1933 A.D. from Pune, Maharashtra.
* On July 1924 A.D., B.R. Ambedkar in Bombay founded an institute called ‘Bahiskrit Hitakarini Sabha’ whose aim was to promote untouchables morally and physically. He started a fortnightly bulletin Bahishkrit Bharat (first published in 1927)
* Abul Kalam Azad in 1912 A.D. started an Urdu weekly called Al-Hilal. Al-Balagh was published in 1915 A.D. by him. Al-Hilal was banned in 1914 A.D. by Press Act.
* Lala Lajpat Rai started the Vande Mataram (1905-06) an Urdu daily and The People (1925, Lahore), a weekly in English. Before this, he had already published Young India (1916) in America.
* George Allen published ‘The Pioneer’ newspaper in 1865 at Allahabad (Prayagraj).
* Qaumi Awaz an Urdu newspaper was started by Jawaharlal Nehru and Rafi Ahmed Kidwai in 1945 A.D. Its publication was stopped in 2008.
* Rast Goftar is a newspaper related to Dadabhai Naoroji. Motilal Nehru demanding independence of India (or Home Rule for India) started a newspaper called The Independent in February 1919 at Allahabad.
* The Commonweal was an English newspaper published in Madras by Annie Besant.

5. Who among the following repealed the Vernacular Press Act of 1878?

(a) Lord Ripon
(b) Lord Lytton
(c) Lord Curzon
(d) Lord Minto
I.A.S. (Pre) 2005; 39th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 1994
Ans. (a)
Lord Lytton (1876-1880 A.D.) passed the Vernacular Press Act in 1878 A.D. By this Act, the Magistrates of the districts were empowered without the prior permission of the Government to call upon a publisher of any kind to enter into a bond, undertaking not to publish anything that might “rouse” feelings ofdisaffection against the Government. The magistrate was also authorized to deposit security, which could be confiscated if the publisher violated the bond. If a publisher repeated the violation, their press could be seized. Thus, the Vernacular Press Act of 1878 gagged the native language press. There was a popular protest against this Act. This Act was later repealed by Lord Ripon in 1882.
