CHAPTER 87: Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Clement Attlee became British Prime Minister on 26th July, 1945 A.D. British Prime Minister Clement Attlee announced on February 19th, 1946 A.D., the dispatch of the Cabinet Mission for constitutional reforms. Cabinet Mission arrived in Delhi on 24th March 1946 A.D. The Cabinet Mission to India consisted Lord Pethick Lawrence was secretory of State for India Sir Stafford Cripps the President of the Board of Trade and A.V. Alexander, First Lord of the Admiralty. They arrived on March 24, 1946 A.D. and held extensive discussions in April with almost all major leaders ofthe country. The Cabinet Mission proposed a three-tier polity for India. The major recommendation of the Cabinet Mission was to divide the provinces into three sections i.e. A, B and C.
* On 16th May, 1946 A.D. Cabinet Mission announced its recommendations and its main proposals were-
(1) There should be a Union ofIndia, embracing both British India and the states, which should deal with the following subjects – foreign affairs, defence and communications; of India would be given Independence.
(2) The Muslim League’s demand for Pakistan was rejected. (3) The Union should have an Executive and Legislature constituted from British India Province and Princely State Representatives.
(4) A Constituent Assembly would be formed with the representatives ofProvincial Legislative Assembly Chief Commissioners Provinces and the Princely States.
* Cabinet Mission also recommended reorganization of Viceroy’s Executive Council and formation of the interim government in which all the ministerial posts including war member would be held by Indians.
* Gandhiji supported Cabinet Mission and said this proposal is the best document the British Government could have produced in the circumstances, while cautioning that their best may possible be harmful. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was the Congress President during the arrival of Cabinet Mission.

Ans. (a)
The Cabinet Mission reached Delhi on 24 March, 1946 AD. The Cabinet Mission announced its recommendations on 16th May, 1946 AD.
The main provision of the recommendations were:
(1) The whole ofIndia, including the Princely States should form a Federation.
(2)The Central Government should be in charge offoreign affairs, defence and communication.
(3) The provinces and the states should enjoy all the powers. The British Indian Provinces and territories were to be divided into three groups; Group A: Madras, Central Provinces and Berar, United Province, Bihar, Bombay and Orissa.
Group B: Punjab, North-West Frontier Province and Sindh. Group C: Bengal and Assam.
Apart from this, the Provinces of Chief Commissioners, Delhi, Ajmer-Marwar and Coorg will be included in category (A) and Baluchistan will be included in category (B). The Constituent Assembly was to be elected to frame a Constitution for the Indian Union.
The province should have the right to leave the Union in future ifit so likes after elections under its new Constitution. There should be an interim National Government with leaders of the Indian parties to take charge of the administration.

U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2018
Ans. (b)
The Cabinet Mission of 1946 AD came to India to discuss the transfer of power from the British Government to the Indian Leadership, with the aim of preserving India's unity and granting it independence formulated at the initiative of Clement Attlee, the Prime Minister of United Kingdom, the mission included Lord Pethick-Lawrence, the secretary of state for India, Sir Stafford Cripps, President of the Board ofTrade and A.V. Alexander, the first Lord of the Admiralty. Lord Wavell, the Viceroy of India, did not participate in every step but was present. The Mission arrived on March 24, 1946 AD.
According to the Mission, the representation ofthe Provincial Legislatures were to be broken up into 3 sections
(a) Section A: Madras, United Provinces, Central Province and Berar, Bombay, Bihar & Orissa.

The Muslim League first approved the Plan. But when congress, declared that it could change the scheme through majority in the Constituent Assembly they rejected the plan. Muslim League on repeated request by Lord Wavell joined the Interim Government on 13 October, 1946 AD.
