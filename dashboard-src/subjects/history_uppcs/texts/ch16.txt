CHAPTER 16: Muslim Invasion of India

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Mecca is situated in Saudi Arabia. The propounder of Islam religion, Hazarat Muhammad, was born here in 570 A.D. The place is a religious pilgrimage for Islamic followers. Muhammad died in 632 A.D.
* Rigveda mentions the Bharatas as a tribe/people. The land name Bharat/Bharatavarsa is stated in later sanskrit texts (e.g. Vishnu Purana). According to tradition, the country was named after Dushyanta's son, 'Bharat'.
* According to some scholars, the country is named after the name ofRishabhanath’s eldest son 'Bharat'.
* Iranians addressed this country as ‘Hindustan’, and Greeks called it ‘India’. With respect to the people of Hind (Bharat), the word ‘Hindu’ was first used by Iranian/Persian.
* The first successful Muslim invasion of India took place in 712 AD under the leadership ofArabian invader “Muhammad-Bin Qasim’. Earlier before 712 AD, a campaign was undertaken to India under the leadership of Ubaidullah, but he was defeated and killed in the encounter.After a great struggleArabians conquered Sindh in 712 AD. Sindh was ruled by Dahir at that time. According to Persian texts ‘Chachnama’ we came to know about the conquest of Sindh by Arabians.
Note: The year of the first successful invasion of India by Muslims is a subject of dispute among scholars. According to V.D. Mahajan, it is dated in 711 A.D. whereas according to Harishchandra Verma, it took place in 712 A.D.
* The founder ofGhaznavid Empire was Alptigin. Ghaznavid dynasty is also known as Yamini dynasty. Alptigin made Ghazni his capital. In 998 AD, Mahmud became the ruler of Ghazni. According to Sir Henry Elliot, Mahmud invaded India 17 times. Mahmud Ghaznavi plundered Somnath Temple during the reign of Bhimdev I (1022-63).
* Mahmud Ghaznavi, for the first time, invaded Chandelas in 1019-20 AD. At that time, the ruler of Chandela dynasty was Vidyadhar, who was the most powerful ruler of the dynasty.
He was a Hindu ruler who had successfully resisted Muslim invasions. Between 1000-1027 AD, Mahmud Ghazni (Ghaznavi) attacked India 17 times, but the motive of these invasions was not to establish a stable Muslim rule in India but only to plunder the material assets. Baghdad’s Caliph Al Qadir-Billah titled Mahmud Ghaznavi as ‘Yamin-ud-Daula’ and ‘Amin-ul-Millah’.
* Mahmud Ghaznavi patronised Persian learning. He respected scholars and artists alongwith famous scholars in Ghazni at that time. Utbi was his court historian. He wrote texts like Al-Kitab al-Yamini/ ‘Tarikh-i-Yamini’. Other prominent members ofhis court were Albiruni, Baihaqi (writer ofTarikh-i-Subuktigin), Persian poet Uzari and Unsuri, Asjadi and Farrukhi.
* The author of Shahnama is Firdausi. He was a famous court poet of Mahmud Ghaznavi. He earned the titled of ‘Homer of the East’. Firishta (1560-1620 A.D.) wrote books like Tarikh-i Firishta, also Known as Gulshan-i- Ibrahimi. His full name was Muhammad Qasim Hindu Shah Astarabadi Firishta. His book ‘Tarikh-i-Firishta’ is dedicated to the ruler of Bijapur Ibrahim Adil Shah II. In the 11th century, Al-Biruni came to India with Mahmud Ghazanavi. From his book ‘Kitab-ul-hind’ we come to know about the social and cultural conditions of contemporary India.
* Al-Biruni was the first Muslim to study Puranas. He was born in 973 AD and was a resident of Khiva (ancient Khwarizm). He came to India with Mahmud Ghaznavi. Al-Biruni was not only a historian, his knowledge and interest included Astronomy, Geography, logic, medicine, mathematics, religion and theology. He studied Sanskrit, including many literary works of Brahmagupta, Balabhadra and Varahmihira are outlined especially. Al-Biruni wrote ‘Tahqiq-i-Hind’ in the Arabic language.
* Edward Sachau translated this text from Arabic into English language. It was translated into Hindi by Rajnikant Sharma. Mahmud Ghazanavi introduced silver coins with Sanskrit legend. The two sides of the silver coin were marked with the legend in two different languages. The legend on the upper side was inscribed in the Arabic language, and the other side was inscribed in the Sanskrit language (Sharada Script). In the middle part of the coin was written in Sanskrit language - “Avyaktmekam Muhammad Avatar Nripati Mahmud”.
* Ghurid ruler from Ghor (present day Afghanistan) Shihabuddin Muhammad Ghori first invaded India in 1175 AD. In 1178 AD, Muhammad Ghori invaded Gujarat, but Mularaja-II or Bhimdev-II, under the leadership ofhis qualified and courageous widowed mother Naika Devi fought and defeated Ghori near Mount Abu. It was the first defeat ofGhori in India.
* In 1191 AD, Prithviraj Chauhan and Ghori fought the first battle ofTarain. In 1192 AD, Muhammad Ghori defeated Prithviraj Chauhan in the second battle of Tarain, and laid the foundation of Muslim power in India. Ghori’s alertness and skilled battle techniques were the main factors in the victory of the Muslims. Prithviraj Chauhan while trying to escape, was caught near Sursati (modern Sirsa, Haryana).
* For the death of Prithviraj Chauhan different opinions were given, but among those opinions, the opinion of Hasan Nizami is widely accepted, which says that Prithviraj's son was appointed to govern Ajmer when Prithviraj tried to conspire for a revolt, and was then awarded the death penalty. This battle was the most important battle in Indian History.
* In 1194 AD, Muhammad Ghori defeated Kannauj’s Gahadavala King Jayachandra in the battle of Chandawar. Chandawar is situated in present Firozabad district (U.P.) near banks ofYamuna.
* Coins issued by Ghori had image of Goddess Laxmi and Kalma was inscribed in Arabic on some other.
* Soon after the victories of Muhammad Ghori ‘Iqta system’ was introduced in Northern India.
* Muhammad Ghori appointed Qutb Al-Din Aybak, (also spelled Qutbuddin Aibak, Qutb-Ud-Din-Aybak) in 1192 AD as incharge of his Indian territories. He played a significant role in the second battle of Tarain of 1192 AD. Pleased with his works, Ghori appointed him the administrator of Kuhram and Samana. He controlled the administration ofNorthern parts of India as Ghori’s representative till 1206 AD. In this period, Aybak expanded the Turkish power in Northern India.
* Ghori or Aybak never thought ofconquering Bihar and Bengal. The task was accomplished by an ordinary slave of Muhammad Ghori named Ikhtiyar-ud-din Muhammad-Bin Bakhtiyar Khalji (1193 A.D. to 1206 A.D.). He occupied the capital Odantapuri by conquering Bihar and destroyed Nalanda and Vikramshila. He invaded Bengal from 1198 A.D. to 1204-05 A.D. At that time the ruler there was Lakshmana Sena. He left the place without actually fighting the battle. Turkish army plundered the people entering the capital city of Nadia. In the absence of the king, the city surrendered. Escaping from there Lakshmana Sena took refuge in East Bengal and ruled there for some time.
* Bakhtiyar Khalji did not try to conquer complete Bengal. He made Lakhnauti his capital.
* According to Professor Habib, the victory of North-Western province by Turks had given birth to ‘Urban Revolution’ and ‘Rural Revolution’ respectively.

40. Below are two statements, one of which is an Assertion (A) and the other is a Reason (R): Assertion (A): The Turkish invasion of India was successful. Reason (R): There was no political unity in northern India. Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(c) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(d) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2024
Ans. (a)
Several Turkish rulers, including Mahmud of Ghazni and Muhammad Ghori, invaded northern India between the 11th and 12th centuries. Among these, Ghori's invasion had a more significant impact, leading to the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate, which laid the foundation for Muslim rule in India. During this period, the political conditions in India were highly fragmented, with many small and independent states. Due to this lack of unity, it was easy for the invaders to achieve victory and establish control. There was no unified resistance against them.
Thus, both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) correctly explains (A).
