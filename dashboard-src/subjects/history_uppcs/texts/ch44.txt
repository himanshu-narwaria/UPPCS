CHAPTER 44: Governor/Governor General/ Viceroy

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Both Civilian and Military reforms took place during the period of Robert Clive. Under the civilian reform, a ban was imposed on the gift to officials and controlled private trade (Clive imposed conditioned ban on accepting gifts; i.e. for accepting gifts above Rs. 1000 of value, a pre-permission of governor is necessary). Under the Military reforms, he ended ‘double batta’ for European officers and from January 1, 1766 AD this allowance was only granted to soldiers who served outside the boundaries ofBengal and Bihar (except Allahabad).
* Under administrative reforms, Warren Hastings in 1772 A.D. ended the dual government system of Bengal by order of the Court of Directors. He transferred the State’s treasury from Murshidabad to Calcutta. Warren Hastings believed that the whole land belonged to the ruler. To reform in Revenue field, he chose trial and error method. Warren Hastings in 1774 A.D. was made Governor-General of Bengal by the Regulating Act 1773. The act provides for Governor-General and four consellors (“Council of four”), total five (including Governor-general). The Governor General was head of the government and had casting vote if equally divided. It was established to look after the affairs of Bengal. The Chairman ofthe Executive Council was called Governor-General. Several types ofreforms in Police, Judiciary, Taxation, Faujdari related Acts etc. took place during the time of Cornwallis.
* Asiatic Society of Bengal was established on 15th January, 1784 A.D. in the tenure of Governor General Warren Hastings.
* Warren Hastings and Wellesley were related to the Ring-Fence theory.
* Impeachment proceedings were initiated on Hastings for injustices and atrocities committed by him but in the light of his service, proceedings which took place between the years 1788 A.D. and 1795 A.D. (until he was acquitted in April 1795) The following were the policies of Britishers towards Indian States (Princely States):-
1- Company’s struggle for equality with Indian provinces (1740-1765 AD)
2- Policy of Ring Fence (1765-1813 AD)
3- Policy of Subordinate Isolation (1813-1857 AD)
4- Policy of Subordinate Union (1858-1935 AD)
5- Policy of Equal Federation (1935-1947 AD)
* Britishers developed a new system forjustice delivery through hierarchical institutions consisting of Diwani and Faujdari Courts. This system was started by Warren Hastings but Cornwallis made it more rigid. He concentrated all powers in the hands of Collector. In 1787 A.D. the District Collector was made the Diwani Judge ofDiwani Courts and was granted some Faujdari Powers to deliver Justice in some Faujdari cases too.
* In 1790-92 AD, District Faujdari Courts with Indian Judges were abolished and in their place four Circuit Courts, three for Bengal and one for Bihar, were appointed. These Courts were headed by European officials and the Justice was delivered with the help of Indian Kazis and Muftis. Cornwallis initiated his rule of specification of law, it was a set of regulations that codified administrative, revenue and judicial reforms of East India Company in Bengal.
* ‘Cornwallis Code’ is the collection of judicial reforms by Cornwallis in 1793 A.D. This reform was based on the law of ‘Separation of Powers’. He separated Revenue and Judicial administration. The judicial and faujdari power of collector was taken back and he was entrusted with powers related to taxation only.
* A new hierarchy of officers was created for jobs in the District Diwani Courts. District Judges were given works ofFaujdari and Police too. The modern Indian Civil Services was reorganised and professionalised by Cornwallis (1793). He forbade private trade and gifts, raised salaries, separated functions and gave legal form to civil service rules; he was regarded as the reformer of civil service. Lord Cornwallis was the Governor General of Bengal from 1786 to 1793 and from 30th July, 1805 A.D. to 5th October, 1805 A.D. He died at Ghazipur (Uttar Pradesh) on 5th October 1805 A.D. His grave is present in Ghazipur.
* Treaty of Bassein (December, 1802) was made between Peshwa Bajirao II and Britishers. Subsidiary Alliance of Lord Wellesley was accepted by the Peshwa Bajirao II becoming the first Maratha chief to do so. According to subsidiary alliance, Peshwa accepted the offer of protection of English. As per the treaty, Britishers provided 6 thousands soldiers to reinstate Peshwa at Poona and the Peshwa promised lands are having revenues worth 26 lakhs annually as a payback.
* Lord Wellesley (1798-1805 A.D.) cleverly used subsidiary alliance as a tool to make Indian provinces as puppets of Britishers. He did not discover this method. This method was in practice earlier also but it gradually evolved and the final shape was provided by Wellesley. Dupleix was the first European to give his Army to an Indian ruler on the promise of a fixed payment to the Company. Britishers used these tactics too. First such instances can be traced as 1765 treaty in Oudh/ Awadh (can be marked as the precursor of subsidiary alliance) where Company promised to protect the boundaries of Oudh on a fixed payment to the Company.
* The evolved version of the Subsidiary Alliance of Lord Wellesley was accepted by the Princely States in the following Sequence- Hyderabad (1798 A.D. and 1800 A.D.), Mysore (1799 A.D.), Tanjore (October 1799 A.D.), Awadh (November 1801 A.D.), Peshwa (December 1802 A.D.), Bhonsle of Nagpur/ Berar (December 1803 A.D.). Sindhiya Bharatpur (1803-05) (February 1804 A.D.), Some other alliances include Jodhpur (1818), Udaipur/ Mewar (1818), Bundi (Feb., 1818), Bikaner (March, 1818) and Jaipur (April, 1818).
* Lord William Bentinck in July 1828 A.D. assumed the office of Governor-General of Bengal. He took some bold steps to eliminate evil social practices such as Sati system, Female Infanticide etc. On the will of Court of Directors, he tried to adopt neutral policies towards the Princely Indian States wherever possible. Mismanagement in Jaipur one time grew so high that the British Resident was attacked there but William Bentinck did not interfere. Similarly, he did not interfere in the internal matters of Jodhpur, Bundi, Kota and Bhopal when he had enough reasons to do so. Deviating from this policy, he placed Mysore (in 1831 A.D.) and provinces of Kurg (1834 A.D.) and Kachhar (in 1832 A.D.) under British Empire because of extreme mismanagement prevailing there.
* During the seven- year rule of William Bentinck (1828-35 A.D.) the old policies ofwar and annexation were abandoned or reduced. William Bentinck is remembered for his contributions in the field of Administrative and Social Reforms.
* By the Charter Act of 1833 an attempt was made towards totally centralised Administration. Governor-General of Bengal became Governor-General of India through this Act. Captain Sleeman was appointed by the William Bentinck to tackle the Thug Activities. He arrested 1562 thugs between 1826 to 1835 A.D. Many of them hanged and the rest of them were exiled. By 1839 A.D., the organiZed nature of Thugi ended.
* By the declaration of 1789 A.D. the export of slaves from Bengal was stopped. Acts related to slaves were formulated in 1811 A.D. and 1823 A.D. The Charter Act of 1833 A.D. emphasized on formulating law by the Governor to end slavery as soon as possible. By 1843 A.D. slavery was declared illegal in most part of British India (it removed the enforceability of slave property in civil courts). In 1860 A.D., Indian Penal Code declared slavery as a crime. (IPC was repealed and Bhartiya Nyay Samhita came into force in 2024).
* Anglo-Nepal War (1814-16 A.D.) took place during the time of Governor-General Lord Hastings (1813-23 A.D.) which ended with Sugauli’s Treaty.
* In 1854 James Outram was made British resident of Oudh replacing Sleeman. He said the administration of Oudh is very polluted and the condition of the people was miserable. This report of Outram emerged as the base for the annexation of Awadh. Lord Dalhousie (1848-56 A.D.) blamed Nawab ofAwadh Wajid Ali Shah for maladministration and, in February 1856 A.D., Awadh was annexed by Britishers. The States that were annexed through the policy of Doctrine of Lapse are - Satara (1848), Jaitpur and Sambalpur (1849), Baghat (1850), Udaipur (1852), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854) etc.
* Lord Dalhousie formulated the first comprehensive Railway Policy for India in 1853 and strongly promoted railway expansion, though the earliest railway proposals predated his tenure (like early plan of 1832 and Lord Hardinge 1844 plan etc.). The first railway line was laid between Bombay to Thane in 1853 A.D. The Great Indian Peninsula Railway started the train travel in India.
* Main Reason for the development of railways was to export raw materials from the inner parts of the country to different Ports of India and also open Indian markets to British manufactured goods. Its second objective was the swift movement of Army in remote and distant areas to easily suppress any kind ofrevolt taking place in any part of the country.
* Railway expansion in India accelerated particulary between 1880 and 1900, while Lord Curzon’s contribution was mainly administrative such as the establishment of the ‘Railway Board’ in 1905.
* During Dalhousie a separate Public Work Department was established and a significant amount was spent on public work through this body.
* With the efforts of Ishwarchandra Vidyasagar, Lord Canning in 1856 A.D., passed Hindu Widow Remarriage Act. According to Act XV (15) of 1856, widow remarriage and the Child born to that marriage was legalized. Dhondo Keshav Karve and Vishnu Shashtri were two propagators of widow remarriage. Dhondo Keshav in 1896 A.D. established a widow Ashram in Poona. On August 2, 1858 the British Parliament passed the Government of India Act 1858, transferring British power over India from East India Company. The act legally abolished the company and created the secretary of State. This act led to Queen’s Proclamation.
* Queen’s Proclamation of 1858 was the most important result of the 1857 revolt. This proclamation was announced on 1st November, 1858 AD by Lord Canning at a Court held in Allahabad. The 1858 Act ended the Company rule and put the affairs of India under the direct control of the British Crown. Lord Canning was made first Viceroy of India. Queen Victoria was made the ruler of India in 1858 AD. A Grand Delhi Darbar organized in January 1877 AD. This Grand Durbar was organised by Lord Lytton, following the ‘Royal Titles Act 1876. During this, Queen Victoria was proclaimed ‘Empress/Sovereign of India’ & conferred with the title Kaiser-e-Hind (Qaiser-i-Hind).
* Under Governor-General Auckland, first Anglo-Afghan war took place in 1839-1842 A.D. Lord Lytton was the Governor-General when second Anglo-Afghan war broke in 1878-1880 A.D. By Act-V of 1843 Slavery was abolished (removed legal enforce ability) by Governor-General of India Lord Ellenborough. During his tenure (1842-44 AD) in March 1843 AD Sindh was fully merged into British Empire. The policy of Masterly Inactivity in Anglo-Afghan relations was started during Lord Ellenborough and continued till Lord North Brooke. This period is called the Period of Masterly Inactivity. This policy is often recognized with John Lawrence because during his time at the office, he got full chance to follow this policy and evolution of this policy took place in his time only. Masterly Inactivity word is taken from an Article published by J.W.S. Wiley.
* First Census took place in 1872 A.D. during Lord Mayo (1869-72 A.D.). Proper census took place in 1881 A.D. during Lord Rippon. Lord Mayo (1869-72 A.D.) was killed during an excursion in Andaman and Nicobar Islands group by a prisoner named Sher Ali Afridi. Mayo was the Viceroy who was killed while holding the office.
* In 1876 A.D., Lytton became Viceroy and changed his policies towards Afghanistan.
* Vernacular Press Act of 1878 A.D. was passed during Lord Lytton. This Act is also called the Gagging Act. (some historians also called this as silencing act due to its strict censorship). Sir Erskine Perry termed this act as a dangerous step towards India’s right to expression. Sri S.N. Banerjee termed this act as lightning from the sky.
* Sir C.P. Ilbert who was the law member ofViceroy executive council, on 2nd February, 1883 in Legislative council presented a bill known as Ilbert Bill. This Bill was aimed at equalizing both European and Indian Judges so that Indian judges can hereby try a cases involving British subjects in various criminal cases changing the decade-old provision of barring Indian civil servants from doing so. This Bill was aimed at ending any judicial disqualification based on races. The Bill faced a severe opposition by Britishers as they perceived this as an attack on their privileges.
* Lord Ripon is believed to be most pro Indian Viceroy of British India. He made some lenient laws in favour of children employed in factories numbering 100 or more, this was called as First Factory Act, 1881. According to this act, a ban was imposed on the employment of children below 7 years in factories and the working hours of children aged 7 to 12 years were reduced to 9 hours daily and they got 4 holidays in a month. Second Factory Act, 1891 made week holidays possible. Lord Ripon is known as the father ofthe local government. Charles Metcalfe removed the restrictions on the press, he is also known as the liberator ofIndian Press. In 1784 A.D., Sir William Jones founded Asiatic Society in Calcutta and steps were taken towards Archaeological Survey of India. Alexander Cunningham was appointed as the Archaeological Surveyor in 1861. In 1871, Archaeological Survey was made a separate body and Cunningham was made its Director General.
* During the tenure ofLord Curzon (1899-1905 A.D.) in 1901 The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) became centralized and the post of DG was restored and in 1902, John Marshall (who was appointed in 1901) assumed office of the Director General (DG) of this body. The Ancient Monument Preservation Act, 1904 allocated about 50,000 Pound for the protection and maintenance of the historical monuments of India.
* Gopal Krishna Gokhale compared the rule ofCurzon with Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb.
* In November, 1905 Lord Minto replaced Lord Curzon and John Morley was made Secretary of State for India. They both proposed Morley-Minto Reforms (1909 A.D.) which granted separate electorate to Muslims. During the tenure of Lord Hardinge-II (Charles Hardinge) Capital of India was shifted to Delhi from Calcutta. He invited Queen Mary and George V in a Grand Darbar organized at Delhi which is known as third Delhi Darbar (1911 A.D.). In this Darbar, the partition of Bengal was abolished and the announcement was made for the transfer of Capital from Calcutta to Delhi.

21. Which one ofthe following statements does not apply to the system of Subsidiary Alliance introduced by Lord Wellesley?

(a) To maintain a large standing army at other’s expense
(b) To keep India safe from Napoleonic danger
(c) To secure a fixed income for the company
(d) To establish British paramountcy over the Indian States
I.A.S. (Pre) 2018
Ans. (c)
Governor-General Lord Wellesley came to India in 1798 A.D. at a time when the British were locked in a life and death struggle with France all over the world. The fear of Napoleon haunted the Britishers everywhere. To achieve his political aims, Wellesley relied on the system of Subsidiary Alliance. While the practice of helping Indian rulers with a paid British force was quite old, it was given shape by Wellesley who used it to sub-ordinate the Indian states to the paramountcy ofthe company. Under the subsidiary Alliance system, the ruler ofthe allying state was compelled to accept the permanent stationing ofa British force within his territory and to pay a subsidy for its maintenance. However, to secure a fixed income for the company was not under the agenda of the Subsidiary Alliance.

22. First Maratha Sardar to accept the subsidiary alliance of Lord Wellesley was-

(a) Peshwa Bajirao- II
(b) Raghuji Bhosle
(c) Daulat Rao Sciandia
(d) None of the above
41st B.P.S.C. (Pre) 1996
Ans. (a)
The subsidiary alliance system was introduced in India by Lord Wellesley. The main principles ofa subsidiary alliance were –
1. An Indian ruler entering into a subsidiary alliance with the British had to accept British forces within his territory and also agree to pay for their maintenance.
2. The ruler would accept a British resident in his state.
3. An Indian ruler who entered into a subsidiary alliance would not enter into any further alliance with any other power.
4. The ruler would not employ any Europeans other than the British.
5. In case ofa conflict with any other state, he would agree to the resolution decided by the British.
6. The ruler would acknowledge East India Company as the paramount power in India.
7. In return for the ruler accepting its conditions, the company undertook the protection of the state from external dangers and disorders.
8. If the Indian ruler failed to make the payments required by the alliance, then part of its territory was to be taken away as a penalty.
The Nizam of Hyderabad was the first to enter into such an alliance. Maratha ruler Bajirao II (1802 A.D.) also accepted a subsidiary alliance. Several states like Hyderabad (1798 A.D.), Tanjore and Mysore (1799 A.D.), Awadh (1801 A.D.), Bhonsle (1803), Sindhiya (1804 A.D.), Holkar (1818 A.D.) etc. adopted this system.

The Vernacular Press Act or Native Press Act was passed in 1878 during the period of Lord Lytton. It is also known as ‘Gagging Act.’ lt provides the extensive government rights to censor report and editorial in Vernacular Press. The purpose ofthe Act was to control the printing and circulation of seditious material, specifically that which could produce disaffection against the British Government in India in the minds of the masses.
