CHAPTER 8: Greek Invasion

EXPLANATION SECTION
* In the 6th century BC, there was an ambitious man named Kurush or Cyrus-II (558-529 BC) who established the Ach aemenid Empire in central Iran. Behistun, Persepolis and Naqsh-e- Rostam inscriptions are from Darius I (522-486 BC) and later King's reign. These inscriptions provide important information about India-Persia relations. The 23 provinces in the empire of Darius-I have been mentioned in Behistun inscription.
* Herodotus described that India was the twentieth province of Darius's empire.
* Ctesias was the State Doctor (Rajya Vaidya) of Artaxerxes II. A new script named 'Kharo shti' originated out of the Iranian Aramaic script that evolved in Northwestern India as a result ofthe Persian relations. There were eight inheritors of Mahapadamananda found in Puranas and Buddhist texts.
* Dhanananda was the last ruler of this dynasty who was also the contemporary of Alexander. Greek writers referred him as Agrammese. Xenophon describes him as a wealthy person. Bhaddasala was his commander.
* After demolishing the Achaemenid Empire in 326 BC, Alexander started his Indian campaign with a huge army. After two years, he turned to Greek from Paatal in 325 BC.
* Ashvaka was a frontier republic at the time of the invasion ofAlexander, the capital of which was Massaga. According to Greek writers, the women had taken up arms against Alexander when a large number of men were killed in the war against him. In the descriptions, it is found that Alexander killed the women of this town.
* The realm of Puru (Porus) was located between Jhelum and Chenab River. Alexander defeated him on the bank of Jhelum river, but he was impressed by his courage and became his friend and returned his state and also gifted him some more areas.
