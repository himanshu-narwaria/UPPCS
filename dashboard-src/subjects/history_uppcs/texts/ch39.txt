CHAPTER 39: Maratha State and Confederacy

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Shivaji was born at the fort of Shivneri in 1627 or 1630 A.D. He adopted the title ofChhatrapati after his coronation in 1674 A.D. He made Raigarh his capital.
* Pandit Vishweshwar alias ‘Gagabhatta’, a great scholar of that era, declared Shivaji a Kshatriya at his coronation. Shivaji died in 1680 A.D. at the age of 53 or 50 years.
* The reason for the rise of Maratha power was its residents.
* The unity ofthe people on the grounds ofregional/collective, consciousness along with religious toleration and integration of social groups, flourished the feeling of nationalism among them. The geographical conditions ofMaharashtra were helpful in flourishing the Maratha power.
* The high leadership quality of Shivaji and other Maratha leaders contributed to the rise of Maratha power.
* In 1659 A.D., the Sultan of Bijapur Adil Shah II sent his experienced and trusted commander Afzal Khan for suppression of Shivaji, but the diplomat and clever Shivaji killed him.
* Treaty ofPurandar was signed between Shivaji and Mughals under Jai Singh in 1665 A.D. Shivaji was put under house arrest by Aurangzeb in 1666 in Jaipur Bhawan at Agra when he escaped from the custody of Mughals. Aurangzeb imprisoned Shivaji in 1666 A.D. at Agra.
* ‘Ashtapradhan’ was the Council ofEight Ministers (reaching 8 in no. by 1674) under Shivaji in Maratha administration. The eight ministers were –
(i) Peshwa - Prime Minister of the King.
(ii) Amatya - Finance and Revenue Minister.
(iii) Waqianavis/Mantri - Interior Minister, managing internal affairs, especially intelligence and espionage.
(iv) Sachiv/Surnavis - Responsible for all correspondence.
(v) Sumant/Dabir - Foreign Minister.
(vi) Senapati/Sar-i-Naubat - Maintenance of appointments, organization and food supply of military.
(vii) Pandit Rao - Appointed for religious aid.
(viii) Nyayadhish - Chief Justice.
* Rajaram led the Marathas as a representative of Shahu from 1689 A.D. to 1700 A.D. After the death of Rajaram, his widow Tarabai continued the struggle with the Mughals placing his four year old son Shivaji-II on the throne. At the time of Aurangzeb’s death, the Maratha leadership was in the hands of Tarabai.
* Under Shahu, the power of Maratha kingdom came in the hands of Balaji Vishwanath who made the administration easy and convenient.
* Maratha Chhatrapati Shahu appointed Balaji to the post of Peshwa. November 1713 was an important day not only for Balaji but also for the whole Marathas as the power gradually shifted from Chhatrapati to the Peshwa.
* Balaji Vishwanath served as Peshwa from 1713 A.D. to 1720 A.D.
* Shahu appointed the elder son of Balaji Vishwanath, Bajirao - I as Peshwa whose tenure was from 1720 A.D. to 1740 A.D.
* Balaji Bajirao served as Peshwa from 1740 A.D. to 1761 A.D. after the death of Bajirao. Madhavrao’s tenure was from 1761 A.D. to 1772 A.D.
* After the death of Madhavrao in 1772 A.D., his brother Narayanrao became a victim of the conspiracy of his uncle Raghunathrao, who himself wanted to become Peshwa.
* By the time of Balaji Bajirao, the Post of Peshwa became hereditary. Previously the power was in the hands of the Chhatrapati, but now the centre ofpower shifted into the hands of Peshwas. By the treaty of Sangola in 1750 A.D., Peshwa emerged as the real and effective head ofMaratha confederacy.
* Saranzami system was related to Feudal-military land grant system in Maratha period. The land given under Saranzami system was provided for the livelihood of Maratha Jagirdar (for maintenance of forts/troops for Public Service) during the Maratha period.
* In January, 1757 A.D., Ahmad Shah Abdali entered Delhi and looted up to Agra and Mathura. Before returning to his motherland, he confirmed Alamgir II as Emperor, Imadulmulk as prime minister and Rohilla leader Najib-Ud-Daulah as his chief agent and Mir Bakshi of the Empire.
* The third battle of Panipat was fought between Maratha Sadashivrao Bhau and Afghan invader Ahmad Shah Abdali on 14th January 1761. Marathas were badly defeated by Afghans in this battle. According to Kashiraj Pandit, a historian who was eyewitness of this battle 'Third battle of Panipat proved catastrophic for Marathas.' “Two pearls have been dissolved, 22 gold coins have been lost and of the silver and copper the total cannot be cast up” that was the message which was sent to Balaji Bajirao by a merchant after this agonising defeat of Marathas. The immediate reason for the battle of Panipat was the Ahmad Shah Abdali’s desire to avenge Marathas as they expelled his Viceroy Timur Shah from Lahore.
* Ahilyabai was a Maratha ruler from Holkar dynasty. Holkars belong to Indore and under Ahilyabai, the capital was moved to Maheshwar.
