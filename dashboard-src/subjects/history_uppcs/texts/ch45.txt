CHAPTER 45: Economic Impact of British Rule on India

EXPLANATION SECTION
* The Indian administration was in British hands and was conducted in a manner subserving the interests of England.
* Home charges refer to the expenditure incurred in England by the Secretary ofState for India on behalfofIndia. It includes dividends to the shareholders of the East India Company, interest on public debt raised abroad, Civil and Military charges, store purchases in England etc.
* Interest and profit on private foreign capital were another important leakage from the National Income System.
* For banking, insurance and shipping services India had to make huge payments.
* In British India, a term Imperial Preference was often used for the empire-wide preferential tariffarrangement (Concession given to British Imports in India). Under these arrangements, Britain pledged to maintain free entry for most colonial goods while imposing tariffs on foreign imports. This resulted in, almost no charge levied on British Imports but Indian Exports were charged heavily in Britain.
* Strong preference to invest in land property by the rich class of India during British Rule proved detrimental for the development of Industries as they were low on Capital Investments.
* In 1793 A.D. Cornwallis started Permanent Settlement of Land Revenue. Permanent Settlement was called by different names such as Istimrari, Malgujari and Bishwedari. This system was launched in Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Varanasi of U.P. etc. Around 19% of British India was under Permanent Settlement. A new section of Zamindars was announced as landowners who were to provide 10/11 of the total revenue to Company and were paid the rest 1/11 of the total revenue for their duties. In the Permanent Settlement, Zamindars were small capitalist.
* Ryotwari System was generated by Thomas Munro and Alexander Read. Alexander Read first implemented this land system in Baramahal district of Tamil Nadu. Later on, this system spread to Madras, some parts of Bombay, Assam and Coorg (a part of modern Karnataka) etc.
* According to this system, the Ryots were given the ownership and rights to occupy the lands and they were required to pay the revenue directly to the government. By this system, the farmer was the owner of his land. A total of around 51% British India area was covered under this settlement. The rate of land taxes in this system was very high and the recovery of agricultural rent was handled very strictly.
* Under Mahalwari System the unit for revenue settlement is the village or the mahal. The village land belongs to the village community. The community was jointly responsible for the payment of the land revenue.
* This system prevailed in around 30% ofBritish India covering United Provinces, Central Provinces and Punjab (with some changes).
* The constant flow of wealth from India to England for which India did not receive any adequate economic, commercial or material return has been described by national leaders and economists as ‘drain’ of wealth from India. Before 1813 AD company had a recurring surplus which accrued from (a) profits from oppressive land revenue policy (b) profits from its trade resulting from monopolistic control over Indian markets and
* (c) exactions made by the company’s officials. The surplus was used by the Company as an “investment”, i.e. for making a purchase ofexportable items in India and elsewhere. Against the exports of goods made out of this investment, India did not receive anything in return. This system was brought to an end by the charter act of 1813.
* From 1813 AD onwards economic drain took the form of ‘unrequited’ exports. Dadabhai Naoroji described the drain of wealth as the “evil of all evils” and the main cause of Indian poverty.
* Dadabhai Naoroji was one of the earliest and most prominent propounder of the Drain of Wealth theory. He with his articles and books ‘England’s Duties to India’ (paper), ‘Poverty and Un-British Rule in India’, ‘The wants and means of India’ and ‘On the commerce of India’ propounded his Drain of wealth theory.
* Romesh Chunder Dutt also emphasized the drain of wealth theory. In his Article ‘The Economic History of India’ He proposed that half of the net revenues of India is being outflowed every year.
* Indian National Congress in 1896 A.D. accepted the Drain Theory in Calcutta session and announced that the real cause for frequent famines and the poverty ofIndian nationals is the ongoing Drain ofWealth for the previous years.
* Syed Ahmad Khan was loyal towards British Rule and he believed that the development of Muslim Community could take place with British government only.
* Dadabhai Naoroji (1825-1917 A.D.) along with W.C. Bonnerjee established London Indian Society in 1865 A.D. which popularized the severe exploitation and the pain ofIndian nationals.
* Dadabhai Naoroji was the first Indian to be elected to the British House of Commons on the ticket of the Liberal Party in 1892 A.D.

30. With reference to revenue collection by Cornwallis, consider the following statements:

(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2
