CHAPTER 73: Gandhi-Irwin Pact

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Viceroy Irwin released Gandhiji from jail on 26th January 1931 AD considering the increasing influence of the Civil Disobedience Movement. Talks between Gandhiji and Irwin started in February 1931 AD with the efforts of Tej Bahadur Sapru and M.R. Jaykar. Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed on 5th March, 1931. Sarojini Naidu termed Irwin and Gandhiji as ‘Two Mahatmas’. According to this pact -
(1) Under the leadership of Gandhiji, Congress decided to withdraw the Civil Disobedience Movement,
(2) All political prisoners who had not convicted of violence were to be released,
(3) Right to peaceful protest at shops offoreign cloth and liquor shops,
(4) Permission to make salt in the Coastal States without Salt Tax,
(5) Congress agreed to participate in the Second Round Table Conference.
People were disappointed by this pact because it did not include the commulation of the death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru. Biographer of Irwin, Alan Campbell Johnson termed the benefits to Gandhi in Gandhi-Irwin Pact as consolation prizes and said this pact was the only surrender of Irwin agreeing to enter into negotiations.

1. In which year did the famous Gandhi-Irwin Pact take place?

(a) 1929 A.D.
(b) 1930 A.D.
(c) 1931 A.D.
(d) 1932 A.D.
(e) None of the above/More than one of the above
67th B.P.S.C. (Pre) (Re. Exam) 2022
Ans. (c)
The Gandhi-Irwin Pact was a political agreement signed by Mahatma Gandhi and the then Viceroy ofIndia, Lord Irwin on 5th March, 1931 before the Second Round Table Conference in London. Below are the proposed conditions –
Discontinuation ofCivil Disobedience Movement by Indian National Congress.
Participation of the Indian National Congress in Second Round Table Conference.
Withdrawal of all ordinances issued by British Government imposing curbs on the activities of INC.
Withdrawal of all prosecutions except those involving violence.
The release ofprisoners arrested for participating in the Civil Disobedience Movement.
