CHAPTER 59: Morley-Minto Reforms

EXPLANATION SECTION
* In 1905, Lord Curzon was replaced by Lord Minto as Viceroy of India and John Morley was secretary of state for India then. Morley-Minto Reforms are related to the reforms initiated by these two. These constitutional reforms were passed as an act by British Parliament and officially they were called Indian Councils Acts, 1909. It is generally known as Morley-Minto Reforms. The biggest blunder of this reform was the separate electorate given to Muslim community under the Communal representation system. According to this system, the Muslim members ofthe council were not elected by general electorate but were elected by the Muslim voters only. This system accepted Muslims as a separate community in India. This created many fundamental issues in India. The unity of India was attacked and demolished in just a stroke. Gandhiji said - " The Morley – Minto Reforms have been our undoing. Had it not been for the separate electorates then established then we should have settled our differences by now."

1. Which of the following events took place in the year 1911?
1. Partition of Bengal revoked
2. Transfer of capital from Calcutta to Delhi
3. Lucknow pact between Congress and Muslim League
Select the correct answer from the code given below:
Code:
(a) 1 and 2
(b) Only 3
(c) 2 and 3
(d) Only 1
U.P. P.C.S (Pre) 2025
Ans. (a)
Annulment ofthe Partition ofBengal (1911): The Partition ofBengal done in 1905 was announced to be annulled at the Delhi Durbar on 12 December 1911. Administrative reorganisation (e.g., formation of'Bihar and Orissa') became effective in 1912, but the announcement of annulment was made in 1911; therefore, this event is associated with 1911. Transfer of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi (1911): In the same Delhi Durbar (12 December 1911), the decision to shift India’s capital from Calcutta to Delhi was announced. The formal and gradual transfer of offices was completed in 1912; however, the decision/announcement was made in 1911.
Lucknow Pact (1916): The Lucknow Pact between the Congress and the Muslim League took place in 1916. Hence, the intended answer is option (a).

(a) 1905
(b) 1909
(c) 1911
(d) 1920
U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1994
Ans. (b)
In 1905, Lord Minto was appointed as Viceroy of India in place of Lord Curzon and John Morley was appointed as Secretary ofState for India. The reforms introduced by them are known as Morley-Minto Reforms. The Indian Councils Act, 1909, commonly known as the Morley-Minto Reforms was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that brought about a limited increase in the involvement ofIndians in the governance ofBritish India. The major dispute regarding this act was separate electorate for Muslims in India.

3. The Indian Council Act of 1909 was provided for:

(a) Dyarchy
(b) Communal representation
(c) Federation
(d) Provincial autonomy
U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1996
Ans. (b)
The main fault ofthe Indian CouncilAct, 1909 (Morley-Minto) was a system of communal representation for Muslims by accepting the concept of “separate electorate.” According to this system, Muslim members were to be elected only by Muslim electorates, not by general electorates in Councils. Indeed it meant that the Muslim community was considered as an absolutely separate class from India. Antiquated National integrity vanished in only one storm. Gandhi Stated:- "The Morley – Minto Reforms have been our undoing. Had it not been for the separate electorates then established then we should have settled our differences by now."

4. Seeds of discord were in which event during National Movement and which eventually divided the country, was–

(a) Establishment of Muslim League in 1906.
(b) Division of Bengal in 1905.
(c) Khilafat Movement Supported by Gandhiji.
(d) Reservation of seats and separate electorates for Muslims in legislative assemblies.
U.P P.C.S. (Spl) (Pre) 2004; U.P U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2001
Ans. (d)
The Minto-Morley reforms are known to envisage a separate electorate for Muslims, and this had a long lasting impact on Indian polity. This was for the first time that Muslim community was recognized as a completely separate section of the Indian Nation and this triggered “A Cancer” in India called “Hindu-Muslim Disharmony” which later culminated in the Partition of India and Pakistan. Separate constituencies were marked for the Muslims, and only Muslim community members were given the right to elect their representatives. The principle of communal representation which was accepted under the Morley-Minto Reforms was retained and was pushed further by the Government of India Act, 1919.
