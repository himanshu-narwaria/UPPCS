CHAPTER 85: Quit India Movement

EXPLANATION SECTION
* During 5-14 July, 1942 A.D., a meeting of Congress Committee was held at Wardha. It was attended by prominent leaders ofCongress like Jawahar Lal Nehru, Abul Kalam Azad, Vallabh Bhai Patel, Mahatma Gandhi, Aruna AsafAli, Achyut Patwardhan, Dr. Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Pattabhi Sitaramayya etc.
* ‘Quit India’ resolution was passed in this meeting on July 14, 1942. It was chaired by then Congress President Abul Kalam Azad.
* On 7th -8th August, 1942 A.D., meeting of All India Congress Committee was held at Gowalia Tank (now August Kranti ground) in Bombay. Wardha Resolution was confirmed in this meeting.
* After a few modifications, the resolution for Quit India movement was passed on 8th August, 1942 A.D. Quit India Movement for India’s Independence was launched on 9th August 1942. Gandhiji gave a statement before the official launch of Quit India Movement. The statement had the following important points.
1) Government officials should announce their support and devotion to Congress.
2) Soldiers should refuse to fire on their countrymen.
3) Students should quit studies only ifthey stood firmly on this decision until independence.
4) Kings should accept the sovereignty of their people and declare their province as a part ofthe Indian nation. Masses should accept the leadership of the king only if he sees his future in the future of Masses.
* In his speech Gandhiji said :- "I am not going to be satisfied with anything short than complete freedom". It may be possible that Salt Tax and drink evil, etc. may be abolished or stopped by the English Government but my words will demand 'Nothing less than complete Freedom'. Today I give you a mantra “Do or Die”. This mantra means that either we will free our country or will die fighting for Independence.
* On 8th August, 1942 A.D., Jawahar Lal Nehru presented the ‘Quit India resolution’ to All India Congress Committee. This proposal was supported by Sardar Vallabh Bhai Patel. The draft of this proposal was prepared by Mahatma Gandhi himself with the help of Jawahar Lal.
* Hindu Mahasabha, Communist Party of India, Unionist Party (Punjab) and Muslim League were not in favour of this movement. On 9th August, 1942 A.D., Quit India Movement was launched. In the early hours ofthe Movement, Gandhiji and other important leaders of Congress were arrested under Operation Zero Hour.
* Gandhiji, Sarojini Naidu and other leaders were arrested and taken to Aga Khan Palace.
* Members of Congress working Committee (Jawaharlal Nehru, Abul Kalam Azad, Govind Ballabh Pant, Dr. Prafful Chandra Ghosh, Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya, Dr. Syed Mahmud, Acharya Kriplani) were arrested and taken to Ahmednagar Fort.
* Dr. Rajendra Prasad, member of Congress Working Committee couldn't come to Bombay, he was arrested and kept at Patna (Bankipur) Jail. Shivkumar and Ram-nandan Mishra (he was first arrested in Cuttack but later he was transferred to Hzaribagh). were arrested in Hazaribagh.
* Movement of 1942 showed maximum impact in Bombay, Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, United Province of Agra and Oudh and Madras but the participation of the whole country was necessary for this movement to succeed.
* Lord Wavell was the commander in chief of India during Quit India Movement (1942 A.D.). He served as Viceroy from the year 1943 A.D. to 1947 A.D.
* Winston Churchill was the Prime Minister of England during Quit India Movement.
* Abul Kalam Azad was the President of Congress when the resolution of Quit India Movement was passed. He was elected President in Ramgarh Session of Congress in 1940 A.D. and after that for five years (1941-45 A.D.) no session ofCongress was held and Abul Kalam Azad continued to be the President of Congress for these years. He was the President of Congress for the maximum years before independence, also the political scenario of India was very fragile during his tenure.
* During Quit India Movement, a secret radio station of Congress was broadcasted from different parts ofBombay Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia played a significant role in the Quit India Movement. He directed the movement while remaining underground. He also established an undergraound radio station.
* Usha Mehta was an important member of the group of Congress that broadcasted radio being underground.
* Louis Fischer, an Americanjournalist, was with Gandhiji just before Quit India Movement as he came to India in May 1942 and spent one week with Gandhiji at Sevagram Ashram, Wardha. (He was biographer of Gandhiji).
* Louis Fischer, with American intellectuals Pearl Buck, Edgar Snow, Fredrick L. Schuman and Norman Thomas demanded India’s Independence.
* Riots emerging out of Quit India Movement were widespread in Bihar and United Provinces It emerged as a revolt. Eastern United Provinces (including Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur) and Gaya, Champaran, Saran, Purnia, Shahabad, Muzaffarpur, and Bhagalpur areas of Bihar were self instilled centres of mass revolt.
* August Movement of 1942 A.D. became widespread among the farmers. Some groups of farmers performed agricultural activities in the day and took part in rioting activities at night. Their fierceness and strength demonstrated the glimpses ofthe revolt of1857, that is why Viceroy Linlithgow in a telegram to British Prime Minister Winston Churchill termed this movement as by far the most serious rebellion after the Revolt of 1857.
* During the Quit India Movement, Jayaprakash Narayan fled from Hazaribagh jail and became active in the underground activities. Yogendra Shukla too fled with J.P. Narayan on 9th November, 1942 A.D. but was later arrested in Muzaffarpur and sent to Patna.
* On the speech given by Gandhiji during Quit India Movement (1942 A.D.), Pattabhi Sitaramayya said, “In reality that day Gandhiji was speaking after being motivated with the power ofAvatar and Paigambar.
* Rashtriya Swayam Sevak Sangh didn’t participate in Quit India Movement. It didn’t participate in any movement between 1940 A.D. and 1947 A.D. till foreign rule ended.
* Aruna AsafAli was a famous freedom fighter. She will always be remembered for hoisting the tricolour Flag at Gowalia Tank ground (Bombay) during Quit India Movement.
* ArunaAsafAli, Usha Mehta, J.P. Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, etc being underground provided alternate leadership to the Quit India Movement when all other prominent leaders were arrested.
* Establishment ofParallel Government at several places was an important feature of Quit India Movement. These were:-
1. Ballia - August 1942 A.D. - Chittu Pandey led the Government.
2. Tamluk (Midnapore) Bengal - From December 1942 to August 1944 AD the Tamralipta Jatiya Sarkar in Tamluk started the relief programmes for the persons affected by storms.
3. Satara (Maharashtra) - A Parallel Government named Prati Sarkar was formed in August-1943 A.D. It was the longest-serving Parallel Government till May 1946 A.D. Its main leaders were - Y.B. Chavan and Nana Patil. There was a Parallel Government in Talchar, Orissa too for some time.
* Gandhiji was released on 6th May, 1944 A.D. on account of bad health conditions. On Gandhiji's release, Winston Churchill remarked, “When we are winning around the globe how can we be defeated by this old man who is our enemy for a long time.”

24. Which one of the following observations is not true about the Quit India Movement of 1942?

(a) It was a non-violent Movement.
(b) It was led by Mahatma Gandhi.
(c) It was a spontaneous Movements.
(d) It did not attract the labour class in general.
I.A.S. (Pre) 2011
Ans. (*)
Quit India Movement of 1942 A.D. was not so non-violent. According to official assessments, in first week 250 railway stations were either damaged or destroyed, and more than 500 post offices and 150 police stations were attacked. Heavy violence was used for the suppression ofthe movement from the ruling party. Mahatma Gandhi himself in 1942 A.D. refused to condemn the violence. He said it was an answer to the huge violence of the rulers. The arrest of the main leaders (including Gandhiji) before even start of movement had made the movement more aggressive and spontaneous. The movement did not attract the labour class in general (due to reasons like monetary constraints, fear ofrepression, effect of trade unions etc.) in comparison to other sections of society like students, farmers etc.
Thus, options (a) and (b) are individually not correct.
