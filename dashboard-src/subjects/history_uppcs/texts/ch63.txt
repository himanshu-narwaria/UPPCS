CHAPTER 63: Gandhi and His Early Movements

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi was born on 2nd October, 1869 AD at Porbandar, Gujarat. Gandhiji's education started in a Pathshala in Porbandar but at the age of seven he moved to Rajkot due to his father's transfer and he studied in Rajkot till high school. Mahatma Gandhi was married to Kasturba Gandhi at the age of 13. He took training as Barrister from ‘Inner Temple’, London. Karamchand Gandhi was Diwan of Porbandar, Wankaner and Rajkot. He is also known as ‘Kaba Gandhi’.
* Gandhi, in 1894 A.D., founded Natal Indian Congress in South Africa.
* He along with his colleagues established a new colony named as Tolstoy Farm in 1910 and started living there.
* He started a newspaper there named Indian Opinion (Published in English, Gujarati, Hindi and Tamil). The first issue of this newspaper was released in June 1903.
* He established an Ashram at Phoenix (Durban, South Africa) in 1904 A.D. It was reopened on 27th February, 2000 A.D. Phoenix Ashram was the first Ashram established by Gandhiji.
* In his thoughts Gandhiji emphasized more on practical idealism than idealism. Like Marx he also believed in the removal of State and had no problem on being called as Philosophical Anarchist.
* Truth and Non-Violence were the two principles of Ram Rajya of Gandhiji. He made these two as the base of Modern society of his dreams. He was a true socialist. He took care of both private and social interests. He wanted to bring functionality to the noble ideals of Social Justice. Gandhiji himself said to Louis Fischer, “I am a true socialist and the aim of my socialism is progress of all”. Socialism ofGandhiji has some glimpses ofMarxism. Gandhiji gave extraordinary importance to labours.
* He wanted to bring into action the principle that everyone is given work according to their capacity and everyone to be paid remuneration according to their needs.
* Gandhiji said, “I am engaged in solving the same problem that faces scientific socialists. It is true, however that my approach is always and only through unalterated non-violence”. Gandhiji is called an Individualist and Marxist among the socialists.
* Strike was the last option in the Satyagraha strategy whereas fasting was his first weapon. According to Gandhiji Satyagraha was ‘Agni-Baan’. The objective of Satyagraha of Gandhiji was to change the heart of enemy according to the demand of protestor instead of defeating the enemy. This was to be done by the suffering that Satyagrahi took upon himself. Gandhiji termed self- control as the best solution for Family Planning.
* Mahatma Gandhi used to say that “Ahimsa and Truth are so intertwined that it is practically impossible to disentangle and separate them. They are like the two sides of a coin, or rather of a smooth unstamped metallic disc. Who can say, which is the obverse, and which is the reverse? Nevertheless, Ahimsa is the means; Truth is the end. Means to be means must always be within our reach, and so Ahimsa is our supreme duty. If we take care of the means, we are bound to reach the end sooner or later. When once we have grasped this point, final victory is beyond question. Whatever difficulties we encounter, whatever apparent reverses we sustain, we may not give up the quest for Truth which alone is, being God Himself”. Mahatma Gandhi was convinced that if we take care of our means, the end will be taken care of itself.
* In 1893 (24 May), at the age of23, Gandhiji went to Durban (South Africa) to fight a case ofGujarati trader Dada Abdullah. He stayed in Africa for 21 years and returned to India in 1915 A.D. The public welcomed him with enthusiasm, his struggle and victories in South Africa made him popular here too.
* After coming to India, he met Gopal Krishna Gokhale. He was his political mentor. Gandhiji, for the first time, participated in a Congress session at Calcutta in 1901 A.D. In this session resolution of Gandhiji on Africa was passed. This session was chaired by Dinshaw Wacha. During his stay in South Africa, Gandhiji came to India in 1901 A.D. and established his office of law at Bombay in 1902 A.D. Gandhiji, in 1915 A.D., established Satyagraha Ashram at Ahmedabad. The Ashram was moved to the banks of Sabarmati River on 17th June, 1917 A.D. considering various economic activities.
* Gandhiji believed that active politics was an important tool for social upliftment and public welfare. In this context the decentralization of power was the most important suggestion ofGandhiji. With the decentralization ofpower Gandhiji meant the power to Gram Panchayats to manage and administer their villages. The political system shown by Gandhiji was the inclusion of Morality, Religion and Humanity but the complete ideal political system of Gandhiji had no place for State power. He wanted the establishment of stateless society. Gandhiji named the ongoing struggle in Africa as passive resistance, but later on, it was replaced by Satyagraha. It was widely used in India’s struggle for Independence. But both the terms were not the same for Gandhiji. According to him, passive resistance is a political weapon whereas Satyagraha is moral power.
* During India’s freedom struggle, Gandhiji, in 1917 A.D., started Champaran Satyagraha at Bihar. This was his first successful Satyagraha in India.
* Gandhiji linked the boycott of foreign clothes with Nation’s Respect. Boycott of foreign clothes by Gandhiji’s Movement was effective as India was believed to be the market for foreign manufactured goods. With Gandhiji’s formula ofBoycott offoreign goods the production and sale ofIndian goods were promoted.
* Gandhiji read the book ‘Unto This Last’ written by John Ruskin during his stay in Africa. Gandhiji wrote in his biography that this book changed his life. According to Gandhiji, the Message of this book was - “The good of the individual is contained in the good of all”. On this basis, Gandhiji rendered his ideology of the welfare of all. Among the western thinkers ‘Thoreau, Ruskin and Tolstoy’ highly influenced the Gandhian ideology. From ‘Thoreau’ he learnt civil disobedience and benefits of refusal to pay takes. Ruskin taught him to respect the physical labour. Gandhiji was highly influenced by the statement of Tolstoy- "The Kingdom of God is within you" and this became the base of his non-violence and non-cooperation.
* Gandhiji worked very hard for the improvement in the conditions of untouchables in India. Gandhiji said, “Service of Harijan is the breath of my life without which I won’t survive one single minute”. Champaran and Kheda were Farmers’ Movement. Champaran movement in Bihar was the first Satyagraha of Gandhiji in India. In the beginning of 19th century white owners of plantations made an obligation with the farmer to cultivate Indigo on 3/20th part of their land. This was called as Teen Kathiya System. Plantation owners imposed Tawan (a lump-sum compensation or illegal levy charges) and other charges/cesses to exploit farmers and make it impossible to get out of the contract. Against this exploitation of the farmers Gandhiji started Champaran Satyagraha. To investigate the matter, the Government constituted a committee and Gandhiji was also one of the members. Plantation owners agreed to return the 25% of illegally collected money. In this way the first Satyagraha of Gandhiji was successful.
* Pt. Rajkumar Shukla persuaded Gandhiji to look into the matters of the exploitation of farmers in Champaran. The people that accompanied Gandhiji to Champaran for investigation included - Acharya J.B. Kriplani, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Mahadev Desai, C.F. Andrews, Dr. Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Braj Kishor Prasad, etc. N.G. Ranga opposed Gandhiji’s support to Champaran movement, whereas Rabindranath Tagore gave him the title of 'Mahatma'.
* After Champaran Movment, the next experiment of Gandhiji was the dispute between cotton mill owners and labours of Ahmedabad on the issue of plague bonus. Gandhiji convinced workers and owners to hand over this issue to a tribunal. To encourage and motivate the labourers, he himself sat on fasting. This protest showed some result and the owners handed over all these matters to the tribunal. Later on tribunal pronounced a 35% increase in dearness allowance (wage increment).
* In 1918 A.D., at Kheda, after the failure of crops. The Government didn’t give any concession in revenue, nor did they stopped the collection ofland revenue. On this matter, Gandhiji constituted a Satyagraha in favour offarmers.
* Subhash Chandra Bose, for the first time called Gandhiji as the Father of Nation. In July, 1944, speaking on Azad Hind radio, Subhash Chandra Bose addressed Gandhiji and said, “India's last war ofindependence has begun. Father of Nation we need your blessings and good wishes on this expedition.”
* Pyarelal Nayyar was the secretary of Gandhiji (since 1942) during Noakhali communal riots. His sister Sushila Nayyar was the personal Doctor of Gandhiji. Pyarelal played an important role in Gandhiji’s Dandi March. Jamnalal Bajaj (Cloth trader, Banker, Congress Social Worker) was a close associate ofMahatama Gandhi. Jamnalal Bajaj was born at Sikar District (Rajasthan). He was adopted by a rich businessman ofWardha. He got in touch with Gandhiji in 1915 A.D. and remained his follower for life. He took part in the Indian National Congress at the age of 30. He renounced the title of Rai Bahadur protesting the policies of British towards India. He was treasurer of Congress and founder of Gandhi Seva Sangh. He took a deep interest in village industry and handloom clothes. He was jailed in 1923 A.D. for taking part in Nagpur Flag Satyagraha and again in 1930 A.D. during Civil Disobedience Movement.
* Charles Andrews (Dinbandhu Andrews) began teaching at Saint Stephen's College, Delhi. He was deeply attached with Indians and wanted to be an Indian in all forms. He had strong relations with Rabindranath Tagore, Gopal Krishna Gokhale and Mahatma Gandhi.
* He stayed too at Phoenix Settlements (Ashram) with Gandhiji. Gandhiji, after seeing him completely devoted towards upliftments of poor, gave him the title of Dinbandhu.
* Andrews was the president of All India Trade Union Congress in 1925 A.D. and 1927 A.D. He accompanied Gandhiji when he went to London to take part in Round Table Conference.
* Gandhiji was not a member of Congress during India’s Independence. In the year 1934 A.D., he resigned from Congress. Afterwards, he never became a member ofCongress officially but his ideologies played a very significant role in the then political scenario.
* In 1903, Gandhiji founded a law firm in Johannesburg and stayed there till 1910 (before transit to Tolstoy farm). Gandhiji applied the principles ofTrusteeship. Gandhiji believed that the economy of each country should be determined after keeping into consideration the climate, land, nature of residents of that country. This economics is totally opposed to exploitation, whether done by one group to the other group of a country or exploitation ofa country by another country. Gandhiji wanted to establish such a system in which a person with his fundamental efforts in a free environment can choose to work and perform labour. For this, he wanted the revival ofIndian goods industries so that there is enough food to feed all and no one suffers from hunger. Gandhiji emphasized on the importance of cottage industries like - making jaggery, thrashing grass, extracting oil, paper making, leather works etc. with this Gandhiji believed that the poor and exploited will have Social and Economic improvement. On 30th January, 1948, Nathuram Godse shot Gandhiji. On his death, Nehruji said, “The light has gone out of our lives and there is darkness everywhere. I do not know what to tell you and how to say it. Our beloved leader, Bapu as we called him, the Father of The Nation, is no more."

16. In which of the following railway stations in South Africa, Mahatma Gandhi was thrown out of the train?

(c) Durban
(d) Pretoria
U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2015
Ans. (b)
Gandhi arrived in Durban, SouthAfrica in 1893 to serve as legal counsel to the businessman Dada Abdullah. In June, he was asked by Dada Abdullah to undertake a trip to Pretoria in the Transvaal, ajourney which first took Gandhi to Pietermaritzburg. There Gandhi was seated in the first-class compartment since he had purchased a first-class ticket. A European who entered the compartment hastened to summon railway officials, who ordered Gandhi to shift himselfto the van compartment since ‘coolies’ and non-whites were apparently not permitted in first-class compartments. As Gandhi refused to comply with the order, he was summarily pushed out ofthe train, and his luggage was tossed out on to the platform.
