CHAPTER 50: Socio-Religious Movements

EXPLANATION SECTION
* The dawn of the nineteenth century witnessed the birth of a new vision, a modern vision, among some enlightened sections of the Indian society. This enlightened vision was to shape the course of events for decades to come and even beyond. This process of reawakening, sometimes but not with full justification defined as ‘Renaissance’, did not always follow the intended line and gave rise to some undesirable by-products as well, which have become as much a part of daily existence in the whole of the Indian subcontinent as the fruits of these reform movements.
* Raja Ram Mohan Roy often called the Father of Indian Renaissance is also known by names like ‘Prophet of Indian Nationalism’, Father of Modern India, ‘First Modern Man’ and ‘Yugdoot’. In 1815 A.D. he set up the Atmiya Sabha in Calcutta to propagate Monotheistic ideals of Hindu religion among his youth followers (mostly rich and influential). In 1828 A.D. he founded Brahmo Sabha, which was later renamed Brahmo Samaj.
* Devendranath Tagore founded Tattvabodhini Sabha to propagate rational thinking, study ancient texts (like Upanishad) and spread the ideals ofRajaram Mohan Roy in 1839 A.D. Brahmo Samaj was the first reform movement in the Hindu religion which was influenced by modern western liberal ideas and a new reading of ancient Indian texts.
* Mughal Emperor Akbar II gave Raja Ram Mohan Roy the title of Raja and sent him to the court of British King William IV as his ambassador in 1830. Roy was sent there to discuss about the increment in the pension of Akbar II. On 27th September, 1833 Raja Ram Mohan Roy died at Bristol in England. The mausoleum of Roy is present at Bristol.
* Raja Ram Mohan Roy advocated English Education in India. According to him, a moderate Western education is necessary to pull us out of the darkness and to provide Indians with partnership in the administration of the country. He opposed idol worship, Child marriage, practice of Sati etc. evil social practices of India.
* Ramakrishna Paramahamsa emphasized that Hari, Ram, Allah, and Christ are different names for the same God and that there are many ways to God and salvation.
* Swami Vivekananda spread Ramakrishna’s message and tried to reconcile it to needs of contemporary Indian Society. He emerged as the preacher of Neo-Hinduism. At the Parliament of the World's Religions held at Chicago in 1893, Swami Vivekananda delivered his famous speech. Subhash Chandra Bose said, “So far as Bengal is concerned Vivekananda may be regarded as the spiritual father of Modern nationalist movement.” Swami Vivekananda founded Ram Krishna Mission in 1897 A.D. (it was registered officially in 1909 A.D. under Societies Registration Act 1860). Its headquarter was opened at Belur in Howrah (West Bengal). The objective of Ram Krishna Mission was non-political, non-sectarian spiritual movement, engaged in various forms ofhumanitarian, social and cultural service activity, but it was highly inspired by the Ancient culture of India. The Theory of Pure Vedanta is the ideal of this mission. Its main aim is the highest spiritual upliftment of Human’s inner self. It also accepted traditional practices of Hindu religion in their original form such as idol worship, rituals, symbols, etc. The second feature ofthe mission is: ‘Belief in the truth of all religions’. Swami Vivekananda said that all religious beliefs are different ways to reach the same destination that is one God.
* Mulshankar or Dayanand Saraswati was born in the old Morvi Princely State, Kathiyawara in Gujarat in a Brahmin family. From 1845-60 (for fifteen years) he wandered to different places in search of knowledge. In 1860 he came under swami Virajananda Saraswati at Mathura and started preaching in 1864. In November, 1869, he engaged in a mighty Shastrartha with leaders ofHindu orthodoxy at Kashi. In 1867 A.D., he hoisted “Pakhand Khandini Pataka”. In 1875 A.D., he founded ‘Arya Samaj’ in Bombay. His views are inscribed in his famous book Satyartha Prakash. Rigvedadibhashya bhumika, Rigveda Bhashya, Advaitmat Khandan, Panch Mahayajya Vidhi and Ved-Viruddh-mat-Khandanam were important works of Dayanand Saraswati. The Arya Samaj was spreading rapidly as a reaction against the western influences. In 1877 A.D., its headquarter was shifted to Lahore which helped in accelerating the spread of this movement. Dayananda’s slogan of “Go Back to Vedas” was a call for a revival of Vedic learning and purity of religion. He rejected the religious practices like idol worship, incarnations, tantra-mantra, etc. His aim was the oneness of all religions, society, and of the nation itself. He is also called as “The (Martin) Luther of India” for his efforts in religious reforms. Arya Samaj got divided into two groups, one supported western education and other one opposed western education. Swami Sraddhanand (Mahatma Munshi Ram Vij), Lekhraj etc. were opposed to western education. They established ‘Gurukul’ in 1902. Lala Lajpat Rai and Hansraj were supporters of western education. They established Dayanand Anglo Vedic College. Dayanand Saraswati was the first to use word Swaraj and advocated Hindi as the official language of India. He was the first to suggest renunciation of foreign goods and the adoption of country-made indigenous goods.
* After being inspired by Keshab Chandra Sen, Atmaram Pandurang founded Prarthana Samaj in 1867 A.D.
* Mahadev Govind Ranade in 1869 A.D. joined this organization. The main aim of this organization was to oppose the caste system, increment in the age of men and women for marriage, promotion of widow remarriage and women education. Ranade is also called as “Precursor of cultural Renaissance of Western India”.
* Shiv Narayan Agnihotri at Lahore in 1887 A.D. founded ‘Dev Samaj’. The Preaching of this Samaj is mentioned in a book, Dev Shastra.
* Jyotirao Phule in 1873 A.D. founded Satya Shodhak Samaj. He was born to a gardener family in 1827 A.D. With his book Gulamgiri and his Samaj, he criticised the opportunistic Vedas of the Brahmans and called for the need for protection of lower castes from exploitation.
* Radhakant Deb opposed Socio-Religious reforms and supported orthodox elements by founding Dharma Sabha in 1830 A.D. at Culcutta (Bengal).
* Radhasoami (Radhaswami) Satsang movement began in Agra in 1861. The first and founding member of this movement was Shiv Dayal Singh (Soamiji) or Swamiji Maharaj originally he was ‘Persian Language translator’ later joined his father’s moneylending business. His followers believed in one god, the greatness of teacher, Satsang and ordinary social life.
* Social reformer of Maharashtra Gopal Hari Deshmukh (1823-92 A.D.) was famous as Lokahitwadi. He was also a member of Governor-General Council in 1880.
* Vishnu Shastri Pandit was a social reformer and advocate of widow remarriage during 19th century. (specifically in Maharashtra). In 1850’she founded ‘Punarvivahottejak Mandali to promote the practice of widow remarriages. Vishnu Shastri published a Marathi translation of Vidyasagar’s book in Indu Prakash in 1868.
* Behramji M. Malabari, a Parsi social reformer, was born in 1853 A.D. at Baroda. The Age of Consent Act, 1891 was passed through his efforts.
* M.G. Ranade and Raghunath Rao in 1887 A.D. founded Indian National Social Conference. The main aim ofthe Conference was to abolish social evils like Polygamy, Child marriage, and Patriarchy.
* The widow remarriage got legal backing By Hindu Widows Remarriage Act of 1856. The Bengal Sati Regulation, 1829 was passed by then Governor General Lord William Bentinck, making the practice of Sati illegal.
* Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, principal of Sanskrit College, struggled a lot for the Widow Remarriage. He tried to prove the affiliation of widow remarriage through spiritual sactions by Hindu Shastras/Dharmashastras. By his efforts, The Hindu Widows Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856) was passed on 25 July, 1856.
* Raja Ram Mohan Roy opposed Sati Practice strongly. Sati Practice was abolished (by Regulation XVII (1829) passed by the Governor-General in Council) by his efforts in 1829 A.D. Slavery became illegal in 1843 A.D. by an Act passed by Lord Ellenborough.
* Keshab Chandra Sen in 1872 played an important role in the passing of Native Marriage Act (Act III of 1872 commonly referenced as the Special Marriage Act, 1872) according to which the minimum age of girls and boys for marriage was fixed at 14 years and 18 years respectively. This is also called as Brahmo Marriage Act of1872. The Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929 was enacted through the efforts of Indian Judge Dr. Har Bilas Sharda. On his name, it was called Sharda Act. By this Act, the minimum legal age of girls for marriage was 14 years and for boys, it was 18 years. This act was amended in 1978 and later repealed substituted by ‘Prohibition of Child Marriage Act’, 2006. At present marriage age is 18 years for girls and 21 years for boys.
* Theosophical Society was founded by Madame H.P. Blavatsky, Colonel H.S. Olcott (American army officer) and others in 1875 A.D. It was founded in New York. They shifted their Headquarters from New York to Adyar in 1882 on the outskirts of Madras which later on became its International Office. In 1889 A.D. Annie Besant became a member of the Society and in 1893 A.D. she arrived in India and played the most active role for the Society. She believed Hinduism as the most spiritual and esoteric religion.
* Gopal Krishna Gokhale, in 1905, founded Servants of India Society. He chaired the 21st Session of Congress in 1905 A.D. He was the political guru of Gandhi.
* In 1910 A.D., Mukund Rao Patil and Shankar Rao Jadhav in the influence of Jyotiba Phule founded Bahujan Samaj.
* The demand of rights to enter the temple by Nadars led to several riots in Tirunelvelli. In a conference organized on the abolition of untouchability, Tilak said, “If God tolerates the untouchability then I do not believe in God”. Tilak is often called as Lokmanya or Betaj Badshah.
* The founding members of Darul Uloom Deoband, was Muhammad Qasim Nanautawi, Haji Syed Muhammad Abid Deobandi, Mahtab Ali Deobandi, Zulfiqar Ali Deobandi, Fazlur Rahman Usmani Deobandi, Haji Fazle Haq Deobandi, Shaikh Nihal Deobandi and others; and the foundation was laid on 31 May 1866.

22. The founder of ‘Bhartiya Brahmo Samaj’ was:

(a) Devendranath Tagore
(b) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
(c) Keshab Chandra Sen
(d) Raja Ram Mohan Roy
U.P. U.D.A./L.D.A. (Spl) (Pre) 2010; U.P. U.D.A./L.D.A. (Spl) (Mains) 2010; U.P. P.C.S. (Spl) (Mains) 2008
Ans. (c)
The founder of ‘Brahmo Samaj of India’ (Bhartiya Brahmo Samaj) was Keshab Chandra Sen. Devendra Nath stripped the title of ‘Acharya’ from Keshab Chandra in 1865. Hence Keshav Chandra Sen came out from original Brahmo Samaj and group under the influence of Devendra Nath Tagore called themselves as ‘Adi Brahmo Samaj.’ The group under the leadership of Keshab Chandra Sen called themselves as ‘Bhartiya Brahmo Samaj’ or ‘Neo Brahmo Samaj.’ Again in 1878 A.D., ‘ Navin Brahmo Samaj’ was further divided when Keshab Chandra Sen got married his under 14 year old daughter with the king of Coonch Behar. To oppose his action, Anand Mohan Bose and Shivnath Shastri formed ‘Sadharan Brahmo Samaj.’
Note: On 20 August, 1828 A.D. Raja Rammohan Roy rented a house from Feringhee Kamal Bose, where he formed Brahmo Samaj. Tarachand Chakroborty was its first secretary. Devendra Nath Tagore joined this society in 1843 and Keshab Chandra Sen in 1857 A.D. Due to the difference of opinion between Devendra Nath and Keshav Chandra, on 11 November, 1866, Keshav Chandra formally formed ‘Bhartiya Brahmo Samaj’whereas earlier established Brahmo Samaj was known as ‘Adi Brahmo Samaj.’All these details are available on the website www.thebramhmosamaj. net, Gazetteer of India (vol-IInd: History and Culture) and Macmillan published ‘social, cultural and economic history of India’ (writers – Puri, Das, and Chopra). Later famous historians Prof. B. L. Grover and Prof. R. L. Shukla etc. in their book unknowingly described Keshav Chandra’s ‘Neo Brahmo Samaj’ as ‘Adi Brahmo Samaj.’ As described by famous historians, other books, and examination institutions termed Keshab Chandra Sen as the founder of ‘Adi Brahmo Samaj’ whereas the fact is entirely opposite. Therefore Keshav’s ‘Neo Brahmo Samaj’was ‘Bhartiya Brahmo Samaj’ and basic Brahmo Samaj under the leadership of Devendra Nath was ‘Adi Brahmo Samaj’.
