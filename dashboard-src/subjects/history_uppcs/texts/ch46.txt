CHAPTER 46: Revolt of 1857

EXPLANATION SECTION
* In December 1856, the East India Company decided to replace the old iron made Brown Bess (standardised musket) with new and advanced Enfield Rifle (musket). Dum Dum, Ambala and Sialkot were decided the place for the training and trials of the new Gun. The Rifle required the biting of the upper part of the cartridge by mouth the rumours was that catridges were deliberately greased. In January 1857, rumours spread that the cartridge contained fats of cow and pig. The soldiers believed this new Rifle is an attempt to destroy their religions by the British. It became the immediate reason for the Revolt of 1857. The first sepoys to refuse catridged related practice were at Berhampore in late February 1857. Later, the refusal spread to other places. On 29th March, 1857 soldiers of Barrakpore refused to use the fat-containing cartridges and, Mangal Pandey attacked and Shot and wounded two of their officers, they were sergeant Major James Thorton Hewson and Lieutenant and Adjutant Bebpde Henry Baugh, at Barrackpore. One of the reasons for the Revolt of 1857 was the exploitative policies of Britishers and it was the outflow of all the dissents and grievances of Indians all of a sudden as an explosion in Volcano.
* Anglo-Indian historians believed the Soldier’s discontentment and the fat-containing cartridges were the main and important reason for the Revolt of 1857. But modern historians proved this as only a reason followed by several other reasons. The fat-containing cartridges were just a spark which started the explosions of the explosives which were accumulated due to political, social, religious and economic discontent ofIndians against the exploitative policies of British Rule.
* Revolt of1857 started on 10th May, 1857 from Meerut, where the 3rd Bengal Light Cavalry regiment (85 out of90 cavalries) refused to receive the new cartridges and openly rebelled against the authorities. They fired on their officers released their imprisoned comrades from jail and marched towards Delhi. Major General William Hewitt had 2200 European soldiers but he didn’t try to stop this storm. Rebels captured Delhi on 12th May, 1857 AD. During the revolt of 1857, the message was conveyed by circulation ofChappatis (Bread) and lotus flowers, which is marked as the symbol of revolt.
* Khan Bahadur Khan from Rohilkhand, led the Revolt from Bareilly. He was appointed as the Viceroy in Rohilkhand by Mughal King Bahadur Shah II.
* Rani Lakshmi Bai (Manikarnika) was born on C. 19th November 1828 at Kashi/Benaras (now Varanasi). At the age of 14 Laxmibai was married to Maharaja Gangadhar Rao who died in Gwalior. Rani Laxmi Bai was the widow of Maratha king Maharaja Gangadhar Rao. When Gangadhar Rao died by leaving his adopted son (Damodar Rao) as his heir, but the British did not recognise the adoption and imposed Doctrine of Lapse. Dalhousie by Doctrine oflapse, annexed Jhansi to British Empire in 1854.
* In June, 1857, the revolt began in Jhansi under the leadership ofRani Laxmi Bai (the revolt in Jhansi was outbreaked/began by sepoys Laxmibai Later assumed control and led resistance). She died on 17/18 June, 1858 A.D. fighting General Hugh Henry Rose. On her death, Hugh Rose said, “Among the Indian revolutionaries the women lying here is the only man.”
* Revolt in Lucknow began under the leadership of Begum Hazrat Mahal on 30th May, 1857 A.D. She declared her minor son Birjis Qadr as Nawab and took to all affairs ofthe Lucknow into herself. On 21st March, 1858 A.D., Campbell with the help of Gurkha Regiment regained Lucknow.
* On 5 June 1857, the rebel repoys halted at Kalyanpur; on 6 June they returned to Kanpur, where Nana Saheb (Nana Dhondhu Pant) assumed their leadership. He was later proclaimed/declared Peshwa at Kanpur (on June 30, 1857 as recorded by R.C. Majumdar) and the war of Independence spread in Kanpur.
He was helped a lot by Commander-in-Chief (his General Military Advisor) Tatya Tope, The original name ofTatya Tope was Ramchandra Pandurang Tope. After being betrayed by a close friend Man Singh, he was captured when he was camping in a forest. Tope was captured on 7 April 1859, after a hurried trial the military court gave the verdict on 15th April 1859 punishing him with death by hanging and he was hanged on 18th April 1859 at Sipri/Shivpuri.
* Kunwar Singh led the revolt from Jagdishpur (currently Bhojpur district), Bihar. He was from Shahabad region ofBihar.
* Maniram Dutt Deewan in Assam declared Kandarpeswar Singha, the grandson of the last king as the king and planned an armed insurrection conspiracy. But soon after Maniram Deewan was caught and hanged for his promotional activities regarding the revolt.
* Thakur Kushal Singh ofAuwa defeated the combined army ofEnglish and Jodhpur.
* Maulavi Ahmadullah Shah provided leadership to the revolt from Faizabad (Ayodhya) in 1857 A.D. He was a determined opponent ofBritish rule. He was native to Chinapattan of Madras, but he migrated to Faizabad. He asked all the different religious believers to stand as one against these English foreigners and to wipe them out from India. About him, Britishers said a man of great abilities, of undaunted courage of stern determination; and best soldier among the rebels. A bounty of Rs. 50,000 was placed on his arrest (dead or alive).
* Mirza Ghalib saw the revolt of1857 A.D. with his own eyes. He was born on 27 December, 1797 AD in Agra and died in Delhi on 15 February, 1869.
* Shindhiyas of Gwalior helped the Britishers in the suppression of the Revolt.
* Minister of Gwalior Sir Dinkar Rao was praised by European historians for his loyalty to English. Minister of Hyderabad Salar Jung was praised in the same way for his loyalty.
* The revolt of 1857 was spread over a wide area and was substantially supported by the public. But still, this Revolt was unable to bring whole of India together. This revolt did not become an all-India revolt; South India remained largely quiet, while Bengal and Punjab were only marginally affected though parts of eastern and western India were involved.
* Scindia of Gwalior, Holkar of Indore, Nizam of Hyderabad, King of Jodhpur, Nawab of Bhopal, Sikh rulers of Patiala, Nabha and Jind and other Sikh Sardar ofPunjab, Maharaja ofKashmir and other kings were actively helping the Britishers in suppressing the revolts. To these rulers, Governor-General Canning said, that these rulers acted as barriers against the high and fast-moving wave of seas, in the absence of which we would have washed out from India completely. The Educated Class didn’t show any interest in the Revolt of 1857 they generally remained neutral or sided with the British, viewing the rebellion as a regressive, reactionary and anti-modern movement; which was one of the major causes of the failure of this Revolt.
* Lord Canning (1856-62 A.D.) was the Governor-General during the revolt of1857. During the Revolt, he made Allahabad as an emergency headquarter. Lord Canning was the last Governor-General appointed by the Company and was the first Viceroy appointed by the British Crown.
* Under the judicial reforms, Indian High Court Act, 1861 established High Courts at Bombay, Calcutta and Madras. After enabling 1861 Act, the courts were formally established by the ‘Letters Patent of 1862 under Lord Canning. Under the social reforms Canning passed Hindu Widow Remarriage Act, 1856. The Sepoy Mutiny of 1857 is known as First War of Independence in India and as an Indian Mutiny in England.
* Sir Syed Ahmed Khan wrote Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind which was written/ submitted in 1858 and published in 1859 A.D.
* R.C Majumdar was appointed by Government ofIndia to write on the Revolt of 1857, but due to differences with the government committee he rejected this proposal, and he published his book “The Sepoy Mutiny and the Revolt of 1857” on his own in 1957 (although his original proposal was turned down in first instance, later he became member of ‘Board of Editors’ for history of the freedom movement but later discontinued with this service & wrote independently).
* R.C. Majumdar said, “ It is difficult to avoid the conclusion that the so-called First National War of Independence of 1857 is neither First, nor National, nor a war of Independence”.
* Most important outcome of 1857 was the Queen’s Proclamation of 1858. It was declared on 1st November, 1858 in Allahabad by Lord Canning ending the Company Rule and putting the Realm of British India under the direct control of Crown. This declaration included no extension ofBritish India’s then present territorial possessions no further interference in the religious matters of people, equal protection of law to be available to all. British stopped all further annexation ofnative states and kings were allowed to adopt.
* Approximately after the Suppression of 1857 Revolt, ‘Peel Commission’ was constituted for the reorganization of Indian Army by British Government. The Commission divided the army on the basis of caste, religion, race, community, etc. The number of European soldiers was increased from 45,000 in 1857 A.D. to 65,000 by 1863 A.D. Indian troops were reduced from 2,38,000 to 1,40,000.The ratio of Europeans to Indian soldiers in Bengal was 1:2 whereas in Madras and Bombay it was 1:3. After the Revolt the soldiers the recruitment ofGorkha, Sikh and Punjab is increased
* John Bennet Hearsey was the commanding officer of Barrakpore when Revolt of 1857 broke out there.
* Viscount Palmerston was the then Prime Minister of United Kingdom. His tenure as Prime Minister was from 1855 to 1858 and 1859 to 1865.
* The Revolt was poorly organized with least coordination or nominal central leadership. The rebels lacked a clear understanding of Colonial Rule, they have a myopic views of forward-looking programme, a coherent ideology, a political perspective or societal alternatives. Although official sources record substantial Hindu-Muslim and inter-regional unity among many rebels, but many central leaderships lack unity and which proved to be detrimental to this revolt. Modern Nationalism was not known to them at that time, which led to everyone fighting for their own interests and not for the nation. Except with the common shared hatred for alien rule in their own land.
* Brigadier General John Nicholson died on 23rd September, 1857, Who was wounded nine days before while leading his men to capture Delhi. The Delhi was captured by British on September 20-21, 1857. Sir Henry Montgomery Lawrence Chief Commissioner of Oudh (‘Lawrence of Lucknow’), mortally wounded on July 2, died on July 4, 1857 in Lucknow in the 1857 revolt while protecting British residency. Brigadier General James George Smith Neill died in September 1857 while attempting to reach the Residency during the siege. Major-General Sir Henry Havelock was died on dysentery in November 1857 a few days after the siege was lifted. Sir James Outram and W. Taylor termed the Revolt of1857 as a result of a conspiracy of Hindu and Muslim. Outram said that this was a Muslim conspiracy in which the Hindus concerns were used for the Revolt. It was only a Sepoy Mutiny, according to John Lawrence and Seeley. According to T.R. Holmes, it was a war between Barbarism and Civilization. V. D. Savarkar in his book “The Indian War of Independence 1857”, termed this Revolt as an organized struggle for Independence. He said it was the First War of Independence.
* Surendra Nath Sen whose book ‘Eighteen Fifty-Seven’ was published in 1957 was commissioned by Indian government to write history of 1857 and was the first Director of the National Archives of India.

7. What was the main cause of 1857 revolt?

(a) Public Outrage
(b) Military discontent
(c) Management of Christian Missionaries
(d) Policy of the British Empire
U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 1990
Ans. (d)
The Indian Rebellion of1857 had diverse political, economic, military, religious and social causes.
The sepoys, a generic term used for native Indian soldiers of the Bengal Army, had their own list of grievances against the British East India Company administration. The spark that led to the mutiny was the issue of new gunpowder cartridges for Enfield rifle in January 1857 A.D. There was a widespread beliefthat the cartridges contained cow or pig fat. This insulted both the Hindu and Muslim religious practices. Underlying grievances like taxation and land annexations by East India Company were ignited by the sepoy mutineers, and within weeks dozens ofunits ofIndian army joined the peasant army in widespread rebellion. The old aristocracy, both Muslim and Hindu, who were seeing their power being steadily eroded by the East India Company also rebelled against British rule.

13. Which of the following was not a reason for making the sepoys of the East India Company rebellious?

(a) The efforts of the officers of the company to spread Christianity
(b) The order to the sepoys to travel on ships
(c) The stoppage of Bhatta
(d) The inefficiency of the officers
(e) None of the above/More than one of the above
B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2018
Ans. (d)
The inefficiency ofthe officers was not a cause for the Sepoy Mutiny. The Indian Rebellion of1857 occurred as a result of an accumulation of factors over time, rather than any single event such as an effort by officers to spread Christianity among the soldiers, which led to interference in the religious matters of the people. The order of the Company to the sepoys to travel on a ship was another reason as in Hinduism it was believed that travelling by sea could deprive them of their religious status of being a Hindu. A more immediate cause of the sepoy’s dissatisfaction was the recent order that they would not be given the foreign service allowance (Bhatta/Batta) when serving in Sindh or Punjab along with rumors of the use of animal fat in the cartridges of the New Enfield Rifle.

Ramachandra Pandurang Tope, known as Tatya Tope, was an able leader of the great uprising of 1857. He was a personal adherent of Nana Saheb of Kanpur. 31. Which one of the following places did Kunwar Singh, a prominent leader of the Revolt of 1857 belonged to?

(a) Bihar
(b) Madhya Pradesh
(c) Rajasthan
(d) Uttar Pradesh
I.A.S. (Pre) 2005
Ans. (a)
Kunwar Singh was one of the most important freedom fighters in the Sepoy Mutiny of 1857. Kunwar Singh was born in Jagdishpur in Shahabad region ofBihar. He belonged to Arrah in Bihar during the mutiny. When the people of all parts of India rose against British authority in 1857 A.D., Babu Kunwar Singh was nearly eighty years old. At that age, he fought against the English East India Company. Despite his age and failing health, Kunwar Singh plunged into the thick of it and battled against the British forces with grim determination and undaunted courage. In Bihar, Kunwar Singh was the Leader against the British. He assumed command of the soldiers who had revolted at Danapur on 25th July 1857 A.D. Two days later he occupied Arrah, the district headquarters. Major Vincent Eyre relieved the town in August 1857, defeated Kunwar Singh’s force and destroyed Jagdishpur. Kunwar Singh left his ancestral village and reached Lucknow in December 1857 A.D. In March 1858 A.D., he occupied Azamgarh. However, he had to leave the place soon. He was pursued by Brigadier Douglas, and he retreated towards his home in Bihar. On 23 April 1858 A.D., Kunwar Singh had a victory near Jagdishpur over the force led by Captain Le Grand, but on 26 April 1858 A.D., he died in his village. The mantle of the old chief now fell on his brother Amar Singh who, despite heavy odds, continued the struggle and for a considerable time ran a parallel government in the then district of Shahabad. In October 1859 Amar Singh joined the rebel leaders in the Nepal Terai.

96. What was/were the object/objects of Queen Victoria’s Proclamation (1858)?
1. To disclaim any intention to annex the Indian States
2. To place the Indian administration under the British Crown
3. To regulate East India Company’s trade with India Select the correct answer using the code given below.
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
I.A.S. (Pre) 2014
Ans. (a)
British Government passed an Act in 1858, which is known as ‘Act ofAdministrative Reforms in India’. Provisions ofthe Act are mentioned below –
The company’s territories in India were to be governed by Queen, the company ceased to exercise its power and control over these territories. India was now to be governed in the name of the Queen.
The Queen’s principal secretary of state for India received the powers and duties of the company’s court of directors. A council offifteen members was appointed to assist the secretary of state for India. The Council became an advisory body for Indian affairs. For all communications between Britain and India, the secretary of the state became the real channel. The Crown was empowered to appoint a Governor-General and the Governors of the Presidencies.
An Indian Civil Service was to be created under the control of the secretary of state.
All the property and other assets of the East India Company were transferred to the crown. The crown also assumed the responsibilities of the company along with their related treaties, contracts and so forth.
