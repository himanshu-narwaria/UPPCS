CHAPTER 58: Formation of Muslim League (1906)

EXPLANATION SECTION
* In October 1906 A.D., Muslim representatives gathered at Shimla under the leadership of Aga Khan III decided to form a gathered separate muslim representation with an aim to protect the interests of Muslims.
* All India Mohammadan Educational Conference was organized at Dhaka in December 1906 A.D. President of Welcome Committee and organizers of Political meetings, Nawab of Dhaka Salimullah Khan, proposed establishment of an All India Muslim League. A 60 member temporary committee was established with Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Waqar ul-Mulk as joint secretaries. Aga Khan III became its first President. It was headquartered at Lucknow.
* Three main objectives of the organization were -
1. To promote among the Muslims ofIndia feelings ofloyalty to the British government, and to remove any misconception that may arise as to the intention ofgovernment with regard to any of its measures.
2. To profit and advance the political rights and interests ofthe Muslims of India, and to respectfully represent their needs and aspirations to the government.
3. To prevent the rise, among the Muslims of India, of any feeling of hostility toward other communities, without prejudice to the aforementioned objects of the League.
* Sessions ofMuslim League were held at Karachi in 1907 A.D. and at Amritsar in 1908 A.D. The clear and formal demand for the separate electorates by Muslim League was demanded in Amritsar Session (30-31 December, 1908). In this session the league denounce the idea of joint electorates and insisted on separate electorates for Muslims.
* On 1st October, 1906 A.D., a delegation of Muslims (Simla Delegation having a gathering of35 prominent muslim leaders) led by Aga Khan III met Minto and demanded separate electorate for elections at all levels of governance. Morley-Minto reform (1909 A.D.) accepted the provision ofseparate electorate for Muslims.
* In 1908 A.D., the London Muslim League was established by Syed Ameer Ali.

10. The Muslim deputation met Minto in 1906 at Shimla and pleaded for –

(a) Separate electorate for Muslims
(b) A composite electorate
(c) Higher representation for the Hindus
(d) Special representation for Muslim by nomination
46th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2004
Ans. (a)
Shimla Deputation organized by the Indian Muslim leader Aga Khan III met the Governor-General and Viceroy Lord Minto at Shimla on 1 October, 1906 AD. The aim of the deputation was to win the sympathy of the British Rule on matters relating to their interests. Therefore to safeguard their interests, the Muslim leaders drew up a plan for a separate electorate for their community.
