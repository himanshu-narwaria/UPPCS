CHAPTER 57: Congress: Benaras, Calcutta, and Surat Session

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Gopal Krishna Gokhale chaired the 1905 A.D. Benaras session of Congress. He founded the 'Servants of India Society' the same year. In 1906, the Congress party was divided on the issue of the presidentship of the Calcutta session but the division was avoided when an agreement was reached on the name of Dadabhai Naoroji. Resolutions on Swaraj (self government), Swadeshi, Boycott and National education were passed in the Calcutta session in 1906 A.D.
* Dadabhai Naoroji also remembered as 'Grand Old Man of India' was the first Indian who got elected to British Parliament in 1892 from central Finsbury constituency on a ticket ofLiberal Party. He was the president ofCongress in 1886, 1893 and 1906 sessions. He was the first Indian to be appointed as professor of Maths and Natural Philosophy at Elphinstone College, Mumbai. In 1851 A.D., he started Gujarati newspaper named as 'Rast Goftar'.
* C.Y. Chintamani said that many intelligent and selfless leaders had embellished for the people ofIndia, but in his era, no one was as the Dadabhai Naoroji.
* Gokhale said, "If there is the divine in man, it is in Dadabhai Naoroji".
* A split took place in Congress at Surat in 1907 when the dispute again arose for the Presidentship of 23rd Session of Congress. Moderates wanted Rash Behari Ghosh as President while Lala Lajpat Rai was the choice of Extremists. The extremists wanted a guarantee that all four resolution would be accepted. Therefore, to pressurise the moderates, they even decided to oppose the duly elected president Rash Behari Ghosh. Both sides had come to the session prepared for the conflict. At the session the members started to fight with each other and the congress finally split into Moderates and Extremists.

Gopal Krishna Gokhale was born on May 9, 1866 A.D., in Rat nagiri (Maharashtra). Gokhale became as a protege ofsocial reformer Mahadev Govind Ranade. He was a member ofthe Deccan Education Society. Gokhale fought for decades to obtain greater political representation and power over public affairs for common Indians. He first joined Congress in 1889 A.D. at the Bombay session. Gokhale became a member of the Bombay Legislative Council and then the Imperial Legisla tive Council. Gandhiji often referred to Gokhale as ‘Mahatma.’ He was the political mentor of Gandhiji.
