CHAPTER 27: Bhakti and Sufi Movement

EXPLANATION SECTION
* Devotional Worship had a long history of almost a thousand years across the subcontinent. The early medieval Bhakti movement originated in Dravida region and began to spread to the north during the late medieval ages (13th-14th centuries onwards) when north India was under Islamic rule.
* Some of the earliest Bhakti movements (6th Century) were led by Vaishnava Alvars and Nayanars of South (Shaiva). Bhakti Movement spread with the rise of the great philosopher Adi Shankaracharya in South India in 8th century who propagated pure monism ‘Advaitavada’.
* Bhakti Movement was reborn in the 15th-16th century A.D. when the Kabir, Nanak, Tulsi, Sur, Meerabai etc. encouraged the movement.
* In medieval India, the emergence of Sufis helped in promoting harmony in society.
* They used to do meditation and practised spiritual disciplines such as remembrance and devotional practices like 'Sama' and sometimes breath-focused remembrance.
* Sufism originated within Islam and later some strands developed theosophical systems adopting tradition such as Neoplatonism and other currents. Some believe that four philosophies influenced the Sufism –
(1) Advaitavada and Vishishta Dvaitavada ofAryans
(2) New Aflatuni theory
(3) Liberal thinking
(4) Deep rooted knowledge of Islam
They took the help ofholy songs and music to spread unity and harmony in society and to arouse a state of spiritual ecstasy in their audience.
* Kamarupa was an ancient Indian state situated roughly in Assam. Vaishnavism in Kamarupa was popularized by Shankaradeva, the founder of Ekasarana sect. They stressed devotion to one deity–krishna (identified with Vishnu). They also opposed both rituals and idolatry. They were known as Chaitanya ofAssam.
* Shuddha Advaitavada is the “purely non-dual” philosophy propounded by Shri Vallabhacharya, the founder and guru ofthe Vallabha sect. Vallabhacharya was the son ofTelugu Brahmin Shri Lakshmana Bhatta of Somyazi clan, who is said to have developed the views of Vishnu Swami into Shuddha Advaita or pure non-dualism.
* The founder of ‘Dvaitavada’ was Madhvacharya.
* Nimbarkacharya was the founder of ‘Dvaitadvaitavada (Dvait-Advait Vad)’ and Ramanujacharya was the founder of ‘Vishishtadvaitavad’. Nimbarkacharya is related to the ‘Sanaka’ sect.
* Ramananda was the promoter of the Bhakti Movement in northern India. He was born in the Kanyakubja Brahmin family of Prayaga (Prayagraj) in 1299 AD.
* These used to believe in ‘Saguna’ God.
* He was the first Bhakti saint who promoted his messages in the Hindi language.
* Kabir (1398 - 1518 AD) was one of the 12 disciples of Ramananda. He was born near Varanasi to a widowed Brahmin and grew up with a weaver couple Neeru and Neema.
* ‘Bijak’ is a compilation of the works of Saint Kabir. It is a holy book ofthose who believe in the Kabirpanthi sect.
* 'Sabad', 'Sakhi' and 'Ramaini' are compositions ofKabir but his dialogues with Dharmadasa are found in the book ‘Amarmul/Anurag Sagar'.
* Saint Malukdasa was born in the family of Lala Sundar Das Khatri in 1574 AD in Kada (present-day Kaushambi district of Uttar Pradesh).
* Guru Ghasidas was born on 18 December 1756 AD in Giraudpuri village in Balodabazar-Bhatapara district of Chhattisgarh.
12 Jyotirlingas devoted to lord Shiva are found in different parts of India.
These are - Kedarnath, Vishwanath, Vaidyanath, Mahakaleshwara, Omkareshwara, Nageshwara, Somnath, Trayambakeshwara, Grishneshwara, Bhimashankara, Mallikarjun Swami and Rameshwaram.
* Guru Nanak was born in Talwandi in Punjab in 1469 AD. Now this place is called ‘Nankana Sahib’.
* It is located in Nankana Sahib district of western Punjab (now in Pakistan).
* Guru Nanak died in 1539 AD at Kartarpur (present day in Pakistan).
* Guru Nanak (1469 - 1539 AD) was the founder of Sikh religion during the reign ofSikandar Lodi (1489 - 1517 AD).
* Nanak believed in monotheism and emphasized the worship of the one formless God (IK Onkar).
* Guru Nanak says, "God doesnot ask about social class or birth; you must find your true home."
* Guru Nanak started the community kitchen known as ‘Guru Ka Langar’. The followers used to eat together without paying any attention to the caste.
* Mirabai was the only daughter of Ratan Singh Rathore ofMerta.
* She was born in village Kudaki of Merta in 1498 AD. She was married to Prince Bhojraja, the eldest son of famous ruler Rana Sanga of Mewar.
* Namdev had an important role in popularizing the Bhakti movement in Maharashtra.
* He was born in Narsi Village (Maharashtra) in 1270 AD. Visoba Khechar was his teacher. He belonged to the Varkari sect.
* Visoba Khechar had him educated for mystical life and introduced the omnipresent nature of God. Namdev said that “a stone is worshipped, the other is walked upon, if one is God, then the other must also be a God.”
* The time period of Dadu Dayal was from 1544 AD to 1603 AD.
* The time period ofTyagraja was from 1767 AD to 1847 AD. He was a devotee poet of Bhakti path and was a great musician of Carnatic music.
* The famous Saint Mahaprabhu Chaitanya (1486 - 1534 A.D.) of Bhakti Movement was born in Nadia district of Bengal in a Brahmin family.
* His father’s name was Jagannatha Mishra and his mother’s name was Shachi Devi.
* Chaitanya’s childhood name was Nimai (Vishwambhar Mishra).
* Chaitanya was a follower of Lord Krishna.
* Famous devotee /saint Goswami Tulsidas was contemporary of Akbar and Jahangir.
* Tulsidas wrote many texts in which Ramcharitmanasa and Vinaya patrika are the major work.
* ‘Ramcharitmanas’ was composed by Goswami Tulsidas (1532-1623 A.D.) in Awadhi language.
* Sufism was born in a foreign land within early Islamic asceticism but it was mainly influenced by Indian Vedanta philosophy later.
* Chishtiya order of Sufi was founded by Abu Ishaq Sami Chishti and his disciple Khwaja Abu Ahmad Abdal in Chishti Sharif ofAfghanistan.
* But, it was primarily promoted by Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti in India.
* He came to India with Muhammad Ghori’s Army in 1192 AD (12th century).
* He made his residence in Ajmer (Rajasthan).
* He died in 1236 AD.
* Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki was his main disciple. Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti became a disciple of Khwaja Usman Chishti Haruni at Nishapur.

* Chishtia order gained more popularity in India due to his efforts.
* Some of Baba Farid's compositions were included by Guru Arjan in the Adi Granth Sahib. He was the son-in-law of Balban.
* He was the spiritual teacher of Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya.
* Alauddin Sabir Kaliyari was his other prominent disciple.
* Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya’s Dargah is located in Delhi.
* Nizamuddin Auliya died in 1325 AD.
* He was buried in Ghiyaspur (Delhi)
* Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya saw the reign of more than seven sultans, who came to power one after the other, but he never went to anyone’s court.

* He was popularly known as Mehboob-i-Elahi and ‘Sultan-ul-Mashaikh’ (King of Saints).
* Shaikh Salim Chishti’s name is notable in the last Sufis of Chishti Branch. His father’s name was Shaikh Bahauddin.
* He lived in Arabia for several years and was conferred with the title of ‘Shaikh-Ul-Hind’. After that, he returned to India and stayed at a place called Sikri, which was about 37 km from Agra. The place was later developed by Akbar as Fatehpur Sikri.
* It is said that Jahangir was born with the blessings of Shaikh Salim Chishti.
* The impact of Chishti order was in Delhi, Rajasthan, and neighbouring areas while the influence ofthe Suhrawardi order was extended in the region of Sindh and Punjab.
* Firdausi order traces its origins to Najmuddinkubra and is treated as a collateral line of the Suhrawardiya; it later had close relation with Chishtis in India which had influence in the areas of Bihar.
* Shaikh Muhiuddin Qadir Jilani ofBaghdad was the founder ofthe Qadiriyya order (Qadiri) and was considered as one ofthe great saints of Islam.
* The main titles of Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jailani were - ‘Mehboob-i-Sumani’ (God’s lover), ‘Piran-i Pir’ (Chief of Pirs), ‘Pir-i-Dastagir’ (Helpful Saint) etc. This order was promoted by Shah Niyamatullah in Deccan and Kashmir and Makhdum Jilani in Punjab.
* Makhdoom Jilani made Uchha as his education centre.
* Naqshbandi order was established by Khwaja Bahauddin Naqshband in the 14th century.
* Khwaja Khawand Mahmud was a prominent saint of this branch who came to India and made Kashmir his centre. This order was majorly propagated by Khwaja Baqi Billah (1563-1603), who came from Kabul with the orders of his master.
* Shaikh Ahmed Sirhindi, also known as ‘Mujaddid’, the reformer ofIslam is religion, was his main disciple. He propounded ‘Wahadat-ul-Shuhud’ (positivist philosophy).
* The Sirhindi/Mujaddadi Naqshbandi branch opposed Sama, but Ferozabadi (Delhi) branch practiced sama.
* It was the most radical order among Sufis. They opposed Akbar’s liberal policies.
* Aurangzeb had influence of this Sufi order.
* Sufi Saint Shah Muhammad Ghaus has accepted Krishna as Wali/Auliya.
* He was the most famous saint of Sattariyya order.

16. Which ofthe following statements regarding the Bhakti Movement is not correct?

(a) Vallabhacharya established the Pushtimarg.
(b) Nimbarka propounded the philosophy ofDvaitadvaita.

(e) Question not attempted
Rajasthan P.C.S (Pre) 2024
Ans. (c)
The renowned saint of the Bhakti Movement tradition, Madhvacharya, was a proponent of Dvaita (Dualism) philosophy. His central doctrine is "Dvaitavada", which states that the individual soul (ātman), the material world, and the Supreme Being (Paramatma) are distinct and fundamentally different from each other. Madhvacharya followed the Saguna Bhakti tradition, not the Nirguna Bhakti stream. He considered Lord Vishnu as the supreme deity and strongly advocated his worship.
