CHAPTER 89: Formation of Interim Government (1946)

EXPLANATION SECTION
* On 12th August, 1946 AD Lord Wavell invited Congress President Jawahar Lal Nehru to form the Interim Government. On 24th August, 1946 AD under the leadership ofJawaharlal Nehru, Congress announced a communique offirst Interim Government ofIndia. On 2nd September, 1946 AD First Interim Government of India was formed in which Muslim League didn’t participate in the beginning.
* Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the Minister of Agriculture and Food in the Interim Government. Baldev Singh was Defence Minister. External affairs and Commonwealth Affairs were looked after by Jawahar Lal Nehru in this interim government. Asaf Ali held Communication (Rail and Transport) Ministry in the interim government of 1946 AD.
* Muslim League after repeated requests by Lord Wavell joined the Interim Government in 1946 AD. In October, 1946 AD five members of League joined the Interim Government.
* Liaqat Ali Khan was given Finance Ministry, who in his Budget presentation imposed several taxes on businessmen and industrialists.
* Abul Kalam Azad was not a member of this Interim Government.
* This Interim Government had twelve (out of planned 14) Ministers- Jawahar Lal Nehru, Sardar Vallabh Bhai Patel, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, AsafAli, C. Rajagopalachari, Sharat Chandra Bose, Dr. John Mathai, Sardar Baldev Singh, Sir Shafat Ahmed Khan, Jagjivan Ram, Syed Ali Zaheer and C.H. Bhabha. Three of these twelve were to be removed when Muslim League joined the Interim Government, they were - Syed Ali Zaheer, Sharat Chandra Bose and Sir Safat Ahmed.
* Krishak Praja Party in the leadership of A.K. Fazlul Haq joined hands with Muslim League and formed coalition government after the election in Bengal and Cabinet of the Muslim League was formed in Bengal which ruled till 14th August 1947 AD.
* H.S. Suhrawardy was its Prime Minister.
* 16th August, 1946 AD was decided as Direct Action day by Muslim League. On the Direct Action Day rioting activities were prevalent. It was shown through violence that Hindu and Muslim couldn't live together in one country. ‘Calcutta’ was the main centre of these activities Noakhali riots began later (in October 1946). The All India Muslim League formally demanded a separate state for muslims, known as the Pakistan Resolution (or Lahore Resolution) on March 23, 1940 as its general session in Lahore. The League officially decided to join the interim Government on October 13, 1946 (after the direct action day). Initially they boycotted the government established on September 2, 1946.
