CHAPTER 53: Moderates and Extremists in Congress

EXPLANATION SECTION
* The first two decades of INC are often described in history as a moderate era. Some of the prominent moderate leaders were Dadabhai Naoroji, Surendra Nath Banerjee, Mahadev Govind Ranade, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gopal Krishna Gokhale and Anand Mohan Bose. They had a sense of confidence in British justice and their generosity. They never aimed at aggressive nationalism for attaining independence. Leaders like Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal, raised extremist ideology.
* Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Dinshaw Wacha, Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee and Surendra Nath Banerjee were leaders of Moderate group. They all belonged to urban areas, in fact most of the Moderate leaders who believed in moderate politics belonged to Urban areas. The Congress during moderate phase was dominated by people having professions like Advocates, Doctors, Engineers, Journalists and Person related to Literature works. the Connection to common masses was missing in Congress during Moderate domination.
* Pherozeshah Mehta once said, that the voice of Congress is not of Masses, but it is the responsibility of citizens in contact with Congress to understand these emotions and propagate these to the Nation.
* GopalKrishnaGokhalewasamoderateleader. Hebelievedinthe purity ofMeans and Ends. He was the political mentor (Guru) of Mahatma Gandhi. For the first time, he tookpart in politics in 1889.
* In 1897 A.D., Gokhale and Dinshaw Wacha were asked to present proofs of Indian expenditure in front of ‘Welby Commission’. In 1899 A.D., Gokhale was elected to Bombay legislative council and in 1901 A.D. was elected to Imperial council of the Governor general of India.
* In early 1900s Extremist leaders of Congress emerged in Indian Political Scenario. Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal and Aurobindo Ghose were four important extremist leaders. Their main objective was ‘Swaraj’. They didn’t believe in the agitation through constitutional means of the Moderates. They blamed the Congress for politics of Petition, Prayers and Protest. Under their leadership, the policy of petition and prayers was criticised/challenged.
* Moderates believed in constitutional agitation, annual sessions, speeches, sending petitions to British Parliament, etc. but extremists believed in self-sacrifice, mass movement, aggressive resistance and strong determination.
* Extremists wanted ‘Swaraj’; as Congress presidential address, Calcutta, 1906 defines Swaraj as – self government like that of the United Kingdom or the Colonies on the other hand moderates believed in “Selfrule under the colonial empire”.
* Lala Lajpat Rai also known as Sher-e-Panjab, was the representative from Punjab and an extremist leader. He is also known as Punjab Kesari.
* Lala Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal and Bal Gangadhar Tilak are together called Lal-Bal-Pal. During a protest against Simon Commission Lala Lajpat Rai was injured in lathi charge due to which he died on 17 November, 1928 A.D. After reading the Biography of Mazzini Lala Lajpat Rai accepted him as his political mentor.
* He translated a work ofMazzini ‘The Duties of Man’ into Urdu.
* During the Partition of Bengal, Aurobindo Ghosh was one of the prominent leader of the Swadeshi Movement. Other important leaders of this movement were Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Bipin Chandra Pal. Tilak believed in sacrifice and had courage to challenge British establishment. He was called the “Father of Indian unrest” by Sir Valentine Chirol. After Bal Gangadhar Tilak’s 1897 sedition conviction and the rejection of his Privy Council appeal, Professor Max Muller and Sir William Hunter led an influential petition to the Queen, asking mercy for Tilak on the ground that he was a great Sanskrit scholar.
* Later in 1908 A.D., Tilak was jailed for 6 Years in Mandalay (Myanmar then Burma) on the charges of sedition initiated against him for printing an anti-British Article in his Newspaper ‘Kesari’.
* Tilak wrote ‘Gita Rahasya’ from ‘Mandalay’ prison. Starting Shivaji Mahotsav and Ganpati festival, he tried to grow the feeling of Nationalism amongst the masses. He died on 1st August, 1920 A.D., and his funeral bier was lifted by Mahatma Gandhi, Maulana Shaukat Ali and Saiffudin Kichlew. Maulana Hasrat Mohani read the eulogy after his death.

Ans. (c)
During the Moderate Phase (1885-1905 AD) of the Indian National Movement, there was support for the rich democratic and scientific culture ofmodern Europe. The moderate leaders advocated for constitutional reforms, political participation and the expansion of modern education. They supported English education, as they believed it would help develop a modern outlook and social progress in India. However, the growing masses viewed British colonial policies with increasing resentment. Notably, Dadabhai Naoroji strongly criticized the economic aspects of British colonialism. His book, "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India," highlighted issues related to the economic drain, heavy taxation, and the decline ofIndian industries. The moderates attempted to address such concerns through constitutional means rather than direct confrontation.

3. The protest method of moderate leaders of the Con gress was?

(a) Non-Cooperation
(b) Constitutional agitation
(c) Passive resistance
(d) Civil disobedience
48th to 52nd B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2008
Ans. (b)
The methods employed by the two groups (Moderates and Extremists) of Congress were different in their tempo and approach. Congress split into two different groups at Surat Session of Congress in 1907 A.D. While the moderates had infinite faith in the efficacy of constitutional agitation and in appealing to the British sense of justice and fair play, in holding an annual conference, making speeches, passing elaborate resolutions and sending deputations to England. The extremists had no faith in the benevolence of British public or Parliament, nor were they convinced ofthe efficacy of merely holding conferences. Extremists had faith in the concept of passive resistance.

(a) Swadeshi Movement
(b) Quit India Movement
(c) Non-cooperation Movement
(d) Civil Disobedience Movement
I.A.S. (Pre) 2015
Ans. (a)
Surat split (1907 A.D.), refers to the split in the Congress party into two groups: ‘Moderates’ and ‘Extremists.’ Moder ates believed in the policy ofsettlement ofminor issues with Government by deliberation, strikes, and boycotts to force their demands. The moderates opposed the resolutions on Swaraj (self-governance), Swadeshi National Education and boycott offoreign goods but extremists were not in favour of these. Hence, the difference of opinion between moderates and extremists finally reached to split in the Congress at the Surat Session (1907).
