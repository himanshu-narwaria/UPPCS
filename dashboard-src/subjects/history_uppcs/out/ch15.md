## Chauhans and Tomars
- **Prithviraj III**, ruler of Ajmer, is commonly known as **Prithviraj Chauhan**. {1}
- Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Muhammad Ghori in the 1st battle of Tarain (1191 AD) but was defeated in the 2nd battle of Tarain (1192 AD).
- **Vigraharaj IV** = most powerful Chauhan ruler; he defeated the Tomaras and took Eastern Punjab and Delhi; he also won over the Muslim ruler of Punjab. {2}
- **Hammir Epic (Hammir Mahakavya)** describes the Chauhans as **Suryavanshi**: scions of 'Chahman', the son of Sun. {6}
- Chauhans were one of the four fire clans; Vasudeva and Naradev are mentioned as the initial kings of the dynasty.
- **Jodhraj** wrote **Hammir Raso** (not Abdur Rehman). {11}
- **Prithviraj Raso** was written by **Chand Bardai**. {9}
- **Visal Dev Raso** was written by Narpati Nalh.
- **'Prithviraja Vijaya'** was written by **Jayanak**. {10}
- **Anangpal Tomar** (Tomar Rajput dynasty), originally feudatories of the Gurjar-Pratiharas, founded the city of **Dhillika** (modern Delhi) in 1052 AD. {12}
## Chandelas of Jejakabhukti
- **Jejakabhukti** = ancient name of **Bundelkhand** (not Kaushambi); named after Jay Shakti or Jeja/Jejak, grandson of Nannuk. {13,56}
- **Nannuk (Nannuka)** founded the Chandel dynasty; Jayashakti was also a Chandela ruler.
- **Dhangadev (Dhanga)** was a king of the Chandel dynasty of Jejakabhukti. {14}
- Dhanga attained Moksha by abandoning his body in the waters of Ganga and Yamuna at the Sangam, Prayagraj.
- **Kandariya Mahadev temple** was built by **Vidyadhara** (another passage: by Dhanga).
- Chandela order: Yashovarman (925–950 AD) → Dhanga (950–1002 AD) → Vidyadhara (1017/19–1029 AD) → Keertivarman (1060–1100 AD). {15}
- **Alha and Udal** were related to **Mahoba**; they were commandants of Chandel king Parmardidev (Parmal, 1165–1203 A.D.). {7}
- Parmardidev died after the battle with Prithviraj Chauhan.
- The Chandel–Chauhan struggle is described in "Prithviraj Raso" by Chand Bardai and in **"Alhakhand"** of Parmal Raso by **Jagnik**. {8}

## Pala dynasty and universities
- **Gopala** founded the Pala dynasty; per Tibetan historian Lama Taranath he was born near 'Pundravardhana' and was elected king of Bengal. {18}
- Kings chosen directly by the public: Pala founder **Gopal** and Pallava ruler **Nandivarman Pallavamalla** (not Harshavardhan or Yashovarman). {31}
- Khalimpur inscription of Dharmapala describes the election of Gopala by the people to end Matsya Nyaya (Law of the Jungle).
- After the unexpected death of Pallava ruler Permeshwar Varman, the people elected Nandivarman Pallavamalla as king.
- **Odantapuri (Udnapur)** = prominent education centre in **Bihar**, established by Pala ruler Gopala. {19}
- **Pundravardhana Bhukti** was located in **North Bengal**; its territory expanded beyond northern Bihar under the Pala, Chandra and Sena dynasties. {17}
- **Dharmapala** (770–810 A.D.), an enthusiastic Buddhist, was called **"Paramsaugat"** in his inscriptions.
- Dharmapala built famous Buddhist monasteries at Vikramshila and **Somapura (Paharpur)**. {20}
- Renowned Buddhist commentator Haribhadra was part of Dharmapala's court.
- Per Taranath, Dharmapala founded 50 religious schools but had no religious intolerance or bigotry.
- **Vikramshila University (Mahavihara)** was established by King **Dharmapala** of the **Pala dynasty** of Bengal. {21,22,23,24,25,26,27,28}
- After the downfall of Nalanda, Vikramshila became the main centre of Buddhist studies.
- Vikramshila was located at **Bhagalpur in Bihar**. {29}
- Vikramshila's curriculum: formalized Vedic learning, Vedic texts, rituals and the Vedangas — reasoning, medicine, law, astronomy and city planning.
- Wrong statements on Vikramshila: located in Banka district; established by Gopala I. {30}
- Correct statements: the 'Vajrayana' sect of Buddhism flourished there; Astronomy, Logic, Law, Grammar and Philosophy were also taught.
- Vikramshila University was demolished by **Bakhtiyar Khalji**.
- **Devapala** (Pala king) was Buddhist, also known as "Param Saugat".
- Devapala donated five villages at the request of **Balaputradeva**, Sailendra king of Java (ruler of Suvarnabhumi), who founded a Buddhist monastery at Nalanda; so Bihar's rulers had contact with **Java-Sumatra**. {3,4}
- The Nalanda copper-plate of Devapala records that Balaputradeva built the monastery with Devapala issuing the grant.
- **Taxila** was not a centre of learning in the early medieval period; it was an early Buddhist centre of learning. {32}
- Taxila = capital of Gandhara State, in present-day Rawalpindi district of Pakistan.
- Chanakya (Kautilya), Maurya emperor Chandragupta, Ayurvedic healer Charaka and Jivaka studied at Takshashila.
- Taxila taught the Vedas and the eighteen arts, including archery and elephant lore.
- Nalanda University was established in the 5th century A.D. by Kumar Gupta; Muslim invader Bakhtiyar Khalji sacked it at the beginning of the 12th century.
- The university at Odantapuri was established during Pala rule.
## Gahadavalas and Senas
- **Kumaradevi**, Buddhist queen of Govind Chandra Gahadavala, built the Dharmachakra Jain Vihar at **Sarnath**. {5}
- **Krityakalpataru** was written by Lakshmidhar, minister of Govind Chandra.
- Last powerful ruler of the Gahadavala dynasty = **Jayachandra**.
- **Lakshmanasena** (1178–1206 A.D.) of the **Sena dynasty** reigned 28 years and started a new Samvat, the **'Lakshmana Samvat'**. {43,44}
- Sena succession (father to son, fixed by inscriptions): Samantasena → Hemantasena → Vijayasena → Vallalasena (Ballal Sena) → Lakshmanasena → Visvarupasena. {46}
## Gurjara-Pratiharas
- Pratiharas = most famous Rajput dynasty of the 'Agni' clan; also called Gurjara-Pratihara because of its link with the Gurjars.
- Gurjars were first mentioned in the Aihole inscription of Pulakesin II and in Harshacharita of Banabhatta.
- **Nagabhatta I** (730–760 A.D.) is considered the founder of the Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty; the chapter also calls **Vatsaraja** the actual founder. {51}
- Nagabhatta I destroyed the army of the Maleksha ruler and saved western India from the Arab invasion — mentioned in the Gwalior inscription.
- Gurjara-Pratiharas ruled much of northern India from the mid-8th to the 11th century; first at Ujjain, later at Kannauj.
- Pratihara rulers: Nagabhatta I (730–760), Vatsaraja (775–800), Nagabhatta II (800–833), Mihir Bhoja I (836–885), Mahendrapala I (885–910), Devapala (948–949); Bharatrabhatta-I was not one. {40}
- Order: Vatsaraja (775–800 AD) → Nagabhatta II (800–833 AD) → Mahendrapala I (885–910 AD) → Mahipala (912–944 AD). {39}
- **Mihir Bhoj** (836–885 A.D.) = greatest and most prominent Pratihara king. {36}
- Mihir Bhoja I was a follower of Vaishnavism; titles **'Aadi Varaha'** and **'Prabhas'**. {37}
- Mihir Bhoj was the son of Emperor **Ramabhadra** and queen Appa Devi, not of Nagabhatta-II. {38}
- Mihir Bhoj rebuilt the Pratihara empire, seized Kannauj around 836 AD and made it the capital.
- Al-Masudi of Baghdad visited Gujarat in the 10th century A.D.; he testifies to the power, prestige and vast empire of the Pratiharas.
- Al-Masudi calls the Gurjara-Pratihara kingdom **Al-Juzr** and the king **Baura** (probably a mispronunciation of Adivaraha), though Bhoja had died by then.
- Arab traveller **Suleman** visited India in the reign of Mihir Bhoja (**Bhoja I**). {41}
- Tripartite struggle for Kannauj (8th–9th century A.D.): **Pala, Gurjara-Pratihara and Rashtrakuta**; Cholas were not part; Pratiharas finally won. {52,53}
- After Harsha's death Kannauj attracted powerful dynasties; it was compared with Magadha of the Gupta period.
- **Kannauj** = ancient city earlier called **Mahodaya / Mahodaya Shri** and Kanyakubja in the time of Mihir Bhoj. {54,55}
- **Rajashekhara** was court scholar of Gurjara-Pratihara king **Mahendrapala I** and his son **Mahipala I**. {47}
- Rajashekhara's works: Karpuramanjari, Kavyamimamsa, Viddhasalabhanjika, Bal Ramayana, Bhuvankosh (not Prabandhachintamani). {48}
- Dharmapala = prominent Pala king (770–810 A.D.); Harsha Vardhana = Pushyabhuti king (606–647 A.D.).
## Rashtrakutas
- **Danti Durga** laid the foundation of the Rashtrakuta Empire in 753 AD; capital Manyakheta. {33}
- Danti Durga is said to have performed the **Hiranyagarbha** oblation at Ujjayini. {34}
- **Amoghavarsha I** (Rashtrakuta) was born in 800 AD at Shribhavan near the Narmada in a military camp, when his father Govind III was returning from his North India campaigns. {35}
- **Govind III**, successor of Rashtrakuta king Dhruva, attacked North India after consolidating the south and defeated Pratihara ruler **Nagabhatta II**. {57}
- Sanjan and Radhanpur copper plate inscriptions mention Govind III's victory.

## Paramaras and Kalachuris
- Paramara dynasty was founded in the 9th century A.D. by **Upendra or Krishnaraj**; Dhara was the capital in the later phase.
! Founder of Parmara dynasty: the commission accepted Kanakpala, whereas the correct answer is Upendra or Krishnaraj.
- **King Bhoj** was a Paramara ruler who ruled over **Dhara**; the early capital of the Paramaras was Ujjain, later moved to Dhara. {60}
- After Bhoj's death scholars said: "Adya Dhara Nira Dhara, Niralamba Saraswati, Pandita Khandita sarve Bhoj Raje Divangate".
- **'Samarangana Sutradhara'** by Paramara king **Bhoj** is based on architecture and artificial scientific equipment. {61}
! Book on artificial scientific equipment: the commission first took Bhoja as correct but changed the answer to Govindraja in its revised answer sheet.
- Bhoj's other works: Saraswati Kanthabharan, Siddhanta Sangraha, Yoga Sutra Vritti, Rajamartand, Yukti Kalpataru, Charucharya.
- **Udaipur Prashasti** praises Paramara King **Bhoja**: he obtained, accomplished and understood everything that no one else could. {62}
- It says Bhoja, comparable to King Prithu, ruled the earth from Kailash to the Malaya mountains and from the rising to the setting mountains.
- **Samidheshwar temple** of Chittor (also called **Tribhuvan Narayan temple** and Bhoj Jagti) was built by Paramara Raja Bhoj of Malwa. {16}
- It has a three-faced idol of Lord Shiva, very similar to the main Shiva idol of Elephanta cave, Mumbai.
- **Bhojshala temple**, Dhar district (Madhya Pradesh), was set up by Paramara King Bhoj in 1035 A.D. as a Sanskrit school; presiding deity = Goddess **Saraswati**. {63}
- Bhojshala is now in the premises of the Kamal Maula Mosque.
- **Gangeyadeva** was a **Kalachuri** ruler, not a Parmar; Upendra, Munj and Udayaditya were Parmara rulers. {64}
- Gangeyadeva took the title Vikramaditya and restarted the issue of gold coins in early medieval north India after their extinction.
- Arab writer **Al-Biruni** described Kalachuri ruler Gangeyadeva and his capital **Tripuri**. {65}
- Al-Biruni = Khwarazmian Iranian scholar and polymath of the Islamic Golden Age; wrote Tarikh al-Hind on Indian culture.
## Jurists, literature and economy
- Famous jurists of early medieval India: **Hemadri, Vigyaneshwara and Jimutavahana** (not Rajashekhara). {45}
- Vigyaneshwara wrote "Mitakshara"; Jimutavahana wrote "Dayabhaga".
- **"Gaudavaho"** was written by **Vakpati**, court poet of Yashovarman, king of Kannauj. {67}
- **Araghatta** = Persian wheel: a water-lifting device worked by bullocks, buffaloes or camels for irrigation; mentioned in Panchatantra and Rajatarangini. {68}
- **Motupalli** = very important seaport of the Kakatiya kingdom; now in Bapatla district, Andhra Pradesh; visited by Italian traveller Marco Polo.
- Motupalli epigraph gives rates on sandal, camphor, rose-water, ivory, pearls, corals, copper, zinc, lead, silk, pepper and areca nuts — shows the port's exports and imports.
## Kashmir, Mithila and Bastar
- Shahi Khan, brother of Ali Shah, took the throne of Kashmir in 1420 A.D. as **Zain-ul-Abidin**. {70}
- Zain-ul-Abidin = greatest ruler of Kashmir, ruled nearly 50 years; called **'Akbar of Kashmir'** for liberal religious policy, promotion of learning, and interest in art and architecture. {69}
- **Nanyadev** (1097–1147 AD) founded the **Karnata dynasty**; a great warrior; capital Simraungadh; ruled the Mithila region. {71}
- **Harisimha** (1295/1296–1324 AD) = last ruler of the Karnata dynasty; protector of art and literature; started the Panji system. {72}
- **Kuruspal** village is in Bastar district, Chhattisgarh; the Kuruspal inscription gives information about **Someshwar I**. {73}
- Kuruspal stone inscription of Someswardeva (much mutilated): records a village grant by king Someswardeva of the Nagavamsa.
- Kuruspal Tank slab of Dharana Mahadevi: queen Dharna Mahadevi granted land near Kalamba village to god Kamesvara (Shiva) in Someshwardeva's reign.
