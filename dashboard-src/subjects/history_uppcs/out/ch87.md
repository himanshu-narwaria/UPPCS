@ 87 | Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)
## Sending and members of the Mission
- After World War II the Labour Party under Attlee won elections in Britain; Attlee was sympathetic to India's demand for freedom.
- **Clement Attlee** became British Prime Minister on 26 July 1945.
- On **19 February 1946** Attlee announced the dispatch of the Cabinet Mission for constitutional reforms.
- The Cabinet Mission arrived in Delhi on **24 March 1946**, after the Second World War. {5,16}
- The Mission was led (presided over) by **Lord Pethick Lawrence**, Secretary of State for India. {1,2,4}
- Its three members (cabinet ministers): **Lord Pethick Lawrence**, **Sir Stafford Cripps** (President of the Board of Trade) and **A.V. Alexander** (First Lord of the Admiralty). {3,7,8,9,10,11}
- Not members: Wavell, Ramsay MacDonald, J. Andrew, Lord Emery, John Simon, William Wood.
- Viceroy Lord Wavell did not take part in every step but was present.
- John Simon's Simon Commission was set up to report on the Government of India Act, 1919.
- The Mission was sent to work out a **constitutional arrangement for the transfer of power**; not to set up a national government, nor to work out Jinnah's Pakistan demand. {17}
- Its aim was to preserve India's unity and grant it independence.
- It held extensive discussions in April with almost all major leaders of the country.
## Proposals of 16 May 1946
- The Mission announced its recommendations on **16 May 1946**; the plan for a united Dominion of India came to be known by this date. {12}
- It proposed a **three-tier polity** for India. {6}
- It recommended a **federal government**: all India, including the Princely States, to form a Federation (Union of India). It did not enlarge powers of Indian courts or provide for more Indians in the ICS. {15}
- The Union (Centre) would handle only **foreign affairs, defence and communications**.
- Provinces and states would enjoy all other powers.
- The Muslim League's **demand for Pakistan was rejected**. {13}
- Parts of the plan: provincial grouping, interim cabinet of Indians, right to frame the Constitution.
- The Union would have an Executive and Legislature made of representatives of British Indian Provinces and Princely States.
- A **Constituent Assembly** would be formed from representatives of Provincial Legislative Assemblies, Chief Commissioners' Provinces and Princely States, to frame a Constitution for the Indian Union.
- A province would have the right to leave the Union in future, after elections under its new Constitution.
- It suggested reconstitution of the **Viceroy's Executive Council** and an interim government with all portfolios, including War Member, held by Indian leaders. {14}
## Grouping of provinces
- The major recommendation was to divide the provinces into three sections/groups: A, B and C.
| Group | Provinces |
| A | Madras, Central Provinces and Berar, United Provinces, Bihar, Bombay, Orissa; also Chief Commissioners' Provinces Delhi, Ajmer-Marwar and Coorg |
| B | Punjab, North-West Frontier Province, Sindh; also Baluchistan |
| C | Bengal and Assam |
## Reactions
- **Mahatma Gandhi** was totally in favour of the Cabinet Mission Plan. {19}
- Gandhi said: "It is the best document the British Government could have produced in the circumstances"; he cautioned that their best may possibly be harmful.
- Congress accepted the proposals related to the Constituent Assembly.
- Congress rejected the idea of the interim government because the Muslim League had been given disproportionate representation.
- Muslim League first approved the Plan, then rejected it when Congress said it could change the scheme by majority in the Constituent Assembly.
- Muslim League withdrew its acceptance in 1946 and also joined the Interim Government in 1946; both true, but the second does not explain the first. {18}
- Muslim League joined the Interim Government on **13 October 1946** on repeated request of Lord Wavell.
## Maulana Azad
- **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** was Congress President when the Cabinet Mission came. {21}
- Congress Working Committee authorised him to hold talks; he negotiated with the Mission members on 6 April 1946.
- Azad, as Congress President (1940 to 1946), negotiated with both the **Cripps Mission and Lord Wavell**. {20}
