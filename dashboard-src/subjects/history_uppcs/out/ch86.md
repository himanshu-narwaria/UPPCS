@ 86 | Subhas Chandra Bose and Azad Hind Fauj
## Life of Subhas Chandra Bose
- Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose was born on **23 January 1897** at **Cuttack**, Orissa (Odisha) division of Bengal province. {1}
- He graduated from Scottish Church College (affiliated to University of Calcutta) in 1919 and passed the Indian Civil Services exam in 1920.
- **C.R. Das** was the political guru of Subhas Chandra Bose. {45}
- Bose presided over the 51st Congress session at Haripura (Gujarat) in 1938 and the 52nd at Tripuri (Madhya Pradesh) in 1939; he later resigned.
- After the Tripuri crisis he resigned as Congress President and founded the **Forward Bloc** in **1939**. {2,3,4,6}
- **Sheel Bhadra Yajee**, freedom fighter from Bihar linked with Gandhi and Bose, joined Bose to found the All India Forward Bloc and was active in the INA movement. {25}
- **Rabindranath Tagore** gave Bose the title **"Desh Nayak"** (National Leader) in 1938 at Shantiniketan, after Bose became Congress President. {29}
- Mahatma Gandhi called Bose **"Patriot of Patriots"**.
## Bose in Germany
- Bose escaped at night from house arrest in **January 1941** in disguise and left Calcutta; the 1938 session he presided was Haripura, not Tripuri. {7}

- Bose reached Germany on 3 April 1941.
- The honorific **"Netaji"** was given to Bose in **Germany**. {5}
- Six months later, with German government help, he founded the **"Free India Centre"**; there he printed pamphlets for India's independence and gave many speeches.
- Adolf Hitler approved it and in late 1941 allowed Bose to organise an army for the independence war.
- Bose raised **"The Free Indian Legion"** from 10,000 Indian prisoners of war of General Erwin Rommel's North Africa battles. {26}
## Origin of the Indian National Army
- In December 1941, in the Malay forests, the Punjab battalion led by Captain Mohan Singh was defeated by the Japanese army.
- Lt. Colonel L.V. Fitz Patrick (English origin) was a prisoner of war; Bangkok-resident Indian Giani Pritam Singh took their responsibility.
- Giani Pritam Singh built friendship between the Japanese and Mohan Singh with other Indian soldiers.
- Giani Pritam Singh Dhillon was active in the Indian Independence Movement and the Gadar Party.
- He and Iwaichi Fujiwara reached an agreement of collaboration at Bangkok in December 1941, even before Japan declared war.
- The plans began long before the war among a group of revolutionaries based in Bangkok.
- It was Giani Pritam Singh's idea, with Fujiwara, to release Indian soldiers and ask them to join an Indian National Army.
- The INA was the brainchild of **Giani Pritam Singh** and Japanese **Major Iwaichi Fujiwara** (along with Mohan Singh). {8}
- Where Giani Pritam Singh and Fujiwara are not named, the idea and founding of the INA is credited to **Captain Mohan Singh**. {9,10}
- Mohan Singh was a British Indian Army officer; when the British Army retreated before Japan he joined hands with the Japanese.
- The Japanese handed Indian soldiers to Mohan Singh and he recruited them into the Azad Hind Fauj.
- Mohan Singh agreed in December 1941 to lead the army, at the insistence of Giani Pritam Singh and Fujiwara.
- The Indian National Army was founded in **1942** (February 1942) under Mohan Singh, from defeated Indian soldiers of the British Army's Malay expedition. {11}
- Another date given for the INA's formation under Mohan Singh: 15 July 1942.
- Azad Hind Fauj (INA) was officially constituted in September 1942.
- **Captain Mohan Singh** was the first Commander of the INA. {12}
- One explanation says Mohan Singh was not the founder but only the first operational Commander of the INA.
- The first INA was dissolved in December 1942 after doubts about Japanese motives caused distrust between Mohan Singh and the League leadership, notably Rash Behari Bose.
- Many of the first INA's volunteers later joined the second INA under Subhas Chandra Bose.
- The INA was first formed in the Second World War with Japan's support; Captain Mohan Singh and Rash Behari Bose had key roles in its early organisation.
## Bose takes over the INA
- Bose gained control of the Azad Hind Fauj in 1943; he is remembered as Netaji.
- Subhas Chandra Bose reached Tokyo in June 1943 and arrived in Singapore on 2 July 1943.
- **Rash Behari Bose** offered to resign as President and gave command of the INA to Subhas Bose on **4 July 1943**; Subhas Bose became Supreme Commander. {19}
- On 26 August 1943 Bose officially took charge of the INA and renamed it Azad Hind Fauj.
- Bose took command of the INA, reorganised it and gave the battle cry **"Chalo Delhi / Delhi Chalo"**. {30}
- In October 1943 in **Singapore**, Bose named the Indian Independence League as Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj). {18}
- Singapore island was then part of British **Malaya**, so the Azad Hind Fauj of 1943 was set up in Malaya. {17}
- Singapore was under Japanese occupation 1942-1945; it became part of Malaysia in 1963.
- On 9 August 1965 the Malaysian Parliament separated Singapore from Malaysia by an agreement; Singapore became a free country that day.
- Head office of the Azad Hind Fauj was at **Rangoon (Yangon)**. {22}
## Provisional Government of Free India
- On **21 October 1943** Bose proclaimed the **Provisional Government of Free India** (Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind, Azad Hind Sarkar) at Singapore. {13,14}
- It was recognised by **9 countries** including Japan. {15}
- The nine: Japan, Germany, Italy, Thailand, Burma (Japanese controlled), Philippines (Japanese-sponsored Second Philippine Republic), Manchukuo (Japanese puppet state in Manchuria), Independent State of Croatia, Japanese-controlled government in China.
- **Indonesia** did not recognise the Azad Hind Government. {16}
- After freeing the Andaman and Nicobar Islands from the British, Bose hoisted his flag there and named Andaman **"Shaheed"** and Nicobar **"Swaraj"** Island. {32}
## Regiments and slogans
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** formed the **Rani of Jhansi Regiment**, the women's regiment of the INA, in 1943. {27,28}
- It was named after Rani Lakshmi Bai of Jhansi; it aimed to overthrow British rule with help from Imperial Japan.
- Other INA units: **Subhash Brigade, Nehru Brigade, Azad Brigade and Gandhi Brigade**.
- In Burma (Myanmar) Bose told his soldiers: **"Give me blood and I promise you freedom"** (Tum mujhe khoon do, main tumhe azadi dunga). {24}
- From the same speech: "It is blood alone that can avenge the blood that the enemy has spilt. It is blood alone that can pay the price of freedom."
- Bose also said the ultimate sacrifice of life remained and that priests ready to give their lives to the Goddess of freedom were needed.
- The slogan **"Jai Hind"** was given by **S.C. Bose**; it was the greeting/salute of the Azad Hind Fauj and is now the nation's slogan. {31}
- "Jai Hind" was suggested by **Abid Hasan**, Bose's secretary, and formally adopted as the greeting in the Azad Hind Fauj.
## People linked and not linked with the INA
- Associated with Azad Hind Fauj: Major General Shahnawaz Khan, Colonel Prem Kumar Sahgal, Colonel Shaukat Ali Malik; **Major Kartar Singh** was not. {20}
- **S.A. Dange** (Shripad Amrut Dange) was not linked with the INA; he belonged to the Communist Movement in India. {21}
- **B.C. Dutta** was not related to the INA. {23}
- **Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon** were officers of the Indian National Army tried in the Red Fort trial of 1945. {43}
## INA trials at Red Fort
- INA officers were court-martialled at the **Red Fort, Delhi**, in **November 1945**. {41,42}
- Azad Hind Fauj week was observed 5-11 November 1945 and **Azad Hind Fauj Day on 12 November 1945**. {33}
- Colonel Prem Sahgal, Colonel Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon and Major General Shahnawaz Khan, prisoners of war in Singapore, faced a joint court-martial.
- Charges: waging war against the King (treason), torture, murder, attempt to murder and abetment to murder during World War II.
- Prem Sahgal, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon and Shah Nawaz were sentenced to transportation for life.
- **Abdul Rashid (Rashid Ali)** was sentenced to **7 years** imprisonment. {34}
- **Mohan Singh** did not face the Red Fort trials. {35}
- Congress formed the **INA Defence Committee**; **Bhulabhai Desai** was its leader and the leading defence counsel. {36}
- Advocates on the Committee: Bhulabhai Desai, **Tej Bahadur Sapru**, Kailash Nath Katju, **Asaf Ali** and Jawaharlal Nehru. {37,39,40}
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** did not advocate in the INA Red Fort trial. {38}
- Muslim League also joined the all-India agitation over the trials.
- Countrywide protests and party pressure forced Commander-in-Chief Field Marshal Sir **Claude Auchinleck** to commute the three life sentences and free the other INA soldiers.

## Nehru and guerrilla war plan
- At the Congress Working Committee meeting at Allahabad (Prayagraj; printed date April-May 1947), **Jawaharlal Nehru** got a majority for his plan of guerrilla war against the Japanese. {46}
- This rested on India's clear stand against Nazism, Fascism and imperialism, which Nehru opposed.
