@ 27 | Bhakti and Sufi Movement
## Bhakti movement: origin and features
- Devotional worship had a long history of almost a thousand years across the subcontinent.
- Bhakti movement = Hindu religious movement whose main spiritual practice is loving devotion, among the Shaiva Nayanars and Vaishnava Alvars.
- The earliest Bhakti movements (6th century) were led by the Vaishnava **Alvars** and the Shaiva Nayanars of the South; Alvar saints started the Bhakti movement. {1}
- The early medieval Bhakti movement began in the Dravida region; spread north in the late medieval ages (13th–14th centuries onwards), when north India was under Islamic rule.
- The movement spread with (elsewhere: was started by) the great philosopher Adi Shankaracharya in South India in the 8th century; he propounded pure monism 'Advaitavada' to remove the influence of Buddhism.
- Bhakti movement was **reborn in the 15th–16th century AD**, encouraged by Kabir, Nanak, Tulsi, Sur and Mirabai. {2}
- Buddha and Mirabai shared the belief that the world is full of sorrow; sorrow is the first of the Four Arya Truths of Buddhism. {3}
- Bhakti saints gave their verses in regional and local languages that followers could understand; this was the main reason local languages developed. {5}
- Bhakti saints' conduct was of a very high standard; many toured the country and met people of different views.
- Bhakti saints helped the advance of Hindi, Kannada, Punjabi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil and other languages.

- **Ramanujacharya** = earliest propagator of the Bhakti movement; his philosophy is called Vishishtadvaita in Sanskrit. {10}
- Vishishtadvaita literally = "non-duality of reality as characterized by attributes".
- Central idea of Vishishtadvaita: there is an ultimate principle, an absolute being, the source and substratum of all that exists.
- The followers of Ramanuja are known as **Vaishnava**. {29}
- **Shuddha Advaitavada** ("purely non-dual" philosophy) was propounded by Shri Vallabhacharya, founder and guru of the Vallabha sect. {11}
- Vallabhacharya was the son of Telugu Brahmin Shri Lakshmana Bhatta of the Somyaji clan; said to have developed Vishnu Swami's views into Shuddha Advaita.
- Mahaprabhu Vallabhacharya was born in the forest named **Champaranya** (Vikram Samvat 1535). {12}
- Vallabhacharya, a devotional philosopher, founded the Krishna-centred **Pushti** sect (Pushtimarg), a Vaishnava sect of Hinduism. {14,17}
- Vallabhacharya was the second saint of the Krishnamargi branch.
- Nimbarkacharya is related to the 'Sanaka' sect.
- Nimbarka = Hindu philosopher and commentator; spread the Vaishnava doctrine of bhedabheda dvaitadvaita (duality in unity).
- Nimbarka's dates are much disputed; generally believed to be 11th–12th century, so not a contemporary of Akbar. {81}
- Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi (1564–1624 AD) was born after Kabir (1398–1518), so Kabir was not influenced by him.
- Madhvacharya's Dvaitavada: the individual soul (atman), the material world and the Supreme Being (Paramatma) are distinct and fundamentally different.
- Madhvacharya followed **Saguna** Bhakti, not Nirguna; held Lord Vishnu as the supreme deity and advocated his worship. {16}
- True statements: Vallabhacharya established Pushtimarg; Nimbarka propounded Dvaitadvaita; Ramananda advocated Rama Bhakti.
- Order: Shankaracharya (8th century) → Ramanuja (1017–1137 AD) → Chaitanya (1486–1533 AD). {26}
- Nagarjuna was not a Bhakti proponent; he founded 'Nihilism' in the first/second centuries AD. {52}
- Tukaram, Tyagaraja and Vallabhacharya belonged to the medieval Bhakti movement.
## Shankaradeva
- Kamarupa = ancient Indian state situated roughly in Assam.
- Vaishnavism was popularised in Kamarupa, and introduced in Assam and Cooch Behar, by **Shankaradeva**, founder of the Ekasarana sect. {7,8,9}
- Ekasarana sect stressed devotion to one deity, Krishna (identified with Vishnu); opposed both rituals and idolatry.
- Shankaradeva was known as the 'Chaitanya of Assam'.
## Ramananda
- **Ramananda** = promoter of the Bhakti movement in northern India; great saint of North India.
- Ramananda was born in 1299 AD at **Prayag** (Prayagraj) in an orthodox Kanyakubja Brahmin family; educated at Prayag and Varanasi. {19}
- He believed in 'Saguna' God; spread the worship of Rama and Sita among the masses; changed the spiritual landscape of northern India.
- Ramananda was the **first Bhakti saint to use Hindi** to spread his message. {18}
- Dictum 'Let no man ask a man's sect or caste' is Ramananda's; his pupils were from all castes, religions and sects. {4}

## Kabir and other sants
- **Kabir** (1398–1518 AD) was prominent among the 12 disciples of Ramananda. {20}
- Kabir was born near Varanasi to a widowed Brahmin; brought up by the weaver couple Neeru and Neema.
- **Bijak** = best-known compilation of Kabir's works (couplets); holy book of the Kabirpanthi sect (not of Dadu Dayal). {21}
- "Pothi Padh Padh Jag Mua, Pandit Bhaya Na Koye, Dhai Akhar Prem Ka, Padhe So Pandit Hoye" was written by Kabir Das. {22}
- Sabad, Sakhi and Ramaini are Kabir's compositions; his dialogues with Dharmadasa are in **Amarmul (Anurag Sagar)**. {23}
- Saint **Malukdas** was born in the family of Lala Sundar Das Khatri in 1574 AD at Kada (now Kaushambi district, U.P.). {24}
- Guru Ghasidas was born on 18 December 1756 AD at Giraudpuri village, Balodabazar-Bhatapara district, Chhattisgarh.
- Guru Ghasidas's parents: **Mahangudas** and Amarautin. {25}
- Dadu Dayal lived 1544–1603 AD.
- Tyagaraja lived 1767–1847 AD; devotee poet of the Bhakti path and a great musician of Carnatic music.

## Guru Nanak
- Guru Nanak was born on April 15, 1469 AD in a Khatri family at Talwandi, Punjab, now called **Nankana Sahib**. {30}
- Nankana Sahib = city and capital of Nankana Sahib district, western Punjab province (now in Pakistan).
- Guru Nanak died in 1539 AD at Kartarpur (Derababa Nanak), now in Pakistan.
- Guru Nanak (1469–1539 AD) founded the Sikh religion in the reign of **Sikandar Lodi** (1489–1517 AD). {31}
- Nanak believed in monotheism; stressed worship of the one formless God (Ik Onkar), Nirakar Brahma; did not believe in anthropomorphism.
- Guru Nanak's saying: "God does not ask about social class or birth; you must find your true home." {32}
- Guru Nanak started the free community kitchen **Guru Ka Langar**; followers ate together without caste distinction.
- Guru Nanak was preaching when the Lodi dynasty fell and Babur took over (1526, first Battle of Panipat, Babur beat Ibrahim Lodi). {39}
## Mirabai
- Mirabai was the only daughter of Ratan Singh Rathore of Merta; born in a royal family in 1498 AD at village Kudaki, Merta, Rajasthan.
- Mirabai was married to Prince **Bhojraj**, eldest son of Rana Sanga of Mewar. {34}
- Bhojraj died after a few years; then Mirabai spent her whole life worshipping Lord Krishna.
- Mirabai was a contemporary of Tulsidas (1532–1623), Guru Nanak (1469–1539) and Chaitanya (1486–1533); not of Ramakrishna (1836–1886).
- 'Rag-Govind' was written by Mirabai. {35}
## Maharashtra saints: Namdev, Varkari sect, Tukaram
- **Namdev** helped popularise the Bhakti movement in Maharashtra; born in 1270 AD at Narsi village (Maharashtra).
- Namdev's teacher Visoba Khechar trained him for mystic life and showed him the omnipresent form of God.
- Namdev belonged to the **Varkari sect**; had an important role in founding its ideology and glorious tradition. {49}
- Among Bhakti saints Namdev was much **influenced by Islam**; opposed idolatry, fasting, pilgrimage and harsh physical practices. {38}
- Namdev: "One stone is lovingly decorated (worshipped), another is walked upon; if one is a God, the other must also be a God."
- Main seat (Peeth) of the Varkari sect = **Pandharpur** (Maharashtra), on the banks of River Chandrabhaga (Bhima) in Solapur district. {50}
- Varkari = religious movement within the Bhakti tradition of Vaishnavite Hinduism, linked with Maharashtra.
- Varkaris worship Vitthal, presiding deity of Pandharpur, regarded as a form of Krishna.
- **Tukaram** (born 1608, died 1650 AD) was a contemporary of Jahangir (1605–1627) and Shahjahan (1628–1658). {51}

## Chaitanya
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534 AD in the chapter notes) was born in Nadia district of Bengal in a Brahmin family; one of the greatest Bhakti saints.
- His father = Jagannatha Mishra; mother = Shachi Devi; childhood name = Nimai (Vishwambhar Mishra).
- Chaitanya was a follower of Lord Krishna and preached his devotion (**Vaishnava** sect); also lived in Vrindavan for many days. {41}
- Chaitanya Mahaprabhu founded the Madhyagauriya sect.
## Tulsidas and Hindi poets
- Goswami **Tulsidas** was a contemporary of Akbar and Jahangir; Humayun, Akbar and Jahangir were emperors in his lifetime. {42}
- Tulsidas had no relation with the court; spent most of his time in Banaras (Varanasi); mentioned in Abul Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari.
- **Ramcharitmanas** was composed by Goswami Tulsidas (1532–1623 AD) in Awadhi. {43}
- Tulsidas wrote Gitavali, Kavitavali and **Vinay Patrika**; Ramcharitmanas and Vinay Patrika are his best works; 'Sahitya Ratna' is not his. {44,45}
- **Sahitya Lahari** was composed by Surdas, not Tulsidas. {46}
- Tulsidas was born at Rajapur village in **Chitrakoot** district (not Banda); his wife's name was **Ratnawali** (not Geetawali). {47}
- U.P. Government will develop Rajapur village in Chitrakoot district, the birthplace of Tulsidas.
- **Rasik Priya** is a famous work of Acharya Keshavdas (not Raskhan); his other famous works: Ramchandrika and Kavi Priya. {48}

- Raskhan = poet and follower of Lord Krishna; real name Sayyid Ibrahim; 'Sujan Raskhan' is his other famous text; his language is pure Braj.
- Rahim and Raskhan were poets of Hindi literature in the medieval Bhakti period, not Sufis.
- **Barahmasa** was written by Malik Muhammad Jayasi; it is a part of Padmavat. {93}
- Jayasi's works: Padmavat (important place in Hindi literature), Barahmasa, Akharawat, Akhiri Kalam.

## Sufism: basics and terms
- In medieval India the emergence of Sufis helped promote (communal) harmony in society.
- Sufi practices: meditation, remembrance, devotional practices like 'Sama', sometimes breath-focused remembrance (control of breath), severe ascetic exercises in a lonely place.
- Sufis used holy songs and music to spread unity and harmony and to arouse spiritual ecstasy in their audience. {6}
- Sufism was born in a foreign land within early Islamic asceticism; later mainly influenced by Indian Vedanta philosophy.
- Later some strands of Sufism developed theosophical systems, adopting traditions such as Neoplatonism.
- Four philosophies some believe influenced Sufism: Advaitavada and Vishishta Dvaitavada of Aryans; New Aflatuni theory; liberal thinking; deep-rooted knowledge of Islam.
- **Khanqah** = residence of Sufi saints; **Sama** = Sufi ceremony (music assembly); **Shaikh** = person authorised to teach and guide in Sufism.
- **Ulema** = scholars of the religious laws of Islam; not related to Sufism. {90}
- In the medieval period many Sanskrit works on music were translated into Persian, owing to the interest of rulers. {54}
- Mahmud of Ghazni took many books to Ghazni; Firuz Tughluq and Sikandar Lodi had Sanskrit books translated into Persian; later Mughal emperors did so too.
- Early Chishtis were fond of 'sama'; they supported music as a medium to reach God (true, but not the reason for the translations).
- In India Hath Yog was practised by **Nathpanthis**; later Sufis also adopted Hath Yog practices. {80}
- Nathpanth founder Guru Matsyendranath and his disciple started the practice of Hath Yog.
## Chishti order
- Chishti order was founded at Chishti Sharif, Afghanistan, by Abu Ishaq Shami Chishti and his disciple **Khwaja Abu Ahmad Abdal**. {58}
- Khwaja Abu Ishaq Shami of Syria migrated to Chist (Chishti Sharif, Afghanistan), hence called Chishti; 9th-generation descendant of Hazrat Ali.
- Chishti order's name is from Chist in Afghanistan, not from a village in Ajmer; Muinuddin Chishti was its sponsor and most prominent figure in India. {64}
- Chishti order was established in India by **Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti** (first Chishti saint in India); he made Ajmer (Rajasthan) his abode. {55,56,57}
- Muinuddin Chishti came to India with Muhammad Ghori's army in 1192 AD (12th century); died in 1236 AD.
- Muinuddin Chishti was born in Sijistan (Afghanistan and Iran) in 1138–39 AD; later moved with his parents to Khurasan province.
- Muinuddin Chishti became a disciple of **Khwaja Usman Chishti Haruni** at Nishapur. {59,60}
- Muinuddin Chishti held that devotional music was one way to come closer to God. {65}
- Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki was Muinuddin Chishti's main disciple; a Chishti saint from Delhi.
- Raja **Sahu**, grandson of Shivaji, was the first Maratha chieftain to offer nazr (gift) at the Dargah of Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti, Ajmer. {61}
- Chishti order = a Sunni Sufi order within the mystic Sufi tradition of Islam.
- Shaikh Abdul Jilani did not belong to the Chishti order; he founded the Qadiri order. {66}
- **Baba Farid** (Shaikh Fariduddin Ganj-i-Shakar) = Chishti saint; Chishti order gained more popularity in India by his efforts.
- Some of Baba Farid's compositions were included by Guru Arjan in the **Adi Granth** (Guru Granth Sahib). {72}
- Baba Farid was the son-in-law of Balban; born in Kothewal village, Multan district (Pakistan); Hansi and Ajodhan were his centres.
- Baba Farid was the spiritual teacher of **Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya**, his most reputed disciple. {62,71}
- Baba Farid's two main disciples: Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya and Hazrat Alauddin Sabir Kaliyari.
- Nizamuddin Auliya was born in Badayun, UP; first lived with Baba Farid at Ajodhan (Pakistan), later shifted to Delhi.
- Nizamuddin Auliya's Dargah is in **Delhi** (not Multan); it is one of the most revered places in the Sufi order; he died in 1325 AD and was buried at Ghiyaspur, Delhi. {63,77}
- Correct pairs: Shaikh Moinuddin Chishti – Ajmer; Shaikh Burhanuddin Gharib – Daulatabad; Shaikh Mohammad Hussaini – Gulbarga.
- Nizamuddin Auliya saw the reign of more than seven sultans but never went to anyone's court.
- Sultan Jalaluddin tried to meet him through Amir Khusrau; on hearing the Sultan was coming, he moved to Ajodhan.
- He refused to meet **Alauddin Khalji**: "There are two doors in my house; if the Sultan comes by one, I will go out by the other." {68}
- Nizamuddin Auliya was known as **Mehboob-i-Ilahi** (Favourite of God) and Sultan-ul-Mashaikh (King of Saints). {69,70}
- Nizamuddin Auliya, like Baba Farid, gave peace to human hearts through his preaching.
- He was called Mehboob-i-Ilahi for his generous, tolerant attitude; he adopted Yoga and was called 'Yogasiddha'.
- Nizamuddin Auliya and Muinuddin Chishti were Sufi saints of the Chishti order. {82}
- Shaikh Nasiruddin Mahmud, a chief disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya, had the title **Chirag-i-Dehlavi** (Chirag-i-Delhi). {75,76}
- Nasiruddin preached the teachings of his Pir but could not be as generous as him.
- **Shaikh Salim Chishti** = notable among the last Chishti Sufis; son of Shaikh Bahauddin.
- He lived in Arabia for several years and got the title **Shaikh-ul-Hind**. {74}
- On return he lived at **Sikri**, about 37 km from Agra, later developed by Akbar as Fatehpur Sikri. {73}
- Jahangir is said to have been born with the blessings of Shaikh Salim Chishti; Akbar (not Aurangzeb) was his contemporary.

- Chishti influence: Delhi, Rajasthan and nearby areas (Delhi and Doab); Suhrawardi influence: Sindh and Punjab.
- 'Auliya' is not an order but a title given to Nizamuddin, the famous saint of Delhi; his dominion was in Delhi (not Madhya Pradesh). {85}
- **Shaikh Bahauddin Zakaria** started the Suhrawardi order in India. {88}
- Shaikh Sadr-ud-din Arif, Shaikh Rakh-ud-din Abul Fateh and Shaikh Jalaluddin Surkh were linked with the Suhrawardi sect.
- **Suhrawardi** order entered India about the same time as the Chishtis; its activities stayed largely in Punjab, Multan and Sindh. {89}
- **Firdausi** (Firdausia) Silsila was established in Bihar; its most prominent Sufi and key propagator in Bihar = Sheikh Sharfuddin Yahya Maneri. {86}
- Firdausi order traces its origin to Najmuddin Kubra; treated as a collateral line of the Suhrawardiya; later close to the Chishtis in India (called a part of the Chishti order).
- **Qadiri** order was founded by Shaikh Muhiuddin Abdul Qadir Jilani of Baghdad, counted among the great saints of Islam.
- Abdul Qadir Jilani's titles: Mehboob-i-Sumani (God's lover), Piran-i-Pir (Chief of Pirs), Pir-i-Dastagir (Helpful Saint).
- Qadiri order was preached in India in the 15th century by Shah Niamatullah (Deccan and Kashmir) and Makhdum Muhammad Jilani (Punjab), who first introduced it in India. {79}
- Makhdum Jilani made Uchha his education centre.
- **Naqshbandi** order was founded by Khwaja Bahauddin Naqshband in the 14th century.
- Khwaja Khawand Mahmud, a prominent Naqshbandi saint, came to India and made Kashmir his centre.
- Naqshbandi order was mainly spread by Khwaja Baqi Billah (1563–1603), who came from Kabul on his master's orders.
- Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi, called 'Mujaddid' / Mujaddid Alif Sani (reformer of Islam), was Baqi Billah's main disciple; contemporary of Akbar and Jahangir (not Ibrahim Lodi).
- Sirhindi propounded **Wahdat-ul-Shuhud** (positivist philosophy) over the mystic philosophy Wahdat-ul-Wajud.
- **Naqshbandi** order was against music (Sama); the most radical/orthodox Sufi order. {83,84}
- The Sirhindi/Mujaddadi Naqshbandi branch opposed Sama, but the Ferozabadi (Delhi) branch practised Sama.
- Naqshbandis opposed Akbar's liberal policies; Aurangzeb was influenced by this order.
- Naqshbandi order was made popular in India by Babur; its essence = rigid adherence to Shariat and love for the Prophet.

- Sufi saint **Shah Muhammad Ghaus** accepted Krishna as Wali/Auliya; most famous saint of the Shattari (Sattariyya) order. {87}
- Muhammad Ghaus had close relations with Mughal emperor Humayun and with Tansen.
- His famous work 'Jawahir-i-Khamsa' expresses his spiritual quest and mystical practices.
- He translated 'Amritkund' of Hatha Yoga into Persian as 'Bahar-al-Hayat'; it mentions Hath Yog practices.
- Shattari saints tried to reconcile Muslims and Hindus by showing equality in religious ideas and practices.
- **Dewa Sharif** (Barabanki, U.P.) = mazar of Sufi saint Haji Waris Ali Shah; a fair is held at the tomb every year. {95}
## Christianity and Islam: general facts
- Jesus Christ was born in **Bethlehem** (south of Jerusalem). {96}
- **Easter** = Christian festival of the resurrection of Jesus Christ on the third day after his crucifixion. {97}
- **Good Friday** is observed in remembrance of the sacrificial death of Jesus Christ; by the Bible he was crucified on a Friday. {99}
- **Saint Francis of Assisi** (1181 or 82–1226 AD) = Christian saint famous for his love of birds and animals. {98}
- Vatican City = sovereign state in Europe; area about 44 hectares; world's smallest independent country by both population and area.
- Vatican City is governed by the Bishop of Rome (the Pope); also known as the capital of the Roman Catholic Church.
- Medina = city in the Hejaz region of western Saudi Arabia; Islam's second holiest city after Mecca.
