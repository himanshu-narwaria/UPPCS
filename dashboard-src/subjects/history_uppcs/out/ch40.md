## Bahadur Shah I and Jahandar Shah
- After Aurangzeb's death in 1707 A.D. his 63-year-old son Muazzam (Shah Alam) took the throne as **Bahadur Shah I**. {1}
- Khafi Khan gave Bahadur Shah I the title **Shah-e-Bekhabar**.
- **Jahandar Shah** was the first Mughal emperor who proved weak and unfit for governance.
- Jahandar Shah got the throne with the help of the powerful Amir Zulfiqar Khan.
- Jahandar Shah was defeated in battle by his nephew Farrukh Siyar and killed in February 1713. {2}
- People called Jahandar Shah 'lewd Moron'.
## Farrukh Siyar and Sayyid brothers
- **Farrukh Siyar** by a farman of 1717 A.D. granted duty-free trading facilities to the English in Bengal. {3}
- Under it the East India Company could trade without duty on paying an annual tax of Rs. 3000.
- In the later Mughal period, nobles who came to power were called king-makers.
- **Sayyid brothers Hussain Ali and Abdullah** were the 'King Makers'; most powerful from 1713 to 1720 AD. {4}
- Sayyid brothers were leaders of the Hindustani group.
- They made Farrukh Siyar king in 1713 AD and dethroned him in 1719 AD.
- Then puppet kings Rafi-ud-Darjat, Rafi-ud-Daula and Muhammad Shah sat on the throne with their help.
- Sayyid brothers lost power during Muhammad Shah's reign.
## Muhammad Shah and Nadir Shah
- **Muhammad Shah** (1719-1748 A.D.) was the last Mughal emperor to sit on the Peacock Throne (Mayur Singhasan / Takht-i-Taus), built by Shah Jahan. {5}
- Nadir Shah attacked India in **1739 A.D.** and defeated the Mughals in the battle of Karnal. {7}
- Muhammad Shah was the Mughal emperor at the time of Nadir Shah's attack. {6}
- The invasion ended in the sack of Delhi: wide destruction, looting and loss of many lives.
- It hastened Mughal decline and the rise of regional powers.
- Contemporary historian Anand Ram Mukhlis: Nadir Shah took to Iran sixty thousand rupees, many thousand gold coins, gold of one crore rupees, jewels of fifty crores, the Kohinoor and the Peacock Throne.
- Mukhlis remarked: "the accumulated wealth of 348 years changed owners in a moment."
- Muhammad Shah spent most of his time watching animal fights.
- People called Muhammad Shah **Rangeela** for his obsession with wine and beauty and apathy to administration. {9}
- A group of eunuchs and ladies dominated the Mughal Darbar (government) under Muhammad Shah (1719-48). {8}
## Shah Alam II
- **Shah Alam II** (1759-1806 A.D.): real name Ali Gauhar.
- Shah Alam II was not allowed to enter Delhi by Wazir Ghaziuddin; he was a victim of court conspiracies. {12}
- Shah Alam II reached Delhi with Maratha help in 1772 A.D.
- The English captured Delhi in 1803 A.D. during his rule.
## Bahadur Shah Zafar
- The last Mughal emperor was **Bahadur Shah Zafar** / Bahadur Shah II (1837-1857 A.D.). {14}
- His father was **Akbar II** (Akbar Shah II, 1806-1837 A.D.). {13}
- Zafar was called the 'Emperor without Empire'.
- Zafar was a pensioner of the British and head of the royal court of the Red Fort, Delhi; he died in 1862 A.D.
- Ibrahim Zauq and Asad Ullah Khan Ghalib taught poetry to Zafar.
- Hasan Askari, a Sufi saint, was Zafar's Pir (spiritual guide).
- Zafar got Rs 1 lakh per month pension from the East India Company (not less than one lakh). {15}
- Pension: Rs 99,000 given in Delhi and Rs 1,000 for regular family expenses in Lucknow.
- "The rule of the Lord of the world extends from Delhi to Palam" was said about the last Tughlaq ruler Nasiruddin Mahmud, not Zafar. {16}

## Awadh
- **Saadat Khan** was the first Nawab of Oudh (Awadh). {17}
- Turanis established Hyderabad and Iranis established Oudh.
- Saadat Khan was appointed governor of Oudh in 1722 A.D.; he governed through his heir, nephew Safdarjung, and played high politics at court.
- After Saadat Khan's death, Safdarjung refused even nominal loyalty to the Mughal Emperor.
- Independent Awadh state was formed in Muhammad Shah's reign; Saadat Khan was its founder and had joined the conspiracy against the Sayyid brothers. {18}
- Saadat Khan sided with Muhammad Shah against the Sayyid brothers; pleased, Muhammad Shah gave him the title **Burhan-ul-Mulk**.
## Hyderabad and other states
- **Chin Qilich Khan** alias Nizam-ul-Mulk founded the independent Kingdom of Hyderabad in 1724 A.D. {19}
- Nizam-ul-Mulk defeated and killed Mughal governor Mubariz Khan in the Battle of Sakhar-Kheda (Deccan), 1724 A.D., and became the real king.
- Qilich Khan was called Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah (he was not Mubariz Khan).
- Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah gave Nizam-ul-Mulk the title Asaf-Jah (not Zulfiqar Khan).
- Nizamat of Arcot was set up by Aurangzeb; it was a legal dependency of the Nizam of Hyderabad, not an offshoot of Hyderabad state.
- **Mysore** Kingdom emerged out of the Vijayanagara Empire: powerful under the Wodeyar dynasty, first a vassal of Vijayanagar. {21}
- Ali Muhammad Khan set up the petty kingdom of Rohilkhand, using the collapse of authority in north India after Nadir Shah's invasion (1739 A.D.).
- Ahmad Shah Durrani began invading India in 1748 A.D.; Rohilkhand was not formed from his territories.
## Sawai Jai Singh
- Sawai Jai Singh (Jai Singh II) (1688-1743 A.D.) of Amber (Kachhwaha) was a great Rajput king of the 18th century.
- He founded **Jaipur** city and made it a centre of science and art.
- **Jai Singh II**, a renowned astronomer, built the Jantar Mantar observatory in Delhi. {22}
- He built well-equipped observatories at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Mathura and Varanasi. {23}
- He did not build an observatory at Allahabad. {25}
- He prepared a set of astronomical charts named **Jiz Muhammad Shahi**. {24}
- He patronised the translation of Euclid's 'Geometry' into Sanskrit.
- He performed two Ashvamedh sacrifices during his reign.
