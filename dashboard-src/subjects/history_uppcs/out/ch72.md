@ 72 | Civil Disobedience Movement
## Start of the movement
- **Lahore Session** (1929) authorised the Congress Working Committee to launch Civil Disobedience. {1}
- February 1930: CWC's second meeting at Sabarmati Ashram gave the leadership of the movement to Mahatma Gandhi.
- Before the movement Gandhi put 11 demands before Viceroy Irwin, including abolition of the Salt Tax and of government monopoly on salt.
- Britain's Salt Act of 1882 barred Indians from collecting or selling salt; they could only buy it.
- The Government had banned sale of salt and taxed it, which hit the poor hard.
- Gandhi launched the salt agitation in 1930; his object was not to make salt free for the poor. {20}
- Aims of the Salt Satyagraha: break (repeal) the salt laws and curtail the government's power; Purna Swaraj was not its aim. {10}
## Dandi March
- **Dandi March** began on 12 March 1930. {4,5}
- It started from **Sabarmati Ashram**. {6,7}
- Gandhi walked with 80 chosen volunteers, 241 miles, to Dandi, a coastal village in Navsari district, Gujarat.
- The march lasted 24 days; it reached Dandi on 5 April 1930. {17}
- On 6 April 1930 Gandhi broke the Salt Law at Dandi by picking up a lump (handful) of salt; also given as making salt by boiling seawater. {3,19}
- Dandi March was started to break the Salt Law. {15,16}
- The Civil Disobedience Movement began with the Dandi March. {2,14}
- Gandhi started the Civil Disobedience Movement from **Dandi** in 1930. {8,13}
! BPSC's final answer key gave Sabarmati for where Gandhi started Civil Disobedience in 1930; the book calls that incorrect and keys Dandi.
- Not correct about the march: that Gandhi prepared salt at the seashore; it was an all-pedestrian march from Sabarmati ending at Dandi village by the sea. {24}
- Not correct: that the march started on 15 March 1930; it started on 12 March and took 24 days. {25}
- **Gujarat** sent the highest number of satyagrahis in the Dandi March. {9}
- "I want world sympathy in this battle of Right against Might": Gandhi at Dandi, 5 April 1930, to Indian and foreign journalists. {22}
- "Sedition has become my religion": Gandhi during the Salt Satyagraha–Dandi March (speech of 18 March 1930). {23}
- Gandhi said the British Empire deserved sedition, not loyalty, so he made sedition "My Dharma".
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** compared the Dandi March with Napoleon's march from Elba to Paris.
- English editor **Brailsford** mocked: "Can an Emperor be unseated by boiling sea water in a kettle?"
- The Statesman replied: "Mr. Gandhi can boil the seawater till they got the Dominion Status".

## Arrests and Dharsana
- Convicted for breaking the salt law: **Madan Mohan Malviya**, Devadas Gandhi, K.M. Munshi, B.G. Kher, Mahadev Desai, C. Rajagopalachari, Vithalbhai Patel; Gandhi too was arrested. {21}
- Gandhi was arrested on 5 May 1930, before the planned raid on **Dharsana** salt works, and sent to **Yerwada Jail**. {27}
- **Abbas Tayabji** led the movement after Gandhi's arrest (also given: Gandhi was arrested at Sholapur). {26}
- **Sarojini Naidu** led the movement after Abbas Tayabji's arrest and led the Dharsana salt depot raid on 21 May 1930.
- **Webb Miller**, American journalist, stayed with Gandhi at Sabarmati Ashram during the Dandi March and reported the Dharsana Satyagraha. {29,30,31}
- Webb Miller wrote that in 18 years of reporting riots and revolts he had never seen such a horrible event as Dharsana.
- Gandhi asked the Dharsana marchers not to resist, not even raise a hand against blows.
- **Acharya Vinoba Bhave**, a close ally of Gandhi, was first arrested in 1930 for joining the Civil Disobedience Movement. {28}
## Women
- Sarojini Naidu, Rajkumari Amrit Kaur and Kamladevi Chattopadhyaya all took part in Gandhi's Salt Satyagraha. {11}
- Women's participation is considered maximum in the Salt Satyagraha (Civil Disobedience Movement). {12}
- Gandhi saw a supportive role for women, but they demanded a more active one; he chose them for picketing liquor and foreign cloth shops for their capacity for non-violence.
- **Dr. Radhabai** was arrested on 13 June 1932 while leading a procession in the Second Civil Disobedience Movement. {44}
- Dr. Radhabai was at the forefront of all of Gandhi's movements.
## Spread in the regions
- **C. Rajagopalachari** led the Vedaranyam March (April 1930) from Trichinopoly (Tiruchirappalli) to Vedaranyam on the Tanjore coast to break the Salt Law. {32}
- Vedaranyam is now on the Vedaranyam beach of Nagapattinam district; the march was the second of its kind after Dandi; marchers collected salt from the sea.
- **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** founded the **Khudai Khidmatgar** (Red Shirts), a volunteer body in NWFP (organised in 1929), and led it. {35,36}
- Khudai Khidmatgars called for Pathan regional nationalist unity and a struggle against colonialism. {33}
- The Red Shirt organisation was founded to throw out the British. {34}
- They also sought better conditions for wage-earners (labourers).
- NWFP Muslims played an important role in Civil Disobedience under Ghaffar Khan; Muslims of other provinces stayed away.
- **Peshawar Incident** (1930): Garhwal Rifles soldiers under **Chandra Singh Garhwali** refused to fire on an unarmed crowd during Civil Disobedience. {37,38}
- **Jiatrang Movement** arose in **Manipur**, led by Naga tribal woman Rani Gaidinliu during Civil Disobedience (1930s). {39}
- Agitation against Chaukidari Tax in Begusarai was part of the Civil Disobedience Movement. {40}
- In Bihar, besides making salt, people opposed the Chowkidari tax; farmers refused to pay it in 1930. {42}
- Chowkidari tax was collected from farmers to pay village watchmen (chowkidars), a kind of police.
- Salt Satyagraha at Bihpur, Bhagalpur was led by **Mahadev Lal Sarraf**, Deep Narayan Singh, Rameshwar Narayan Agrawal, Kailash Bihari Lal and Zeharul Hasan Hashmi. {41}
- **Prabhavati Devi** was a freedom fighter from Patna. {45}
- Bihar women in the freedom movement: Prabhavati Devi, Rajvanshi Devi, Suniti Devi and Radhika Devi.
## After the movement
- After the failure of Civil Disobedience, Gandhiji gave importance to constructive programmes. {43}
- 17 September 1934: Gandhi announced his resignation from Congress by public statement; October 1934: he formally retired from active politics for Harijan uplift.
- September 1932: Gandhi founded the **All India Anti-Untouchability League**; 1933: he started the weekly **'Harijan'**.
