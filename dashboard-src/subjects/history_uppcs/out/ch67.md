@ 67 | Khilafat Movement
## Background and aims
- Indian Muslims considered the Sultan of Turkey the **Khalifa (Caliph)** of the Islamic empire.
- In the First World War Turkey fought against the Allied countries.
- British statesmen promised Indian Muslims fair treatment of Turkey and not to break up its empire, to win their support.
- After the war the Turkish Empire was dismembered by the Treaty of Sevres; the Khalifa was stripped of his post.
- It ended in 1922 (after Treaty of Lausanne, 1922): the last Ottoman Sultan was deposed and left Constantinople in a British warship.
- Main objectives of the Khilafat Movement: rouse anti-British feelings among Indian Muslims; save the Ottoman Empire and preserve the Khilafat. {3}
## Leaders and committee
- Khilafat Movement was mainly started by the **Ali Brothers**: Maulana Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali; Abul Kalam Azad also joined. {1,2}
- Khilafat Committee members: Shaukat Ali, Muhammad Ali, Abul Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani and Dr. Ansari.
- The All India Khilafat Committee was created at Lucknow.
- The Ali brothers, Hasrat Mohani and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad organised a Khilafat Party.
- Haji Shariatullah = Islamic reformer of British India who founded the **Faraizi Movement** (not linked to Khilafat).
- **Hakim Ajmal Khan** gave up the title **Haziq-ul-Mulk** during the Khilafat agitation; the British had given it in 1908. {8}
## Gandhi and the Congress
- **Mahatma Gandhi** was elected President of the All India Khilafat Conference at Delhi (23–24 November 1919) on 24 November; it was chaired by Fazlul Haq. {4}
- Gandhiji supported Khilafat to win Indian Muslims' support against the British. {5,6}
- Gandhi saw Khilafat as a golden opportunity for Hindu-Muslim unity that would not come again in a hundred years. {7}
- Congress supported the Khilafat Movement mainly for reinstatement of the Caliph and getting the sympathy of Muslims. {13}
- The movement gained popularity and an aggressive pace when Gandhiji joined it.
- The Amritsar Congress session of December 1919 gave a great fillip to the Khilafat agitation.
- Result of Khilafat: Hindu-Muslim differences narrowed; unity arose from anger against the British over Turkey. {11}
- Gandhi suggested non-violent non-cooperation to the Khilafat Committee in 1920; in June 1920 the Committee at **Allahabad** (now Prayagraj) accepted it unanimously and asked Gandhi to lead. {15}
- September 1920: special Calcutta session under Lala Lajpat Rai adopted the Non-Cooperation resolution.
## Opponents and other facts
- **M.A. Jinnah** (then a Nationalist) opposed linking Khilafat with Swaraj and denounced Gandhi's participation; told him not to bring religion into politics. {10}
- According to K.M. Munshi, Jinnah warned Gandhiji not to encourage the fanaticism of Muslim religious leaders and their followers; Jinnah saw Gandhi's support of Khilafat as an endorsement of religious zealotry. {9}
- **Madan Mohan Malviya** did not support the Khilafat Movement; he opposed Gandhi's endorsement of it. {14}
- Malviya was the foremost educationalist, legal luminary, scholar and editor of pre-Independent India.
- **Swami Shraddhanand** spoke on Hindu-Muslim unity from the pulpit of Jama Masjid, Delhi, on 4 April 1919 before 30,000 Muslims. {12}
- Aitchison's remark "In this instance, we could not play off the Mohammedans against the Hindus" relates to the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement (1919–22). {16}
- In 1919–22 Muslims joined the nationalist stream with unprecedented Hindu-Muslim unity; at this time Jinnah said "we are first Muslims then Indians".
## Moplah Rebellion
- **Moplah Rebellion** (1921) was an offshoot of the Khilafat Movement. {17}
- Moplah peasants revolted against oppressive Hindu landlords allied with the British; a pitched battle with a British regiment killed several Europeans.
- Khilafat meetings and Moplah peasant meetings became hard to tell apart, so the two movements merged.
