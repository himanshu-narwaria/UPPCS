@ 23 | Delhi Sultanate: Art and Architecture
## Use of paper
- Use of paper in India is considered to have started around the **7th–8th century CE**. {1}
- Written documents on paper are found from around the 11th century CE; in the 12th–13th century CE paper was also used in royal scriptures.
! Start of paper use in India: BPSC gave "12th century CE" as the correct answer, though paper use is considered to have started around the 7th–8th century CE.
## Aybak and Iltutmish: early buildings and Qutub Minar
- Qutbuddin Aybak built the **Adhai Din Ka Jhopara** mosque in Ajmer.
- Aybak built the **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque near Raipithora Fort in Delhi (it is in Delhi, not Ajmer). {14}
- Qutbuddin Aybak = first Muslim ruler of the Delhi Sultanate; he started Qutub Minar in Delhi but completed only its basement.
- Qutub Minar was completed by **Iltutmish**.
- In Firuz Shah Tughluq's time the fourth floor of Qutub Minar was damaged; he restored the fourth floor and built the **fifth storey**. {5}
- Alauddin Khalji did NOT contribute to the building of Qutub Minar; Aybak, Iltutmish and Firuz Shah Tughluq did. {4}
- Qutub Minar is named after the famous Sufi saint **Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki**.
- Qutub Minar: built in the 13th century, **238 feet (72.5 m)** high (not 180 feet), tapering tower of five storeys.
- Qutub Minar is a mix of polygonal and circular shapes; built largely of red and buff sandstone, with some marble in the upper storey.
- **Chand Minar**: built in the 15th century, 210 feet high, tapering tower of four storeys. {6}
- Chand Minar is painted peach now; its facade once had chevron patterning on encaustic tile work and bold bands of Quranic verses.
- Iltutmish built Sultangarhi, Hauz-e-Shamsi, Shamsi Eidgaah, Jama Masjid Shamsi (Badayun) and Atarkin-ka-Darwaza (Nagaur).
## Balban's tomb
- Tomb of Sultan Ghiyasuddin Balban: in Mehrauli, New Delhi; built by himself in 1287 AD in rubble masonry.
- Balban's tomb = first tomb in India built in a purely **Indo-Islamic style**; important in the development of Indo-Islamic architecture. {7}
- The **first true arches** in India appeared in the Tomb of Balban. {8}
## Khalji buildings
- **Alai Darwaza** was built by Alauddin Khalji near the Qutub Minar. {2}
- Alai Darwaza: domed entrance of red sandstone adorned with white marble; completed in 1311 AD by Turkish craftsmen.
- Alai Darwaza is one of the first buildings in India in the Indo-Islamic architectural style.
- The **horse-shoe arch** was first introduced in the Alai Darwaza. {3}
- Alauddin Khalji built Hazar Sutun and Hauz-e-Alai in Delhi (Siri fort).
- Ukha Mosque (Bayana, Rajasthan) was built by Malik Kafur Sultani in the reign of Sultan Mubarakshah Khalji.
- Jamat Khana Masjid = oldest monument in the dargah complex of Hazrat Nizamuddin Aulia; built by Khizr Khan, son of Sultan Alauddin Khalji.
## Tughluq and Lodi buildings
- Ghiyasuddin Tughluq (1320–25 AD), after founding the Tughluq dynasty, set up a city in Delhi named **Tughluqabad**.
- The Moth Masjid was built in the reign of Sikandar Lodi.
- Sikandar Lodi's tomb was built by Sultan Ibrahim Lodi.
- Order of building: Qutub Minar (Aybak 1206–10 AD and Iltutmish 1211–36 AD) → Tughluqabad fort (Ghiyasuddin Tughluq 1320–1325 AD) → Lodi Garden (Lodi dynasty 1451–1526 AD) → Fatehpur Sikri (Akbar 1556–1605 AD). {9}
## Kirti Stambha of Chittor
- **Kirti Stambha** of Chittor was built by Rana Kumbha in memory of his victory over Mahmud Khalji of Malwa; it is a unique monument of his achievements. {13}
- Kirti Stambha was built under the supervision of **Jaita**; its panegyrists were Atri and Mahesh.
- Kirti-Stambha Prashasti was composed by **Atri Kavi**. {12}
