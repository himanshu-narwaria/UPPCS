@ 53 | Moderates and Extremists in Congress
## Moderate era
- The first two decades of the Congress (1885–1905) are called the moderate (liberal) era.
- Prominent Moderates: Dadabhai Naoroji, Surendra Nath Banerjee, Mahadev Govind Ranade, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gopal Krishna Gokhale and Anand Mohan Bose.
- Moderates trusted British justice and generosity; they never aimed at aggressive nationalism for independence.
- Moderate leaders supported Europe's rich democratic and scientific culture and strongly criticised the economic aspects of British imperialism. {2}
- Moderates advocated constitutional reforms, political participation and expansion of modern education.
- They supported English education, believing it would build a modern outlook and social progress.
- Dadabhai Naoroji's book "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India" highlighted the economic drain, heavy taxation and decline of Indian industries.
- Moderates tried to address such concerns by constitutional means, not direct confrontation.
- Moderates' method of protest = **constitutional agitation**. {3}
- Moderate methods: annual conferences, speeches, elaborate resolutions, petitions to the British Parliament, deputations to England, appeal to the British sense of justice and fair play.
- Most Moderate leaders came from **urban areas**. {5}
- Urban Moderates named: Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Dinshaw Wacha, Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee and Surendra Nath Banerjee.
- The Moderate-phase Congress was dominated by prosperous and middle-class advocates (barristers), doctors, engineers, journalists and literary people.
- Titles and high posts charmed them; they came from metropolitan cities and had no link with the common masses.
- Pherozeshah Mehta: the Congress voice is not the voice of the masses, but citizens in touch with it must understand and spread these feelings.
- **Dadabhai Naoroji** first demanded 'Swaraj' (self-rule) for India in 1904 at the International Socialist Congress and kept stressing it. {6}
- Naoroji demanded Swaraj in his presidential address at the 1906 Congress session.
## Gokhale
- **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** was a Moderate, not an Extremist. {7,8,9}
- Gokhale believed in the purity of means and ends; he was the political guru of Mahatma Gandhi.
- Gokhale first took part in politics in 1889.
- In 1897 Gokhale and Dinshaw Wacha were asked to give evidence on Indian expenditure before the **Welby Commission**.
- Gokhale was elected to the Bombay Legislative Council in 1899 and to the Imperial Council of the Governor-General in 1901.
- Gokhale led the moderate nationalists; he advocated moderate, constitutional agitation and gradual reform.
- Gokhale was a social reformer, senior Congress leader and founder of the Servants of India Society, which worked for the underprivileged.
## Rise of the Extremists
- International events that influenced the national movement before Gandhi: Italian-Abyssinian War (1898), Boxer Movement in China, revolutionary movement in Ireland, Japan's victory over Russia. {1}
- In the Italian-Abyssinian War the Italian colonial power was defeated; the Boxer movement against imperialists in China ran 1899–1901.
- Japan's defeat of Czarist Russia in 1905 had the most impact; it broke the illusion that the white race was invincible.
- Extremist leaders emerged in the early 1900s; after **1906** extremism came to dominate the national movement. {15}
- Four main Extremist leaders: **Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal and Aurobindo Ghosh**; they were not Moderates. {16,17,18,20,24}
- The neo-nationalist group of Lal, Bal, Pal was disillusioned with the early moderate policies; Tilak pioneered the new ideology.
- Extremist ideology = getting self-government by aggressive means instead of petitions and constitutional ways. {34}
- Extremists' main aim was 'Swaraj'; they believed in self-sacrifice, mass movement, aggressive resistance, strong determination and passive resistance.
- Extremists had no faith in the goodwill of the British public or Parliament, nor in merely holding conferences.
- Swaraj as defined in the Congress presidential address, Calcutta, 1906 = self-government like that of the United Kingdom or the Colonies.
- For Extremists independence meant complete freedom from foreign control; for Moderates, self-rule under the colonial empire.
- **Tilak** accused the Congress of practising the politics of **prayer, petition and protest**. {10,11}
- The Congress policy of prayer and petition ended, or shrank to a minimum, under **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**. {12}
- Tilak was one of the strongest opponents of the British Empire and strongly recommended agitation.
- Many intellectual Congressmen became Tilak's followers; Lala Lajpat Rai of Punjab and Bipin Chandra Pal of Calcutta became his staunch followers and opposed the moderate policies.
- First Congress session: Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, 28–30 December 1885; Extremists commented negatively on the Congress.
- **Bipin Chandra Pal** called the Congress a **"Begging Institute"**. {13}
- Bipin Chandra Pal was a Brahmo Samaj leader and social reformer; he published the weekly 'New India' in 1901. {14}
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** called the Congress conference the **"Annual National Festival of Educated Indians"**. {23}
- Indian Muslims in general stayed away from the Extremist movement because of the Extremists' policy of harping on the Hindu past. {35}
- Aurobindo Ghosh: "Independence is the purpose of our lives and only Hindu religion will fulfil this objective. Nationality is a religion, a gift of God."
## Swadeshi Movement and Surat split
- The **Swadeshi Movement** led to the Congress split into Moderates and Extremists. {4}
- **Surat split**: the Congress divided at the Surat session in December 1907, when revolutionary terrorism was gaining momentum.
- Moderates opposed the resolutions on Swaraj, Swadeshi, national education and boycott of foreign goods; this difference led to the split.
- Moderates believed in settling issues with the Government by deliberation.
- **Aurobindo Ghosh** was the main leader of the Swadeshi Movement (begun 1905 against the partition of Bengal). {25}
- Other Swadeshi leaders: Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra) and Bipin Chandra Pal (Bengal).
- Aurobindo was one of the four pillars of the Extremists (Lal, Bal, Pal, Ghose).
- Aurobindo was arrested in 1908 in the Maniktalla garden raid; given one year's imprisonment but released for lack of evidence.
- He later became a sage, withdrew from revolutionary activity and moved to Pondicherry (Puducherry).
## Lala Lajpat Rai
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** is known as **'Sher-e-Punjab'** and 'Punjab Kesari'; Extremist representative of Punjab. {19}
- Lajpat Rai, Tilak and Bipin Chandra Pal together are called **Lal-Bal-Pal**.
- Lajpat Rai was injured in a lathi charge during a protest against the Simon Commission and died on 17 November 1928.
- After reading his biography, Lajpat Rai accepted the Italian revolutionary **Mazzini** as his political guru. {21}
- He translated Mazzini's 'The Duties of Man' into Urdu.
- Lajpat Rai wrote short biographies of Mazzini, Garibaldi, Shivaji, Shri Krishna and Swami Dayanand Saraswati; he stayed in America and was elected to the Central Assembly. {22}
- He lived in America during his exile from 1907 and returned during the First World War.
- His other works: 'Unhappy India' and 'The Story of My Deportation'.
## Bal Gangadhar Tilak
- Tilak believed in service and sacrifice and dared to challenge the British Empire; staunch supporter of Swadeshi.
- Through his efforts the Congress began to criticise British policies instead of praising British rule.
- **Sir Valentine Chirol** called Tilak the **"Father of Indian Unrest"**. {26,27,28,29}
- Tilak went to England to file a defamation suit against Chirol but lost.
- Tilak founded the English weekly 'The Mahratta' and the Marathi weekly 'Kesari'; both were extreme nationalist papers, which brought him within reach of criminal law; his speeches and writings were judged radical and he was sentenced.
- The Government accused Tilak in the murder case of Rand (Plague Commissioner of Pune) and Ayerst; he got 18 months' rigorous imprisonment.
- He came out of jail a national hero; several events together made him **'Lokmanya'** Tilak. {31}
- After his 1897 sedition conviction and the rejection of his Privy Council appeal, Max Muller and Sir William Hunter led a petition to the Queen.
- **Max Muller** pleaded for mercy for Tilak: "My interest in Tilak is that of a Sanskrit scholar." {30}
- In **1908** Tilak was sentenced to **6 years** for sedition and kept in Mandalay Jail, Burma (Myanmar). {32,33}
- The 1908 charge was for printing an anti-British article in 'Kesari'.
- Tilak wrote **'Gita Rahasya'** in Mandalay prison.
- Tilak declared: "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it."
- Tilak started the **Ganpati festival** and **Shivaji Mahotsav** in Maharashtra to rouse nationalist feeling among the masses. {37,38,39}
- Tilak was not a communalist but a staunch nationalist; he used religion as a political weapon, not for communal ends. {36}
- Tilak's approach of close contact with the people is seen as a forerunner of M.K. Gandhi's.
- Tilak died on 1 August 1920; his bier was lifted by Mahatma Gandhi, **Maulana Shaukat Ali** and Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew. {40}
- Maulana Hasrat Mohani read the elegy after Tilak's death.
