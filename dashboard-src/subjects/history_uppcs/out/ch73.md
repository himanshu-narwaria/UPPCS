@ 73 | Gandhi-Irwin Pact
## Signing of the pact
- Viceroy **Lord Irwin** released Gandhiji from jail on 26 January 1931, seeing the growing Civil Disobedience Movement.
- Gandhi–Irwin talks began in (mid) February 1931 through the efforts of **Tej Bahadur Sapru** and M.R. Jayakar, who acted as mediators. {8,9}
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact** was signed on 5 March 1931, before the Second Round Table Conference in London. {1,4,5}
- The Second Round Table Conference in London was held in the backdrop of the Gandhi-Irwin Pact. {6}
- Main purpose of the pact: make Congress participation in the Round Table Conference easier. {2}
- **Sarojini Naidu** called Irwin and Gandhi 'The Two Mahatmas'. {10}
## Terms
- Congress, under Gandhiji, agreed to withdraw (discontinue) the Civil Disobedience Movement. {7}
- Congress agreed to take part in the Second Round Table Conference; Gandhi also agreed to stop boycotting British goods.
- The pact included: invitation to Congress to the Round Table Conference, withdrawal of ordinances against Civil Disobedience, release of only those prisoners not charged with violence. {3}
- Gandhiji's suggestion for an enquiry into police excesses was not accepted in the pact.
- Withdrawal of all ordinances curbing Congress activities and of all prosecutions except those involving violence.
- Right to peaceful picketing at foreign cloth shops and liquor shops.
- Permission to make salt in coastal areas without Salt Tax.
## Reactions
- People were disappointed: the pact did not commute the death sentences of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru.
- **Alan Campbell Johnson**, Irwin's biographer, called Gandhi's gains in the pact "Consolation Prizes". {11}
- He said Irwin's only surrender was agreeing to negotiate.
