@ 35 | Music and Paintings of Mughal Period
## Beginning of Mughal painting
- Mughal paintings showed battle (war) scenes, animals, birds, natural scenery and courtiers. {1}
- Mughal paintings were a unique blend of Indian, Persian and Islamic painting.
- **Humayun** laid the foundation of Mughal painting during his exile in Persia and Afghanistan. {2}
- In exile Humayun met two noted painters: **Khwaja Abdus Samad** and **Mir Sayyid Ali**.
- Humayun and his son Akbar learned drawing and painting from these two artists.
- An advanced atelier was set up in the palace.
## Painting under Akbar
- **Akbar** set up Karkhanas for painting; the imperial painting establishment was called **Tasvir Khana**. {3}
- Akbar's painting Karkhanas: Agra 1558-1572 A.D., Fatehpur Sikri 1572-1585 A.D., Lahore 1585-1601 A.D., again Agra 1603-1605 A.D.
- Akbar got many texts painted; the first major project was **Dastan-i-Amir Hamza**.
- Dastan-i-Amir Hamza was illustrated first under Mir Sayyid Ali and later under Abdus Samad. {5}
- Dastan-i-Amir Hamza: earliest Mughal illustrated manuscript, begun in Humayun's period; took 15 years; about 100 painters; about 1400 paintings.
- It tells the heroic deeds of the Persian hero Amir Hamza, uncle of Prophet Muhammad.
- **Daswant** and **Basawan** were famous painters of Akbar's court. {6}
- Akbar was so impressed by Daswant that he supported him to become the leading painter of his time.
- Akbar-era painters named in Abul Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari: Daswant, Basawan, Keshav Lal, Mukund, Mishkin, Farrukh, Jagan, Mahesh, Khemkaran, Tara, Sanwal, Harivansh, Rai.
- European paintings were introduced in **Akbar's** court through the Portuguese. {7}
- Mughal painters got two things from Europeans: portrait of a person; making far objects small in size.
- Painting began to develop in Humayun's period; European influence on Mughal painting began under Akbar.
- Mishkin was a European painter in Akbar's court.
## Painting under Jahangir
- Mughal painting reached its zenith under **Jahangir** (1605-27); his period = 'Golden Age of Mughal Paintings'. {10}
- Jahangir mainly patronised painting; he was a highly skilled connoisseur and had his own atelier. {11}
- Jahangir shifted emphasis from illustrated manuscripts to albums and individual portraits. {12}
- Jahangir encouraged paintings of events of his own life, individual portraits, and studies of birds, flowers and animals, rather than fiction illustrations.
- Jahangir's interest in portraiture led to much growth of this art form.
- Jahangir was fascinated with art, science and architecture from a young age; painting also served his scientific interest in nature.
- Jahangir kept a huge aviary, recorded every specimen and organised experiments.
- Jahangir patronised European and Persian arts.
- Jahangir's court painters: Abul Hasan, Ustad Mansur, Farrukh Beg, Bishan Das, Aqa Reza, Muhammad Nadir, Muhammad Murad, Manohar, Madhav, Govardhan. {8}
- Abul Hasan had the title **Nadir-uz-Zaman**; Ustad Mansur had the title **Nadir-ul-Asr**.
- Abul Hasan painted Jahangir's coronation; it is the frontispiece of Jahangir's autobiography **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri**.
- Ustad Mansur, a 17th-century Mughal court artist, excelled in painting nature, plants, birds and animals.
- Mansur documented animals and plants that Jahangir met on military expeditions or received as donations.
- Khwaja Abdus Samad was a painter of Humayun's and Akbar's reigns, not of Jahangir's period. {9}
## Schools of painting
- Pahari School, Rajput School, Mughal School and Kangra School are different styles of painting (medieval). {13}
- Hill (Pahari) paintings = paintings of the hill states of the north-western Himalayan region.
- Pahari area: Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, and Tehri Garhwal and Pauri Garhwal of Uttarakhand.
- Mughal school spread from the court to regional centres and gave rise to Pahari and Rajasthani schools.
- **Kalighat** school was indigenous and not affected by Mughal painting. {4}
- **Kishangarh** school (Rajasthan) is famous for painting, known across India for courtesan paintings. {14}
- Raja Sawant Singh, scholar and art patron who became king in 1748 A.D., inspired the Kishangarh school of Radha-Krishna theme.
- Main Kishangarh artists: Amirchand, Chhotu, Bhawanidas, Nihalchand, Sitaram.
- The famous Kishangarh portrait of the courtesan **Bani-Thani** was made by Nihalchand.
## Music: Aurangzeb and ragas
- Aurangzeb banned music as anti-Islamic.
- Yet most books on Indian classical music in Persian were written in Aurangzeb's period.
- Aurangzeb was a skilled instrumentalist who played the **Veena**. {15}
- **Todi** Raga is sung early in the morning. {16}
- Todi, a Hindustani classical raga, gives its name to the Todi thaat, one of the ten modes of Hindustani music.
- Todi was sung by Bhats and Charans (minstrels) in the emperor's court.
## Tansen and Swami Haridas
- Tansen and Swami Haridas were the leading **Dhrupad** singers of Akbar's reign. {19}
- Swami Haridas: spiritual poet and classical musician with many devotional compositions, especially in Dhrupad style.
- Tansen, Baiju Bawra and Gopal Nayak were trained by Swami Haridas.
- Swami Haridas founded the Haridasi school of mysticism, still found in North India.
- His followers built **5** worship centres (ashrams): Sri Banke Bihariji, Nidhivan, Temple of Gorelal, Sri Rasik Bihari, Thatti Khana. {17}
- Tansen's original name was **Ramtanu Pandey**. {22}
- Tansen was one of Akbar's Navratnas (nine jewels) and a famous musician of his court.
- The title 'Tansen' was given by Raja Vikramjit Singh of Gwalior, not by Akbar; Akbar gave the title 'Mian'. {18}
- Akbar also gave Tansen the title **Kanthabharanvanivilas**.
- Tansen composed Dhrupads on Hindu gods and goddesses, composed songs on his patrons and invented many Ragas.
- Tansen was born in a Hindu family and was a Vaishnava musician.
- Before Akbar, Tansen was patronised by Raja Ramchandra Singh of Rewa (Bhata region); Akbar called him from there and gave a high post. {21}
- Tansen's tomb is in **Gwalior**, near the tomb of Saint Hazrat Ghaus. {20}
- Hazrat Ghaus's teachings influenced Tansen to convert to Islam.
## Other musicians and styles
- Akbar was a learned musician; he studied Hindu music under **Lala Kalawant**. {24}
- Vilas Khan was the main singer of Jahangir's court.
- Singers of Shah Jahan's court: Jagannath, Ramdas, Sukhsen, Sursen, Lal Khan, Durrang Khan.
- Raja Mansingh Tomar, Tansen and Sadarang-Adarang were greats of Hindustani music; **Mal Gurjari** is a Raga invented by Raja Mansingh Tomar. {23}
- Khyal singing became popular in the time of Muhammad Shah 'Rangila'.
- **Tappa** style was refined and encouraged at the court of Mughal Emperor Muhammad Shah (18th century). {25}
- Sadaranga and Adaranga were two great composers of Khayal gayaki at Muhammad Shah's court.
- New forms of music of this time: Tarana, Dadra and Ghazal.
- Folk forms such as Thumri and Tappa were also taken into the country's music.
- Tappa is based on folk music sung by the camel drivers of Punjab.
