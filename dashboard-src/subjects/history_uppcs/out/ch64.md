@ 64 | Peasant Movement and Kisan Sabha
## Bijolia Movement
- **Bijolia Movement** = first peasant movement of India; in Mewar state (modern Rajasthan). {1}
- Cause: feudal exploitation of peasants was common in Mewar in the latter half of the 19th century.
- Bijolia Movement began in 1897, led by local farmers; lasted about 44 years, one of India's longest peasant movements.
- First phase leader: **Sadhu Sitaram Das** (led in 1913); second phase leader: **Vijay Singh Pathik** (from 1915).
- Vijay Singh Pathik: renowned freedom fighter and peasant leader; organised Mewar farmers against excessive taxes imposed by the rulers.
- Prayag's **Abhyudaya** and Calcutta's **Bharat Mitra** newspapers regularly published Bijolia news; Pathik did not go to Gandhi to inform him about it. {2}
## Kisan Sabhas in U.P. and Awadh
- Home Rule League workers were very active in Awadh (Oudh); they organised peasants into 'Kisan Sabha'.
- **U.P. Kisan Sabha** formed February 1918 by Indra Narayan Dwivedi and Gauri Shankar Mishra, supported by Madan Mohan Malaviya; Jawaharlal Nehru was not associated. {3,4}
- These sabhas spread so much awareness that a huge number of U.P. farmers joined the 1918 Delhi Session of Congress.
- **'Nai-Dhobi Bandh'** = social boycott against exploiting landlords, started by farmers in an estate of Pratapgarh district (Oudh), end of 1919; first sign of grass-roots peasant activity. {5}
- Summer 1920: Kisan meetings called by village panchayats became frequent in villages of Taluqdari Oudh.
- Jhinguri Singh and Durgapal Singh played an important role in this movement at first.
- **Baba Ramchandra** emerged in mid-1920 as peasant leader of Oudh (Awadh); made the movement strong and combative; had extraordinary organisational capability. {6}
- **Oudh Kisan Sabha** was established in 1920 by Baba Ramchandra's efforts.
- Baba Ramchandra: a sanyasi, earlier an indentured labourer in Fiji (island nation in the Pacific); carried Tulsidas' Ramayana on his back and recited verses to rural audiences. {7}
- Baba Ramchandra took active part in the Non-Cooperation Movement in Oudh and organised peasants against landlords.
## Eka Movement
- **Eka (Unity) Movement** (1921–22): grass-roots leaders were **Madari Pasi** (of a lower class family), other low-caste leaders and many small zamindars.
- Eka Movement was started by peasants of Hardoi, Barabanki, Bahraich and Sitapur (U.P.); discontent resurfaced towards end of 1921. {11}
- Eka issues: rents 50% higher than recorded rates; oppression by thikedars in charge of revenue collection; practice of share-rents (rent collected in produce).
- Eka aim: change of rent into cash; peasants did not refuse rent but insisted on cash payment because of high inflation. {10}
- Eka meetings had a symbolic religious ritual with vows: pay only recorded rent but on time; not leave when evicted; refuse forced labour; no help to criminals; abide by Panchayat decisions.
- Severe repression by the authorities ended the Eka Movement by March 1922.
## Swami Sahajanand and Bihar Kisan Sabha
- **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati** founded the **Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha (BPKS)** in 1929. {12}
- Swami Sahajanand led the peasant (farmers') movement in Bihar; closely linked with the Kisan Sabha Movement of the 1930s. {9,15,22}
- His agricultural reform programme aimed to end the Zamindari system and give ownership rights to farmers; he is called **'Kisan-Pran'**.
- Karyanand Sharma, Rahul Sankrityayan, Panchanan Sharma, Yadunandan Sharma and many other communist leaders helped him take the Bihar Kisan Sabha movement to village level. {23}
- Sahajanand published the newspaper **'Lok Sangraha'** to highlight peasants' hardships. {13}
- Sahajanand (1889–1950), sanyasi, farmers' rights activist and freedom fighter, ran the Hindi weekly **'Hunkar'** from Patna. {14}
- Just before his death (1950) he formed the **All India United Kisan Sabha (AIUKS)**; basic demand: nationalisation of land, waterways and all sources of energy and wealth. {24}
- Dr. Rajendra Prasad was associated with the Kisan Movement in Bihar; he was President of the Constituent Assembly and first President of India. {26}
- Bakashat agitation of Bihar is linked to Swami Shraddhanand Saraswati.
## All India Kisan Sabha
- **All India Kisan Congress** was formed at Lucknow on 11 April 1936. {19,20}
- Swami Sahajanand Saraswati presided over the first session and became its first (founder) President. {16,17,21}
- **N.G. Ranga** became the first General Secretary. {18}
- Jawaharlal Nehru also addressed the first meeting of the All India Kisan Sabha (Lucknow, April 1936). {25}
- Kisan Congress was later renamed **All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS)**.
- A Kisan manifesto was issued and a periodical was started under **Indulal Yagnik**.
- AIKS held its second session along with the Congress session at Faizpur in 1936; chaired by N.G. Ranga.
- Congress manifesto (especially agrarian policy) for the 1937 provincial elections was strongly influenced by the AIKS agenda.
- N.G. Ranga founded the **Bhartiya Kisan Vidyalaya**.
- Fazlul Haq founded the **Bengal Praja Party (Krishak Praja Party)** in 1929.

## Bardoli Satyagraha
- **Bardoli Satyagraha** (1928): peasant movement in Bardoli taluka, Surat district, led by **Vallabhbhai Patel**. {27,28,29,30}
- Bardoli taluka saw intense politicisation after Gandhi joined national politics; Gandhi's followers like the Mehta brothers ran a sustained campaign there since 1922.
- On its success, Mahatma Gandhi, on behalf of the women of Bardoli, gave Patel the title **'Sardar'**. {31,32}
## Tebhaga Movement
- **Tebhaga Movement** (Bengal, 1946) was started under the Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha. {34}
- Bengal Provincial Kisan Council decided in September 1946 to launch it in the harvesting season of November 1946; Bargadars (share-croppers) were eager for struggle.
- Tebhaga demand ("sharing by third"): landlord's share reduced from one-half of the crop to one-third; two-thirds to the share-cropper. {33}
- The movement sought to implement the Flood Commission's recommendation of one-third share to the landowner.
- In response the Bargadari Act was passed: share-croppers pay only one-third of harvest to landlords.
- Districts of North Bengal were most affected by the Tebhaga Movement.
## Bhoodan Movement
- **Acharya Vinoba Bhave** started the **Bhoodan Movement** on 18 April 1951; aim: give land to landless farmers. {38}
- Bhoodan began at **Pochampalli** village, then in Nalgonda district. {36}
- Pochampalli was in Andhra Pradesh, now Telangana (Yadadri Bhuvanagiri district, carved out of Nalgonda). {35,37}
- Vinoba visited Nalgonda district for the Sarvodaya conference; the district was then a centre of communist activities; he stayed at Bhudan Pochampalli village.
- First donation: landlord (zamindar) Ramchandra Reddy gave 100 acres to 40 Harijan families.
- Vinoba organised the Sarvodaya Samaj for non-violent social change through land reforms with landlords' willing cooperation.
- Large landlords were persuaded to gift at least one-sixth of their land; Bhoodan was most successful in Orissa.
- Bhoodan was an attempt at land reform and institutional change in agriculture.
- October 1951: Vinoba set a target of 50 million acres by 1957; the movement continued beyond 1957 but later waned.
- Jaya Prakash Narayan left active politics and joined the Bhoodan Movement in 1954.
- Vinoba Bhave: great spiritual leader and reformer; first person chosen for Individual Satyagraha in 1940.
