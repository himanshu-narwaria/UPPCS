@ 85 | Quit India Movement
## Background
- Quit India Movement was launched in response to the failure of the **Cripps Mission/Proposals** (March 1942). {22}
- After Cripps failed, it was clear Britain wanted India as a partner in the Second World War without Indian consent but was not ready for any real agreement.
- By spring 1942 the struggle seemed inevitable.
- Causes of the movement: Indian disappointment with the Cripps Mission, threat of Japanese attack on India, and the AICC resolution of August 1942. {35}
- Gandhiji never provoked people to adopt violent means in his articles.
- The slogan **"Quit India"** was coined by **Yusuf Meher Ali**. {31}
## Wardha resolution, July 1942
- Congress Working Committee met at **Wardha** during 5-14 July 1942 and passed the **Quit India resolution on 14 July 1942**. {2}
- On 6 July 1942 Gandhi first discussed his "Quit India Policy" at the Working Committee.
- Leaders present at Wardha: Jawaharlal Nehru, Abul Kalam Azad, Vallabhbhai Patel, Mahatma Gandhi, Aruna Asaf Ali, Achyut Patwardhan, Dr. Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
- **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** chaired the meeting and was Congress President when the Quit India resolution was passed. {1,32}
- Azad was elected Congress President at the **Ramgarh Session, 1940**; no Congress session was held for five years (1941-45), so he stayed President.
- Azad was Congress President for **six consecutive years**, the most years before independence; India's political situation was very fragile in his tenure. {33}
## Bombay session, August 1942
- All India Congress Committee met on **7-8 August 1942** at **Gowalia Tank Maidan** (now August Kranti Ground), **Bombay**; the movement was launched from here. {8,10,11}
- The Wardha Resolution was confirmed here; after a few changes the Quit India Resolution was adopted by the AICC on **8 August 1942**. {7}
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved (presented) the Quit India resolution in the AICC on 8 August 1942. {29}
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** supported the resolution. {28}
- **Mahatma Gandhi** himself prepared the draft of the resolution, with the help of Jawaharlal Nehru and Maulana Abul Kalam Azad. {30}
- The Committee asserted India's right to freedom and decided on a non-violent mass struggle on the widest possible scale.
- Quit India Movement began on **9 August 1942**. {4,5,6,9,12,13}
- The movement was led by **Mahatma Gandhi**, who launched it in August 1942. {25}
## Gandhiji's statement and Do or Die
- Gandhi on the eve of the movement: government servants should not leave work but openly declare their allegiance to Congress.
- Soldiers should refuse to fire on their countrymen (not leave their posts).
- Students should leave studies only if they could stay firm on that decision until independence.
- Princes should accept the **sovereignty of their people**; states to be declared part of the Indian nation; people to accept a king's leadership only if he links his future with theirs. {14}
- Gandhiji gave the mantra **"Do or Die"** (Karo ya Maro) in the Quit India Movement: "We shall either free India or die in the attempt." {15,16,17,18,19,20,21}
- Gandhi said: "I am not going to be satisfied with anything short of complete freedom."
- Gandhi said the British might offer abolition of salt tax or the drink evil, but he would say "nothing less than freedom".
- Gandhi said of the mantra: "You may imprint it on your hearts and let every breath of yours give expression to it."
- In the Do or Die speech Gandhi said: "I do not want to make the instrument either of Russia's defeat or of China's."
- Gandhi asked all Congressmen and Indians to keep discipline through non-violence.
- Pattabhi Sitaramayya on Gandhi's speech: that day Gandhiji spoke as if moved by the power of an Avatar and Paigambar.
- Quit India Movement awakened and encouraged people because they absorbed the slogan "Do or Die". {60}
## Arrests: Operation Zero Hour
- In the early hours of 9 August 1942, Gandhiji and other top Congress leaders were arrested under **Operation Zero Hour**, before the movement could start.
- Gandhiji was arrested in **Bombay**. {51}
- Gandhiji and Sarojini Naidu were kept in the **Aga Khan Palace, Poona (Pune)**. {52}
- Congress Working Committee members kept in **Ahmednagar Fort**: Jawaharlal Nehru, Abul Kalam Azad, Govind Ballabh Pant, Dr. Prafulla Chandra Ghosh, Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya, Dr. Syed Mahmud, Acharya Kripalani.
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** could not come to Bombay; arrested at Patna on 9 August 1942 under Bharat Security Rules and sent to **Bankipur (Patna) Jail**. {48}
- Shivkumar and Ram-nandan Mishra were arrested in Hazaribagh; Ram-nandan Mishra was first arrested in Cuttack and later moved to Hazaribagh.
- After the movement was announced, Congress was declared an illegal body.
- **Baldev Sahai** resigned as Advocate General in **1942** in protest against the arrests and other oppressive acts of government. {23}
## Nature and spread of the movement
- The 1942 movement had maximum impact in Bombay, Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, United Provinces of Agra and Oudh, and Madras; the whole country took part.
- The movement was not so non-violent: official figures say in the first week 250 railway stations were damaged or destroyed, over 500 post offices and 150 police stations attacked.
- The rulers used heavy violence to suppress it; Gandhi in 1942 refused to condemn the people's violence, calling it an answer to the rulers' huge violence.
- Arrest of main leaders before the start (also printed as arrest of all major leaders on 8 August 1942) made the movement more aggressive and **spontaneous**.
- Since Gandhiji was in jail, he did not actually get to lead the movement.
- The movement did not attract the labour class in general (monetary constraints, fear of repression, effect of trade unions), unlike students and farmers.
! The book marks two questions on "not true about Quit India Movement" as having no clear key: both "non-violent" and "led by Gandhi" are not correct; UPPSC accepted "Congress was declared an illegal body" as the answer.
- After Gandhi's arrest, rioting broke out at once across the country with many violent demonstrations; thousands of freedom fighters were arrested.
- Quit India (**Bharat Chhodo Andolan**; Gandhi gave the call on 8 August 1942) became the most powerful movement in the freedom struggle.
- Riots from the movement were widest in **Bihar and United Provinces**, where it became a revolt. {46}
- Self-instilled centres of mass revolt: Azamgarh, Ballia, Gorakhpur (Eastern United Provinces); Gaya, Champaran, Saran, Purnia, Shahabad, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur (Bihar).
- Police repression angered the public, who attacked police stations, railway stations and post offices; rail lines were uprooted and rail services totally disrupted.
- Centres of public anger: Medinipur district (Bengal), Ballia district (Uttar Pradesh) and Munger district (Bihar).
- Uprooting of railway tracks happened mostly in Bihar; **Munger** was the most affected district, cut off from the outside world for 2 weeks. {53}
- The movement spread among farmers; some groups farmed by day and rioted at night, showing glimpses of the Revolt of 1857.
- Viceroy **Linlithgow**, in a telegram to PM Churchill, called it by far the **most serious rebellion since the Revolt of 1857**, because of the massive peasant upsurge. {47}
## Officials of the time
- **Lord Wavell** was Commander-in-Chief of the Indian Army during the Quit India Movement; later Viceroy and Governor-General, 1943-1947. {3}
- **Winston Churchill** (1940-1945) was Prime Minister of England during the Quit India Movement. {41}
## Who stayed away
- **Hindu Mahasabha, Communist Party of India, Unionist Party (Punjab) and Muslim League** did not support the Quit India Movement. {27}
- **RSS** kept away from the Quit India Movement; from 1940 to the end of foreign rule in 1947 it joined no national movement. It is false that RSS thought the movement would delay freedom. {61}
- Muslim League under Jinnah condemned the movement; Jinnah said lawlessness would capture India if the British left.
- Jinnah acted as a staunch ally of the British and asked Muslims to stay away; so the British and Muslims came closer through shared hatred of Congress. {62}
## Underground leadership and Congress Radio
- Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta, J.P. Narayan and Ram Manohar Lohia gave alternate underground leadership when other leaders were arrested.
- Leaders who escaped arrest organised the public secretly; main ones: Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia and Aruna Asaf Ali.
- A secret **Congress Radio** was broadcast from different parts of Bombay (also printed: different parts of India); it could be heard up to Madras.
- **Usha Mehta** ran the secret Congress Radio; she was a key member of the small underground group that managed it. {36,37,38}
- **Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia** broadcast regularly on Congress Radio; he directed the movement from underground and set up an underground radio station. {39}
- **Aruna Asaf Ali** was a major woman organiser of underground activities in the Quit India Movement. {63,64,65}
- Aruna Asaf Ali is remembered for hoisting the tricolour (Congress flag) at Gowalia Tank ground, Bombay, in 1942; she was also an educator, political activist and publisher.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan** gained recognition as a national leader during the Quit India Movement. {54}
- Jayaprakash Narayan escaped from the high-security **Hazaribagh jail** and organised underground activities. {56,57}
- He escaped with his comrades on **Diwali**, 9 November 1942. {55}
- **Yogendra Shukla** escaped with J.P. Narayan on 9 November 1942, was later arrested at Muzaffarpur and sent to **Patna** (brought there on 7 December 1942). {49}
- **All India Azad Dasta** was formed by **Jayaprakash Narayan** at Bakri Ka Tapu in the Terai region of Nepal for guerrilla war against the British. {40}
## Other participants and martyrs
- **Shri Krishna Singh**, known as **"Bihar Kesari"**, took active part in the movement; later first Chief Minister of Bihar. {34}
- **Rampyari Devi**, wife of Jagat Narayan Lal, addressed an assembly in Bankipur and called on people to quit government jobs. {50}
- Brothers **Gangaram and Kheemdev** were martyred in police firing at Almora in 1942 during the Quit India Movement. {58}
- In the Dictionary of Martyrs Vol. 2 they are named Gangadutt and Kheemananda.
- **Matangini Hazra**, aged 73, fell to British bullets leading a protest march at Tamluk, Bengal, in 1942.
- **Kanaklata Barua** was shot dead while leading a procession to hoist the national flag at the police station of **Gohpur** subdivision, Sonitpur district, Assam. {71}
- Kanaklata Barua: AISF leader; joined the Mrityu Bahini ("Death Army") of Gohpur youth; shot in September 1942 at about age 17 at Borangabari, Gohpur.
- **Shanti Ghosh** was not linked to the Quit India Movement; she was a member of the revolutionary group Yugantar. {59}
- On 14 December 1931 Shanti Ghosh and Suniti entered a District Magistrate's bungalow and shot him dead at point-blank range.
## Louis Fischer
- **Louis Fischer**, American journalist and biographer of Gandhi, was with Gandhiji at the time of the Quit India Movement. {42,43,44,45}
- Fischer came to India in May 1942 and spent one week with Gandhiji at Sevagram Ashram, Wardha.
- Fischer with American intellectuals Pearl Buck, Edgar Snow, Fredrick L. Schuman and Norman Thomas demanded India's independence.

## Gandhi in jail and release
- **"Operation Rubicon"** was the secret British code word to be used if Gandhiji died during his fast unto death in Aga Khan Palace. {74}
- Gandhiji was released on **6 May 1944** because of bad health.
- Churchill on Gandhi's release: "When we are winning around the globe, how can we be defeated by this old man who is our enemy for a long time."
## Significance and after
- The movement, though short-lived and badly crushed, showed British rule was not wanted; it was the culmination of the national movement. {72}
- People's firm determination hastened the British decision to quit; a mechanism for transfer of power now had to be found.
- **C. Rajagopalachari's** pamphlet **"The Way Out"** (1944) proposed a solution to the constitutional deadlock between Congress and Muslim League. {73}
- The Rajagopalachari formula: Congress to offer the League a Muslim Pakistan based on plebiscite.
- Plebiscite = direct vote of all members of an electorate on an important public question, such as a change in the Constitution.
- The formula was opposed even by Congress; Jinnah rejected it and the talks failed.
