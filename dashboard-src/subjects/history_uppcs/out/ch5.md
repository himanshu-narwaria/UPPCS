## Basic terms and the 24 Tirthankaras
- **Jain** comes from the Sanskrit word **'Jina'** = conqueror/victor.
- Founders of Jainism are called **Tirthankara**; Jain Mahatmas are called **Nirgranth**.
- The word Tirthankara is related to the Jain religion; Jainism has **24 Tirthankaras** who spread Jain philosophy from time to time. {14}
- The 24 Tirthankaras in order: 1 Rishabhdev (Adinath), 2 Ajitnath, 3 Sambhavnath, 4 Abhinandan(nath), 5 Sumatinath, 6 Padmaprabha, 7 Suparshvanath, 8 Chandraprabha, 9 Pushpadant (Suvidhinath), 10 Sheetalnath, 11 Shreyansanath, 12 Vasupujya, 13 Vimalnath, 14 Anantnath, 15 Dharmanath, 16 Shantinath, 17 Kunthunath, 18 Aranath, 19 Mallinath, 20 Munisuvrata, 21 Naminath, 22 Neminath (Arishtanemi), 23 Parshvanath, 24 Mahavira Swami.
- Nathamuni was NOT a Jain Tirthankara; Chandraprabha, Nemi and Sambhav were. {16}
- Chronological order: Abhinandan (4th) → Padmaprabhu (6th) → Vimalnath (13th) → Munisuvrata Nath (20th). {8}
- **Rishabhdev** = first Tirthankara and originator of the Jain religion; other names: Rishabhnath, Adinath, Vrishabhnath. {1,2}
- Vasupujya = 12th Tirthankara; attained Nirvana (Moksha) at **Champapur (Bihar)**, not Sammed Shikhar. {6}

## Parshvanath
- **Parshvanath** = 23rd Tirthankara; born in Kashi (Varanasi/Banaras) in 850 BC; mainly associated with Varanasi. {3}
- Parshvanath's father = Ashwasen (Aswasena), king of Kashi; mother = Queen Vama; he belonged to the Ikshvaku dynasty.
- Parshvanath lived as a prince of Varanasi; at age 30 he renounced the world and became a monk.
- Parshvanath meditated for 84 days before attaining Kaivalya Jnana; got Kaivalya in Ashrampad Udyan near Varanasi.
- Parshvanath attained Parinirvana on Mount **Sammed Shikhar (Sammet Shikhar/Sammeta)**; so it is a Jain pilgrimage place. {37}
- Parshvanath's four restraints: Ahimsa (non-violence), Aparigraha (non-possession), Asteya (not stealing), Satya (truth).
## Mahavira Swami: life
- **Mahavira Swami** was born in **Kundagram (Kundalpur)** near Vaishali (Bihar) about 599 BC. {9,10}
- Childhood (original) name of Mahavira = **Vardhaman**.
- Father Siddhartha = chief of the Jnatrika (Gyatrik) Kshatriya Sangh/clan, under the Vajji of Vaishali.
- Mother Trishala (Videhdatta) = sister (not daughter) of Chetaka, head/king of the Lichchhavi republic. {45}
- Nandivardhan was Mahavira's elder brother.
- Mahavira was married: wife = **Yashoda** (daughter of Kundinya Gotra). {13}
- Mahavira's daughter = Anojja (Priyadarshana); she was married to Jamali.
- **Jamali**, Mahavira's son-in-law, was his first disciple/follower. {40,41}
- Jamali met Mahavira at Kshatriya-Kundagram (Mahavira's birth village) and took the vow together with 500 of the warrior caste.
- Mahavira was related to Bimbisara of Magadha, who married Chellana, daughter of Chetaka.
- **'Kaivalya'** = the Jain word for complete knowledge (complete intellect). {18}
- Mahavira got Kaivalya under a **Sal** tree on the bank of the **Rijupalika** river near Jrimbhik village, after 12 years of hard austerity. {19}
- After Kaivalya Mahavira was called Kevalin, Arhat (Yogya), Jin (conqueror) and Nirgranth (free from bonds).
- After Kaivalya Mahavira began to preach his principles; he propagated Jain philosophy in the 6th century BC.
- Chetaka, Lichchhavi chief of Vaishali and Mahavira's uncle, greatly helped spread Jainism.
- Mahavira = **24th and last Tirthankara** of the Jain religion. {15}
- Mahavira attained Nirvana at about age 72 in **527 BC** at **Pavapuri**, near Rajgriha (Rajgir), near Patna. {11,12}
- Mahavira did not accept the authority of the Vedas and opposed the related social-cultural traditions and humbugs.
## Jain philosophy and doctrines
- Leaving the extreme views of selfish people and atheists, Mahavira took the middle path called **'Syadvad'** — a Jain doctrine. {24,25}
- Syadvad is also called **Saptabhanginaya** = knowledge is relative.
- Jain philosophy revolves around two closely connected main doctrines: **Anekantavada** (core theory, a metaphysical concept) and **Syadvada**. {26,28,29}
- Anekantavada: every being has numerous properties; permanent property that makes a thing's nature = attribute (**guna**); accidental property = mode (**Paryaya**).
- Syadvada: our knowledge is partial and relative because passion, anger, greed obstruct it; yet we take it as complete and absolute.
- Sunyavada and Sarvastivada are related to Buddhism, not Jainism.
- Monks give up all worldly enjoyments and take five great vows (**Mahavratas**): Ahimsa, Satya, Brahmacharya, Asteya, Aparigraha.
- Mahavratas are hard for ordinary people, so they follow **Anuvratas** (Ahimsa, Satya, Brahmacharya, Asteya, Aparigraha Anuvrat) — a Jain concept. {23}
- Three means to Moksha = **Ratnatraya/Triratna** of Jainism: Right Belief (faith), Right Conduct (action), Right Knowledge. {20,21}
- Jain Triratna names: Samyag darshana, Samyag jnan, Samyag charitra.
- Triratna exists in Buddhism too: the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha.
- **Sanvar Tattva** = freedom from bondage and stopping the inflow of karmic matter into the soul.
- **Nirjara** element = destruction of Karma.
- When the remaining karma of life is exhausted, the soul attains **Moksha**.
- **Anant Chattushtya** = Anant Gyan (infinite knowledge), Anant Darshan (infinite perception), Anant Virya (infinite power), Anant Anand (infinite bliss).
- Jain belief: the world is created and maintained by **Universal Law**; the universe runs by itself on its cosmic laws (laws of nature); no almighty is needed. {27}
- Jainism: the universe and all its substances are eternal, without beginning or end; so no concept of 'final annihilation of the world'. {31}
- All substances change gradually; old forms give way to new ones without losing their inherent qualities.
- Jain reality (**Tattva**) has three characteristics: origination, decay and permanence (Nityata); also described as attributes and modifications. {30}
- Origination and decay are only changes of form; permanence = essential attributes that stay constant; an entity is real (**Sat**) when it has both permanence and transformation.
- Basic point of Jainism = **Ahimsa** (non-violence); Jainism is called the religion of Ahimsa; Ahimsa is above all concepts, rules, customs and practices. {32}
- Cardinal principles of Jainism: Ahimsa, Anekantvad and Aparigraha.
- Jainism: every object, even the smallest particle, has a soul — also earth, water, fire, air, plants, rocks, running water. {57}
- Jain acharya Gurudev Chitrabhanu: "the universe is not for humanity alone; it is a field of evolution for all living beings."
- Jain doctrine: Karma is the bane of the soul and must be ended; the surest way to annihilate karma is penance. {47}
- Law of Karma: every action (thought, word, deed) produces an effect that causes another action; this chain of cause and effect = Karma.
- Karma is also seen as extremely subtle matter that enters the soul and hides its natural, transparent, pure qualities.
- By its karma a soul transmigrates and is reborn in heaven or hell, or as human or animal.
- **Samadhi Maran (Sallekhana, Sanyasna Maran)** = last vow of the Jain ethical code. {48}
- Samadhi Maran: ascetics and lay votaries slowly reduce food and liquid at life's end; allowed in old age, incurable disease or near death.
## Jain Sangha, split and sects
- Mahavira set up a Sangh in his lifetime; it had 11 chief followers called **'Ganadhar'**.
- The 11 Ganadhars: Indrabhuti, Agnibhuti, Vayubhuti (three brothers), Arya Vyakta, Sudharma, Mandavya, Mauryaputra, Akampana, Achal, Metarya, Prabhasa.
- After Mahavira's death **Sudharma** became the first head of the Jain Sangha.
- After Sudharma, **Jambu** was president of the Sangh for 44 years.
- Sambhutavijay and Bhadrabahu were presidents of the Sangha (printed both as "at the time of the Maurya ruler" and "at the time of the last Nand ruler").
- Sambhutavijay and Bhadrabahu were the last to know the ancient Jain texts, the 14 Purvas.
- **Fourteen Purvas** = earliest (ancient) Jain scripture, preached by all Tirthankaras, given by Mahavira. {35}
- About 150 years after Mahavira, when Bhadrabahu headed the order, a severe famine raged for 12 years in Magadha (ruler printed as Nand dynasty in one place, Maurya in another).
- Pataliputra, the capital city, had become the centre of learning and knowledge at that time.
- In the famine monks could not keep the code of conduct; **Bhadrabahu** migrated to the South with many followers and spread Jainism in South India.
- Bhadrabahu's migrant followers were mostly unclad → called **Digambars** (skyclad).
- **Sthulabhadra** and those who stayed in the north covered the body with plain white cloth → **Shwetambars**; Sthulabhadra = first founder of the Shwetambar creed. {39}

- **Yapaniya** = a Jain sect, believed to have come out of the Digambaras, but it also followed some Shwetambar views. {33}
- Yapaniyas worshipped nude images of the Tirthankaras in their temples.
- **Sthanakvasi** = a sect of Shwetambara Jainism; it arose from the earlier reformist sect 'Lonka' founded by Lonkasha. {34}
- Sthanakvasi belief: idol worship is not essential for soul purification and Nirvana.
## Jain councils and literature
- **First Jain Council (Sabha)**: Pataliputra, 4th century BC (about 310 BC), reign of Chandragupta Maurya, president Sthulbhadra; 12 Angas compiled and Shwetambara Agama edited there. {42,43,44}
- Followers of Bhadrabahu did not take part in the first Jain council.
- As all the ancient Jain texts had been destroyed, a Jain council was organised in the 4th century BC to recompile them.
- The Shwetambara canon includes the 12 Angas, the canonical texts based on Mahavira's teaching; they were compiled at Pataliputra, not first at Vallabhi.
- **Second Jain Council**: Vallabhi (Gujarat), 6th century AD (512 AD), president **Devardhigani Kshamashraman**.
- Second council compiled: 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 10 Prakirnas, 6 Chedsutras, 4 Mulsutras, 2 Chulikasutras; the holy books got final form here.
- Jain literature is called **'Aagam'** (principle): 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, 10 Prakirnas, 6 Chedsutras, 4 Mulsutras, 2 Chulikasutras.
- All the earliest Jain religious literature, including the twelve Angas, was written in **Ardha-Magadhi**. {36}
- The 12 Angas: Acharanga-Sutra, Sutrakritanga, Sthananga, Samavayanga, Bhagavati Vyakhyaprajnapti, Jnatrdharmakatha, Upasakadasah, Antakrddasah, Anuttaraupapatikadasah, Prasnavyakarana, Vipaksruta, Dristivada.
- Each of the 12 Angas has a related Upanga; Upangas describe the universe, classes of beings, astronomy, time division, life after death.
- 10 Prakirnas = supplements to the major texts.
- 6 Chedsutras = rules for Jain monks: Jitakalpa, Brihatkalpa, Nishith, Mahanishith, Vyavhar (behaviour), Aachar Dasha.
- 4 Mulsutras hold Jain sermons, forest life, duties of monks, rules of Yam: Dashvaikalik, Utaradhyayan, Avshayak, Pindniryukti (Pakshik Sutra).
- 2 Chulikasutras = Nandi-sutra and Anuyogadwara-Sutra: independent Jain texts, a kind of encyclopaedia; they have moral stories for monks.
- **Therigatha** is a Buddhist scripture (early nuns/bhikshunis tell their struggles and attainments), not Jain; Acarangasutra, Sutrakritanga, Brihatkalpasutra are Jain. {38}

## Patrons, centres, temples
- Jain literature calls **Samprati**, grandson of Ashoka, the patron of Jainism; Ujjain became a major Jain centre in his rule.
- **Mathura** = second major Jain centre; many temples, statues and records found there.
- **Kharavela**, Chedi king of Kalinga (1st century BC), was a follower and great patron of Jainism; he supported Jain monks and gave for their livelihood. {46}
- Kharavela cut caves for Jain monks in the Udaygiri and Khandagiri hills near Bhubaneswar.
- Khajuraho temples are related to Hinduism and Jainism; built under the Rajput Chandela dynasty.
- **Dilwara** Jain temples of Mount Abu are made of marble; **Vimal Vasahi** is the most prominent among them.
- Vimal Vasahi was built by Vimalshah, Samanta of Chalukya (Solanki) king Bhimdev I of Gujarat.
- **Shravanabelagola** is in Karnataka; its Gomateshwara statue is one of the most important tirthas of Jainism.
- Gomateshwara statue shows **Bahubali**, son of first Tirthankara Rishabhdev — not the last Tirthankara. {61}
- The Bahubali (Gomateshwar) statue was built about 981 AD by **Chamundaraya**, minister of Ganga ruler Rachamalla IV (Panchamalla). {62}
- **Mahamastakabhisheka** = Jain festival for Lord Bahubali, held at Shravanabelagola every 12 years; the statue is 18 metres high. {63}
- Tawang Monastery (Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh) = India's largest monastery.
- Hoysaleshwara temple is dedicated to Shiva.
- **Kaushambi** = famous pilgrimage of both Jains and Buddhists in Uttar Pradesh. {60}
- Present Kaushambi district was carved out of Prayagraj on 4 April 1997; it is 55 km south-west of Prayagraj.
- Kaushambi is mentioned many times in Vedic and Jain literature; Jain texts: named for its many Kushamb trees.
- Puranas: Nicakshu, sixth in line from Parikshita, shifted the capital from Hastinapur to Kaushambi after floods, locust invasion and upheavals in the Kuru family.
- Buddha preached many times at Kaushambi and made many disciples; its most famous monastery = Ghoshitram, built by Shresthi Ghoshit.
- Ikshvakus of southern India (3rd–4th century) followed Vedic religion but also supported Buddhism — they were not hostile to it.
- **Pala** rulers of eastern India were patrons of Buddhism; Bengal became the main nucleus of Buddhism under them. {49}
- Gautama Buddha's mother Maya, wife of Suddhodana, was a Koliya princess.
## Ajivika sect
- **Makkhali Gosala** was first a disciple of Mahavira, then fell out with him and founded the **Ajivika** sect. {50,51}
- Sources name Ajivika scholars Nanda Vaccha and Kisa Samkicca even before Gosala; Gosala is credited with first popularising the sect.
- Ajivika belief = **'Niyativad'**: everything in the world is controlled by destiny (fate); man is incapable. {52,53}
- Makkhali Gosala, contemporary of Mahavira and Buddha, was the most important Ajivika leader; central idea "Niyati"; Ajivikas held regular sabhas (meetings). {56}
- Ajivikas were egalitarian and rejected the rigid caste and class system.
- Ajivikas believed in determinism (fatalism) and in austerity; they held the rebirth cycle cannot be changed by personal effort. {54}
- Ajivikas: all joys and sorrows are preordained by an unchangeable cosmic order; no free will or moral effort can change fate.
- Ajivika austerities: long silence and fasting, seen as part of their predestined path.
- Ajivikas enjoyed great respect in the Maurya era; they were influential even under Bindusara. {55}
! Ajivika question on forest-dwelling: UPPSC gave "both statements" as answer, but by the sources "only II" (respect in Maurya era) is more appropriate.
- Ajivikas were nude wandering ascetics who begged in villages and towns and lived in rock-cut caves; not described as forest dwellers.
- Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha had caves cut in the **Barabar** and **Nagarjuni** hills as residences for Ajivika monks. {59}
