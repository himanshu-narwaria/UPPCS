## Travellers
| Traveller | Country |
| Ibn Battuta | Morocco |
| Marco Polo | Venice (Italy) |
| Abdur Razzaq | Iran (not Turkey) {5} |
| Nuniz | Portugal |
- Marco Polo visited India during the rule of Pandya ruler Marvarman Kulshekhara (1268–1310 AD).
- Iranian Ambassador Abdur Razzaq visited Vijayanagara in the rule of Deva Raya II (1422–46 AD).
- Nuniz, a Portuguese traveller, also visited Vijayanagara.
- Order of arrival: Al-Biruni → Ibn Battuta → Tavernier → Manucci. {6}
- Al-Biruni = resident of Khiva (ancient Khwarizm); scholar and diplomat in the court of the last emperor of the Khiva dynasty before Mahmud Ghazni's victory over India.
- Al-Biruni came to India with Mahmud Ghazni during his invasion of India.
- Ibn Battuta (1333–1342 AD) = Moroccan traveller; came to India in the reign of Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq (1325–1351 AD).
- Muhammad Tughluq appointed Ibn Battuta 'Qazi' of Delhi; later sent him to China as ambassador in 1342.
- Ibn Battuta wrote his travel experiences in his book **Rihla**.
- Jean-Baptiste Tavernier (17th century AD) = French gem merchant and traveller; began his voyage in the period of Shahjahan; made six voyages to the East.
- Tavernier's travel account: "The Six Voyages of John Baptista Tavernier".
- Niccolao Manucci (1653–1708 AD) = Italian traveller; reached India from his hometown Venice, travelling via Turkiye and Asia.
- Manucci served Prince Dara Shukoh as a cannoneer; later took up the medical profession.
- Manucci is famous for his work "Storia do Mogor or Mogul India, 1653–1708".
- **Ibn Battuta** = world's greatest traveller of premodern times: about 44 countries and 73,000 miles (117,482 km) in 30 years. {15}
- Ibn Battuta crossed South Africa, Western Africa, Southern and Eastern Europe, Middle-East Asia, Indian Subcontinent, Middle Asia, South-Eastern Asia and China.
- Marco Polo travelled approx. 24,000 miles over 24 years, starting in 1271; one of the pioneer nomads.
- Marco Polo's travel account covers Middle East, Middle Asia, China and Mongolia; he was aided by Mongolian ruler Kublai Khan.
## Society: terms and customs
- In the Sultanate period, high religious and judicial authorities (**Ulema**) were called **Dastar-Bandan**, as they wore an official turban. {7}
- In Islam, Sayyids and Pirs are important groups of religious thinkers; those appointed as religious and legal officers were collectively called Dastar-Bandan, i.e. one who wears a cap or Kulah.
- Among them, **Sayyids** wore pointed caps and were known as **Kulah-Daran**. {8}
- The earliest reference to the use of the **magnetic compass** in the Indian seas was given by Sadiduddin (Sadruddin) Muhammad Ibn Muhammad 'Aufi'. {9}
- Aufi's books: Lubab-ul-Albab and Jawami-ul-Hikayat; the compass reference is in Jawami-ul-Hikayat.
- **Jauhar** practice started in the Rajput period; a distinct form of Sati done by Rajputs in the period of Hindu-Muslim conflict, as a response to Muslim invasions. {21}
- Rajput women committed Jauhar to protect their honour from Muslim invaders.
- Hindu women feared becoming "booty for the captor"; committed Jauhar and Sati to avoid rape, torture and other ignominies.
- **Hemachandra Suri** = great scholar and follower of Jainism. {22}
- Hemachandra was introduced to Jai Singh Siddharaj and soon rose to prominence in the Solanki royal court.
- Later Jai Singh built a Mandap on Abu Mountain and set up statues of Jain Tirthankaras.
- In medieval India 'Panahi' and 'Upanah' were mentioned in the context of **shoes**. {25}
- **Fanam** = small gold coins issued from the medieval period to the 19th century in South India. {26}
- Indian peasants did not cultivate **maize** in the 13th–14th centuries; maize was brought by the Portuguese. {27}
- Ibn Battuta stated they usually cultivated rice, wheat, sugarcane and cotton thrice a year; various spices were sown in southern India then.
- In medieval India 'Mahattara' and 'Pattakila' were designations of **village headmen**. {28}
- Article 'Village Administration in Ancient India' (Indian Political Science Association, January–March 2004, volume 65, No. 1): village was the centre of administration in 600–1200 AD.
- Village government ran under the village headman, also known as Gramani.
- Headman's names: Garmika or Grameyaka in northern India; Pattakila or Gramakuta in Maharashtra; Gavunda in Karnataka; Mahattaka in Uttar Pradesh.
- Indian Epigraphical Glossary (writer Dinesh Chandra Sarkar) states Mahattaka = headman or a member of the Panchayat Board.
! Dr. Harishchandra Verma's 'Madhyakalin Bharat Bhag-1' describes Mahattar differently: head of the categories of artisans found in inscriptions.

- Order: building of Qutub Minar (begun by Aybak, completed by Iltutmish) → death of Firuz Tughluq (1388) → arrival of Portuguese, Vasco-da-Gama (1498) → reign of Krishnadeva Raya of Vijayanagara (1509–1529). {13}
- Order: Second Battle of Tarain (1192) → murder of Jalaluddin Khalji (1296) → Afghanpur conspiracy → transfer of capital by Muhammad Tughluq. {14}
- Prithviraj Chauhan's defeat by Muhammad Ghori in the Second Battle of Tarain (1192) led to Muslim rule in India.
- Ulugh Khan (Muhammad Bin Tughluq) is accused as the murderer of Sultan Ghiyasuddin in the Afghanpur conspiracy.
- Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq (1325–51 AD) shifted his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devgiri).

- Battle of Plassey, June 23, 1757: East India Company forces under Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah, the then Nawab of Bengal.
- Mir Zafar, Rai Durlabh and Yar Latif Khan headed the Nawab's army; Mir Zafar was bribed by Clive and caused the Nawab's defeat.
- Kalinga War: Ashoka led a vast army against Kalinga in 261 BC; freedom-loving Kalingans gave stiff resistance to the Mauryan army.
- Ashoka's 13th Major Rock Edict throws light on the Kalinga war.
- Kalinga war: at least 1 million Kalingans killed, another 50 thousand taken prisoner; almost an equal number of Magadha soldiers also killed.
- Battle of Haldighati: fought at Haldighati, Rajasthan, in 1576 AD between Maharana Pratap and Akbar's forces.
- Second Battle of Tarain: Ghurid army of Muhammad Ghori vs Rajput army of Prithviraj Chauhan, 1192 AD near Tarain; Prithviraj was defeated.
