@ 61 | Lucknow Session of Congress (The Lucknow Pact)
## Lucknow Pact, 1916
- In December 1916 both the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League held their sessions at **Lucknow**. {1}
- Both passed separate resolutions on constitutional reforms and agreed to work together on a common programme: the **Lucknow Pact** or Congress-League Scheme, signed in 1916. {3}
- The agreement was confirmed by the annual sessions of the Congress on 29 December and of the League on 31 December 1916.
- It settled the composition of the legislatures and the quantum of representation for the two communities.
- **Ambika Charan Majumdar** presided over the Lucknow Congress session of 1916. {2,9}
- Congress and League together released 19 bulletins to give a positive direction to the political scene.
- Anti-British feeling among Muslims after the war between Britain and Turkey opened the way for Congress-League unity.
- The Lucknow Pact was an important step towards Hindu-Muslim unity; this unity weakened the British attitude.
- Under the pact the Congress for the first time accepted **separate electorates** for Muslims, a great gain for the League. {6,7}
- Some Congress leaders still opposed this demand.
- Both organisations jointly demanded dominion status for the country.
- In 1916 the British Government announced a policy to increase Indian involvement in government and gradually develop local self-government institutions.
## Reunion and architects
- The session was significant for two reasons: Extremists, separated for nine years (since 1907), rejoined the Congress; and the Congress-League pact was made.
- **Annie Besant** and Tilak played the main role in bringing the Extremists back into the Congress. {4}
- **Tilak** and Muhammad Ali Jinnah were the main architects of the Congress-League (Lucknow) Pact. {5}
- Sarojini Naidu called Jinnah, chief architect of the Lucknow Pact, the **'Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity'**.
- At the Lucknow session of 1916 **Raj Kumar Shukla** told Mahatma Gandhi of the problems of the Champaran peasants. {10}
## Congress-League consensus
- 1916 to 1922 was the period of consensus between the Congress and the League. {8}
- The rupture began in 1920, when Jinnah resigned from the Congress over disagreement with Mahatma Gandhi's approach.
- The Lucknow Pact was only a temporary arrangement; the League kept its own identity and pressed separate political rights for Muslims.
- Both acted jointly till 1922; the pact dissolved with the Non-Cooperation Movement and the League went back to its old ideology.
