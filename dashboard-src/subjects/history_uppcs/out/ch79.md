@ 79 | Tripuri Crisis of Congress (1939)
## Haripura Session 1938
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** became Congress President in 1938, elected unopposed at the **Haripura Session** (February 1938); his first time as President. {1,2}
- Haripura is in **Gujarat**. {3}
## Tripuri Session 1939
- Tripuri is in **Jabalpur (Madhya Pradesh)**. {4}
- Bose became Congress President for the **second time** at the **Tripuri Session**, held in **1939**. {5,8,10}
- At Tripuri, Bose defeated **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**, the candidate supported by Gandhiji. {6,9}
- Vote count at Tripuri: Bose **1580** votes, Pattabhi Sitaramayya **1377** votes.
## The dispute and resignation
- Dispute between Bose and Gandhi (right wing) after Tripuri centred on **formation of the Congress Working Committee**; Bose resigned over it. {12}
- Gandhi was against Bose's point of view on modernization and his working conduct.
- After Bose resigned in 1939, **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** became Congress President. {7,11}
- After the Tripuri crisis (1939), Bose resigned as Congress President and founded the **Forward Bloc**.
- Forward Bloc was based on leftist ideology: a left-wing nationalist political party.
## Bose and the Second World War
- Bose saw a war between Germany and Britain as a **godsend opportunity** for Indians to exploit to their advantage. {13}
- When Second World War clouds were over Europe, Bose wanted freedom for India through a military blow to the British Government in India.
- Bose believed the old Irish saying: **"England's difficulty is Ireland's opportunity"** (also put as "Need of England is an opportunity for Ireland").
- So Bose tried to persuade Congress and Gandhiji to take the help of Britain's enemy to free India.
