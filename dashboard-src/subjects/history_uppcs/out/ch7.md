@ 7 | Sixth century B.C.E - Political Condition
## Sixteen Mahajanapadas: sources
- Before Buddha's birth, ancient India was divided into 16 major states = **'Sixteen (Shodasha) Mahajanapadas'**.
- The list of 16 Mahajanapadas is in the Buddhist text **Anguttara Nikaya** and the Jain text **Bhagavati Sutra**. {22,23,24,26}
- Anguttara Nikaya list: Koshal, Kashi, Magadha, Anga, Vajji, Chedi, Malla, Vatsa, Kuru, Panchal, Matsya, Shurasena, Kamboja, Avanti, Assaka (Ashmaka), Gandhar.
- Bhagavati Sutra list (some different names): Anga, Banga, Malay, Acha, Vacha (Vatsa), Magah (Magadha), Malav, Kochachha, Ladh, Moli (Malla), Koshala, Kashi, Padh, Sambhutar, Avaha, Vajji.
- **Panini's Ashtadhyayi** mentions 22 Janapadas, and 3 most important: Magadha, Ashmak, Kamboj. {25}
- Panini, famous for Sanskrit grammar, was born at Salatura in Pushkalavati, Gandhara.
- Ashtadhyayi = one of the earliest Sanskrit grammars: 4000 sutras (rules) in eight chapters, each divided into four sections (padas).
- Four of the sixteen were very powerful: Koshal, Magadha, Vatsa, Avanti.
- **Eight** of the 16 Mahajanapadas lay in present Uttar Pradesh: Kashi, Koshal, Vatsa, Malla, Kuru, Panchal, Shurasena, Chedi. {60}

- Hastinapur and Indraprastha were Kuru capitals; Hastinapur city of the Mahabharata lay in Kuru.
- Panchal at first had two parts; the famous city Kanyakubja lay in Panchala.
- Panchal boundaries: Himalayan foothills (north), Chambal river (south), Koshala (east), Kuru (west).
- Panchal was first a monarchy; it seems to have become a republic in the 6th–5th century BC.
- Greek writers called Shurasena 'Sourasenoi' and its capital 'Mathora'; Shurasena was an ancient Yadava ruler of Mathura.
- Anga's capital Champa: old name **'Malini'** in the Mahabharata and Puranas; Bimbisara later annexed Anga to Magadha, which lay to its west.
- Six great cities (metros) of Buddha's time, in the Mahaparinirvana-sutra: Champa, Rajgriha, Benaras, Saket, Kaushambi, Shravasti.
- **Ujjain** (Malwa, Madhya Pradesh), one of Avanti's two capitals, was anciently called **Avantika**; capital of Avanti in Buddha's time. {6}
- Uttarapatha (north-west ancient India) had two Janapadas: Gandhara and Kamboj.
- Ramayana: Takshashila city was founded by Taksha, son of Bharat; Takshashila lay between the Indus and Jhelum.
- Viratnagar (capital of Matsya) was founded by King Virat.
- Cities named in both the Mahabharata and the Mahabhashya: Madhyamika (Nagari) and Viratnagar (Bairat).
- **Shravasti**: Alexander Cunningham identified it with Sahet-Mahet (U.P.) in 1861; excavations show a **crescent-shaped** town plan. {38}
- **Kalpi** town: Jalaun district (U.P.), on the bank of the **Yamuna**. {55}
- Kalpi's ancient name = Kalpriya (Kalpriyanath / Kalpriya Nagari); built by King Vasudeva (printed as 4th century B.C. in one place, 4th century A.D. in another).

## Republics
- **Lichchhavis of Vaishali** founded the world's first republic (6th century BC). {19,20}
- Vaishali is named after King Vishal of the Mahabharata era; a great Buddhist pilgrimage site and birthplace of Mahavira.
- **Vajji** = largest and most powerful republic of Buddha's time; Vaishali was the capital of the Vajji confederacy; its rulers were Lichchhavis.
- **Chetak** = head (king) of the Lichchhavi republic; Lichchhavi was one clan of the Vajji union. {21}
- Chetak's daughter Chelana was married to Magadha king Bimbisara.
- Ten republics of Buddha's time: Shakya of Kapilavastu, Bhagga of Sumsumaragiri, Buli of Alakappa, Kalam of Kesaputta, Koliya of Ramgama, Malla of Kusinara, Malla of Pava, Moriya of Pipphalivan, Lichchhavi of Vaishali, Videha of Mithila.
- **Yaudheya** was not among the early republics. {18}
## Magadha: capitals and Pataliputra
- In ancient times the area along the river south of the Ganga was called **Magadha** (not Anarta, Takshashila or Chola). {14}
- Chola lay south of the Godavari river; Anarta was a Vedic-era kingdom.
- Puranas: first dynasty of Magadha = **Brihadratha**; Brihadratha's son **Jarasandha** made Girivraj (Rajgriha) his capital.
- Sabha Parva of the Mahabharata calls Rajgriha the capital of Jarasandha; Suttanipata calls Rajgriha 'Magadhham Puram'.
- First (initial) capital of Magadha = **Rajgriha (Girivraj, modern Rajgir)**. {33,34,35,36}
- At Rajgriha the first Buddhist Council was held and Mahavira gave his first sermon.
- **Pataliputra** was founded by **Ajatashatru** of the Haryanka dynasty, successor of Bimbisara. {12}
- **Udayin** (Haryanka, son of Ajatashatru) first shifted the capital of Magadha from Rajgriha to Pataliputra. {13}
- Among Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka, Chandragupta Vikramaditya and Kanishka, the first to have Pataliputra as capital = **Chandragupta Maurya**. {11}
! One question on who first made Pataliputra his capital had no Udayin option; there Chandragupta Maurya is taken as the answer.
- Capitals of the Magadhan empire in turn: Girivraja/Rajgriha, then Pataliputra; Kaushambi was never one — it belonged to Vatsa. {37}
- Pataliputra was also the capital of the Shishunaga, Nanda and Maurya dynasties.
- Ancient Pataliputra (modern Patna) had the **Ganga on the north** and the **Son on the west**. {10}
- Ashoka held the Third Buddhist Council at Pataliputra (247 BC), presided by Moggaliputta Tissa; the first Jain council was also held there.
- Megasthenes' **'Indica'** describes the prosperity and rule of Chandragupta Maurya's time.
- Pataliputra became the prime Buddhist centre in the Gupta period; its importance declined in the later Gupta period.
## Magadha: Haryanka dynasty
- The first Magadhan empire rose in the **6th century BC**; its real founder was Bimbisara (about 544–492 BC). {16}
- In the early 6th century BC **Magadha** was the most powerful city-state, among the richest Mahajanapadas; long rivalry with Koshala, Anga and Kashi. {17}
- The chapter note prints the same of Kashi: most powerful, richest in the early 6th century BC, rival of Koshala, Anga and Magadha.
- **Bimbisara** = founder of the Haryanka dynasty, its first and most powerful emperor; Buddhist texts call him the first great ruler of Magadha. {40}
- **Jivaka**, famous physician and contemporary of Buddha, belonged to Bimbisara's court. {52,53}
- Bimbisara sent Jivaka (Raj Vaidya) to Avanti king Chandapradyota to treat his jaundice.
- Bimbisara got Kashi (Varanasi) as dowry on marrying Mahakoshala, sister of Koshala king Prasenjit.
- Bimbisara died in 492 BC.
- **Ajatashatru 'Kunika'** (about 492–460 BC), son of Bimbisara, of the **Haryanka** dynasty; an imperialist like his father. {41}
- Ajatashatru killed his father Bimbisara to take the throne, and was killed by his own son Udayin for the same reason. {39,42}
- Udayin of Magadha is different from King Udayan of Vatsa.
- Magadha–Koshala struggle began early in Ajatashatru's reign; later a treaty: Prasenjit married his daughter Vajira to him and gave Kashi as dowry (bath money).
- Magadha–Vajji estrangement began under Bimbisara and became open conflict under Ajatashatru.
- Nirayavali Sutra: Lichchhavi chief Chetak formed a united front of 9 Lichchhavis, 9 Mallas and 18 Ganarajyas of Kashi-Koshala against Magadha.
- Bhagavati Sutta: **Ajatashatru** won over them all, defeated the Lichchhavis and annexed the Vajjis to Magadha. {43}

- Matsya Purana: **Shishunaga** defeated Nandivardhan and annexed Avanti (Malwa) to Magadha. {44}
- When Shishunaga made Vaishali his capital, he appointed his son as deputy-king of Varanasi.
- In the 6th century BC Varanasi was the capital of Kashi Mahajanapada.
! Who made Varanasi his second capital is not clear from the options given; U.P.P.S.C. accepted Shishunag.
- The **Nanda** dynasty ruled Magadha after the Shishunaga dynasty ended. {46}
- **Mahapadmananda (Ugrasen)** = founder of the Nanda dynasty; Puranas: born of a Shudra woman; title **'Ekarat'**. {45}
- Puranas also call Mahapadmananda **Sarvakshatrantaka** and **Aparoparashurama**. {47}
- Nine Nanda kings: Ugrasen (Mahapadmananda), Panduk, Pandugati, Bhootpal, Rastrapal, Govishanak, Dashasiddhak, Kaivart, Dhanananda.
- Last Nanda years: unrest due to extortion and heavy taxes on small items.
- **Chandragupta Maurya**, helped by his preceptor Chanakya, killed the last Nanda Dhanananda and founded the **Maurya** empire — the dynasty after the Nandas. {49}
- Shunga dynasty: founded by Mauryan commander **Pushyamitra Shunga** after killing the last Maurya, Brihadratha, in 185 BC.
- **Kharavela** = most important emperor of the Chedi dynasty of Kalinga; his **Hathigumpha inscription** is the main source on his reign.
- Hathigumpha inscription records a canal dug in **Kalinga** by Nanda king Mahapadmananda — evidence of King Nanda; first inscription to mention canals. {5,50}
- It shows Mahapadmananda's Kalinga victory: King Nanda took away the statue of King Jinsen.
- The inscription also records donation of villages to Jain monks and Kharavela's defeat of the Chola, Chera and Pandya states of the south.

## Coins, guilds, religious ferment, Karnata dynasty
- Earliest extant coins of India = **silver** Aahat (Punch-marked) coins; marks were made by a stamp, hence the name. {1}
- Punch-marked coins circulated from the 7th–6th centuries BC to the 1st century AD.
- **Shreni** = organisation of traders and artisans; it controlled quality and price of goods and the conduct of members; prevalent in both North and South India. {61}
- 6th century BC: great religious upheaval across the world, and Vedic religion had become very complex. {54}
- Many sects arose in the middle Ganga valley; hermits of different faiths preached and refuted each other.
- Elsewhere too: Confucius (China), Zarathustra (Iran), Pythagoras (Greece) challenged traditional assumptions.
- Vedic religion stressed purity of Shlokas and Yajnas; priests' importance rose hugely; Yajnas cost much money and led to animal killing.
- **Nanyadev**, a great warrior, founded the **Karnata** dynasty; capital Simraon.
- Karnata period (1097–1324 AD) = golden period of Mithila.
- Last Karnata ruler **Harisimha Dev**: great patron of art and literature; propounder of the **Panji** (genealogical) system.
