@ 70 | Simon Commission (1927)
## Appointment
- Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford reform) provided for a commission after ten years to review its working and suggest new steps.
- The 1919 Act was passed on the recommendations of Lord Chelmsford and Samuel Montagu to introduce self-governing institutions gradually; it introduced diarchy in the provinces.
- **Simon Commission** (Indian Statutory Commission) was appointed in November 1927 by the British Conservative Government of PM Stanley Baldwin. {4}
- Its task: report on the working of the 1919 Act and whether India was ready for further constitutional (administrative) reforms. {5}
- Chairman: **Sir John Simon**; 7 members, all white, no Indian.
- Sir John Simon belonged to the **Liberal Party**. {7}
- Clement Attlee (Labour Party), UK Prime Minister at India's independence, was also a member.
- Indians were kept out of the Commission on the suggestion of **Lord Irwin**. {8}
## Boycott and visit
- It was boycotted because it had no Indian member; this angered all political groups; Indians called it the 'White Commission'. {2,3,6,9}
- Boycotters: Indian National Congress and a section of the Muslim League headed by Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
- Congress decided to boycott it at its Madras session, December 1927.
- Simon Commission reached Bombay (India) on 3 February 1928; a countrywide strike was held that day. {1}
- Places it visited saw complete strikes and the slogan "Simon! Go Back".
- The Commission visited India twice: 3 February to 31 March 1928, and 11 October 1928 to 13 April 1929. {12}
## Recommendations
- True of the Commission: set up to inquire into the 1919 Act, headed by Sir John Simon, recommended a federal form of government, opposed by Indian leaders. {10}
- It advised a Central legislature of federal (not unitary) character, with members elected indirectly from provincial legislatures.
- It recommended replacing diarchy with responsible Government in the provinces. {11}
## Lala Lajpat Rai
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** led a non-violent protest against the Simon Commission at Lahore in 1928; the reason was that it had no Indian member. {15}
- He was severely injured in the police lathi charge during this protest and died on 17 November 1928. {13}
- Lala Lajpat Rai, a major political leader of Punjab, is known as **'Punjab Kesari'**. {14}
## Nehru Report
- **All Parties Conference** was held at Delhi in February 1928; President: **Dr. M.A. Ansari**. {23}
- 29 organisations attended, in response to the Simon Commission and the challenge of Lord Birkenhead, Secretary of State for India.
- The conference proposed drafting a constitution for responsible government; parties present besides Congress: Hindu Mahasabha, Muslim League, Khilafat Committee.
- **Nehru Report** (1928) was prepared by an all-party committee headed by **Motilal Nehru**, as an answer to the Simon Commission. {16}
- Its subject was constitutional arrangements in India. {20}
- Jawaharlal Nehru was the committee's secretary; it had 10 members including two Muslims.
- Tej Bahadur Sapru and Jayakar were members of the Nehru Report committee; they are taken as the first to demand dominion status. {17}
- Motilal Nehru in March 1926 demanded a representative conference to frame a Constitution for Dominion Status; Govind Ballabh Pant was not linked to it.
- Nehru Report asked for Dominion Status, not complete independence.
- It recommended joint electorates with seats reserved for minorities, and fundamental rights; no separate electorates or weightage for minorities. {18}
- Fundamental rights named: free expression of opinion (speech); peaceful assembly without arms; forming associations or unions.
## Reactions to Nehru Report
- Radical youth nationalists led by Jawaharlal Nehru opposed the Nehru Report and wanted complete independence as the immediate goal. {22}
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Subhas Chandra Bose** founded the **Independence for India League** in 1928 to demand complete independence, not Dominion Status. {19}
- The League helped unite progressive forces against British rule.
- **Jinnah** put forth his **Fourteen Points** in March 1929 (Muslim League) against the Nehru Report, to safeguard Muslim political rights in a self-governing India. {21}
