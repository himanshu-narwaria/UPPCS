## Rise of Maratha power
- Reasons of Maratha rise: religious consciousness, geographical security, political awakening and high quality of leadership (all). {1}
- Maratha rise was neither specific nor the result of unstable circumstances of one specific period.
- The main reason for the rise was its residents (people).
- Unity on regional/collective consciousness, religious toleration and integration of social groups grew a feeling of nationalism.
- Geographical conditions of Maharashtra helped Maratha power to flourish.
- High leadership quality of Shivaji and other Maratha leaders contributed to the rise.
- Map question: the shaded area shows the extent of territories ruled by the **Marathas**. {2}
## Shivaji: life and events
- Shivaji was born at **Shivneri** fort in 1627 or 1630 A.D.; took the title Chhatrapati in 1674 A.D. {4}
- Shivaji (Shivaji Bhonsle) was formally crowned Chhatrapati in 1674 A.D. at **Raigarh**. {9}
- Shivaji made **Raigarh** his capital. {7,8}
- Pandit Vishweshwar alias **Gaga Bhatta**, great scholar of Benares (Varanasi), performed the coronation and declared Shivaji a Kshatriya.
- Shivaji died in 1680 A.D. at the age of 53 or 50 years.
- **Samarth Ramdas** was Shivaji's guru. {10}
- Ramdas is remembered for the Advaita Vedantic text 'Daasbodh'; he also wrote the poem 'Shivastuti' in praise of Shivaji.
- Order of events: victory on Fort of Chakan 1648 A.D. → start of clash with Mughals 1657 A.D. → Afzal Khan episode 1659 A.D. → attack and sack of Surat 1664 A.D. {11}
- In 1659 A.D. Bijapur Sultan Adil Shah II sent trusted commander **Afzal Khan** to suppress Shivaji; Shivaji killed him. {5}
- **Treaty of Purandar**, 1665 A.D.: between Shivaji and the Mughals under Jai Singh.
- In 1666 A.D. Aurangzeb put Shivaji under house arrest in Jaipur Bhawan at **Agra**; Shivaji escaped from there. {6}
- Shivaji defeated the Mughals in the battle of **Salher** in 1672 A.D. {3}
- Shivaji faced opposition from big Deshmukhs (powerful landlords) who wanted to keep their power and influence. {23}
- Deshmukhs did not favour an independent Maratha state; they wanted to stay feudal subordinates of Bijapur for autonomy and special privileges.
- Silver coins of Shivaji's kingdom: Lari, Rupaya and Taka; **Ruka** was a coin of the Kakatiya dynasty. {24}
## Ashtapradhan
- **Ashtapradhan** = Council of Eight Ministers under Shivaji in the Maratha administration (reached 8 by 1674). {12,14,15,16,21}
| Minister | Other name | Work |
| Peshwa | — | Prime Minister of the King {13} |
| Amatya | Mazumdar | Finance and Revenue Minister |
| Mantri | Waqianavis (Waknis) | Interior Minister: internal affairs, especially intelligence and espionage |
| Sachiv | Surnavis (Surnis) | In charge of all correspondence |
| Sumant | Dabir | Foreign Minister {19,20} |
| Senapati | Sar-i-Naubat (Sarnobat) | Military: appointments, organisation and food supply of the army {18} |
| Pandit Rao | — | High Priest; religious aid |
| Nyayadhish | — | Chief Justice |
- Mazumdar, Dabir, Waknis and Surnis were all officers of Shivaji's Ashtapradhan. {17}
- Shivaji was advised by a Council of Ministers, but ministers did not hold independent charge. {22}
- Ashtapradhan did not work as a cabinet; each minister was head of his department.
- Shivaji could accept or reject their advice; the Ashtapradhan acted as Shivaji's secretaries.
- Ministers' main work: follow Shivaji's orders and look after the state.
## Successors of Shivaji
| Ruler | Reign | Relation |
| Sambhaji | 1680-1689 A.D. | Elder son of Shivaji {30} |
| Rajaram | 1689-1700 A.D. | Second son of Shivaji |
| Shivaji II | 1700-1708 A.D. | Son of Rajaram |
| Chhatrapati Shahuji | 1708-1749 A.D. | Son of Sambhaji |
- Rajaram led the Marathas as representative of Shahu from 1689 to 1700 A.D.
- After Rajaram's death, widow **Tarabai** put his four-year-old son Shivaji II on the throne and became regent.
- Tarabai led the struggle against the Mughals from 1700 A.D. and took the forts of Singhgarh, Raigarh and Satara. {35}
- At Aurangzeb's death the Maratha leadership was in the hands of **Tarabai**. {34}
## Peshwas
| Peshwa | Tenure |
| Balaji Vishwanath | 1713-1720 {26,27,28,29} |
| Bajirao I (elder son of Balaji Vishwanath; appointed by Shahu) | 1720-1740 |
| Balaji Bajirao (appointed by Shahu after Bajirao's death) | 1740-1761 |
| Madhavrao I | 1761-1772 |
| Narayan Rao | 1772-1773 |
| Raghunath Rao (Raghoba) | 1773-1774 |
- After Sambhaji, power came to **Balaji Vishwanath**, who made the administration easy and convenient (under Shahu). {25}
- Chhatrapati Shahu appointed Balaji Vishwanath as Peshwa; November 1713 marked the shift of power from Chhatrapati to Peshwa.
- Balaji Vishwanath's main achievement: a temporary Mughal-Maratha agreement that properly defined authority and region.
- By the time of **Balaji Bajirao** the post of Peshwa became hereditary; the king became a non-entity and Peshwa the virtual ruler. {32}
- By the **Treaty of Sangola**, 1750 A.D., the Peshwa became the real and effective head of the Maratha confederacy; Shahu's successors depended on the Peshwa's will. {31}
- After Madhavrao's death in 1772 A.D., his brother Narayanrao fell victim to a conspiracy of uncle Raghunathrao, who wanted to be Peshwa.
- After Mughal decline the Marathas became the strongest native power, able to take the Mughals' place. {33}
- Maratha power rested on the people of Maharashtra, united on Maratha identity; they failed to develop a concept of unified India (a major criticism).
## Maratha revenue terms and script
- **Saranzami** system = feudal-military land grant (land revenue) system of the Marathas. {36}
- Saranzami land was given for livelihood of Maratha Jagirdars, for upkeep of forts and troops for public service.
- **Chauth** = Maratha claim of revenue for protection; collected from provinces known as Mughalai. {37}
- **Modi script** was used in Maratha documents, especially revenue and administrative records. {46}
## Abdali and Third Battle of Panipat
- In January 1757 A.D. Ahmad Shah Abdali entered Delhi and looted up to Agra and Mathura.
- Before returning he confirmed Alamgir II as Emperor and Imad-ul-Mulk as Prime Minister.
- Abdali made Rohilla chief **Najib-ud-Daulah (Najib Khan)** his chief agent and Mir Bakshi of the empire. {45}
- Third Battle of Panipat: 14 January 1761, Marathas under Sadashivrao Bhau vs Afghan invader Ahmad Shah Abdali. {41,42,43,44}
- The Marathas were badly defeated by the Afghans. {40}
- Immediate reason: Abdali wanted revenge as the Marathas had expelled his Viceroy Timur Shah from Lahore. {39}
- **Kashiraj Pandit**, eyewitness historian, said the Third Battle of Panipat proved catastrophic for the Marathas. {38}
- A merchant's message to Balaji Bajirao: "Two pearls have been dissolved, 22 gold coins have been lost and of the silver and copper the total cannot be cast up."
## Ahilyabai
- Ahilyabai was a Maratha ruler of the Holkar dynasty; Holkars belong to Indore; under her the capital moved to Maheshwar.
