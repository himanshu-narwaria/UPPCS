## Aryans and their homeland
- 'Vedic' comes from the Sanskrit word 'Veda', meaning knowledge.
- The civilization that grew in India after the Indus Valley Civilization is called Vedic or Aryan Civilization.
- **Arya** means great or highborn; in classical Sanskrit Arya means 'a noble man'. {1,2}
- 'Aryan' (meaning 'noble') was a self-designation of Indo-Iranian people; Vedic people used it as an ethnic label for themselves and for the noble class.
- Scholars say that even in ancient times the idea of 'Aryan' was religious, cultural and linguistic, not racial.
- History of Aryans is known mainly from the Vedas; Aryans are generally said to be the authors of the Vedas.
- Scholars differ on the original home of the Aryans.

- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**, in his book 'The Arctic Home in the Vedas', said the North Pole (Arctic region) was the original home of Aryans; most historians do not accept this. {51}
- The Indo-Iranian group that composed the Zoroastrian Avesta and the Hindu Vedas called themselves Aryans; the Avesta, written by Zoroastrians, relates to present-day **Iran**. {105}
- **Boghaz-Koi inscription** (Asia Minor, Turkiye), of 14th century B.C., mentions four Vedic gods: Indra, Varuna, Mitra and Nasatya; it tells of the Aryans' movement from Iran to India. {48,49,50}
- Vedic Age has two parts: Rigvedic or Early Vedic Age (1500-1000 BCE) and Later Vedic Age (1000-600 BCE).
- 800 B.C. to 600 B.C. is the Brahmin (Brahmana) era. {81}
- Sutra period = from 7th or 6th century B.C. to 3rd century B.C.
## Geography and rivers
- Rigveda mentions four rivers of Afghanistan: Kubha (Kabul), Krumu (Kurram), Gomati (Gomal) and Suvastu (Swat); this shows the Aryans' deep relations with Afghanistan. {34,35}
- Rigveda mentions seven rivers of Sapta-Sindhu (land of seven rivers): Saraswati, Vipasa, Parushni, Vitasta, Sindhu (Indus), Shutudri and Asakini.
- Yamuna is mentioned three times and Ganga once in the Rigveda.
- Marudvridha, a river of Kashmir, is also mentioned.
- **Sindhu (Indus)** is the river mentioned most in the Rigveda; it was the most important river of the Vedic period. {32}
- Indus is called 'Hiranyani' (Hiranyayi) for its link with gold; it ends in the Arabian Sea.
- **Saraswati** was the most sacred river: called 'Matetama' (best of all mothers), 'Devitama' (best of all goddesses) and 'Naditama' (best of all rivers). {60}
- Saraswati is believed to be the scene of activities of the Vedic tribes Bharatas, Purus and Kurus.

- Chenab is also called Chandrabhaga; it rises at Tandi in Lahul Spiti, where the rivulets Chandra and Bhaga meet.
- Aryans called the Ganga-Yamuna doab and nearby areas **Brahmarshi Desh**.
- Area between the Himalaya and Vindhyachal ranges was named **Madhya Desh**.
- Later the Aryans spread over the whole of North India, which came to be called **Aryavarta**.
- In the Mahabharata period the Mahanadi was called **Chitrotpala**; the Bhishma Parv praises it as Punayadayini and Paap (sin) Nashini; Indian people drank its water. {37}
- Mahoba copper plate of Someshwar Dev calls the Mahanadi 'Chitrotpala Ganga'.
- Vayu Purana names the Mahanadi **Nilotapala**; Matsya Purana calls it Chitrotpala. {38}
## Vedic literature: the four Vedas
- Vedic literature is also called **Shruti**; Vedas are the most ancient literature of India.
- Four Vedas: Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda and Atharvaveda; **Rigveda** is the oldest and most important. {3,4}
- Rigveda, Yajurveda and Samaveda together are called **Vedatrayi** or **Trayi**. {5}
- Each Veda has four parts, in this order: Samhitas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas and Upanishads. {31}
- Samhitas and Brahmanas form the Karma Kanda of the Vedas.
- Aranyakas and Upanishads form the Gyan Kanda; they focus on philosophy and spirituality.

## Rigveda
- History of the Rigvedic age is known from the Rigveda; it is the oldest known book in the world, though its author and date are disputed.
- Rigveda is an ancient collection of Vedic Sanskrit hymns by several seers, each hymn averaging about ten verses; one of the four canonical sacred texts (Shruti) of Hinduism. {12}
- Rigveda has 10 mandals, 1028 suktas (hymns) and 10552 verses (Richas / mantras).
- Mandals 2 to 7 of the Rigveda are the most ancient.

- **Gayatri Mantra** is in the third Mandal of the Rigveda; written in Sanskrit by Brahmarshi Vishwamitra; dedicated to Savitra (Surya). {82,83,84}
- Meaning of Gayatri Mantra: "May the Almighty God illuminate our intellect to lead us along the righteous path."
- Trishtubh Mantra was prescribed in the Upanayana ceremony of Kshatriyas; it stood for their martial, heroic qualities and warrior duties. {85}
- All 114 hymns of the **ninth mandal** are dedicated to 'Soma'. {18,19}
- At first three Varnas are described: Brahman, Kshatriya and Vaishya.
- Shudra is first mentioned in the **Purush Sukta** of the 10th Mandal of the Rigveda. {7}
- 'Varna' is first found in the Rigveda: a hymn shows Brahmin (priest), Kshatriya (noble), Vaishya (commoner) and Shudra (servant) coming from the mouth, arms, thighs and feet of the primeval Purusha. {6}
- Literal meaning of Varna (Sanskrit) is colour or class.
- Priests of the Rigveda were called **Hotri / Hota**. {104}
- According to Patanjali, the Rigveda has 21 branches.
- Basic script of the Rigveda was **Brahmi**; work on script or 'Bhasa' is said to be started by Brahma, so the script was called Brahmi. {103}
- Iron is not mentioned in the Rigveda but is found in later Vedic literature.
## Yajurveda, Samaveda, Atharvaveda
- **Yajurveda** in Sanskrit means 'wisdom of sacrificial formulas'; compiled a century or two after the Rigveda.
- Yajurveda has prose and verse formulas spoken by priests doing the manual part of the sacrifice; it gives procedure of rituals and Yajnas using Rigveda hymns. {20}
- Yajurveda Samhitas have two parts: **Shukla (White)** Yajurveda and **Krishna (Black)** Yajurveda. {21}
- In Krishna Yajurveda mantras are irregular and mixed; in Shukla Yajurveda their order and recitation are more systematic.
- Shukla Yajurveda has a separate Brahmana text, the Shatpatha Brahmana.
- **Vajasneyi** is the Samhita of Shukla Yajurveda. {17}
- Shukla Yajurveda is only in poetic form; Krishna Yajurveda has both poetic and prose form.
- **Shatapatha Brahmana**: prose text on Vedic rituals, history and mythology, linked to the Yajurveda; placed after the Rigveda in Vedic literature; also describes social subjects.
- 'Purushamedha' is mentioned in the Shatpatha Brahmana of Shukla Yajurveda and the Taittiriya Brahmana of Krishna Yajurveda.
- Many historians discuss the Purushmedh Yajna only with the Shatpatha Brahmana.
! The question on which scripture mentions 'Purusha Medha' has no single answer: Shukla Yajurveda, Krishna Yajurveda and Shatpatha Brahmana were all among its options.
- Shatpatha Brahmana tells of King **Videgha Madhava** (a king of Videha) and his priest **Rishi Gautama Rahugana**, carrying the sacred fire to the east. {53}
- The king carried fire (Agni Vaisvanara) in his mouth; on his guru's prompting it fell out and burned the earth towards the east.
- This story keeps the tradition of how the Kosalas and the Videhas received their culture.
- **Samaveda** is the shortest of the four Vedas; closely tied to the Rigveda, from which it took many verses. {22}
- Samaveda was compiled for ritual use; all its verses are meant to be chanted in ceremonies; its hymns are lyrical and melodious.
- Samaveda Samhita has 1875 verses; all except 75 or 99 are from the Rigveda.
- In **Atharvaveda** the priest chanting mantras was called the Brahmana priest.
- 'Anga' and 'Magadha' are first mentioned in the Atharvaveda, along with Gandhari and Mujavats.
- The earliest account of magical charms and spells is in the Atharvaveda. {10}
- **Ayurveda** ('Science of Life') first appeared in the Atharvaveda; it is an Upaveda (also taken as Upanga) of the Atharvaveda. {72}
- Atharvaveda describes thoughts and superstitions of common people; contents: disease prevention, coordination, loyalty, marriage and love poetry.
- Yajurveda contains the description of stotras and recitals.
- Atharvaveda holds much knowledge of the physical world and spirituality.
- **Gopatha Brahmana** is the only Brahmana of the Atharvaveda; a prose text on Vedic rituals; linked with both Shaunaka and Paippalada recensions.
- In the post-Vedic period the **Kuru-Panchala** region was the axis of Aryan culture, for its achievements in science, mathematics, astronomy, religion and philosophy. {54}
## Upanishads
- Upanishad means pupils sitting near the teacher to learn the secret doctrine.
- Upanishads are books of **philosophy**, for all times and all people. {25,26}
- Upanishadic philosophy opposes no school of thought, religion or reading of scripture, but its method of explaining is unique.
- The word salvation (Moksha) is not used in the Vedas; it was first discussed in the **Upanishads**. {27}
- Moksha means 'liberation'; the endless cycle of life and death is not the real aim of the human soul.
- As per the Upanishads, the core of the self is not body or mind but the Atman (Soul).
- **Kathopanishad** (Katha Upanishad) has the dialogue of Yama, lord of death, and Nachiketa, a 12-year-old boy who left home to find the meaning of death and beyond. {28,29}
- **"Satyameva Jayate"** ("Truth alone triumphs") is from the **Mundakopanishad**. {95,96,97}
- Satyameva Jayate is India's national motto, inscribed at the base of the national emblem; adopted by the Government of India on 26 January 1950.
- **Satyakama Jabala**, a boy and later a Vedic sage, first appears in chapter IV of the **Chhandogya Upanishad**; the legend challenges the stigma of an unmarried mother. {102}
- **"Tamso Ma Jyotirgamaya"** is from the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad; it means "lead us from darkness to light, from ignorance to truth". {98}
- The dialogue of Yagnavalkya and Gargi is in the **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad**. {100}
- Gargi was a philosopher of the Later Vedic/Upanishadic period, not of the Rigvedic period. {99}
- **Shvetashvatara Upanishad**, embedded in the Yajurveda, literally means 'carried on a white horse'. {101}
- Upanishads contain several parables and stories; they were composed mainly in the later Vedic period, much before the Puranas (which began around the early centuries of the common era). {88}
- A parable is a short simple story teaching a religious or moral lesson.
- Parable of the two birds (about the jivatma, a blend of human and divine) is in the Kathopanishad, and also in the Mundaka and Svetasvatara Upanishads.
- Upanishads say some Kshatriya kings, great scholars, taught even Brahmins: Janak of Videha, Pravahan Jabali of Panchal, Asvapati of Kekaya and Ajatshatru of Kashi.
- **King Asvapati**, a Kshatriya and a famed learned teacher, ruled **Kekaya** when Janak was king of Videha. {30}
- Kekaya kingdom lay on the bank of river Beas, east of Gandhara.
## Puranas and epics
- Puranas are the sacred literature of Hinduism; there are **18 Puranas**. {87}
- The 18 Puranas: Matsya, Markandeya, Bhagavat, Bhavishya, Brahmanda, Brahma Vaivrata, Brahma, Vamana, Varaha, Vishnu, Vayu, Agni, Narada, Padma, Linga, Garuda, Skanda, Kurma.
- Srimad Bhagvatam is called the 'Crown Jewel' of all Vedic literature.
- Five characteristics of Puranas: Sarga, Pratisarga, Vansa, Manvantara and Vanshanucharita. {86}
- Sarga and Pratisarga = natural creation and renovation (cosmogony); Vansa = history of sages and gods.
- Manvantara = period of different Manus; Vanshanucharita = genealogy of kings.
- Vishnu Purana tells of the Mauryan dynasty; Vayu Purana relates to the Gupta dynasty; Matsya Purana relates to the Andhra Satavahana dynasty. {89}
- **Shrimad Bhagavad Gita** was originally written in Sanskrit; it has 700 verses and is part of the Mahabharata. {90}
- Mahabharata originally had only 8,800 shlokas; its original name was 'Jaya' or **Jayasamhita**. {91}
- Mahabharata today has 100,000 shlokas (hence Shatasahasri-Samhita); believed to be written by Sage Veda Vyasa. {92}
- Churning of the ocean of milk (Ksira Sagar): Mount Mandara was the churning rod; **Vasuki**, king of serpents who stays on Shiva's neck, was the rope. {93}
- Lord Vishnu, as Kurma Avatar, held the mountain on his back.
## Rigvedic society
- Family or **Kul** was the smallest unit of Rigvedic society.
- Rigvedic society was patriarchal; the Varun Sukta shows a father could sell his child.
- **Pancha-Jana** = five tribes named after their founder ancestors: Yadu, Turvasu, Druhyu, Anu and Puru; Kikat was not among them. {61}
- **Dasharajnya** ('Battle of Ten Kings'), told in the Rigveda, was fought on the bank of the **Parushni** river. {58,59}
- The Dasharajnya story is placed long before the Ramayana.
- Sudas, a descendant of King Bharata, won a great victory over a confederacy of ten kings.
- Women who composed Rigveda hymns were called **Brahmavadini**: Lopamudra, Vishwawara, Sikta, Nivavari, Ghosa. {44}
- Lopamudra was the wife of Agastya Rishi.
- Ghosha, Apala and Vishvavara are female seers with hymns in the Rigveda.
- Vansha, Gotra and Kula are family-system words; **Kosa** relates to the treasury. {112}
- **Gotra** first occurs in the Rigveda, meaning "cow shelter" or "herd of cows". {55}
- The sense "family lineage kin" for gotra is more recent, first recorded around the post-Vedic period.
- Later Vedic period: four **ashramas** were recognised (Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sanyasa) and four **Purusharthas** (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha). {39}
- **Pancha Mahayajnas** were performed in the Grihastha Ashram: Brahma Yajna, Deva Yajna, Pitra Yajna, Bhuta Yajna, Atithi Yajna. {40}
- Grihastha Ashram was the foundation of society: one earned wealth and supported the other three ashramas.
- There are **sixteen Sanskaras**: three before birth, twelve during life, one after death. {113}
- The 16 Sanskaras: Garbhadhana (conception), Punsavana (foetus protection), Simantonnayana (satisfying cravings of the pregnant mother), Jatakarman (childbirth), Namakaran (naming), Nishkramana (first outing), Annaprasana (solid food), Chudakarma (hair cutting), Karnavedha (ear piercing), Vidhyarambha (starting education), Upanayana (sacred thread), Vidyarambha (study of Vedas), Keshant, Samavartana (return home after education), Vivaha (marriage), Antyesthi (cremation).
- Upanayana seems the most important and common Sanskara, besides the wedding and the ritual after death.
- **Upadhyaya** = one who took up teaching for a livelihood and taught only a part of the Veda or Vedangas. {114}
- Untouchability took definite shape in the early Christian era, the **Dharmashastra** era, when Brahmanical religion was rising. {94}
- In the Dharmashastra era scriptures were being written and social customs were strictly practised; untouchability was one of them.

## Economy and daily life
- In ancient times the Aryans depended on **agriculture** and hunting for livelihood. {62}
- Cattle breeding was the most important economic activity of Rigvedic people; prayers were made for more cattle; cattle were a medium of exchange; the **cow** was the most valued property. {110}
- The cow is treated as a deity in some suktas of the Rigveda.
- The cow was called **"Aghanya"** (not worthy to be killed) in the Vedic period, showing how pious it was held. {106,107,108}
- **'Yava'** in the Rigveda means the food grain barley. {63}
- Crop names: Vrihi = rice; Mudga = mung bean; Yava = millet (in this matching list); Ikshu = sugarcane. {64}
- **'Nishka'** (Nishka-Griva) was a gold necklace in the Rigvedic period; later the word came to mean gold coins. {45,46,47}
- **"Pani"** in the Rigvedic period was a class of traders. {65}

- Earliest evidence of iron use in India is from **Atranjikhera**, dating to around 1000 B.C. {23}
- Atranjikhera: prehistoric and historic site on the Kali river, a tributary of the Ganga; first identified by Sir Alexander Cunningham in 1861-62; excavated by R.C. Gaur.
- Atranjikhera digging: trial trench 1962; small-scale digging next year; regular excavation from 1964.
- Early iron smelting by tribal artisans in India dates to 1300-1200 B.C.; most archaeologists take about 1000 B.C. (around 3000 years ago) as the start of the Iron Age in the subcontinent. {24}
- Rigveda mentions a coat of mail and helmet of gold and iron, possibly used in war; Indus people left no evidence of weapons and were possibly ruled by a class of merchants. {109}
- Rigvedic Aryans knew gold, silver and copper; Indus people knew only copper and bronze; iron spread in North India between 1000 BC and 600 BC.
- Rigvedic Aryans owed their success in India to horses and chariots; horse bones were found at Bhagwanpura (Haryana, Rigvedic period).
- Indus horse evidence: from a superficial level of Mohenjo-daro and a doubtful terracotta figurine from Lothal.
## Religion and gods
- Religion of early Vedic Aryans was mainly worship of nature and performing Yajnas. {56}
- Early Aryans saw life as a manifestation of nature and believed a divine power works behind fire, water, wind.
- Sacrifice (Yajna) was the central feature of Rigvedic religion; domestic sacrifice was the rule; community sacrifices by kings were grand festivals; gods were believed to be pleased by Yajna. {57}
- Rigvedic religion was **polytheistic**: many gods were worshipped; nature was worshipped as having a living soul. {73}
- Vedic gods in three groups: sky = Varuna, Mitra, Surya, Vishnu; space = Indra, Rudra; earth = Agni, Brihaspati, Soma.
- Treating whichever god is worshipped as the Supreme God is called Ekeshwarvaad; Rigveda supports it with "Ekam Satt Vipra bahudha badanti".
- Rigvedic Ekeshwarvaad included Advaityavaad, Sarvadevtavaad and Bahudevatavaad; this one-supreme-God idea differs from modern Ishwarvaad.
- **Indra** is the most powerful and most important god of the Rigveda; about 250 hymns (the largest number) are devoted to him. {74,76,79}
- **Agni** comes second with 200 hymns. {77}
- Indra is called **Purandara** (destroyer of forts); he was the Storm-God, Sky-God and **God of War**, who wields the thunderbolt. {75,80}
- Indra is the king and Dev of Svarga (heaven): god of heaven, lightning, thunder and rain; some hymns pray to Indra to punish sinners. {78}
- **Varuna** upheld 'Rita' (the moral/natural order), so he was called 'Ritasyagopa'.
- **'Rita'** = universal principle of natural order (natural law), of Vedic origin; basis of Indian culture, legal theory, politics and philosophy. {42}
- Dharma and Rita appear as early as the Rigveda; Dharma = cosmic ordinance, natural or divine law; Rita = universal harmony where all things have a proper place and function. {41}
- Rita means an impersonal law; Dharma means the personal actions that create or keep the cosmic order.
- Dharma = idea of obligations and doing one's duties to oneself and to others; Rita = fundamental moral law governing the universe and all in it.
- **Brihaspati** (Deva-guru) is the teacher or priest of the gods. {43}
## Polity and administration
- The most common system of rule in the Vedic era was **hereditary monarchy**; some cases of election by the people existed, but people favoured monarchy. {66}
- 'Sabha' and 'Samiti' are named as checks on the king's autocracy.
- **Sabha** = assembly of the learned and elite (a council of ministers); **Samiti** = assembly of the common people. {67}
- The two cannot be told apart precisely; Rigveda mentions Sabha at least eight times and Samiti nine times.
- **Atharvaveda** calls Sabha and Samiti the two daughters of Prajapati and shows them as two separate institutions. {68,69}
- The tenth Mandal of the Rigveda also mentions Sabha and Samiti and appeals for unity between them, but their roles are not clearly separated there.
- Rigveda mentions tribal or clan assemblies Sabha, Samiti and Vidatha; **Sabha** had the judicial function. {70}
