@ 52 | Indian National Congress
## Founding of the Congress
- **Indian National Congress** was founded by **A.O. Hume** (Allan Octavian Hume), a retired British officer of the Indian Civil Service. {1,3,4}
- In 1884 Hume set up the **Indian National Union**, the precursor of the Congress.
- Hume visited Kolkata, Mumbai and Madras, then took the initiative to call the first meeting of the Union at Poona.
- Its objective: a share in government for educated Indians, and a platform for civil and political dialogue with the British Raj.
- The Congress was set up in **1885**. {5,13}
- First session: 28–30 December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, **Bombay**. {2,7}
- It was first fixed for Poona but shifted to Bombay due to an outbreak of cholera.
- At this session, on Dadabhai Naoroji's advice, the name Indian National Union was changed to Indian National Congress.
- **72 delegates** took part in the first session. {6}
- **W.C. Bonnerjee** (Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee) presided over the first session = first President of the Congress. {8,9,10,11,12}
- **A.O. Hume** was the first General Secretary of the Congress (1885). {16}
- Hume was never President of the Congress.
- **Surendranath Banerjee** was not present at the founding session of the Congress. {23}
- Reason: he was presiding over the second Indian National Conference at Calcutta in December 1885; the first Conference was held in December 1883.
- Indian Association was founded by Surendranath Banerjee and Anand Mohan in Bengal in 1876 to promote youth nationalism.
- The Congress was established during the viceroyalty of **Lord Dufferin** (1884–88). {17,19,20,21}
- Dufferin ridiculed the Congress as representing only a **'microscopic minority'** (short-sighted minority) of the Indian people. {18,22}
## Early objectives and demands
- W.C. Bonnerjee's 1885 objectives did not include expressing views on social issues with the consent of the educated class. {14}
- Objective 1: promote contact and friendship among Indians defending the country's interests.
- Objective 2: build national unity by removing feelings of caste, sect and regional bias.
- Objective 3: unite all classes and communities of India and create a sense of one nation.
- Objective 4: formulate and present public demands.
- Initially there was an arrangement not to raise social issues from the Congress platform.
- In the first stage the Congress demanded **separation** of judiciary from executive; it never demanded their unification. {15}
- Early demands: greater voice for Indians in government and administration; Indians in high posts.
- Early demands: legislative councils to be more representative, with more power, and set up in provinces that had none.
- Early demands: repeal of the Arms Act; freedom of speech and expression.
## Early sessions
- Second session: Calcutta, 27–30 December 1886, President Dadabhai Naoroji; 436 registered delegates took part.
- Muslim delegates: 2 in the first session, **33** in the second, 81 in the third (1887), 221 in the fourth (1888). {25}
- **Badruddin Tyabji** = first Muslim President of the Congress; presided over the 3rd session at Madras, 27–30 December 1887. {27,28,29,31,32}
- 607 delegates took part in the 1887 Madras session.
- At Madras 1887 the working of the Congress was first handed to a committee of delegates; a Subjects Committee was appointed for the first time.
- **British Committee of the Indian National Congress** was set up in London in July 1889 under the presidency of **Sir W. Wedderburn** to gain support for the Congress. {26}
- It was a committee of the Indian National Congress.
- It brought out the journal 'India' to tell the British about Indian matters (a monthly that later became weekly; also printed simply as a weekly).
## Critics of the Congress
- **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** was never connected with the Indian National Congress. {34}
- Sir Syed was in judicial service during the revolt of 1857 and was loyal to the British.
- He became an opponent of the Congress due to misinterpretations by the British.
- He published the magazine "Rajbhakt Muslim" to show loyalty to the British; he spread his ideas through "Tehzeeb-ul-Akhlaq".
- In 1888 Sir Syed Ahmed Khan and Raja Shiv Prasad Singh "Sitar-i-Hind" of Banaras founded the **United Indian Patriotic Association**.
- Its aim was to stop the Congress spreading its ideals to the masses and to keep people away from the Congress.
- Sir Syed founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh, teaching Western subjects, science and Muslim religion.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** wrote that the "Congress movement was neither inspired by the people nor devised or planned by them." {56}
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** first used the **safety valve theory**, in 'Young India', to attack the Moderates. {57,58}
- Lajpat Rai considered the Congress the brainchild of Dufferin, meant to protect the British Government from threats.
- **Lord Curzon** declared in 1900: "The Congress is tottering to its fall, and one of my greatest ambitions while in India is to assist it to a peaceful demise." {59}
- The British assumed the Congress under the Moderates was weak, lacked Indians' trust and would collapse easily.

## Women presidents
- **Annie Besant** = first woman President of the Congress (32nd session, Calcutta, 26–29 December 1917). {36,39,40,41,42,43,44}
- Annie Besant was of Anglo-Irish origin, one of the few foreigners with a major role in the freedom movement.
- She was President of the Theosophical Society 1907–1933 and founded the Home Rule League in 1916.
- **Sarojini Naidu** (1879–1949) = first Indian woman President of the Congress (40th session, Kanpur, 1925). {45,46}
- Sarojini Naidu was a famous poet, a trusted colleague of Gandhiji and went to jail many times.
- She was Governor of U.P. during 1947–49, the first Indian woman to hold that post.
## Lal-Bal-Pal and the Lucknow session
- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** was never elected President of the Congress. {35,37}
- Among Lal, Bal and Pal, only **Lala Lajpat Rai** became Congress President (special session, Calcutta, 1920). {38}
- Lajpat Rai was called 'Sher-e-Punjab' (Lion of Punjab) and wrote 'Unhappy India'.
- Motilal Nehru was Congress President in 1919 and 1928; he set up the Swaraj Party in 1923.
- Tilak was born in a Chitpavan Brahmin family of Maharashtra; he edited 'Kesari' and 'Mahratta'.
- In 1916 both the Congress and the Muslim League met at Lucknow and signed the **Lucknow Pact**; the second session (1886) was under Dadabhai Naoroji. {47}
- At Lucknow 1916 the Extremists, excluded for 9 years, rejoined the Congress.
- Lucknow Pact was signed through the efforts of Tilak and M.A. Jinnah; also called the 'Congress-League Agreement'.
- Tilak proclaimed "Swaraj is my birthright, I shall have it" at the **Lucknow session, 1916**. {50}
- The slogan "Swaraj mera janma siddha adhikar hai, aur main ise lekar rahunga" is Tilak's. {51,52,53,54}
- A.O. Hume was called the Father of the Congress at the 1912 Bankipore session.
- **Lord Wellingdon** took part in the Bombay session of 1915. {63}
- Wellingdon was then Governor of Bombay, not Governor-General; he was Governor-General and Viceroy from 1931 to 1936.
## 1919 to 1924
- The last Congress session attended by Tilak was **Amritsar, 1919**. {87}
- At Amritsar Tilak opposed Gandhi's policy of boycotting council elections under the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms.
- Tilak advised 'responsive cooperation' in working the reforms; he died on 1 August 1920.
- Montagu-Chelmsford Report went to Parliament in 1918; its reforms gave some Indian participation in regional government.
- By the Amritsar 1919 resolution Gandhi was to write the new Congress Constitution; **N.C. Kelkar and I.B. Sen** were elected to help him. {62}
- Gandhi wanted Tilak and C.R. Das, but they were overloaded with work, so their juniors Kelkar and Sen replaced them.
- At the **Nagpur session, 1920** the Congress first declared its policy towards the Indian States. {65}
- It asked the princely states to give full responsible government to their people.
- Nagpur 1920 approved Provincial Congress Committees on a linguistic basis; in 1948 the Congress rejected linguistic provinces. {66}
- **C.R. Das** acted as President from prison (Ahmedabad, 1921); **Alfred Webb** was President in 1894 (Madras). {55}
- C.R. Das (Deshbandhu) was an eminent lawyer who successfully defended Aurobindo Ghosh in the Alipore Bomb Case.
- C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru formed the Swaraj Party within the Congress, to enter the councils and disrupt their working.
- **All India Harijan Sevak Sangh** was established by M.K. Gandhi in 1932. {70}
## 1936 to independence
- At the **Lucknow session, 1936** Nehru's presidential address called socialism the key to India's problems. {77}
- In his 1938 presidential address **Subhas Chandra Bose** advocated Roman script for the Hindi language. {60}
- **National Planning Committee** was set up in 1938 by Congress President Subhas Chandra Bose with **Jawaharlal Nehru** as Chairman; K.T. Shah was its secretary. {79}
- **Abul Kalam Azad** (1888–1958) = youngest Congress President (Delhi special session, 1923). {81}
- **Azad** was President for six consecutive years, 1940–46, during the Quit India Movement; the longest tenure before independence. {83}
- After independence the longest-serving Congress President is Sonia Gandhi: 1998 to 2017 and 2019 to 2022.
- Life spans: Jawaharlal Nehru 1889–1964; Ananda Mohan Bose 1847–1906; Bhupendra Nath Bose 1859–1924.
- **J.B. Kripalani** was Congress President at the time of independence and partition; elected at Meerut in November 1946. {84,85}
- Acharya Jivatram Bhagwandas Kripalani = Gandhian socialist, environmentalist, mystic; noted for incorruptibility and determination.
- Kripalani was in top Congress affairs for over a decade and helped organise the Salt Satyagraha and the Quit India Movement.
- **Mahatma Gandhi** suggested winding up the Congress after independence. {61}
- **'Jana-Gana-Mana'** was first sung at the Calcutta session of the Congress on 27 December 1911. {86}
- It is the first of five stanzas of a Brahmo hymn in Bengali, composed and scored by Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore.
- The Constituent Assembly adopted it as the National Anthem on 24 January 1950.
