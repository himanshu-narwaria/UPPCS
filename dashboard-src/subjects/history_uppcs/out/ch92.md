## Anglo-Afghan relations
- 19th century: fear of **Russian** expansion towards India was the basis of Anglo-Afghan relations. {2}
- Treaty of **Turkmenchay** (1828) between Russia and Iran raised British fear of Russian influence; experts in England called it "The Crisis in India".
- British feared a Russian invasion of India through the **Khyber and Bolan** Passes; North-Western passes were the key to entering India.
- British view: Afghanistan should be under a friendly government in line with the British.
- British **Forward Policy** in Afghanistan: aggressive plan to make a buffer state against Russia; advocated by **Lord Auckland**, during the First Anglo-Afghan War (1839-42).

## Famines
- **Orissa (Odisha) famine of 1866-67** was called the "**Sea of Calamity**". {9}
- Orissa famine deaths: about one million; another figure: at least 1.3 million (13 lakh), one-third of the population, died in 1866 from starvation and disease.
- Odisha was then quite isolated from the rest of India, so the famine hit it hardest.
- **Campbell Commission** (George Campbell, 1866): formed after the Orissa famine to study causes and suggest prevention and relief.
- **Strachey Commission** (appointed 1878, after the Great Famine of 1876-78) prepared the **Indian Famine Code, 1883**. {10}
- **Lyall Commission**: famine inquiry after the 1896-97 famine; suggested relief measures.
- Famine commissions = Campbell, Lyall, Strachey; **Hunter Commission** was not one, it dealt mainly with education. {11}

## Pindaris, Thugs and social reformers
- **Lord Hastings** (Governor-General from 1813) suppressed the **Pindaris**. {20}
- Pindaris first mentioned in 1689, at the time of the Mughal attack on Maharashtra.
- Early 19th century Pindari leaders: Chittu, Wasil Muhammad, Karim Khan.
- Hastings surrounded the Pindaris: from Bengal in north and east, Gujarat in west, Deccan in south.
- Army of 113,000 men and 300 cannons: northern part under Hastings, southern under Thomas Hislop.
- End of 1817: Pindaris driven out of Malwa and across the Chambal; January 1818: practically exterminated.
- Major General Sir William Henry **Sleeman** suppressed **Thuggee**.
- **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**: pillar of Bengal Renaissance; carried on Raja Rammohan Roy's reform; started widow remarriage.
- Vidyasagar opened colleges to lower-caste students (earlier only for Brahmins); people called him "Daya Sagar" (ocean of kindness).
- Justice **M.G. Ranade**: judge, politician, writer, reformer; set up the Social Conference Movement against child marriage and heavy spending on marriages; backed widow remarriage and female education.

## Pacts and national movement details
- Khilafat Movement (1919-24): movement of Indian Muslims led by Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali, supported by Gandhi.
- Moplah Revolt (1921): peak of a series of riots by Mappila Muslims of Malabar, Kerala against the British and Hindu landlords in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
- **Simon Commission**: appointed 1927, came to India 1928 to study constitutional reform.
- **Dandi March** (Salt March), began 12 March 1930: from Sabarmati Ashram, to make salt at coastal village Dandi and break the salt tax law; started Civil Disobedience.
- Gandhi-Irwin Pact opened the way for Congress/Gandhi to attend the Second Round Table Conference, London, September-December 1931.
- **Poona Pact**, 24 September 1932: between Ambedkar and Gandhi; 148 seats reserved for untouchables under a joint electorate, instead of a separate electorate for Dalits.
- Poona Pact was signed by Pt. Madan Mohan Malviya (for Gandhi) and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, after senior leaders stepped in: efforts of Malviya, C. Rajagopalachari, Rajendra Prasad and Purushottamdas.
- Congress ministers resigned in October 1939 because Britain put India into the Second World War without Indian consent.
- 53rd Congress session: March 1940, Ramgarh (now Jharkhand), president Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
- **Cripps Mission**, 1942: offered limited self-government to gain Indian cooperation in the war.
- **Champaran Satyagraha** (1917): Gandhi's first Satyagraha and first peasant Satyagraha in India; for indigo farmers of Champaran, Bihar.
- In Champaran, Europeans forced cultivators to grow indigo (a blue dye); they could not grow food and were paid poorly.
- Jallianwala Bagh: British troops fired on a large unarmed crowd in an open space at Amritsar on Baisakhi day; about a thousand (or thousands) killed, many more wounded.
- Swadeshi Movement began in protest against the Partition of Bengal (1905).
- **Women's participation** was a major gain of the Swadeshi Movement; urban middle-class women joined processions.
- After Swadeshi, women took part in every movement: Home Rule, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience; all four drew women out of the home. {192}
- Tilak announced his Home Rule League at the provincial conference at Belgaum, April 1916.
- Civil Disobedience was discontinued after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931).

## Industry, labour and social cases
- First advertisement of Indian cinema: The Times of India (Bombay), 7 July **1896**, "The Marvel of the Century". {22}
- Same day (7 July 1896) the **Lumiere brothers** (France) gave the first cinematography show at Watson's Hotel, Bombay.
- First Indian cotton cloth mill: 1818 at Fort Gloaster (Kolkata); it failed.
- First successful modern cloth mill (first cotton textile mill of Bombay): **1854**, by Parsi cotton merchant Cowaszee Nanabhoy Davar. {51}
- **Baden-Powell** = founder of the Boy Scouts and Civil Guides Movement. {53}
- 1 August 1907: Robert Baden-Powell camped with 20 boys on Brownsea Island, Dorsetshire for a fortnight; start of the movement in the UK.
- 1908: scouting spread worldwide with first instalment of his "Scouting for Boys"; in India scouting began 1909, guiding 1911.
- **Rakhmabai case** (1884-1888) was about age of consent and restitution of conjugal rights; she was a child bride. {54}
- Rakhmabai: one of the first practising women doctors of colonial India; her case led to the Age of Consent Act, 1891.
- Word "**Boycott**" comes from Captain Charles C. Boycott, an Irish land agent; first used as a political weapon in 1880. {63}
- International Labour Organisation (ILO) came into being in 1919.
- **N.M. Joshi** went as labour representative to the first ILO summit at Washington, 1919; B.P. Wadia went as his adviser. {64}
- **Whitley Commission** (Royal Commission on Labour, 1929, chairman J.H. Whitley): to report on existing conditions of labour and make recommendations. {95}
- Whitley Commission covered industrial and plantation labour: health, efficiency, standard of living, and employer-employee relations.
- **Indentured labourers**: taken from India to British colonies for fixed periods; their state was like bonded labour.
- Gandhi helped end indentured labour: raised it in South Africa in 1894; talked with Viceroy Chelmsford; system banned in July 1917. {69}
- Gandhi-Smuts Agreement (1914) ended the 3-pound tax on indentured labourers.
- Gandhi broke the salt law on 6 April 1930; Nehru, Mahadev Desai and others jailed; Congress declared illegal.
- **Bengal Famine: 1943**; about 3.0 million people died. {97}

## Rabindranath Tagore
- Tagore was popularly called **Gurudev**; poet, philosopher, musician, writer, educationist. {39}
- Born 7 May 1861 in a wealthy Brahmin family at Calcutta; 14th child of Debendranath Tagore and Sharada Devi (14 children, 13 survived).
- Tagore died on 7 August **1941** at his ancestral home in Calcutta. {40}
- 1901: started a school at Shantiniketan; it became Visva Bharati University in 1921.
- 1913: Nobel Prize in Literature for "Song Offerings", English translation of his poems "Gitanjali"; first Indian and first Asian Nobel laureate. {254}
- Tagore was awarded knighthood by British King George V.
- **Gandhi** called Tagore "the **Great Sentinel**"; Gandhi felt Tagore gave moral force to the freedom struggle. {41}
- Tagore did not favour the economic side of the Swadeshi Movement. {42}
- His novel "**Ghare Baire**" showed the ill effects of economic Swadeshi, including the bitterness of Muslim traders.
- Tagore sang the glories of ancient India; saw Shivaji and Guru Govind Singh as nation builders; many of his songs refute Maratha heroism.
- Tagore emerged as a great painter at the age of **70**. {89}
- 1930: Tagore was the first Indian artist to exhibit across Europe, Russia and the USA.
## Jawaharlal Nehru
- Publicity Department of United Provinces called Nehru "**high priest of Communism in India**" in pamphlet "Communistic Likes and Dislikes". {58}
- This was Government propaganda to confuse people about Nehru's socialist views; higher authorities advised stopping it.
- **Nehru** became champion of socialism; in 1933 wanted to overthrow British rule, princely states, landlordism and capitalism. {59}
- Nehru's socialism was shaped by Soviet socialism; he visited the Soviet Union in November 1927.
- **Frank Moraes** was Nehru's biographer. {60}
- Nehru was influenced by socialism, British liberalism and Gandhi; **not** by German nationalism. {101}
- Nehru's book "Glimpses of World History", articles and speeches show his socialist ideas.
- Nehru introduced the concept of **Non-Alignment** for world peace and cooperation. {98}
- Nehru's three foreign-policy aims: preserve sovereignty, protect territorial integrity, promote rapid economic development.
- Five founders of NAM: Nehru (India), Gamal Abdel Nasser (Egypt), Josip Broz Tito (Yugoslavia), Sukarno (Indonesia), Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana).
- First Non-Aligned summit: Belgrade (capital of Yugoslavia), 1961.
- Nehru's policy in the Cold War stressed peace, disarmament and racial equality.
- Nehru revered the Upanishads and had great faith in them; he also had a scientific outlook. {236}
- Upanishads = philosophical and scientific analysis of Vedic literature.
- Nehru is called the father of science in modern India; saw science as a tool for socio-economic growth.
- Nehru died on 27 May 1964 in his **4th term** as PM (1947-52, 1952-57, 1957-62, 1962-64). {243}
- Nehru represented **Phulpur** (near Allahabad/Prayagraj), never Rae Bareli.
- Teen Murti Bhavan, Nehru's residence, became a memorial after his death in 1964.

## India after independence
- **Goa** stayed under **Portuguese** rule after 15 August 1947. {99,100}
- 1955: America backed Portugal's claim over Goa.
- **Operation Vijay** (Army, Navy, Air Force) freed Goa on 19 December 1961, after 14 years of patience; ended about 450 years of Portuguese rule.
- First Industrial Policy of independent India: 6 April **1948**. {112}
- Industrial Policy 1948 first framed the mixed economy idea; split industries between public and private sectors and clarified their roles.
- First Communist government in India: **Kerala, 1957** (April); Communist Party of India won by a slim majority. {113,114}
- E.M.S. (Elamkulam Manakkal Sankaran) Namboodiripad: first Chief Minister of Kerala, 1957-59 and again 1967-69.
- Order of events: Air India nationalised as national carrier (1953) → Imperial Bank of India renamed State Bank of India (July 1955) → Kerala communist government (April 1957) → Goa joined India (1961). {120}
- Goa became a Union Territory by the 12th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1962 and a full state in 1987.
- Nehru followed Gandhi's ideals of truth, non-violence, transparency and removal of untouchability, but differed on economic policy.
- Nehru favoured **heavy industrialisation**; Gandhi favoured small and cottage industries. {121}
- **Morarji Desai**: first non-Congress PM, from 24 March 1977, heading the multi-party Janata Party; he represented Surat (Gujarat).
- Post-independence order: Khrushchev and Bulganin visit (after Nehru's USSR visit, 1955) → Dalai Lama fled to India (Tibet revolt, 1959) → Chou En-lai in New Delhi (April 1960, fruitless) → Liberation of Goa (1961). {249}
- **Shimla Agreement, 1972** (June-July): Indira Gandhi and Pakistan President Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto, after the 1971 war and birth of Bangladesh. {250}
- **Operation Vijay** (1999): successful military action against **Pakistan** in the Kargil war. {251}
- **Operation Polo**: military action in **Hyderabad State**, 13-18 September 1948; Nizam overthrown and state joined to India. {263}
- **Privy purse** and special rights of princely states were abolished in **1971** by the 26th Constitutional Amendment Act. {259}
- Privy purse: payment to former ruling families under the 1949 agreement to merge their states with India.
- **Hind Mazdoor Sabha** (Calcutta, December 1948): founders Ashok Mehta, T.S. Ramanujam and G.G. Mehta. {261}
- HMS founding conference included Indian Federation of Labour (1941), Hind Mazdoor Panchayat (1948), Forward Bloc unions and independent unions.
- HMS first office-bearers: President R.S. Ruikar; General Secretary Ashok Mehta; Secretaries G.G. Mehta and V.S. Mathur; Vice Presidents Maniben Kara and T.S. Ramanujam; Treasurer R.A. Khedgikar.

## World history and general facts
- **Queen Elizabeth II** reigned 70 years (1952-2022) and died aged 96 (1926-2022). {18}

- First atom bomb: **Hiroshima**, Japan, 6 August 1945, dropped by America; second on Nagasaki, 9 August 1945. {240}

- **French Revolution** began in **1789**. {241}
- It was a revolt of the Third Estate (peasants, artisans/urban workers, bourgeoisie) against feudalism, birth privileges, corrupt bureaucracy and incapable monarchy; also described as a middle-class revolution against feudalism, class privilege and autocracy.
- **Karl Marx** explained class struggle by **Dialectical Materialism**. {266}
- Marx's materialist view of history: slavery was the base of ancient society, land of feudal society, capital of bourgeois society.
- For Marx, economic factors decide the history of society, and they carry class struggle.
- **Mother Teresa** was an Indian citizen when she died on 5 September 1997. {267}
- Mother Teresa: born 1910 at Uskup, Ottoman Empire (now Skopje, North Macedonia), of Albanian origin.
- At 18 she joined the Institute of the Blessed Virgin Mary (Sisters of Loreto); came to India in 1929; taught at St. Mary's School, Calcutta.
- Mother Teresa founded the "**Missionaries of Charity**". {268}
- **China occupied Tibet in 1959**; Tibet's autonomy ended when the PLA crushed the uprising. {269}
- People's Liberation Army invaded Tibet in October 1950; Seventeen Point Agreement (1951) gave China control, Tibet keeping internal affairs.
- **USSR** broke into 15 independent republics, including Russia, on 26 December **1991**. {270}
- Bangladesh freedom movement began 26 March 1971; Indo-Pak war **December 1971**; East Pakistan became Bangladesh on 16 December 1971. {271}
- After World War II Germany was split into West Germany (US, British, French zones) and Soviet-influenced East Germany.
- **Germany reunited on 3 October 1990**. {272}

- First US President: **George Washington** (1789-1797). {274}
- **Richard Nixon**, 37th US President: the only one to resign, over the Watergate Scandal. {275}
- Bishop **Desmond Tutu**: black bishop of **South Africa**, famed for anti-racism movement in the 1980s. {276}
- Tutu got the Nobel Peace Prize in 1984 and Gandhi Peace Prize in 2005.
- **Tsai-Lun (Cai Lun)** made paper in China in 105 AD; he lived at Leiyang, Hunan province. {277}
- **Plato** was the disciple of Socrates; Aristotle was the disciple of Plato. {278}
- Plato discussed justice and the "Ideal State" in his book "The Republic".
- Italian Renaissance began in the 14th century; start of the modern era and end of the medieval feudal era.
- Main cause of the Italian Renaissance: **freedom to express ideas**. {279}
