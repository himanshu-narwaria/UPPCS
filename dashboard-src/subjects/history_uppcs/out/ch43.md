@ 43 | Regional States: Punjab and Mysore
## Ranjit Singh: rise
- **Ranjit Singh** was born on 13 November 1780 A.D. at Gujranwala to Maha Singh, chief of the **Sukerchakia Misl**. {2,3}
- His father Maha Singh died when he was 12 years old.
- A council of regency ran the administration from 1792 to 1797 A.D.: his mother, mother-in-law and Diwan Lakhpat Rai.
- Ranjit Singh took power directly in 1797.
- Punjab was part of Ahmad Shah Abdali's territory; after his death in 1772 A.D. Sikh misls took most of it, except Multan, Kashmir and some smaller regions.
- Ranjit Singh could consolidate his empire as the Afghans were busy in internal fights.
- In 1798 AD Zaman Shah attacked Punjab; while returning, his cannons fell into the Jhelum river.
- Some sources say Chenab river, but Jhelum is accepted by many historians.
- Ranjit Singh sent the cannons back; pleased, Zaman Shah allowed him to rule Lahore.
- In 1799 A.D. Ranjit Singh took Lahore and made it his **capital**. {4}
- Zaman Shah gave him the title of Raja (for his service and for providing tanks and artillery) and made him Subedar of Lahore.
- He captured Amritsar from the Bhangi Misl in 1802.
- Thus political capital Lahore and religious capital Amritsar were both under Ranjit Singh.
## Ranjit Singh: treaty, conquests, rule
- **Treaty of Amritsar**, 25 April 1809: between Ranjit Singh and the British; signed by Charles Metcalfe on behalf of Governor-General **Lord Minto I**. {6}
- Treaty terms: Ranjit Singh to accept river Sutlej as boundary and British suzerainty over his vassals across the river.
- The English recognised the Maharaja as sole sovereign on his side of the Sutlej.
- The treaty stopped expansion south of the Sutlej; as Cunningham said, he was free to spread his kingdom in the west.
- Ranjit Singh annexed Multan (1818), **Kashmir** (1819; so Srinagar was in his kingdom) and Peshawar/North-West Frontier, Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa (1834). {1}
- Ranjit Singh set up the **Adalat-i-Ala** at Lahore; it heard appeals from district and provincial courts. {5}
- Ranjit Singh set up modern foundries (factories) at Lahore and Amritsar to make cannons.
- Ranjit Singh said: "God intended me to look upon all religions with one eye, that is why he took away the light from the other." {8}
- **Shah Shuja**, grandson (descendant) of Ahmad Shah Abdali, gave the Kohinoor diamond to Ranjit Singh. {7}
- Shah Shuja sat on the throne of Kabul in 1803 AD.
- His brother Shah Mahmud, with Barakzai chief Fateh Khan and Dost Muhammad, dethroned him and took Kashmir and Peshawar.
- Shah Shuja then asked Ranjit Singh for help and presented the Kohinoor.
## Successors of Ranjit Singh
- Ranjit Singh died in 1839; his son **Kharak Singh** (Khadag Singh) succeeded him. {10}
- Kharak Singh was an opium addict.
- Two rival groups caused growing chaos in Punjab during his reign.
- Dogra brothers: Dhyan Singh, Gulab Singh and Suchet Singh.
- Sandhawalia Sardars (aides of Chait Singh): Atar Singh, Lahna Singh and his nephew Ajit Singh.
- **Treaty of Lahore**, March 1846: the British army occupied Lahore and forced the Sikhs to sign.
- Lahore terms: Maharaja ceded all territories beyond the Sutlej forever, and gave up all forts between Sutlej and Beas to the Company. {9}
- Other Lahore terms: promise not to interfere in the administration; Sikhs to pay one and a half crore rupees as war indemnity.
- **Battle of Chillianwala**, 13 January 1849 A.D.: English under Lord Gough vs Sikhs under Sher Singh; ended undecided.
- Lord Dalhousie was Governor-General during Chillianwala.
## Duleep Singh and annexation
- **Maharaja Duleep Singh** was the last ruler of the Sikh Empire; he ruled 1843-1849 AD. {11,12}
- By Lord Dalhousie's proclamation of 29 March 1849 Punjab was merged into the British Empire after the Anglo-Sikh war.
- All territories of Duleep Singh became part of the Indian British Empire.
- Duleep Singh was sent (exiled) to England in 1854 on a pension, for study.
- His pension: not less than 4 lakh and not more than 5 lakh rupees a year (£40,000 to £50,000), not Rs 2,50,000. {14}
- The pension was for him, his relatives and maintenance of the state's employees.
- Duleep Singh accepted Christianity in 1853 A.D., visited Russia, and re-accepted Sikhism in 1886 A.D.
- Duleep Singh died in **Paris** (France) on 22 or 23 October 1893; his funeral (cremation) was in England. {13}
- After annexation in 1849 Dalhousie made a three-member Board to administer Punjab.
- Board of Three: Sir Henry Lawrence (President), John Lawrence and Charles Grenville Mansel; H.M. Elliot and Robert Montgomery were not members. {15}
## Haidar Ali
- In 1742 A.D. the Mysore army under Venkata Raya conquered Dindigul.
- There were eighteen Palayams (Palayakkars) under Dindigul Semai, with Dindigul as capital.
- These Palayams wanted independence and refused to pay taxes to Venkata Raya.
- In 1755 A.D. the Mysore king sent **Haidar Ali** to Dindigul; he controlled it, became de facto ruler and set up a modern armoury (sepoy post) in the fort. {17}
- Later Haidar Ali became ruler of Mysore.
- In 1777 Haidar Ali appointed Purshana Mir Saheb as governor of Dindigul.
## Anglo-Mysore Wars
| War | Years | Ended by | British head |
| First Anglo-Mysore War | 1767-69 A.D. | Treaty of Madras, 4 April 1769 | Harry Verelst, Governor of Bengal |
| Second Anglo-Mysore War | 1780-84 A.D. | Treaty of Mangalore | Warren Hastings, Governor-General |
| Third Anglo-Mysore War | 1790-92 A.D. | Treaty of Srirangapatna, March 1792 | Lord Cornwallis (in command) |
| Fourth Anglo-Mysore War | 1799 A.D. | Tipu killed; Srirangapatna taken | Lord Wellesley, Governor-General |
- **Haidar Ali** won the First Anglo-Mysore War (1767-69). {16}
- Haidar captured Mangalore in 1768 and reached Madras in 1769; the helpless English signed the Treaty of Madras on his terms.
- Harry Verelst's dates are printed as 1734-85.
- Second war: Haidar Ali allied with the Nizam and Marathas against the British.
- Haidar besieged Arcot in 1780; in July 1780 he attacked the Carnatic.
- On 10 September 1780 Colonel William Baillie's British army was defeated by Haidar Ali and Tipu at **Pollilur** (Perambakam). {21}
- The British separated the Nizam and the Marathas from Haidar.
- **Sir Eyre Coote** defeated Haidar Ali at Porto Novo (1 July 1781). {18}
- Eyre Coote also fought Haidar at Pollilur and Sholingur in 1781; at Pollilur (as printed) Haidar was outrightly defeated and his army faced heavy loss.
- Treaty of Srirangapatna (March 1792) was signed between **Tipu Sultan** and Lord Cornwallis for the Company. {22}
- It also included representatives of the Nizam of Hyderabad and the Maratha Empire.
- Tipu had to cede half his kingdom; it was divided among the Marathas, British and Nizam.
- Tipu had to pay Rs 330 lakh as war indemnity; his two sons were kept hostage until 3 crore rupees were paid.
- In the third war Tipu sought help from Kustuntunia and France through ambassadors, in vain.
- Tipu Sultan was defeated and killed fighting in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, **1799**. {23,24}
- On 4 May 1799 the British took Srirangapatna Fort; Tipu died in its defence; this ended Mysore's glorious history.
- All members of Tipu's family were imprisoned in Vellore.
- British commanders of the fourth war: Arthur Wellesley (brother of Governor-General Lord Wellesley), Stuart and General Harris.
- For this victory the Lord Society of Ireland gave Wellesley the title of Marquess.
- After the war the minor Krishnaraja Wodeyar III (Mummadi Krishnaraja Wodeyar) of the Wodeyar dynasty was placed on the Mysore throne.
- The British took Kanara, Coimbatore and Srirangapatna.

## Tipu Sultan
- Tipu Sultan made **Srirangapatna** his capital. {19}
- Tipu set up a Jacobin Club there and became its member; it was founded by French officials as a branch of the Jacobin Club of France.
- Tipu planted the 'tree of liberty' at Srirangapatna as a symbol of Mysore-France friendship.
- **Tipu Sultan** set up embassies abroad on modern lines: Iran, Istanbul, Afghanistan, France. {20}
- He sent ambassadors to keep friendly relations and get help against the British.
- Tipu gave money for making the idol of Goddess Sharda in the Sringeri temple.
## Begum Samru and other facts
- **Begum Samru** (1750-1836 A.D.; some sources give birth year 1753) built a famous church at **Sardhana** near Meerut. {26}
- Her husband was the European Walter Reinhardt Sombre (Walter Joseph Reinhardt), who gave military service to many states.
- Sombre got the Jagir of Sardhana for helping Mughal emperor Shah Alam II defeat Zabita Khan (at the instance of Najaf Khan).
- Begum Samru became ruler of Sardhana after her husband's death.
- Revenue farming in Bengal was introduced by **Murshid Quli Khan**, not Ali Mardan Khan. {27}
- Sawai Jai Singh of Amber had Euclid's 'Elements of Geometry' translated into Sanskrit; observatories at Mathura, Ujjain, Jaipur, Delhi and Varanasi.
- **Mir Jafar** conspired with the English against Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah in the Battle of Plassey (1757 A.D.). {28}
- In the Third Battle of Panipat (1761 A.D.) Ahmad Shah Abdali defeated the Marathas, not Ibrahim Lodi.
