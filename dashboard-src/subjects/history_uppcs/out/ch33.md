@ 33 | Aurangzeb
## War of succession and coronation
- Aurangzeb succeeded Shah Jahan not by the rule of primogeniture but by force of the sword; there were no clear rules of succession. {1}
- The sword was the symbol of power in the Mughal period; succession was settled only by its strength.
- Dara was the eldest and Aurangzeb the third son of Shah Jahan; Aurangzeb ruled India from 1658 to 1707 AD, as the sixth Mughal emperor.
- **Battle of Dharmat** (near Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh), April 15, 1658: Aurangzeb defeated Dara Shikoh's forces led by Maharaja Jaswant Singh of Jodhpur and Qasim Khan. {3,5}
- At Dharmat Dara was supported by Raja Jaswant Singh; Aurangzeb by Prince Murad.
- Battle of Samugarh, May 29, 1658: joint forces of Aurangzeb and Murad defeated Dara Shikoh.
- Order: defeat of Shuja near Banaras (February 1658) → Dharmat (15 April 1658) → Samugarh (29 May 1658) → Deorai (1659). {6}
- Order of battles: Bilgram (1540) → Sarnal (1572) → Dharmat (1658) → Jajau (1707). {4}
- Battle of Sarnal (Gujarat), 1572: Mughal army under Akbar defeated Ibrahim Mirza.
- Battle of Bilgram, 1540: Sher Shah Suri defeated Humayun.
- Battle of Jajau, 1707: Bahadur Shah I vs Muhammad Azam Shah, for succession after Aurangzeb's death.
- **Aurangzeb was crowned twice**: first on July 21, 1658 in Delhi; second on June 5, 1659 in Delhi after success at Khajwa and Deorai. {2}
- His title: 'Abul Muzaffar Muhiuddin Muhammad Aurangzeb Bahadur Alamgir Padshah Ghazi'.
- Shahzada **Sulaiman Shikoh**, son of Dara, defeated in the war of succession, took refuge with Prithvi Shah, ruler of Srinagar Garhwal. {7}
- Prithvi Shah's successor Medini Shah handed him to Aurangzeb; he was jailed in Gwalior Fort and died there, killed by an excessive dose of opium.
## Shivaji, Rajputs and Prince Akbar
- At the start of 1665 AD Aurangzeb sent a large army under Raja Jai Singh of Amber to suppress Shivaji.
- Jai Singh = Kachhwaha ruler, expert in arts of peace and war; clever diplomat who saw that friendship with Shivaji was needed to win Bijapur.
- Jai Singh besieged Purandar Fort, where Shivaji had lodged his family and treasure; Shivaji opened talks.
- Despite Mughal victory at Purandar fort and the siege of Raigarh, **Jai Singh** made the **Treaty of Purandar** with Shivaji in June 1665 AD. {9}
- Aurangzeb's rebellious son **Muhammad Akbar** revolted in 1681 AD, weakening Aurangzeb's position against the Rajputs. {8}
- Akbar was disappointed with the Rajput war; liberal in ideas, he did not believe in his father's fanatic policy.
- Maharana Raj Singh of Mewar and Durgadas, Rathore leader of Marwar, offered him the forces of Mewar and Marwar if he declared himself king of India.
## Deccan conquests and the British
- As Subedar of the Deccan for the second time, Aurangzeb took an aggressive policy against Golconda and Bijapur.
- Shah Jahan's peremptory orders stopped the operations; treaties were made with Golconda (1656 AD) and Bijapur (1657 AD).
- As emperor, Aurangzeb conquered **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)**. {14,15}
- **Abul Hasan Qutb Shah** was king of Golconda when Aurangzeb seized it in 1687. {16}
- Aurangzeb is said to have opened Golconda fort with 'keys of gold', as Akbar opened Asirgarh fort.
- Map of Aurangzeb's kingdom at his death in 1707 includes Bijapur and Golconda. {17}
- First Mughal–British conflict was in 1686 AD, in **Aurangzeb's** reign; Mughal governor Shaista Khan drove the British, including Job Charnock, out of Hooghly. {13}
- None of these came in Aurangzeb's reign: Peter Mundy (Shah Jahan's reign), William Hawkins and Thomas Roe (Jahangir's), Antonio Monserrate (Akbar's).
## Personality, nobility and religious policy
- Aurangzeb had extraordinary personal qualities among the Mughals; high moral standards; far from vices, pleasures and extravagance.
- Some contemporaries called him 'Shahi Darvesh'; Muslims considered him **'Zinda Peer'**. {10,11}
- The Mughal army had the largest number of **Hindu generals** under Aurangzeb: 31.6% (Athar Ali's nobility data), over half of them Marathas. {18,19}
- The share was 22.5% in Akbar's reign and 22.4% in Shah Jahan's.
- Akbar had abolished Jizya; **Aurangzeb re-imposed Jizya** on non-Muslims in 1679 AD. {20}
- Jizya classes: income below 200 Dirham a year paid 12 Dirham; 200 to 10,000 Dirham paid 24; above 10,000 Dirham paid 48.
- Exempt from Jizya: women, slaves, children under 14, beggars and unemployed persons; subordinate Hindu kings and Brahmins had to pay.
- A European traveller wrote his motive in reviving Jizya was to increase his treasure; this seems only partly true.
- Aurangzeb was a Sunni Muslim; his religious policy was not moved by any idea of worldly profit.
- He tried to enforce Quranic law: Jihad (holy wars) against non-Muslim lands (Dar-ul-Harb) till they become **Dar-ul-Islam**. {21}
- He stopped putting tilak by hand on the forehead of Hindu kings, a practice started by Akbar.
- Prostitutes were to marry or leave the country; Basant, Holi, Diwali were banned in the court.
## Family and buildings
- **Bibi Ka Maqbara** (Aurangabad, now Chhatrapati Sambhaji Nagar, Maharashtra) was built in Aurangzeb's reign by his son Azam Shah for his mother Rabia-ud-Daurani. {22}
- Tomb of **Rabia-ud-Daurani**, modelled on the Taj Mahal, is called the 'Second Taj Mahal', 'Dakkhani Taj' (Taj of the Deccan) or 'Mini Taj'. {23}
- It has intricate designs, carved motifs, imposing structures and a landscaped Mughal-style garden.
- **Moti Masjid** inside the Red Fort, Delhi, was built by Aurangzeb. {27}
- **Mehrunnisa** was a daughter of Aurangzeb; Jahan Ara, Roshan Ara and Gauhar Ara were daughters of Shah Jahan. {24}
- Aurangzeb's other daughters: Jebunissa, Zinat-un-Nissa, Badrunnisa and Jubadtunnisa.
- Aurangzeb gave the title **'Sahibat-uz-Zamani'** to Jahan Ara. {25}
- Jahan Ara = eldest daughter of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal; elder sister of Aurangzeb.
- **Samarth Ramdas**, great saint of Maharashtra (born 1608, died 1681 AD), was a contemporary of Aurangzeb. {26}
- Guru Ramdas, the fourth Sikh guru, was a contemporary of Akbar.
