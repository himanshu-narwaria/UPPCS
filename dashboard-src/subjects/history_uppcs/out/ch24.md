@ 24 | Delhi Sultanate: Literature
## Al-Biruni
- **Kitab-ul-Hind** (also Tahqiq-i-Hind, Tarikh-i-Hind) was written by **Al-Biruni**, a 10th–11th century AD Persian historian and scholar. {1,2}
- Al-Biruni's real name was Abu Rehan.
- Al-Biruni studied India as a scientist; described its religious, literary and socio-political condition.
- Tahqiq-i-Hind is one of his notable works; gives a detailed account of the history, geography and culture of India.
## Amir Khusrau: life and courts
- Full (real) name: Abul Hassan Yaminuddin Khusrau, often called **Amir Khusrau**.
- Amir Khusrau was born in 1253 AD at **Patiyali** in district Etah (now Kasganj district), Uttar Pradesh. {3,4}
- Khusrau was called, and called himself, **Tuti-i-Hind** (Parrot of India). {5}
- He started composing poems at the early age of nine.
- He referred to himself as a 'Turkish-Indian'.
- Amir Khusrau was a poet, historian and musician; a great scholar and poet of his time. {12}
- Courts he was associated with: Nasiruddin Mahmud, Balban, Kaiqubad, Samsuddin Kayumars, Jalaluddin Khalji, Alauddin Khalji, Shihabuddin Umar, Mubarakshah, Ghiyasuddin Tughluq and Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq. {8}
- Amir Khusrau was the **court poet of Alauddin Khalji**. {9,10,11}
- He was the disciple of the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Auliya.
- Both Amir Khusrau and Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya witnessed the reign of more than seven Sultans of Delhi.
- Khusrau was a prolific classical poet linked with the royal courts of more than seven rulers of the Delhi Sultanate.
- He was the **first Persian poet to depict the Indian environment** in his poetry. {16}
## Amir Khusrau: language and works
- Khusrau learned various Indian dialects, especially Hindavi, which he loved most.
- Khusrau himself said he knew Sanskrit and other Indian languages, which are a part of Hindavi.
- He played a pioneering role in developing **Khari Boli**; known as the father (and promoter) of Hindi Khari Boli. {6,14}
- Khusrau was a scholar of both Hindi (Hindavi) and Persian. {15}
- He was the first author to use Hindi (Hindavi) phrases and words.
- He started the new Persian poetry style **Sabk-i-Hindi** (Hindustani style); considered its father. {13}
- His saying: "Na Lafze Hindiwast aj Farsi Kam" = the words of Hindi are not less than those of Persian.
- Works of Amir Khusrau: Qiran-us-Sadain, Miftah-ul-Futuh, Khazain-ul-Futuh (Tarikh-i-Alai), Ashika, Nuh Sipihr, Tughluq Namah. {17,30}
- **Qiran-us-Sadain** = his first Masnavi on a historical subject.
- **Tughluq Namah** = the last historical Masnavi of Amir Khusrau.
## Amir Khusrau: music
- Amir Khusrau was the originator in India of the early form of the musical style **Qawwali**. {18}
- Kitab-i-Nauras, a collection of songs praising Hindu deities and Muslim saints, was written by Ibrahim Adil Shah II, Sultan of Bijapur.
- Ibrahim Adil Shah II founded the city of Nauraspur and made it his capital.
- Indian music made Muslim society passionate about music; so Amir Khusrau emerged as a great musician of the Sultanate period.
- Khusrau invented the **Sitar** by combining the Irani Tamboora and the Indian Veena; Sitar = finest example of Hindu-Muslim harmony in instruments. {35}
- He combined Indian and Persian melodies and introduced new melody styles like Iman, Zilf, Sajgari.
- **Tabla** and Sitar were invented (made prevalent) by Amir Khusrau in the 13th/14th century. {36}
## Persian and Apabhramsa
- Persian literature in India began to develop with the advent of Muslims; here it took a new, innovative form.
- After the Delhi Sultanate was set up, **Persian** was given the status of official language; Sultans of Delhi patronised Persian. {23}
- Forms of Sanskrit spoken by the people were termed **Apabhramsa**.
- In medieval Sanskrit texts 'Apabhramsa' denoted early forms of some of the modern Indian languages. {24}
- Scholars hold that the Hindi dialects of northern India and Kashmiri, Punjabi, Sindhi, Nepali, Shauryaseni, Marathi developed from Apabhramsa.
## Historical works of the Sultanate period
- **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** was written by Minhaj-us-Siraj (Minhaj-i-Siraj) and dedicated to Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud. {19}
- Tabaqat-i-Nasiri: divided into 23 volumes; translated into English by H.G. Raverty; describes Muhammad Ghori's victory over India.
- Tabaqat-i-Nasiri has the earliest description of Bakhtiyar Khalji's invasion of Bihar. {22}
- Tabaqat-i-Nasiri is an important source for the history of the foundation of Muslim rule in Bengal.
- Literary evidence suggests Nalanda University was sacked in 1193 by Bakhtiyar Khalji, a Turk.
- Minhaj-i-Siraj, a Persian historian, reported thousands of monks burned alive and thousands beheaded as Khalji tried to uproot Buddhism.
- Ziauddin Barani = Muslim political philosopher of the Delhi Sultanate; wrote two famous works, Tarikh-i-Firuzshahi and **Fatwa-i-Jahandari**. {21}
- Barani's Tarikh-i-Firuzshahi = a historical account of the Delhi Sultanate (history of the Tughlaq dynasty).
- Fatwa-i-Jahandari guides Muslim rulers to follow political ideals based on Islamic principles.
- **Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi** has two authors: Ziauddin Barani and Shams-i-Siraj Afif; Afif's is an account of the reign of Sultan Firuz Shah Tughlaq. {28,31}
- Afif's book has the same title as Barani's but is very different in material and approach.
- Order of texts: Kitab-ul-Hind (Al-Biruni) → Prithviraja-Raso (Chandarbardai) → Tabaqat-i-Nasiri (Minhaj-i-Siraj) → Fatawa-i-Jahandari (Ziauddin Barani). {20}
- Taj-ul-Masir was written by Sadruddin Muhammad Hassan Nizami.
- Yahiya Bin Ahmad Sarhindi wrote Tarikh-i-Mubarakshahi under the patronage of Sayyid Mubarakshah; it is the principal source for the history of Sayyid rulers.
- Firuz Shah Tughluq wrote his memoirs, **Futuhat-i-Firuz Shahi**; through it he wanted to prove himself an ideal Muslim ruler. {42}

## Music texts and other books
- **Rana Kumbha** was a patron of music, literature and art; wrote the music text Sangeet Raja (also called Sangita Mimansa). {38}
- Rana Kumbha built the victory tower Kirti Stambha to mark his victory over Mahmud Khalji, in 1440–1448 AD.
