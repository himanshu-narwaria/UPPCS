@ 90 | Partition of India and Independence
## Attlee's declaration
- **Clement Richard Attlee** was Prime Minister of Britain when India became independent; he was PM from 1945 to 1951. {2}
- Attlee belonged to the **Labour Party**, which was in power in the U.K. at India's independence. {1}
- **George VI** was the British monarch at Indian independence; king of the United Kingdom from 1936 until his death in 1952. {50}
- On **20 February 1947** Attlee declared in the House of Commons that the British would leave India by a date before (30) **June 1948**, after transferring power. {3,5}
- Attlee also said that if a constitutional decision was not reached in time, the British could transfer the government to any of them.
- Britain freed India in 1947 because Britain had become weak during the Second World War. {13}
- People of various castes, religions, organisations and parties contributed to independence.
## Mountbatten as Viceroy
- Attlee made **Lord Mountbatten** Viceroy in place of Wavell; he took charge on **24 March 1947** with vast powers and a mandate to oversee the British withdrawal, and began the transfer of power.
- Mountbatten was the **last British Viceroy** and Governor-General of India (1947-48). {37}
- His specific instruction was to **keep India united if possible**. {4}
- He was to transfer power in the way best for India's future happiness and prosperity, decide by Indian conditions, and let Britain leave with honour and least damage.
- From 133 recorded talks with Indian politicians between 24 March and 6 May 1947 he concluded that the Cabinet Mission Plan was impractical.
- He then prepared a substitute plan called **"Plan Balkan"**; the Balkan Plan was Mountbatten's brainchild. {16}
- Plan Balkan: separate dominions for Pakistan, the Princely States and the rest of British India, with certain key areas run by a Central Government.
- He saw that power could not be passed to one central assembly, as Congress and Muslim League could not agree on terms.
- Mountbatten concluded a united India was unachievable and that partition and the formation of Pakistan were a must.
## Gandhi and partition
- Gandhi first met Mountbatten on **31 March 1947**.
- As an alternative to partition, Gandhi suggested inviting **Jinnah** to form the government (hand over the Interim Government to him as Prime Minister), so that communal riots would stop. {19}
- Congress leaders and the Congress Working Committee did not accept this suggestion.
- Gandhi said: "If Congress wishes to accept partition it will be over my dead body"; he would never support partition till his last breath.
- Gandhi told his attendants: "Today I find myself all alone"; even Sardar and Jawaharlal thought he was wrong and that peace would return if partition was agreed.
- Gandhi said the future of independence gained at this price was going to be dark.
- Gandhi said: "Let it not be said that Gandhi was party to Indian vivisection."
- Though opposed in principle, Gandhi accepted the partition resolution at the AICC meeting of 14-15 June 1947 and urged Congress to accept it to avoid more communal bloodshed.
## Mountbatten Plan, 3 June 1947
- The **Mountbatten Plan** for partition was officially declared on **3 June 1947**. {12}
- It was the formula/basis for the transfer of power (sovereignty) and partition of the country; the platform of Indian independence. {6,7,11}
- Mountbatten prepared it keeping Attlee's instructions in mind; the prevailing communal violence led him to announce it.
- He fixed 15 August 1947 as the date for transfer of power.
- The plan provided for immediate transfer of power on the basis of Dominion Status.
- Punjab and Bengal Legislative Assemblies would meet in two groups, Hindus and Muslims, to vote on partition; a simple majority of either group meant partition.
- Sindh would take its own decision.
- **Referendum** in NWFP, Sindh, Baluchistan and Sylhet district of Assam would decide the fate of these areas. {41}
- Since Congress had conceded a unified India, its other points were met: no independence for princely states (they must join India or Pakistan).
- Also ruled out: independence for Bengal, and accession of Hyderabad to Pakistan (Mountbatten backed Congress on this).
- **Jawaharlal Nehru and Sardar Patel** represented Congress in the Partition Council headed by Lord Mountbatten. {40}
- The last chance to avoid partition was lost with the rejection of the **Cabinet Mission** plan. {23}
- The Cabinet Mission plan of 16 May 1946 meant a united India with autonomous Muslim and Hindu majority provinces, rejecting Pakistan.
- Muslim League approved that plan on 6 June 1946, rejected it on 29 July, and called Muslims to "Direct Action" for Pakistan; 16 August 1946 was fixed as Direct Action Day.
## Congress accepts partition
- Congress agreed to partition in 1947 mainly to **avoid large-scale communal riots**; it accepted the Mountbatten Plan but never accepted the Two-Nation Theory. {17,18}
- The AICC at Delhi accepted the partition proposal on 14-15 June 1947 (both dates are found).
- **J.B. Kripalani** was Congress President then, at partition and at the transfer of power on 15 August 1947. {24,25,42,43,45}
- Kripalani became Congress President at the **Meerut Session, 1946**; he held the post from 23 November 1946 to November 1947. {44}
- The partition resolution was moved by **Govind Ballabh Pant** and seconded by **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**; also supported by Sardar Patel and Jawaharlal Nehru. {26}
- **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** (Frontier Gandhi) voted against partition at this meeting. {28}
- **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** called the resolution a "surrender of nationalism in favour of communalism". {27}
- Kitchlew was President of the Punjab Provincial Congress Committee and strongly opposed partition; after independence he left Congress and joined the Communist Party of India.

## Indian Independence Act 1947
- The **Indian Independence Act, 1947** provided for partition into two new dominions, India and Pakistan, from 15 August 1947. {8}
- It implemented the Mountbatten Plan; the British Government gave the plan formal approval through this Act.
- The British Parliament passed the Act in **July 1947**. {9}
- Attlee presented the Bill in the House of Commons on 4 July 1947; Commons passed it on 15 July 1947 and the Lords the next day.
- The Bill received Royal Assent on **18 July 1947**, the date the Act came into force. {10,15}
- Under the Act the British Government kept no control over either dominion from 15 August 1947.
- British sovereignty did not continue in free India; but the British sovereign did appoint the last Governor-General of free India. {14}
## Boundary Commissions
- Mountbatten formed the **Punjab Boundary Commission and Bengal Boundary Commission** on 30 June 1947.
- British lawyer **Cyril Radcliffe** was chairman of both; their task was to demarcate the boundaries between India and Pakistan. {20,21,22}
- They were to mark boundaries in Punjab and Bengal on the basis of contiguous Muslim and non-Muslim majority areas, also weighing other factors.
- Each commission had four members (judges): two from Congress and two from the Muslim League.
- The Radcliffe Line became the India-Pakistan border when the Award was published on **17 August 1947**, two days after partition.
## Midnight of 14-15 August 1947
- At midnight of 14/15 August 1947 the **Constituent Assembly**, as Provisional Parliament, assumed sovereign power to govern the country. {29}
- Under the Act the existing Central Legislature ceased; the Constituent Assembly, mainly a constitution-making body, also became the Dominion Legislature.
- To avoid confusion the two functions were separated; the Constituent Assembly (Legislative) began work from 17 November 1947.
- A special session of the Constituent Assembly was held in New Delhi at midnight; Nehru, as first Prime Minister, gave a historic, inspiring speech.
- **Sucheta Kripalani** sang "Vande Mataram", "Jan-Gan-Man" and Iqbal's "Sare Jahan Se Achha Hindustan Hamara" there. {30}
- **Mahatma Gandhi** took no part in Independence Day celebrations on 15 August 1947; saddened by partition, he spent the day fasting and in prayer. {46}
## First office-holders
- The **Governor-General**, Lord Mountbatten, appointed Jawaharlal Nehru as the first Prime Minister of India. {31}
- **Lord Mountbatten** (1947-48) was the first Governor-General of independent India. {32,33}
- **C. Rajagopalachari** (1948-50) was the first and last Indian Governor-General, and the last Governor-General of independent India. {34,35,36}
- Rajagopalachari held the post until 26 January 1950; he was Chief Minister of Madras 1952-54.
- In 1959 he left Congress over differences and formed the **Swatantra Party**.
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** was the first Law Minister of free India, on Mahatma Gandhi's recommendation; he chaired the Drafting Committee. {38}

## Constitution and Republic Day
- The Constitution was ready on 26 November 1949; provisions like Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60 and 324 came into force from that date.
- It was enforced on **26 January 1950** because Congress had celebrated 26 January 1930 as the first Independence Day. {47}
- 26 January was declared **Republic Day**.
- At the 1929 Lahore session Congress adopted **Purna Swaraj** (Complete Independence) as its goal.
- At midnight of 31 December 1929 the tricolour was unfurled on the banks of the river Ravi.
- 26 January 1930 was marked as the first Independence Day, to be celebrated every year.
## Sayings on British rule and Saurashtra Sangh
- **R. Coupland** said: "Indian nationalism was the child of the British Raj, and the British authorities blessed its cradle." {48}
- But Coupland forgot that Indian nationalism was an unwanted (involuntary) child, denied milk in childhood and then choked (throttled).
- **K.M. Panikkar** said the greatest achievement of British rule was the unity (unification) of India. {49}
- **Saurashtra Sangh** was formed on 15 February 1948 by merging **222** principalities: 217 of Kathiawar Agency and 5 others. {51}
