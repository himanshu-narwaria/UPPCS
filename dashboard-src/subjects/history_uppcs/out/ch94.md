## Cultural institutions
- Pandit Vishnu Digambar Paluskar founded the first **Gandharva Mahavidyalaya** in **Lahore** in 1901 (then in undivided India). {1}
- Gandharva Mahavidyalaya: one of the earliest institutions for organised training in classical music by a modern method.
- Paluskar wanted to take music education out of the hereditary domain of professional musicians and open it to the wider public.
- **Centre for Cultural Resources and Training (CCRT)**: set up in 1979 by Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay and Dr. Kapila Vatsyayan; works to link education with culture. {6}
- CCRT is an autonomous organisation under the Ministry of Culture; committed to holistic education: cognitive, emotional and spiritual development of children.
- **Sangeet Natak Akademi**: India's National Academy of music, dance and drama; first national academy of the arts set up by the Republic of India. {108}
- Sangeet Natak Akademi was set up in 1952 and inaugurated on 28 January 1953.
! Sangeet Natak Akademi's own website gives its founding year as 1953; other sources give 1952 as founding and 1953 as inauguration year.
- **Lalit Kala Akademi**, New Delhi: set up by the Government of India as an autonomous body on 5 August 1954; given statutory authority in 1957. {160}
- **National School of Drama**: theatre training institution, set up in 1959 by Sangeet Natak Akademi as a constituent unit; independent entity and registered autonomous organisation in 1975.
- First Sanskrit chair was established in 1814 at College de France, of the famous Paris University (France). {30}
- **Kala Bhavan**: famous centre of fine arts at Visva-Bharati University, Shantiniketan, West Bengal; founded in 1919 by Rabindranath Tagore.
- Painter **Nandalal Bose** became Principal of Kala Bhavan in 1922. {143}
- **Shantiniketan**: small town near Bolpur in Birbhum district, West Bengal. {159}
- Shantiniketan was renamed Visva Bharati, which Tagore defined as 'Where the world makes a home in a nest'.

## Folk culture and ideas
- **Folk culture** = cultural practices of common people: expressive parts of everyday life of localised, common, tradition-bound groups. {94}
- Folk songs matter most for preserving traditions, passed from generation to generation. {97}
- Preserving intangible cultural heritage across generations keeps the memory of one's origin and identity.
- **Cultural lag**: culture takes time to catch up with technological innovations; this lag causes social problems and conflicts.
- Religion, law and tradition are factors of cultural lag; politics is not. {122}
- Ogburn: cultural lag is common because material culture evolves and changes rapidly.
- Cultural lag is a critical ethical issue: no broad consensus on use of modern technology can break social solidarity and raise social conflict.
- **Tribhanga** (tri-bent pose): standing pose in Indian sculpture, art and classical dance; one leg bent, body curved oppositely at waist and neck. {5}
- Tribhanga literally = three parts break: three bends at neck, waist and knee, giving a gentle 'S' shape.
- **Mimamsa** philosophy stresses the doctrine of Karma; liberation is possible by performing Karma. {37}
- **Vidushaka** in Sanskrit drama is the dear one of the king and is a Brahmana; used by dramatists like Kalidasa and Bhasa. {136}
- Earliest reference to the magnetic compass is found in **Jawamiul Hikayat**. {32}
- **Surkhi** in Indian architecture was introduced by the Kushans; its use is found in Kushana-period buildings. {121}
- At Mathura, Surkhi was found in the third stage (200 BC to end of 1st century) and fifth stage (4th to 6th centuries) of excavation.
- **Vichitravirya** was the grandfather of Arjun; his two sons were Pandu and Dhritarashtra; Arjun was Pandu's son. {27}
- Pampa's **Vikramarjuna Vijaya** is the Kannada version of the Mahabharata. {45}
- **Amir Khusrau** was the first writer to use Urdu for poetic expression; wrote several verses in Urdu; called originator of Qawwali. {46}
- Sardar **Puran Singh** is known as the 'Tagore' of the Punjabi language. {151}

- **Kaaba**: cuboid building in Mecca, Saudi Arabia; holiest place of Muslims. {28}

- Odissi, Mohiniattam and Sattriya are each among the eight classical dance forms of India (recognised by Sangeet Natak Akademi).
- **Mohiniattam**: graceful dance of Kerala performed as solo recitals by women. {62}
- Mohiniattam is called a semi-classical dance form of Kerala in one place, and one of the eight classical dance forms elsewhere.
- 'Mohini' = a maiden who steals the onlooker's heart; Vaishnava devotees are thought to have named the dance.
- Mohiniattam shows feminine moods; usual theme is sringara (love), with suggestive abhinaya, subtle gestures, rhythmic footwork and lilting music.
- The legend of Vishnu as Mohini (the enchantress) is the core of Mohiniattam.
- **Sattriya**: combination of music, dance and drama; centuries-old living tradition of Vaishnavites of Assam. {61}
- Sattriya was recognised as classical by Sangeet Natak Akademi in 2000; most recent entrant among the eight.
- Sattriya was introduced by Vaishnava saint and reformer Mahapurusha **Sankaradeva** to spread the Vaishnava faith.
- Sattriya is accompanied by **Borgeet**, composed by Sankardeva and Madhavdev among others (not Tulsidas, Kabir, Mirabai), based on classical ragas.
- **Kuchipudi** began at Kuchelapuram (Kuchipudi), Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh; popular all over South India. {116,117}
- Kuchipudi has more realistic acting; dancers at times speak dialogues, unlike Bharatanatyam. {65}
- Kuchipudi is called a famous folk dance of Andhra Pradesh in one place, and an Indian classical dance elsewhere.
- **Tarangam** of Kuchipudi (not Bharatanatyam): dancing on the edges of a brass plate, sometimes with a pot of water on the head.
- **Kathak** in its present form has the influence of Mughal tradition. {66}
- **Manipuri** is the dance of Manipur, an eastern (North Eastern) State; Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Bharatanatyam are of South India. {68}
- **Kathakali** (Kerala) is traditionally performed only by males, though it has slowly opened to females; a dance-drama. {123}
- Kathakali is performed at religious festivals by actors with elaborate masks, colourful costumes and painted faces. {124}
- Vallathol Narayana Menon, founder of Kerala Kalamandalam, re-established Kathakali.
- Famous Kathakali dancers: Krishnan Kutty, Madhavan Nair, Anand Sivaraman, Uday Shankar, Ram Gopal, Shantaram.
- **Chakiarkoothu**: stories of Hindu mythology and epics are orally rendered; performed only by the Chakiar community, in the temple theatre Koothambalam, with percussion instrument mizhavu. {52}
- **Manipuri Sankirtana**: singing, drumming and dancing in temples and homes of Manipur; narrates the life and deeds of Lord Krishna. {53}
- Manipuri Sankirtana is on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage; it uses Kartal and Pung, not cymbals alone.
- **Garba**, traditional dance of Gujarat, was added to UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in December 2023 (the latest). {64}
- Garba: ritual, devotional dance on Navaratri, for worship of feminine energy (Shakti).
- UNESCO list: Chhau dance 2010, Kumbh Mela 2017, Durga Puja in Kolkata 2021.

## Music: forms, ragas and texts
- **Dhrupad** traces its origin to the chanting of Vedic hymns and mantras; primarily a form of worship (devotional, spiritual). {48}
- Dhrupad was originated by Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior (1486-1516), well before the Mughal period.
- Dhrupad has two parts, Alap and Bandish; the Alap uses syllables from Sanskrit mantras.
- Umakant and Ramakant Gundecha (Gundecha Brothers): leading Dagarvani dhrupad singers, born in Ujjain in a Jain family. {49}
- Raga **Todi** is sung in the morning; Darbari at night; Bhopali in the evening; Bhimpalasi in the afternoon. {51}
- **Sufi Kalam**, a devotional music, is characteristic of Jammu and Kashmir. {36}
- **Tyagaraja**: renowned Carnatic composer; thousands of devotional compositions, mostly in Telugu, in praise of Lord Rama; created several new ragas. {98}
- Tyagaraja, Shyama Shastri and Muthuswami Dikshitar, contemporaries, are the trinity of Carnatic music.
- **Annamacharya**: 15th-century saint; earliest known Indian musician to compose sankirtanas, in praise of Venkateshwara, a form of Vishnu.
- Pandit Vishnu Digambar Paluskar arranged the music for the song 'Vande Mataram'. {125}
- Venkatamakhi introduced the scheme of Raga classification of Indian music.
- Shyama Shastri: proponent of Carnatic music. Amir Khusrau: proponent of the Khayal form of Hindustani music.

## Musical instruments
- **Veena**: India's national instrument and the most ancient; origin in the Vedic period; goddess Saraswati plays it. {113}
- **Sitar** was invented by Amir Khusrau; it combines the old Indian Veena and the Iranian Tambura. {114}
- Amir Khusrau is also credited with inventing the Tabla.
- Turkic people brought Rabab and Sarangi and a new method of music and instruments. {71}

- **Akum** and **Todi** are wind instruments; others: Bansuri, Mohuri (Madhukari), Shankh, Ate (Jeeka). {82}
