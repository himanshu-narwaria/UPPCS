@ 49 | Development of Press in Modern India
## Beginning of press
- **Portuguese** set up the first printing press in Asia at Goa in 1556. {1}
- **Bengal Gazette** = first newspaper in India; English paper started at Calcutta in 1780 by **James Augustus Hicky**. {2,3}
- Many newspapers were published between 1766 and 1789.
- **Udant Martand** = first Hindi newspaper; published from Calcutta (Kolkata) on 30 May 1826. {30}
- Udant Martand came out every Tuesday; published by Pt. Jugal Kishore Shukla.
## Press laws
- **Lord Wellesley** first imposed press censorship: Censorship of Press Regulations (Act), 1799. {4}
- The 1799 regulations imposed almost wartime press restrictions including pre-censorship.
- In 1807 the 1799 Act was extended to pamphlets, books and magazines.
- Lord Hastings abolished strict pre-censorship in 1818; papers no longer had to be submitted to a government officer before publication.
- John Adams, acting Governor-General, banned the Indian press in 1823: Rs. 400 fine and imprisonment for publishing without licence.
- Under the 1823 rule the District Magistrate could seize the publication house; so Raja Ram Mohan Roy had to stop Mirat-ul-Akhbar.
- Lord William Bentinck was positive towards the press; a bill for press freedom was introduced in his tenure.
! No year option was accepted for Bentinck giving press freedom: the bill came in his tenure, but the Act was passed under Metcalfe.
- The Press Act was enacted in August 1835 under acting Governor-General **Charles Metcalfe**, known as the Liberator of the Indian Press.
- **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** was passed by **Lord Lytton** (1876–1880); it gagged the native-language press and was called the 'Gagging Act'. {7,9}
- The Act let the District Magistrate, without prior Government permission, make the printer and publisher of a vernacular paper sign a bond.
- Bond = not to cause disaffection against the government or antipathy between persons of different religion, caste and race.
- Security could be demanded and forfeited on violation; on repeat offence press equipment could be seized. {6}
- Under the Act, proceedings were started against Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash and Samachar.
- There was popular protest against the Act; **Lord Ripon** repealed it in 1882. {5,8}
## Journalists punished
- **Surendra Nath Banerjee** = first Indian jailed for doing his duty as a journalist; jailed on 2 April 1883 for his article in The Bengalee. {11}
- Also printed: B.G. Tilak was imprisoned for anti-British journalism in 1882; first Indian punished for doing his duty in this field.
- Tilak was arrested after the murder of W.C. Rand and officer Charles Ayerst.
- Grounds: the poem "Shivaji's utterances" in Kesari and his Shivaji festival speech justifying Shivaji's killing of Afzal Khan.
- The prosecution portrayed Tilak's defence of Afzal Khan's killing as incitement to kill British officials.
- In 1897 Tilak got 18 months' imprisonment for sedition: charged with inciting the Chapekar brothers through Kesari articles.
- Abul Kalam Azad's Al-Hilal was banned in 1914 by the Press Act.
## Raja Ram Mohan Roy and early Bengal papers
- Raja Ram Mohan Roy used the press to spread his views; he knew English, Bengali, Persian, Arabic, Greek and Latin.
- He published the Bengali weekly **Sambad Kaumudi** in 1821.
- He published the Persian weekly **Mirat-ul-Akhbar** in 1822. {13}
- **Samachar Darpan** is linked with J.C. Marshman. {61}
- **Som Prakash**, Bengali weekly, was started in 1858 by **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**. {23}
- Vidyasagar mooted the idea of Som Prakash; Dwarkanath Vidyabhushan was its first editor.
- Som Prakash supported the farmers during the Indigo movement.
- **Indian Mirror** was published from Calcutta; founded 1861 by Debendranath Tagore and Manmohan Ghosh; Gandhiji was not associated with it. {14,41}
- Indian Mirror was a daily; Keshav Chandra Sen and Narendra Nath Sen were in its editorial department.
- The Indian Mirror (January 1886) called the Indian National Congress "the nucleus of a future Parliament of our country". {73}
- **Amrita Bazar Patrika** was founded by **Shishir Kumar Ghosh** and Motilal Ghosh. {17}
- It was launched on 20 February 1868 as a Bengali weekly at village Palua Magura, Jessore district (present Bangladesh); later published from Kolkata.
- Amrita Bazar Patrika turned into an English paper overnight in 1878 to escape the Vernacular Press Act. {18}
- Girish Chandra Ghosh started **The Bengalee** in 1862; it passed to S.N. Banerjee in 1879.
## Hindoo Patriot and Indigo Revolt
- **Hindoo Patriot** (1853) = first newspaper published by Indians in English; started by Girish Chandra Ghosh. {31}
- Hindoo Patriot editors: Girish Chandra Ghosh (1853–55), Harish Chandra Mukherjee (1855–61), Kristo Das Pal (1861–1884).
- Later editors were helped by Madan Mohan Ghosh and Dwarka Nath Tagore.
- Surendra Nath Banerjee was editor of Hindoo Patriot during 1874–75 in London and 1878 in Delhi.
- Indigo Revolt = peasant uprising of indigo farmers against indigo planters in Bengal; a direct fight against exploitation of farmers.
- Special feature of the Indigo Movement: intellectuals actively campaigned in support of the struggling farmers.
- **Harish Chandra Mukherjee**, editor of Hindoo Patriot, boldly exposed exploitation of indigo farmers and helped them in every way. {32}
- Girish Chandra Ghosh also worked for the Indigo Movement.
- Kristo Das Pal admired British governance and British liberalism; under him Hindoo Patriot represented high-class Bengali landowners.
## Tilak and revolutionary papers
- **Tilak** started Kesari and Mahratta in 1881 at Poona, now Pune (also printed: Mumbai); **Kesari** served the freedom struggle. {19,65}
- Kesari was in Marathi and was Tilak's main paper of the Home Rule Movement; Mahratta was in English.
- Gopal Ganesh Agarkar was the first editor of Kesari; Tilak was the first editor of Mahratta; later Tilak published both.
- **Bangawasi, Kaal and Kesari** supported revolutionary movements and criticised Congress for its moderate (liberal) policies. {20}
- **Sandhya, Yugantar and Kaal** advocated revolutionary extremism. {21}
- Sandhya and Yugantar were in Bengal; Kaal was in Maharashtra.
- **Yugantar** was published by Bhupendra Nath Dutt and Barindra Kumar Ghosh; Gandhi was not associated with it. {57}
- **Aurobindo Ghosh** was editor of the English weekly Bande Mataram (Vande Mataram). {33}
- Vande Mataram, English paper, was founded in 1906 by Bipin Chandra Pal.
- Bipin Chandra Pal edited it from 6 August 1906 to 15 October 1906.
- Sri Aurobindo edited it from 24 October 1906 to 27 May 1907.
- It was a daily; from 2 June 1907 it became a weekly.
- From 10 September 1909 Vande Mataram was published from Paris with Hardayal as editor; Madame Bhikaji Cama handled publication and distribution.
## Papers abroad and Ghadar
- **The Free Hindustan** was started by **Tarak Nath Das** in North America (Vancouver). {12}
- Hindustan Ghadar Party was formed in 1913 at San Francisco by Indian migrants in USA and Canada.
- Ghadar Party aimed to organise an army to overthrow British rule in India; headquarters at San Francisco.
- Founders: Lala Hardayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna and Kartar Singh Sarabha.
- **Ghadar** was a weekly and the manifesto of the party. {16}
- First issue of Ghadar came out on 1 November 1913 at San Francisco in **Urdu**. {15}
- From 9 December 1913 Ghadar was also printed in Gurmukhi script.
- Ghadar was later published in Marathi, Hindi, English and Gujarati; one edition in Pashto.
## Gandhiji's papers
- **Indian Opinion** was started by Mahatma Gandhi in South Africa in 1903. {26}
- **Mansukh Lal Hiralal Nazar** was the first editor of Indian Opinion; he was Secretary of the Natal Congress. {25}
- Indian Opinion was published in Gujarati, Hindi, Tamil and English; not in Urdu. {27}
- **Young India** began as a bi-weekly from Bombay on 7 May 1919 under Gandhiji's supervision.
- Young India was started by Home Rule members Jamnadas Dwarkadas and Shankarlal Banker (publisher), of Annie Besant's Home Rule League. {28}
- Jamnadas Dwarkadas and Shankarlal Banker were its founding members and editors of its early version.
- On 8 October 1919 Gandhi became editor of Young India; it became a weekly from Ahmedabad.
- Young India = English weekly edited by Gandhi during 1919–1931; an influential platform of the freedom struggle.
! The question on who started 'The Young India' was removed by Bihar PSC because it wrongly wrote "The Young India" instead of "Young India".
- Young India was not linked with revolutionary activities; Gandhi used it to spread his ideas of non-violence. {22}
- Gandhi started the weeklies **Harijan** (English), Harijan Bandhu (Gujarati) and Harijan Sevak (Hindi). {40}
- First issue of Harijan came out on 11 February 1933 from **Pune**, Maharashtra. {42}
- Papers started by Gandhi: Indian Opinion, Harijan, Young India and Navjeevan.
## Ambedkar, Azad, Lajpat Rai, Nehru
- On 20 July 1924 B.R. Ambedkar founded **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha** in Bombay for the moral and material uplift of untouchables.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** started the Marathi fortnightly **Bahishkrit Bharat** (1927). {43,44,45}
- Mook Nayak is linked with B.R. Ambedkar.
- **Abul Kalam Azad** started the Urdu weekly **Al-Hilal** in 1912. {46,47}
- Al-Hilal criticised oppressive British policies; it was shut down in 1914.
- Azad published Al-Balagh in 1915.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** started the Urdu daily **Vande Mataram** from Lahore (printed dates: 1905-06; also 1920). {48}
- Lajpat Rai started The People, an English weekly, from Lahore in 1925.
- Lajpat Rai earlier published Young India in America in 1916.
- **Qaumi Awaz**, Urdu paper, was started by **Jawaharlal Nehru** and Rafi Ahmed Kidwai in 1945 from Lucknow; not by Abul Kalam Azad. {58,59}
- Qaumi Awaz stopped publication in 2008.
- **Motilal Nehru** started the English paper **The Independent** in February 1919 at Allahabad, demanding Home Rule; it was not Ambedkar's. {36,62}
