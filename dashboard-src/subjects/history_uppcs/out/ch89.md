@ 89 | Formation of Interim Government (1946)
## Formation
- On **12 August 1946** Lord Wavell invited Congress President Jawaharlal Nehru to form the Interim Government.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** headed the Interim Government (Cabinet) formed in 1946. {1}
- The first Interim Government was announced on 24 August 1946 and formed (took office) on **2 September 1946**. {2,3}
- It was formed from the newly elected Constituent Assembly as per the Cabinet Mission Plan.
- Muslim League did not join at first; it boycotted the government set up on 2 September 1946.
## Ministers and portfolios
- The Interim Government had twelve ministers (out of a planned 14).
- The twelve: Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Asaf Ali, C. Rajagopalachari, Sarat Chandra Bose, Dr. John Mathai, Sardar Baldev Singh, Sir Shafat Ahmed Khan, Jagjivan Ram, Syed Ali Zaheer, C.H. Bhabha.

- **B.R. Ambedkar** was not a member of the Interim Cabinet. {12}
- **Abul Kalam Azad** was not a member of this Interim Government; Liaquat Ali Khan too was not a minister on 2 September 1946. {13}
## Muslim League joins
- After a series of meetings between Jinnah and Lord Wavell, and Wavell's repeated requests, the League decided to join on **13 October 1946**.
- Five League members joined the Interim Government in October 1946; the League nominees joined on 26 October 1946.
- Three earlier ministers resigned to make room for them: **Syed Ali Zaheer, Sarat Chandra Bose and Sir Shafat Ahmed Khan**.
- Members after the League joined: Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Ibrahim Ismail Chundrigar, C. Rajagopalachari, Baldev Singh, Liaquat Ali Khan, Ghazanfar Ali Khan, Jagjivan Ram, Abdur Rab Nishtar, Asaf Ali, C.H. Bhabha.
- As Finance Minister, Liaquat Ali Khan in his budget imposed several high taxes on traders, businessmen and industrialists.
## Bengal ministry
- After the 1946 elections the Muslim League formed its government in **Bengal**. {14}
- The Krishak Praja Party under A.K. Fazlul Haq joined hands with the League in a coalition; later a Muslim League cabinet was formed, which ruled till 14 August 1947.
- **H.S. Suhrawardy** was the League leader in Bengal and Prime Minister of the province.
## Direct Action Day
- Muslim League observed **Direct Action Day** on **16 August 1946**. {15,16,17,18}
- The League rejected the Viceroy's interim cabinet decision and demanded a separate Pakistan.
- By riots that day it wanted to show that Hindus and Muslims could not live together in one country.
- **Calcutta** was the main centre of these riots; the Noakhali riots began later, in October 1946.
- One explanation names Noakhali as the centre of riots during the action.
- The League had formally demanded a separate Muslim state in the Pakistan (Lahore) Resolution on 23 March 1940 at its Lahore general session.
