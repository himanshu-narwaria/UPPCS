@ 12 | Architecture in Ancient India
## Khajuraho temples
- **Khajuraho temples** show the excellence of **Chandela** architecture; built by the Chandela dynasty, mostly between 950-1050 AD; in Chhatarpur district, Madhya Pradesh. {1,3,4,6,82}
- **Kandariya Mahadev Temple**, attributed to (Chandela) king **Vidyadhar** (11th century), is the largest Khajuraho temple. {2}
- Records describe the building of **85 temples** at Khajuraho.
- Khajuraho is a group of **Hindu and Jain** temples: dedicated to Shiva, Vishnu, Shakta, Surya and Jaina patriarchs. {5}
- **Matangeshvara temple** of Khajuraho is dedicated to Lord **Shiva**; built in King **Dhanga's** reign; Khajuraho temples are in **Nagara style**. {7}
- **Parshvanath temple** is the famous Jain temple at Khajuraho; other temples: Chausath Yogini, Brahma, Lalguan Mahadev, Lakshmana, Vishvanath, Chitragupta.
- **Dashavatara Temple** is not at Khajuraho: it is at Devgarh/Deogarh (Lalitpur), built in the Gupta period. {8}
- In Khajuraho temples all compartments of the temple are connected internally and externally.
- Khajuraho group of monuments, Bhimbetka rock shelters and Sanchi Stupa are UNESCO World Heritage Sites; **Mandu fort** is not. {11}
- Khajuraho is known for Shaiva, Vaishnava and Jain temples.
## Chausath Yogini temple, Morena
- **Chausath Yogini temple** is in Morena district (M.P.); an inscription of 1323 CE (Vikram Samvat 1383) says Kachchhapaghata king **Devapala** built it; its circular shape is popularly believed to have inspired the Indian Parliament building. {9}
- The circular shape likely stands for a **Sri Yantra** in which the Yoginis live, with the Supreme Yogini (Maha Sakti) in the centre (the circular central main shrine).
- It is not the only circular temple in India, and it was not meant to promote the Vaishnava cult.
## Temple styles
- **Nagara, Dravida and Vesara** are the three main styles of Indian temple architecture. {95}
- **Nagara** style developed and prevails across North India; temples of Odisha are purely of this style.
- **Dravida** style developed in South India, especially between the Krishna River and Cape Comorin (Tamil Nadu).
- **Vesara** style grew from a mix of Nagara and Dravida; also called **Chalukya style** because the last Chalukya rulers used it.
- Free-standing (mortar and stone) temples began in the Gupta period; **Ladkhan**, one of the earliest temples, belongs to the Chalukyas of Badami; Kailashnath of Kanchi is NOT the earliest free-standing Dravida temple. {12}
- **Kailashnath temple at Kanchi**: built by the Pallavas; fine Dravida example; built by **Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)**, completed by his son **Mahendravarman II**.
- Kailasanathar Temple of Kanchipuram (Tamil Nadu) is in the **Rajsimha style**: intricate carvings, large Gopurams, finely detailed stone sculptures; main deity Shiva. {78}
- **Teli Temple of Gwalior** fuses Nagara and Dravida: its Shikhar (pinnacle) is Dravidian, its ornamentation Nagara. {13}
- **Hoysaleshwara temple** ('Lord of the Hoysalas') at Halebidu, Karnataka, built in dark schist stone by the Hoysala king, is an example of Vesara style. {14}
- Hoysala temples are called hybrid or Vesara: neither fully Dravida nor Nagara; known by star-like ground-plans and profuse decorative carvings.
- **Panchayatana** = a style of temple building: a central shrine surrounded by four subsidiary shrines; from Sanskrit Pancha (five) + ayatana (containing). {96}
- Panchayatana examples: Kandariya Mahadeo (Khajuraho); Brahmeshwar and Lingaraja (Bhubaneswar); Dashavatara (Deogarh, U.P.).
- **Gopuram** = ornate monumental multi-storied tower over the gateway of a temple, mainly in South India; a key feature of Dravidian Hindu temples. {71}
- Gopuram's origin goes back to early structures of the Pallavas; by the 12th century under the Pandyas these gateways dominated the temple's look, hiding the inner sanctuary by their huge size.
- Under the **Pandyas** temples were built in Dravidian style: temples were small but had ramparts around; the gateways, 'Gopurams', were elegant, spacious and richly crafted.

## Ajanta and Bagh caves
- **Ajanta and Bagh caves** are the two examples of Gupta-period cave painting; Bagh paintings show normal public life, while Ajanta paintings are Buddhist. {23}
- Ajanta and Ellora caves are in **Chhatrapati Sambhaji Nagar (formerly Aurangabad) district, Maharashtra**. {41,42,43}
- An earlier group of Ajanta caves was made in the Satavahana period; the major part later in the **Vakataka** period (main patrons of Ajanta art). {48}
- Ajanta = Buddhist cave temples, carved out of the mountain rock, with figures of the Lord and others. {25}
- Ajanta murals and sculptures are masterpieces of (Buddhist) religious art, the finest surviving examples of Indian art; they show Buddha and the **Jataka stories**. {49}
- Ajanta paintings focus on Jatakas showing Buddha's previous life as a king; they also show flowers, trees and animals.
- Ajanta caves lie in the gorge (valley) of the **Waghora river**. {50}
- Ajanta cave 16 has an important painted theme; cave 17 has a well-preserved Vakataka-age painting with a huge wheel, the **"Wheel of Life"**.
- **Bodhisattva Padmapani** painting in Cave 1 is among Ajanta's most famous murals; Padmapani = "one who holds the lotus".
- Padmapani is shown among devotees, princesses and commoners, long dark hair under a jewelled crown, holding a blue lotus in his right hand.
- Ajanta cave 26 has a grand image of Buddha's **Mahaparinirvana**. {98}
- Huge **Varaha Avatar** image of Vishnu is at the **Udaygiri caves**, Madhya Pradesh, not at Mount Abu.
- **'Arjuna's Penance' (Descent of Ganga)** is sculpted on huge boulders at Mamallapuram, Chengalpattu district, Tamil Nadu.
- Ajanta has 30 rock-cut caves (a series of 29 Buddhist cave temples) built from 2nd century BC to 7th century AD.
- Ajanta and Mahabalipuram are both rock-cut monuments, but not of the same period or the same religion. {54}
- Mural paintings are seen at **Ajanta** and **Lepakshi temple**, not at Sanchi Stupa. {51}
- Sanchi Stupa's core was a simple hemispherical brick structure over Buddha's relics, crowned by the **Chhatra**, a parasol-like structure of high rank to honour and shelter the relics.
- Buddhist caves are not found at **Sanchi and Bulsar**; Bagh caves also have Buddhist art; Ajanta shows Buddha's life and teachings. {52}
- **Sanchi stupa** was built by Maurya emperor Ashoka; lies west of the Betwa river in Raisen district, M.P.
- **Pandu-lena cave shrines** are in Nashik, Maharashtra, in the valley of the Godavari river.
- **Amravati Stupa** = ruined Buddhist stupa at Amravati village, Palnadu district, Andhra Pradesh, on the banks of the Krishna river.
- **Lomas Rishi caves** belong to the Mauryan period; in Barabar hills, Jahanabad district, Bihar; **Nasik caves** belong to the Satavahana period.
## Other rock-cut caves
- Ancient rock-cut caves of western India are at **Nasik, Ellora and Ajanta**. {24}
- **Nasik caves** are also called **'Pandavleni'** or **Trirashmi caves**; they show Buddhist devotional practices.
- **Kanheri caves** (over 100 Buddhist caves): rock-cut monuments north of Borivali on Mumbai's western outskirts; show Buddhist influence; built from the 1st century BC. {15}
- **Bhimbetka rock shelters**, not the Badami caves, are the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India; Ashoka (not Chandragupta Maurya) gave a Barabar cave to the Ajivikas; Ellora caves were made for different faiths. {27}
- Barabar Hill has four caves: Karan Chaupar, Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Vishva Zopari.
- **Bhaja caves** (Bhaje village, Pune, Maharashtra) = Buddhist cave shrine; **Sittanavasal** cave temple = built by Pallava king Mahendravarman I for Jainism; **Besnagar** is not a Shaivite cave shrine. {84}
- **Besnagar** (M.P.) gives evidence of the Bhagwat religion; Greek ambassador **Heliodorus** set up the Garuda Pillar there.
- **Rock-cut elephant at Dhauli**, made in Ashoka's reign (3rd century BC), is older than the Udayagiri Varaha image (5th century AD), Mahabalipuram rock-cut monuments (7th-8th century AD) and Lingaraja Temple (10th-11th century AD). {100}
## Elephanta
- **Elephanta caves** are believed to be built by the **Rashtrakuta** dynasty; Ellora and Elephanta belong to the Rashtrakuta period. {20,21}
- Elephanta island lies on an arm of the Arabian Sea; caves date from the 5th to 6th century AD.
- Elephanta has two groups: a large group of five Hindu caves (Shaiva, dedicated to Shiva, with rock-cut sculptures) and a smaller group of two Buddhist caves. {18,19}
- The famous **Trimurti (Mahesa-murti)** statue of Shiva is at Elephanta. {22}
- The three-faced Shiva Trimurti is like the trinity of Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh, which was the royal insignia of the Rashtrakutas.
## Ellora and Kailasa temple
- **Ellora**, locally **'Verul Leni'**, is 30 km from Chhatrapati Sambhaji Nagar, Maharashtra; world-famous for rock-cut cave temples; the Rashtrakutas built most of the caves. {26}
- Ellora has 34 caves of different periods: caves 1-12 Buddhist (12), 13-29 Hindu (17), 30-34 Jain (5). {28,29,30}
- Ellora caves were made in various periods and are not linked with the Sakas.
- **Kailasa temple** (cave 16) at Ellora is the finest example and a wonder of **rock-cut architecture**. {31,32,33}
- Kailasa temple was built by Rashtrakuta ruler **Krishna I** in Dravidian style. {34,35,36,37,38,39}
- Kailasa is a remarkable Dravidian work for its proportion, workmanship and sculpture; designed to recall Mount Kailasa, home of Shiva.
- Rashtrakuta kings supported Shaiva, Vaishnava and Shakta faiths but leaned towards **Jainism**; many court scholars writing in Sanskrit, Kannada, Apabhramsha and Prakrit were Jains. {40}
- **Amoghavarsha I** was a disciple of Jain Acharya **Jinasena**, author of **Adipurana**.
## Jain temples
- **Dilwara Jain temples** are at **Mount Abu** (Aravali), Rajasthan. {91,92,93}
- **Vimal Vasahi**, most famous Dilwara temple, was built by **Vimal Shah**, minister (feudatory) of Chalukya (Solanki) ruler of Gujarat **Bheemdev I (Bhima I)**.
- The Jain temple of Abu is famed for its extraordinary architecture and marvellous **marble** carvings. {16}
- **Palitana** Jain temples stand on **Shatrunjaya hill** near Palitana city, **Bhavnagar** district, Gujarat; dedicated to the 1st Tirthankara Adinatha. {17}
- **Sonagiri** is 15 km from **Datia** (Madhya Pradesh); sacred to Digambar Jains for self-discipline, austerity and Nirvana. {89,90}
- Sonagiri has 103 Jain temples with sky-high spires; main one is Temple No. 57, of Lord **Chandraprabhu**.
## Temples of Odisha
- **Konark Sun Temple** (Odisha, 13th century) was built by King **Narasimhadeva I** of the Eastern Ganga dynasty. {56,59}
- Konark was designed as a huge solar chariot with twelve pairs of ornamental wheels pulled by seven rearing horses.
- Konark is called the **Black Pagoda** (by European sailors); Jagannath Temple of Puri was called the **White Pagoda**. {57,58}
- **Lingaraj Temple** at **Bhubaneswar** is one of Odisha's most popular and oldest temples; dedicated to Shiva (Lingaraj), also described as Harihara, a form of Shiva. {62}
- Lingaraj temple is about **180 ft** high (largest and tallest among Odisha temples in the options); enshrines a huge **'Swayambhu'** (self-originated) Shivling; a finest example of Nagara style. {63}
- **Jagannath Temple** is in Puri, Odisha (Orissa). {64}
- Temples of Bhubaneswar (Lingaraj) and Puri (Jagannath), and Konark Sun Temple (Puri), are in **Nagara style**. {65}
- Idols of Jagannath, Subhadra and Balbhadra in the sanctum are made of **neem wood**; Bhavishya Purana calls it the most auspicious wood for Vishnu idols. {66}
- The wooden icon is repainted every year before the Ratha-yatra; replaced by a new image about every 12 or 19 years, when the month of Asadha comes twice in one year (luni-solar calendar).
## Other Sun temples
- **Sun Temple of Modhera** is in Gujarat (Patan); built by King **Bhimadeva I** of the Solanki dynasty. {60,61}
- **Arasavalli** (village in Andhra Pradesh) has a prominent 7th-century Sun temple, believed built by King **Devendra Verma** of the Eastern Ganga (Kalinga) dynasty; Amarkantak and Omkareshwar are not Sun temples. {86}
- **Omkareshwar temple**: Khandwa district, M.P.; on Mandhata (Shivpuri) island in the Narmada; one of the 12 Jyotirlingas of Shiva.
- Narmada, Son and Johila rivers rise at **Amarkantak**; Sri Jwaleshwar (Jaleswara) Mahadev temple, 8 km away on the Shahdol road, is dedicated to Shiva.
## Pallava and Chola temples
- **Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram)** rock-cut and Rath temples were built by the **Pallavas**; it was a famous centre of Pallava art and architecture. {72,73,76}
- Mahabalipuram is on the seashore about 55 km south of Chennai, on a hillock over the Coromandel coast (Bay of Bengal), Chengalpattu district, Tamil Nadu; a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- Its wonders: Shore Temple, Rathas (chariots) cut from a single rock, Arjuna's Penance, other cave temples; also called 'Mandapas' rock-cut caves.
- Mahabalipuram was a pilgrimage place even before the Pallavas; built up in the 7th-10th century; its monuments are related to Shiva or Vishnu.
- Pallava king Narasimha Varman built these temples, including the present Shore temple (as one passage says).
- **Narasimhavarman I (Mahamalla)** (630-668 AD) built the **Seven Ratha Temple**, with seven rock-cut pagodas; the **Mamalla style** grew in his time. {74,75}
- His title 'Mahamalla' = 'great wrestler'; the city Mamallapuram is named after him.
- **Draupadi Rath** is the smallest of the monolithic raths of Mamalla style. {77}
- Draupadi Rath: one passage says it has carvings (incl. Goddess Durga); another says no ornamentation and it rests on lion- and elephant-like sculptures.
- The Panch Rathas have statues of an elephant, a lion and a Nandi bull beside them.
- Order of building: **Sapt Pagoda** (Mamalla style, 640-674 AD) → **Shore Temple** (Rajsimha style, 674-800 AD) → **Brihadeeswara** (Rajaraja I) → **Gangaikonda Cholapuram** (Rajendra I). {79}
- **Korangnath Temple** of Srinivasanallur was built under Chola ruler **Parantaka I**.
- **Rajarajeshwara / Brihadeeswara Temple**, Thanjavur, was built in **Rajaraja I's** time, of granite stone.
- Under Rajaraja's son **Rajendra Chola** the temple at **Gangaikonda Cholapuram** was built.
- **Meenakshi Temple** was built by the Pandyas.
- **Virupaksha temple** is at **Hampi**, Karnataka; named after Shiva, called Virupaksha here. {94}
## Temples abroad
- **Angkor Wat** temple complex is in Kampuchea (now **Cambodia**); built by Khmer king **Suryavarman II** in the early 12th century in his capital Yashodharapura (present Angkor). {67,68,69}
- Angkor Wat is dedicated to Vishnu; world's largest religious structure; Khmer style architecture.
- **Borobudur** stupa is on **Java** island, Indonesia; a UNESCO World Heritage Site. {70}

## Folk paintings
- **Pattachitra** art belongs to Odisha; closely linked to Lord Jagannath and Vaishnav cults.
- **Manjusha art** = folk art of Bhagalpur, Bihar; one of India's oldest painting arts.
- Manjushas are temple-shaped boxes with eight pillars, made of bamboo, jute and paper; painted in only three colours: pink, green, yellow; used in **Bishahari Puja**.
- **Pithora painting** = ritual painting of tribes in Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh.
