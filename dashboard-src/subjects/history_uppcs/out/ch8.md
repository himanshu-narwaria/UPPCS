@ 8 | Greek Invasion
## Persian contact
- **Kurush / Cyrus II** (558–529 BC) founded the **Achaemenid Empire** in central Iran in the 6th century BC.
- Behistun, Persepolis and Naqsh-e-Rostam inscriptions belong to **Darius I** (522–486 BC) and later kings; they tell of India–Persia relations.
- Behistun inscription names the 23 provinces of Darius I's empire.
- Herodotus: India was the **twentieth province** of Darius's empire.
- **Ctesias** was the state doctor (Rajya Vaidya) of Artaxerxes II.
- **Kharoshthi** script grew out of the Iranian Aramaic script in north-western India, as a result of Persian contact.
## Nandas at the time of Alexander
- Puranas and Buddhist texts give eight inheritors of Mahapadmananda.
- **Dhanananda**, last ruler of the **Nanda** dynasty, was the contemporary of Alexander; Nandas ruled North India at his invasion. {1,2}
- Greek writers called Dhanananda **Agrammese**; Xenophon describes him as a wealthy person; **Bhaddasala** was his commander.
## Alexander's Indian campaign
- After demolishing the Achaemenid Empire in 326 BC, Alexander began his Indian campaign with a huge army.
- After nearly two years he went back towards Greece from Paatal in **325 BC**; he was NOT defeated by Chandragupta Maurya. {3}
- **Ashvaka** = frontier republic at the time of Alexander's invasion; capital **Massaga**.
- Greek writers: the women of Massaga took up arms against Alexander after many men were killed; Alexander killed the women of the town. {4}
- **Puru (Porus)**'s realm lay between the Jhelum and the Chenab; Alexander defeated him on the bank of the **Jhelum**. {6}
- Impressed by Porus's courage, Alexander became his friend, returned his state and gifted him some more areas.
- Reasons for Alexander's success: no central power in India, his superior army, help from traitorous Indian rulers like Ambhi; not good administration. {5}
- Dr. Hemachandra Rai Chaudhary lists 28 independent powers in India at that time.
- **Deimachus** did not come to India with Alexander; Nearchus, Onesicritus and Aristobulus did. {7}
- Strabo: Seleucid emperor Antiochus sent Deimachus as ambassador to the court of Bindusara.
