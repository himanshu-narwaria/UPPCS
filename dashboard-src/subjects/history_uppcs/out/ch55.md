## Indian Home Rule Society and India House
- **Shyamji Krishna Verma** founded the **Indian Home Rule Society (IHRS)** in London in February 1905. {1,2,3}
- IHRS set up a nationalist student hostel, **'India House'** (1905-10), in London to foster radical Indian nationalist thought. {4,5}
- Aim of the society: self-rule, by spreading nationalist ideas among Indians in Britain and by intimidating the British Government.
- A monthly journal, **'The Indian Sociologist'**, was started from here in January 1905.
- Shyamji left London for Paris because of the over-activeness of the government machinery, and later went to Geneva.
- Abhinav Bharat Society was extended to England in 1906 after revolutionary activities were banned in India; V.D. Savarkar set it up when he went to London to study law. {35}
## Ghadar Party
- The **Ghadar Party** was founded on 21 April 1913 (at Oregon) to free India from British rule. {9}
- It was founded in the United States of America by Indians living in the USA and Canada. {15,16,17}
- It was a revolutionary association of expatriate Indians with headquarters at **San Francisco**. {18,19}
- In 1913 **Sohan Singh Bhakna** founded the 'Hindustan Association of the Pacific Coast' at San Francisco. {11}
- The society published a newspaper named 'Ghadar', so it became famous as the Ghadar Party.
- Sohan Singh Bhakna was the first Chairman (President) of the Ghadar Party. {12}
- **Lala Hardayal** was a founder member and the torchbearer of the Ghadar Party. {7,8,10}
- The 'Ghadar' newspaper was published in Urdu, Punjabi, Gujarati and Hindi.
- Prominent members: Ramchandra, Barakatullah, Rash Behari Bose, Raja Mahendra Pratap, Abdul Rahman, Madam Bhikaji Cama, Bhai Parmanand, Kartar Singh Sarabha and Pt. Kashiram (Kanshiram).
- Rash Behari Bose was actively associated with the Ghadar Party. {13}
- Barindra Kumar Ghosh was linked with the Alipore Bomb case; Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee with the Kakori Conspiracy (Kakori Train Action).
- Khudiram Bose was not associated with the Ghadar Party. {14}
- Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki threw a bomb at Kingsford on 30 April 1908 at Muzaffarpur, Bihar; the wife and daughter of Mr. Kennedy, an Indian sympathiser, died.
- Prafulla Chaki committed suicide; Khudiram Bose was hanged on 11 August 1908 aged 18 years, 8 months, 8 days.
- Lala Har Dayal, revolutionary and scholar, was appointed to teach Indian philosophy and Sanskrit at Stanford University in California (USA); this chance to be Professor of Indian Philosophy came in 1911. {6}
- During World War I, San Francisco was the centre of the Ghadar revolutionaries; it lies in the western part of the USA (West America). {20}
! If America is divided only into North and South America continents, the answer for the Ghadar base becomes North America.
- After the decline of the Swadeshi movement, the torchbearers of nationalism had become inactive.
- The outbreak of World War I in 1914 was the cause of the Ghadar revolution; nationalists took it as an opportunity. {21}
- The popular idea then: "Any distress on England is an advantage for Indians and it should be taken as a chance."
- Ghadar revolutionaries in North America, and Lokmanya Tilak and Annie Besant with their Swadeshi organisations in India, used this opportunity.
- Ghadar revolutionaries tried to overthrow British rule through armed struggle.
- The Ghadar Movement was an early 20th century international political movement of expatriate Indians; its official birth is the meeting of 21 April 1913.
## Provisional Government at Kabul
- **Raja Mahendra Pratap**, with Barakatullah Khan, set up the first provisional Government of India at Kabul (Afghanistan) on 1 December 1915, during World War I. {22,23,24}
- Raja Mahendra Pratap was President and Maulavi Barakatullah Khan was Prime Minister.
- Germany and Russia recognised this government.
## Madam Bhikaji Cama
- **Madam Bhikaiji Rustom Cama** was born on 24 September 1861 to Parsi parents, in an extremely wealthy Parsi business family; her father was Sorabji Patel.
- She was married to Rustomji, an advocate and social worker.
- She served as personal secretary of Dadabhai Naoroji. {26}
- She joined the freedom movement after coming into contact with European scholars and students.
- She supported revolutionary nationalism and ran revolutionary activities from Europe and America.
- At the International Socialist Congress at **Stuttgart (Germany)** on 22 August 1907 she became the first person to hoist the Indian flag on foreign land. {28,29,30}
- The statement that she unfurled the flag at Paris in 1907 is wrong.
- The flag was designed jointly by V.D. Savarkar, Shyamji Krishna Verma and Bhikaiji Cama.
- She is famous as the **'Mother of the Indian Revolution'**. {25,27}
- She worked for Indian freedom while living in Paris; returned to Mumbai in 1935 and died in August 1936.
- Madam Bhikaji Cama, M. Barkatullah, V.V.S. Iyer and M.N. Roy were prominent revolutionaries working for India's freedom from different foreign countries. {34}
## Madan Lal Dhingra and Udham Singh
- Madan Lal Dhingra and Udham Singh were hanged for killing British officials in England. {31}
- On 1 July 1909 **Madan Lal Dhingra** shot dead Curzon Wyllie and Cowasji Lalkaka at a meeting of the National Indian Association. {33}
- Curzon Wyllie was political aide-de-camp to the Secretary of State for India.
- **Udham Singh** killed Michael O'Dwyer, former Lieutenant Governor of Punjab, in London in March 1940. {32}
- O'Dwyer was responsible for endorsing and upholding the actions taken during the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre.
## Komagata Maru
- **Kamagata Maru** was a Japanese steamliner (ship) chartered by Gurdit Singh, a rich Punjabi businessman, to take Indian immigrants to Canada. {36,37,39}
- The 'Kamagata Maru episode' is linked with Baba Gurdit Singh (printed "Gurdip Singh" in the option by error). {38}
- The ship left in 1914 with 376 Indians for Vancouver port, but the Canadian authorities refused them entry.
- A Shore Committee was formed under Hussain Rahim, Balwant Singh and Sohan Lal Pathak to protect the rights of the Indian travellers.
- Bhagwan Singh, Barkatullah, Ramchand and Sohan Singh also ran a movement in favour of the Indian emigrants.
- When the ship reached Budge Budge port at Calcutta, angry passengers clashed with British police.
- In police firing 20 passengers were killed, one drowned and 202 were sent to jail.
## Chronology and headquarters
- Order: Ghadar Party founded (21 April 1913) → Indian Independence Committee at Berlin (after 1915) → Central Assembly Bomb Case (8 April 1929) → Chittagong Armoury Raid (18 April 1930). {40}
- During World War I (1914-1918) the Berlin Committee was founded in Germany; after 1915 it was renamed the Indian Independence Committee.
- Chittagong Armoury Raid (Chittagong uprising): 18 April 1930, led by Surya Sen, in Bengal Presidency (now in Bangladesh).
- On 8 April 1929 Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt hurled two bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly chamber while it was in session.

## Indian Independence League
- Rash Behari Bose was an Indian living in Japan; he held a conference in Tokyo on 28-30 March 1942 to discuss India's political problems.
- It was given organised form at the Bangkok Convention in June 1942, to which Subhas Chandra Bose was invited.
- **Rash Behari Bose** founded the **Indian Independence League** there and was unanimously elected its President. {42}
