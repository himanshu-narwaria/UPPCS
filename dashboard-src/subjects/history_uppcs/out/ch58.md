@ 58 | Formation of Muslim League (1906)
## Shimla Deputation
- On 1 October 1906 a Muslim delegation (Simla Deputation, 35 prominent Muslim leaders) led by **Aga Khan III** met Viceroy Lord Minto at Shimla.
- It demanded a **separate electorate** for Muslims in elections at all levels of governance. {10}
- The deputation aimed to win the sympathy of British rule on matters of Muslim interest.
- In October 1906 the Muslim representatives at Shimla under Aga Khan III decided to form a separate Muslim representation to protect Muslim interests.
- The Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) accepted the separate electorate for Muslims.
## Founding of the League
- The All India Mohammadan Educational Conference met at **Dhaka** in December 1906; the Muslim League was founded there. {3}
- The All India Muslim League was founded in 1906 at Dhaka (Bangladesh) to safeguard the rights of Indian Muslims. {4}
- **Nawab Salimullah Khan** of Dhaka, President of the Welcome Committee and organiser of the political meetings, proposed the League and hosted the founding meeting; he is taken as its founder. {1,2}
- Founder members: Salimullah Khan, Aga Khan III and Mohsin-ul-Mulk.
- A 60-member temporary committee was formed with Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Waqar-ul-Mulk as joint secretaries (also given as jointly appointed secretary of the Muhammadan Educational Conference).
- **Aga Khan III** was the first President of the Muslim League. {5}
- Aga Khan's goal was the advancement of the Muslim agenda and protection of Muslim rights in India.
- The League's headquarters was at Lucknow.
## Objectives
- Objective 1: to promote feelings of loyalty to the British government among Indian Muslims and remove any misconception about the government's intentions in its measures.
- Objective 2: to protect and advance the political rights and interests of Indian Muslims and respectfully represent their needs and aspirations to the government.
- Objective 3: to prevent any feeling of hostility among Indian Muslims towards other communities, without prejudice to the other objects.
## Sessions and stand
- The first annual session of the Muslim League was held at **Karachi** in 1907; the next at Amritsar in 1908. {6}
- After these, League sessions were held erratically in different parts of the country. {7}
! On the year from which the League held regular annual conventions, BPSC accepted 1925, which the book calls incorrect.
- At the Amritsar session (30-31 December 1908) the League made its clear and formal demand for separate electorates, denouncing joint electorates.
- The Muslim League formed in 1906 supported the partition of Bengal and demanded separate electorates under Aga Khan III. {9}
- Hakim Ajmal Khan was one of the leaders who started the nationalist and militant Ahrar movement.
- Sayyed Ahmad Khan opposed the Indian National Congress when it was formed.
- Maulana Barkat Ullah and Maulana Obeidullah Sindhi were among those who formed a Provisional Government of India in Kabul.
- The League refused to accept the Muslims attached to the Congress, posing as the only institution representing Indian Muslims; the claim that only the League had this right is wrong. {8}
- A **London branch** of the Muslim League was set up in 1908 under the presidency of **Syed Ameer Ali**, to press the British Government for the Muslim cause. {11}
- **W.W. Hunter**, in his book 'The Indian Musalmans', wrote: "The Muslims, if contented and satisfied, would become the greatest bulwark of British power in India." {12}
