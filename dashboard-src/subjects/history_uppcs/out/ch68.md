@ 68 | Non-Cooperation Movement
## Launch and resolutions
- Gandhiji launched the **Non-Cooperation Movement** on 1 August 1920. {2,3,5,8}
- It lasted from 1920 to February 1922; it is counted as the first mass movement led by Gandhiji. {4}
- Champaran Movement (1917) for indigo cultivators in Bihar was the first movement started by Gandhi.
- Non-Cooperation was the major event of the freedom struggle after the First War of Independence of 1857; it marked the start of the Gandhian movement against colonialism.
- Causes: Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh massacre (Punjab atrocities) and the Khilafat question; Salt Law was not a cause. {6}
- Salt March (Dandi March) lasted from 12 March 1930 to 6 April 1930.
- Special Congress session at Calcutta, September 1920, presided over by **Lala Lajpat Rai**, passed Gandhi's Non-Cooperation resolution. {7}
- Calcutta session discussed the Khilafat question and the Government's failure to protect innocent people of Punjab and punish British officers.
- Leaders decided the only way to vindicate national honour was Swaraj through Non-Cooperation.
- The session was attended by most national leaders and almost 300 delegates from Bihar; the resolution passed with a great majority.
- At Calcutta the resolution was opposed by senior leaders like C.R. Das and Madan Mohan Malviya.
- At the Nagpur session (December 1920) the resolution was ratified; **C.R. Das** himself moved it. {1}
- At Nagpur 1920 Congress changed its objective: attainment of 'Swaraj' by all legitimate and peaceful means.
- Plan in stages: first non-cooperation and boycott; if Swaraj did not come within a year or the Government used repression, then civil disobedience and non-payment of taxes. {12}
- Gandhiji promised (at the AICC) **'Swaraj within one year'** if the movement was effectively carried out. {9,11}
- After the 1919 reforms failed Indian aspirations, Congress sought Swaraj (self-government) under Mahatma Gandhi. {10}
## Programme
- Resolution's advice: resign from administrative bodies, give up honorary titles, withdraw children from Government schools and colleges, refuse official events, boycott British courts and foreign goods, adopt Swadeshi goods and garments.
- Nagpur programme: renunciation of titles and honours; boycott of law courts and foreign cloth; panchayats to settle disputes; strict non-violence. {41}
- Titles were to be fully renounced; keeping them without public use was not part of the programme.
- Gandhiji called for boycott of the **Prince of Wales'** visit to India in 1921, seen as a symbol of British power. {32}
- The movement urged people to give up alcohol, causing a big fall in government revenue.
- Government of **Bihar** and Odisha circulated a list of famous drinkers (Alexander, Julius Caesar, Napoleon, Shakespeare) to publicise alcohol's benefits. {15}
## Participation
- The movement got wide success in western India, Bengal and northern India.
- C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Rajendra Prasad, Jawaharlal Nehru, Vithalbhai Patel, Vallabhbhai Patel and Lala Lajpat Rai gave up legal practice and boycotted courts. {18}
- **Rajendra Prasad**, a pleader of Bihar, left his lucrative practice for the movement; he wrote the book 'India Divided'. {38}
- **M.A. Jinnah** did not support the Non-Cooperation Movement. {13}
- Gandhiji got the **'Kaiser-i-Hind'** title in 1915 from Lord Hardinge II for humanitarian work in South Africa; he returned it in 1920 during Non-Cooperation. {17}
- Kaisar-i-Hind Medal: given by the British monarch to civilians of any nationality for distinguished service advancing the interests of the British Raj.
- When Gandhiji arrived in India, Britain was in World War I; he supported Britain in the war.
- Jamnalal Bajaj returned the title of 'Rai Bahadur'.
- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** supported Non-Cooperation but died on its first day (1 August 1920), so could not see its result. {20}
- Tilak agreed with non-cooperation (June 1920) but opposed boycott of legislative council elections set up by the 1919 Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms.
- **Swami Vidyanand**, a peasant leader, led cultivators in Bihar and linked peasant issues with Non-Cooperation. {37,45}
- **Rahul Sankrityayan** (original name Kedar Nath Pandey) was active in Chhapra, Bihar; he and Nagnarayan spread the message there. {21}
- Rahul Sankrityayan (1893–1963), born in Azamgarh, U.P.; 'Mahapandit', Father of Hindi travel literature; spent forty-five years on travels.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan** (born Sitab Diyara, Saran, Bihar) left Patna College with 20 days left for his exams; then joined Congress-run Bihar Vidyapeeth. {51}
- **E.V. Ramaswami Nayakar** (Erode Venkatappa Ramasamy, 'Periyar') took part in Non-Cooperation; he left Congress in 1925. {47}
## Non-Cooperation in the towns
- Not true that only upper castes and the rich took part; the Justice Party of Madras did not boycott council elections. {14}
- 1919–1922 saw two mass movements: Khilafat and Non-Cooperation.
- Students left government schools and colleges in large numbers; headmasters and teachers resigned; lawyers gave up practice.
- Indian business groups, gaining from Swadeshi, supported the economic boycott.
- Peasants took part actively, but it led to confrontations between lower and upper castes.
- The movement was first led by the middle classes; later many had reservations about Gandhi's approach.
- Oppressed masses got a chance to voice grievances against the British and their Indian oppressors.
- Women joined in large numbers, gave up purdah and gave ornaments to the Tilak Fund.
- Police reprisals were violent and many lives were lost.
- Justice Party (Madras Presidency) did not support the movement; preferred British presence and working within the system; disliked Gandhi's praise of Brahminism.
## Institutions and Tagore
- Founded during Non-Cooperation (1920–22): Kashi Vidyapith (Varanasi, 1921), Gujarat Vidyapith (Ahmedabad, 1920), Jamia Millia Islamia (Aligarh, 1920; later shifted to Delhi). {44}
- Madan Mohan Malviya founded Banaras Hindu University at Varanasi in 1916; he was its Vice-Chancellor from 1919 to 1939.
- **Rabindranath Tagore** opposed burning of foreign clothes; he wrote to Gandhi calling it 'a ruthless waste' (indiscriminate or callous waste). {42,43}
- Tagore stressed constructive (creative) programmes over mass agitation; called cloth-burning unwise and said he could not obey it.
## Chauri Chaura
- **Chauri Chaura** incident: 4 February 1922. {22}
- Chauri Chaura is in Gorakhpur district, United Provinces (Uttar Pradesh). {23,24}
- Villagers had gathered at the police station to protest the arrest of their leader during a campaign against liquor sale.
- Police fired on the protesters; the angry mob burnt the police station, killing 23 policemen; 3 civilians also died.
- 225 persons were arrested; the Sessions Judge of Gorakhpur sentenced 170 to death.
- No local lawyer came forward; **Madan Mohan Malaviya**, aided by Krishna Kant, defended them before the Chief Justice of Allahabad High Court. {25}
- Malaviya had been away from law for nearly a decade; 151 accused were saved from the gallows, 19 were executed.
- Source cited: Sitaram Chaturvedi's biography "Madan Mohan Malaviya (Builders of Modern India)", Publications Division.
- PM Narendra Modi inaugurated the Chauri Chaura centenary celebration at Chauri Chaura, Gorakhpur, on 4 February 2021 by video conferencing. {30}
## Withdrawal
- Gandhiji withdrew (suspended) the Non-Cooperation Movement because of the violence at Chauri Chaura. {26,27,28,29}
- The movement ended in 1922 (12 February 1922). {34}
- At the time of Chauri Chaura Gandhiji was at **Bardoli** (Gujarat), about to start mass civil disobedience, which was also suspended. {33}
- Congress Working Committee met at Bardoli on 11–12 February 1922 and suspended the movement: the **Bardoli resolution**.
- Sequence: Chauri Chaura incident (4 February 1922) → Bardoli Resolutions (12 February 1922) → suspension of Non-Cooperation. {39}
- Bardoli resolution: suspend mass civil disobedience; advise cultivators to pay revenue and dues; stop all offensive preparatory activity.
- Suspension was to last until the atmosphere was non-violent enough to prevent atrocities as at Gorakhpur or hooliganism as at Bombay and Madras.
- Workers were urged to take up constructive work: popularise khadi, set up national schools, work for Hindu-Muslim unity.
- 24 February 1922: AICC met at Delhi and limited all activities amounting to violation of law; individual law-breaking could continue with permission.
- At that Delhi meeting **Dr. Moonje** tabled a vote of censure against Gandhiji for calling off the movement. {35}
- The sudden suspension shocked Gandhi's followers; C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru strongly opposed it. {50}
- Gandhi was arrested on 10 March 1922 (not 1921) and sentenced for six years. {19}
- Sequence: police firing at Chauri Chaura → crowd burns police station → Gandhi postpones movement → Gandhi arrested. {36}
- Gandhiji gave his first major public speech at Banaras Hindu University.
- Gandhi called the Satyagraha at Nadiad on 14 April, during martial law after Jallianwala Bagh, his "Himalayan Blunder".
## Results
- Results: Congress became a mass movement for the first time, Hindu-Muslim unity grew, fear of British might left people's minds. {16}
- Main outcome of the 1921–22 movement: Hindu-Muslim unity; communal unity and massive Muslim participation gave it a mass character. {46}
- It showed Congress had the support of vast sections; people became conscious of political rights, lost trust in the British and gained self-reliance.
- It introduced people to modern techniques of politics and made them hungry for freedom; showed readiness to sacrifice and face repression.
- Revival of revolutionary activities in 1923–28 was due to Gandhiji's postponement of Non-Cooperation. {40}
- Disappointed nationalists, unhappy with Gandhi's leadership and non-violence, were inspired by violent revolutions in Russia, China, Ireland and Turkey.
- **Anti-Non-Cooperation Association** was started by big businessmen in 1920 against the Non-Cooperation–Khilafat Movement.
- Its founders: Jamnadas Dwarkadas, Purshottamdas Thakurdas, Cowasji Jehangir, Pheroze Sethna and M.C. Setalvad.
- Bombay businessmen feared the movement would encourage labour strikes, which had earlier turned violent in Bombay Presidency.
- Gandhi lacked acceptance in the Maharashtra region, which still remembered Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
