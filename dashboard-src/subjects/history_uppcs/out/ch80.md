@ 80 | Indian Princely States
## Butler Committee 1927
- The Indian States Committee was appointed in **1927** under chairman **Sir Harcourt Butler**; popularly called the **Butler Committee**.
- Purpose: to investigate and clarify the relationship between the paramount power (Crown) and the princes of the Princely States; improve their relations. {1}
- The princes chose **Sir Leslie Scott** as their representative (retained as counsel) to put their view before the Butler Committee.
- Recommendation: relationship of the paramount power with the states was not merely contractual but a living, growing relationship shaped by circumstances and policy.
- Recommendation: British paramountcy preserved the princely states.
- Recommendation: states should not be transferred, without their agreement, to a new government in British India responsible to an Indian legislature.
## States People's Movement
- In the 1920s, people's movements were launched in the princely states; they were called State Peoples Conference or **Praja Mandals**. {10}
- Praja Mandal movements ran parallel to the freedom struggle in Indian-ruled states such as Kashmir, Nizam's Hyderabad and Travancore.
- They were led by people of the princely states, who were under local aristocrats and not the British Raj directly.
- They campaigned against the feudatory rulers, and sometimes British administrators, to improve their civil rights.
- **All India States People's Conference** was set up in **December 1927**. {2}
- More than **1500** political workers from various states took part in it.
- **Balwant Rai Mehta, Manilal Kothari and G.R. Abhayankar** played an important role in organizing it.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** was elected President of the All India States People's Conference in **1939**. {3}
- Nehru was elected to represent the common national interest of political struggles of the Princely States and British India.
## Integration of the states
- British Government set up two independent Dominions; suzerainty over Indian States lapsed, so the princely states had to decide their accession.
- This complicated the merger of states into one Union.
- Most princely states acceded to the Indian Dominion in **1947**; territorial mergers/integration continued later. {4}
- By 15 August 1947 all princely states had joined except **Junagarh, Jammu & Kashmir and Hyderabad**. {6}
- These three states were made to join the Union of India by the end of **1948**.
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** led the integration of states; political integration of India was largely achieved under him. {5}
- Patel took charge of the States Department in 1947 and handled the situation with great statesmanship.
- Patel appealed to the patriotic and nationalist feelings of the Princes.
- Patel asked the states to hand over **External Affairs, Defence and Communications** to the Indian Dominion.
## Kashmir, Hyderabad and Punjab
- On **26 October 1947**, Maharaja **Hari Singh** of Kashmir signed the **Instrument of Accession** and handed it to Jawaharlal Nehru. {7}
- Indian Army entered Jammu and Kashmir on **27 October 1947** to repel the invaders.
- Nehru appointed **Sheikh Abdullah** as Prime Minister of Jammu and Kashmir on 5 March 1948.
- **Standstill Agreement** was signed between the State of **Hyderabad** and the Dominion of India on **29 November 1947**. {9}
- It was signed by the Nizam of Hyderabad, **Mir Osman Ali Khan**, and Lord Mountbatten, Governor-General and Viceroy of India.
- At Partition, **Punjab** province came forward with a plan for a united and independent existence. {8}
- Due to Sardar Patel's effort, **PEPSU** was formed from Eastern Punjab, Patiala and the hilly states; the idea of an independent state was rejected.
