## Chandragupta Maurya: rise and sources
- **Chandragupta Maurya**, one of India's greatest emperors, founded the Maurya Empire, the first Indian empire: he joined small fragmented kingdoms into one vast empire and first unified India politically. {1,2,4}
- Maurya dynasty (321–185 BC) is older than the Kushana, Gupta (275–550 CE) and Vardhana dynasties. {3}
- Chandragupta extended his empire to the edge of present Iran; it ran from Iran (north-west) to present north Karnataka (south).
- Chandragupta's early victories took his boundary from the Beas to the Indus river.
- Unified Maurya empire: Bengal and Assam (east) to Afghanistan (west), Kashmir (north) to the Deccan plateau (south).
- Chandragupta was the first king to conquer Malwa, Gujarat and Maharashtra. {25}
- **Chandragupta** conquered the Deccan: Jain and Tamil sources and southern inscriptions show it; Ashoka conquered only Kalinga; Bindusara's victory is doubtful. {23}
- **Mudrarakshasa** of Vishakhadatta tells of Chandragupta Maurya's reign: its subject is the court intrigues of his time. {5,149}
- Mudrarakshasa calls Chandragupta 'a scion of the Nanda house', son of Nanda (Mahapadmanand), Maurya-putra, "Vrishal" and "Kulheen".
- Dhundiraja wrote a commentary on Mudrarakshasa.
- Dr. K.P. Jayaswal: Vishakhadatta and Chandragupta were contemporary; the play shows 'Durabhisandhi' (intrigue) in Chandragupta's court.
- Other works of Vishakhadatta: Devichandraguptam and Abhisarika-vanchitaka (Abhisarika-bandhitakam; not found).
- Greek writer Justin and others called Chandragupta Maurya **'Sandrokottos'**. {6}
- **William Jones** was the first scholar to identify Sandrokottos with Chandragupta Maurya. {7}
- **Justin** described the meeting of Sandrokottos and Alexander the Great. {8}
- Aerian and Plutarch called him Androkottus.

## Chandragupta: Sudarshan lake and Pataliputra palace
- **Sudarshan Lake** (Girnar hills, Saurashtra) was built for irrigation on Chandragupta Maurya's order by **Pushyagupta**, governor of his western province; first lake in the Girnar area. {31,143}
- **Junagadh inscription of Rudradaman** (150 AD) records this in Anarta and Saurashtra (Gujarat); it proves western India (Gujarat) was in Chandragupta's empire. {27,28}
- Chandragupta Maurya and Ashoka both managed water resources in the Girnar region before the Christian era. {142}
- Ashoka's governor **Tushasp** took out canals from the lake.
- The Junagadh inscription of Mahakshatrapa Rudradaman (Shaka ruler) mentions both Chandragupta and Ashoka. {144}
- In the 2nd century AD Rudradaman's governor **Suvisakh** repaired the lake; later Skandagupta also repaired it.
- Ashoka's edicts are also found at the Junagadh inscription of Rudradaman; the rock also has edicts of Skandagupta. {145}
- A large boulder within 2 km of Uparkot fort carries fourteen edicts of Ashoka.
- Mauryan remains were found at **Bulandibagh** and **Kumrahar** near Patna = ancient **Pataliputra**. {21,22}
- Chandragupta's 80-pillared palace at Pataliputra was mainly of **wood** (wooden floors). {20}
- D.B. Spooner excavated: city wall remains from Bulandibagh, palace remains from Kumrahar.
- Not linked with Chandragupta: **Sanchi** (stupa built by Ashoka); linked: Susima, Vishnugupta, Junagadh inscription. {26}
- Susima = eldest son of Bindusara and grandson of Chandragupta Maurya.

## Kautilya and the Arthashastra
- **Kautilya** greatly helped found the Maurya dynasty; he was prime minister, adviser and chief priest of Chandragupta Maurya. {9}
- Kautilya was also known as **Vishnugupta** and Chanakya; Vishnugupta is taken as his childhood name. {10,11}
- Sage Chanak named his son Chanakya; Arthashastra names its author Kautilya, and Vishnugupta in one verse.
- Researcher Trobman: Chanakya or Kautilya is probably the author's gotra (clan) name.
- Kautilya = pioneer scholar of political science; his **Arthashastra** is a treatise on polity, principles of government and political policies; among the oldest books on India's political condition. {12,13,15}
- Arthashastra gives a detailed account of Mauryan administration and the agrarian system.
- Arthashastra introduced the **Saptang theory** of the state: King (Swami), Minister (Amatya), Territory (Janapada), Fort (Durg), Treasury (Kosa), Army (Danda), Allies (Mitra).
- **Mitra (Ally)** = seventh part of the state, like its ear; allies help the king in peace and war. {14}
- Kautilya separates ideal and fake allies: natural is better than artificial; a king with a greedy, lustful, cowardly friend is ruined.
- Arthashastra is compared with Machiavelli's **'The Prince'** (Il Principe). {17}
- Dasa-Kalpa chapter of Arthashastra: a person could become a slave by judicial punishment; a female slave who bore her master a son became legally free. {16}
- But that son did not get the legal status of the master's son.
- Arthashastra: the head of a convoy (caravan) of merchants was called **Sarthwah**. {44}
- Arthashastra's two courts: **Dharmasthiya** (like civil courts) and **Kantakshodhana** (like criminal courts). {130}
- **Arthashastra** allows divorce and remarriage to a wife deserted by her husband. {133}
- Divorce existed in Mauryan times: wife could divorce for husband's long stay abroad or physical handicap; husband could leave a barren or adulterous wife.
- Kautilya's rural tiers: Sthaniya (800 villages), Dronamukh (400), Karvatika (200), Samgrahan (10); ascending order = Samgrahana, Karvatika, Dronamukh, Sthaniya.

## Bindusara
- Greek writers called Bindusara **'Amitrochates'** = Sanskrit Amitraghat (killer of enemies); Jain texts call him 'Simhasena'.
- Jain texts: Bindusara's mother was 'Durdhara'.
- Divyavadana: a revolt broke out in Taxila in Bindusara's time; he sent his son Ashoka to suppress it.
- Strabo: Syrian king Antiochus I Soter sent **Deimachus** as ambassador to **Bindusara**'s court; he replaced Megasthenes. {18}
- Pliny: Egypt's king Ptolemy II Philadelphus sent Dionysius as ambassador to the Mauryan court.
- Bindusara asked the ruler of Syria for sweet wine, dry figs and a philosopher; Antiochus I sent all except the philosopher.
- Buddhist evidence: Ashoka was viceroy of Avanti in his father's reign.
- **Samantapasadika of Buddhaghosha** says Ashoka won Avanti Mahajanapada and merged it in the empire during Bindusara's rule. {19}
## Ashoka: empire, Kalinga war, Dhamma
- Ashoka's coronation on the throne of Magadha: 269 BC.
- All India except Assam and the far south was in Ashoka's empire; his rock and pillar edicts show its extent (the map of his reign's close). {32}
- **Sri Lanka** was not part of Ashoka's empire; Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputta, Keralaputta and Tamraparni (Sri Lanka) lay outside it — called edge (border) states. {35}
- Kalinga victory = greatest event of Ashoka's reign; conquered eight years after coronation, i.e. 261 BC.
- Ashoka annexed Kalinga because it controlled the land and sea routes to South India; reasons were political and economic. {100}
- Kalinga was like a bridge between north and south; its people dominated the Indian Ocean and overseas trade and closed eastern sea routes to Magadhan merchants.
- Kalinga also controlled the land routes from the Gangetic valley to the Deccan.
- Ashoka announced a logical, practical 'Dhamma Niti'; his **Dhamma** rests on ethical principles for the welfare of humanity, not on any community.
- Triple bases of Ashoka's Rajdharma: longanimity (tolerance), liberty and compassion. {33}
- Jha and Shrimali (Prachin Bharat Ka Itihas): spirit of Ashoka's Dhamma comes from Lakkhana Sutta of Digha Nikaya, **Rahulovada-Sutta** and Dhammapada; he appointed **Dhamma-Mahamatras** in the 14th year of his reign. {34}
- Ashoka's Dhamma was mainly a policy of peace, non-violence and religious toleration. {97}
- Ashoka built India's **first hospital and herbal garden**. {37}
- He set up hospitals, public gardens, medicinal-herb gardens, dug wells, planted shade trees, and made a ministry for the care of aborigines and subject races.
- Epithet **'Devanampriya'** (beloved of the gods / handsome) shows Ashoka kept belief in Hinduism though Buddhist. {38}
- **Third Buddhist Council**: Pataliputra, in Ashoka's reign, president Moggaliputta Tissa; sources = Sinhalese Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa. {39,40}
- Mauryan followers of Buddhism: **Ashoka** and his grandson **Dasaratha**; Dasaratha also took the title 'Devanampiya'. {41}
- After becoming Buddhist, Ashoka gave up hunting and began pilgrimages (dhamma-yatra); first he visited Bodhgaya.
- Dhamma-yatras: Sambodhi (Bodhgaya) 10 years after coronation; Kanakamuni stupa enlarged 14 years after; Lumbini and Nigalisagar 20 years after.
- Ashoka's pilgrimage route: Lumbini → Kapilavastu → Gaya → Sarnath → Kushinagar → Shravasti. {55}
- Ashoka's relations with Syria and Egypt show the **Mauryas** had diplomatic ties with distant countries. {96}
## Ashoka's inscriptions: script, language, decipherment
- Ashoka's inscriptions are of three classes: rock inscriptions, pillar (column) inscriptions, cave inscriptions; more than 40 edicts have been found.
- The 14 Major Rock Edicts are found at: Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra, Kalsi, Girnar, Dhauli, Jaugada, Yerragudi, Sopara.
- Most Ashokan inscriptions are in the **Prakrit** language — the language of the earliest stone inscriptions. {56,60}
- Most are in the **Brahmi** script, as on Ashoka's pillars. {58}
- Only **Shahbazgarhi** and **Mansehra** (near Peshawar, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan) are in **Kharosthi** script, Prakrit language. {57,59,67}
- One ruined Aramaic inscription is from Taxila; another Aramaic one from Laghman.
- Shar-i-Kuna (Kandahar) inscription is bilingual: Greek and Aramaic.
- **Kharosthi** was written from right to left; a cursive script of north-west India (Gandhara culture, modern Afghanistan and Pakistan). {69,70}
- Kharosthi was deciphered by James Prinsep (1835 AD), Friedrich Grotefend (1836 AD), Lassen (1838 AD).
- **James Prinsep** was the first to decipher the Brahmi script and read Ashoka's edicts. {62,63,64,65,66}
- Brahmi was first deciphered from letters inscribed on **stone tablets**. {61}
- Prinsep first took King Devanampiya Piyadassi to be a Sri Lankan king; Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa showed the name was Ashoka's.
- In 1915 the Maski inscription (Karnataka) settled the name 'Ashoka'.
- Ashoka is generally called **'Priyadarshi' (Piyadasi)**, 'Devanampiya Piyadasi' (Beloved of the Gods) in his inscriptions. {71,72,73}
- Puranas call him 'Ashok Vardhan'.
- Personal name 'Ashoka' occurs in the **Gujarra, Maski, Nettur and Udagolam** edicts. {76,77,78}
- **Gujarra** minor rock edict: Datia district, Madhya Pradesh; on the main road from Ujjain to Bharuch. {80}
- **Kanganahalli** relief inscription has 'Ranyo Ashoka' (King Ashoka) under a stone portrait of Ashoka with his queen. {75}
- Kanganahalli Buddhist stupa: bank of the Bhima river, Kalaburagi district, Karnataka.
- Pre-Ashokan Brahmi script was found at **Anuradhapura** (Sri Lanka); also Piparaha, Sohgaura, Mahasthan. {68}
- **Bhabru (Bairat)** inscription proves Ashoka a Buddhist; only here he calls himself 'Piyadasi Raja Magadha' (king of Magadha). {81}
- **Kalsi** (Dehradun district, Uttarakhand) is famous for Ashoka's rock edicts. {82,83}

## Ashoka's edicts: contents
- Rock Edict I: no living being to be slaughtered or sacrificed here; earlier hundreds of thousands of animals were killed daily in the royal kitchen.
- Rock Edict I: now only three are killed — two peacocks and a deer (deer not always); in future not even these.
- **Pillar Edict V** bans killing of animals on certain days: 26 years after anointing, parrots, mainas, the arumna, ruddy geese and others were declared inviolable. {98}
- Pillar Edict V: fish not to be killed or sold on the three chaturmasis, the Tishya full moon (three days) and every fast day.
- **Major Rock Edict II** names the South Indian kingdoms Chola, Pandya, Satiyaputta, Keralaputta and Tamraparni; Satavahana is not named. {36,93}
- Rock Edict II also names Yavana king Antiyoka and his neighbour kings, and medical treatment for men and animals.
- **Rock Edicts II and XIII** tell of the Sangam kingdoms; XIII also names Chola, Pandya and Tamraparni. {92}
- Ashoka's 3rd edict names three officers: Yukta, Rajjuka, Pradeshika.
- Rock Edict XI explains the policy of dhamma: respect to elders, not killing animals, generosity to friends.
- **Rock Edict XII** = religious tolerance and synthesis, growth of the essence of all sects; one who praises his own sect and blames others harms his own sect. {85,89,90}
- **Rock Edict XIII** describes the Kalinga war and victory and Ashoka's deep remorse. {86,87,88}
- Rock Edict XIII: the way of conquest is dhamma, not war — the logical end of the process begun in Rock Edict I.
- Rock Edict XIII names five Greek (Yavana) kings friendly with Ashoka: Antiyok (Antiochus II Theos, Seleucid ruler of Syria), **Turmaya/Turamaya (Ptolemy II Philadelphus, Egypt)**, Antakini/Anikini (Antigonus Gonatas, Macedonia), Mag/Makmaas/Megaras (Cyrene), Alik Sundar/Aliero Sentro (Alexander of Epirus). {94,95}
- **Ashoka**'s edicts state: "All our people should do good deeds, be free from all disasters, enjoy joy with minds unpolluted by jealousy." {91}
! For this quotation UPPSC gave Harshavardhana in its answer key, but no proof is found in the sources; it is in Ashoka's edicts.
- **Rummindei pillar** marks the birthplace of Buddha; Ashoka visited Lumbini 20 years after coronation. {79}
- At Lumbini Ashoka remitted the Bali tax and cut land revenue from 1/6 to 1/8.
- **Sohgaura copper plate** (Gorakhpur, U.P.) = earliest known copper plate; Mauryan record with the earliest royal order to store food-grains for famine. {99}
- Mahasthan inscription (Bogura district, Bangladesh) also describes famine relief measures.
- Mauryan kings did not give land grants on religious basis; no peasant revolt against land grants is recorded. {101}
- Earliest epigraphic record of religious land grants is in a Satavahana inscription; such grants are prominent in post-Mauryan records.
## Mauryan pillars, stupas, caves, art
- **Sarnath Pillar** was built by Ashoka; four lions back to back on top = symbol of power; its replica is India's national emblem. {47}
- Ashokan pillars are the best example of Mauryan architecture; all Mauryan columns are of Chunar sandstone.
- Ashokan stone pillars are highly polished, monolithic, with tapering shafts; they are independent sculptures, not parts of architectural structures. {74}
- Lion Capital of Ashoka is at Sarnath, not Rajgir; Lothal = ancient dockyard; Sarnath = first sermon of Buddha; Nalanda = great seat of Buddhist learning. {148}
- **Sanchi Stupa** (Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh) is considered the best stupa architecturally. {48}
- Sanchi Stupa was built by **Ashoka**. {49,50}
- Sanchi is world-famous for stupas, monasteries, temples and pillars dating from the 3rd century BC.
- Ancient name of Sanchi = **Kakanam**: a 4th-century CE Gupta inscription calls it Kakanam Mahavihara. {51}
- Sanchi has 3 stupas on a hilltop: Stupa 1 is the main one; Stupa 2 holds remains of Ashoka's Dhamma Mahamatyas; Stupa 3 holds remains of Sariputra and Mudgalayan.
- A gateway (torana) of the Great Stupa at **Sanchi** shows Ashoka's pilgrimage to the Bodhi tree with his two queens. {53}
- The Great Stupa at Sanchi is one of the oldest and best preserved Buddhist stupas; its toranas narrate Buddhist events in symbols.
- **Bharhut Stupa** (near Satna, Madhya Pradesh) = fine example of **Shunga** architecture, built about 150 BC (also dated about 3rd century BC). {52}
- Bharhut stupa was discovered by Alexander Cunningham; it shows the people's movement in sculpture and architecture.
- **Amravati Stupa**: right bank of the Krishna, Palnadu district, Andhra Pradesh; traced by Colin Mackenzie in 1797 AD.
- **Dhamekh Stupa** (Sarnath, Varanasi) was first built in Ashoka's time; present form is of the Gupta period; built on a flat surface without a raised base.
- Another place prints: Dhamekh Stupa was built in the 'Gupta period 500 BC' to replace an earlier structure built by Ashoka.

- **'Sita'** = revenue from crown land. {122}

- **Pautavadhyaksha** = in charge of weights and measures. {125}

## Megasthenes' Indica: city and society
- **Indica** describes the municipal (city) administration of Pataliputra. {111,112}
- Pataliputra had a 30-member council in six committees of five members each; town officials were called **'Astynomoi'**.
- Sixth board collected sales tax: one-tenth of earnings; **tax evasion was punished with death**. {110}
- Third committee kept the register of **births and deaths** — still a municipal function today. {117}
- Megasthenes' **Indica** divides Mauryan society into **seven** classes: philosophers, farmers, herdsmen and hunters, artisans and traders, soldiers, overseers or spies, councillors and assessors. {105,106,108}
- **Megasthenes' Indica** states there was no slavery in India. {107}
- Megasthenes: no one could marry outside his caste or take up an occupation other than his caste's.
- **Megasthenes** said philosophers were of two types: Brahmins and Shramanas. {109}
- Brahmins: liberation through knowledge of Brahman; Shramanas: liberation through ascetic practice (shrama).
## Society, law and later texts
- Memorising chronicles, dynastic histories and epic tales was the profession of the **Maagadha**. {131}
- Village rule through autonomous elected panchayats was evolved by the **Dravids (Cholas)**; it was the base of their village administration. {132}
- **Manusmriti** (38th part) prohibits widow remarriage: a widower could remarry, a widow could not. {134}
- Manusmriti called foreign invaders who did not follow priestly customs (Shakas, Kushanas, Hunas, Pratihar) **'Fallen Kshatriyas'**. {135}
## End of the Mauryas
- Last Mauryan emperor = **Brihadratha**. {139}
- Brihadratha was killed by his commander-in-chief **Pushyamitra Shunga** in 185 BC (184 BC in one place), who founded the Shunga dynasty. {140}
- Pushyamitra Shunga was a fanatic Brahmin; he did two Ashwamedha Yajnas under the supervision of Patanjali, author of the Mahabhashya.
- Last Shunga king Devabhuti was killed by a plot of his Brahmin Amatya Vasudeva Kanva, who took the throne.
- Vayu Purana: last Kanva ruler Susharma was deposed by his Andhra servant Simuk (Sindhuk).
