@ 62 | Home Rule League Movement
## Two Home Rule Leagues
- During World War I Annie Besant and Tilak decided to put new life into the national movement; two Home Rule Leagues were started under them. {2}
- **Tilak** started his Home Rule League in April 1916 with headquarters at Poona; he used the term 'Home Rule' in place of Swaraj.
- **Annie Besant** started her Home Rule League in September 1916 with headquarters at Adyar near Madras.
- Annie Besant first spoke of the movement in her weekly newspaper **The Commonweal** on 2 January 1914; so she is taken as launching the first Home Rule League Movement. {1}
- Her programme covered religious freedom, national education, and social and political reforms, with self-government under the British Commonwealth as the objective.
- Joseph Baptista was the first president of Tilak's Home Rule League and N.C. Kelkar its secretary.
- George Arundale was the organising secretary of Annie Besant's Home Rule League.
- S. Subramania Iyer was associated with Annie Besant's Home Rule League; he founded it with her at Adyar.
- H.S. Olcott was not associated with the Home Rule League; he was related to the Theosophical Society. {5}
- C.R. Das was not associated with the Home Rule Movement. {6}
- Tilak's area: Karnataka, Maharashtra (except Bombay), Central Provinces and Berar; the rest of India was Annie Besant's area.
- Tilak's League was the most organised League.
- The two Leagues never merged; Besant: "Some of Tilak's supporters dislike me and some of mine dislike him. We however, had no quarrel with each other".
- The two Leagues were complementary; there was no dispute between Tilak and Besant and both continued to exist.
- Besant promoted her League through The Commonweal (weekly) and New India; Tilak through Mahratta and Kesari.
## Aim and nature
- For both, 'Swaraj' meant responsible government at central and provincial levels under the British Empire, with treatment like the white-ruled dominions Canada and Australia.
- The object was Home Rule or self-governance within the British Empire by all constitutional means, and to educate and organise public opinion for it.
- The movement aimed at self-government being granted to India within the British dominions during the war; it stayed within constitutional limits.
- The Home Rule Movement began a new phase of the freedom movement because it put a perfect picture of self-government before the nation. {8}
- Tilak and Besant toured the whole country carrying the message of Home Rule to the masses.
- The Home Rule Movement became popular and powerful in India during the First World War. {4}
- Tilak linked Swaraj with the demand for linguistic states and education in the vernacular languages.
- Tilak gave the slogan "Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it".
## Lucknow session, 1916
- The Lucknow session of the Congress in December 1916 gave Home Rule supporters the chance to show their strength. {7}
- A train was reserved to carry Tilak's supporters to Lucknow; some called it 'Congress Special', others 'Home Rule Special'.
- George Arundale asked Besant's League supporters to be present at Lucknow in huge numbers.
- Tilak's Home Rule League established a tradition to which the Congress was pinned for many years.
## Annie Besant
- Annie Besant (1847-1933), a theosophist, settled in India and worked for its liberation; she is mostly associated with the **Home Rule Movement**. {3}
- She was inspired by the Irish Home Rule Movement and launched her League on the lines of the Irish Home Rule Leagues.
- She started the movement to demand self-governance at all levels of administration; her League became active throughout India.
- She was arrested with her co-workers and released after widespread agitation against the government's action.
- Annie Besant was a proponent of the **Fabian** movement. {11}
- The **Theosophical Society** was founded in 1875 by Colonel Henry Steel Olcott, Madame Helena Petrovna Blavatsky and William Quan Judge; Annie Besant joined it in 1889.
- Annie Besant started the Home Rule Movement and was the first woman President of the Congress (Calcutta session, 1917), but did not found the Theosophical Society. {12}
- Annie Besant visited Bihar (Patna) twice in 1918: on 18 April to strengthen the Home Rule League's activities and on 25 July to understand the views of the Home Rulers. {13}
- On these visits she held meetings, increased contact with local workers and strengthened the League's organisational base.
- Annie Besant's league was the All India Home Rule League (founded September 1916); Tilak's was the Indian Home Rule League.
- In 1920 the **All India Home Rule League** elected Mahatma Gandhi as President; he changed its name to **'Swarajya Sabha'**. {14}
