## Gandhi's writings
- **Hind Swaraj** was written by Mahatma Gandhi. {27,56}
- Hind Swaraj: written in 1909 in Gujarati, by ship on his return visit to Cape Town (South Africa) from India via London; first published in Gujarati in 1909. {84,85}
- British Government censored (banned) Hind Swaraj; in March 1910 the Bombay Government proscribed its circulation.
- In reply to the ban Gandhi published his own English rendering, 'Indian Home Rule'; it was put into English to avoid the ban.
- In Hind Swaraj Gandhi compared the British Parliament to a sterile woman and a prostitute and commented on the working of the British Government. {60}
- Gandhi wrote his autobiography **The Story of My Experiments with Truth** in Gujarati. {82,83}
- **Gokhale: My Political Guru** by M.K. Gandhi: first published February 1955 by Navajivan Publishing House, Ahmedabad. {61}
- 'Gokhale: My Political Guru' is an English compilation of Gandhi's earlier writings and speeches on Gokhale.
- Gopal Krishna Gokhale: liberal nationalist and social reformer; Gandhi considered him his political guru.
- Gandhi translated **Anasakti Yoga** of the Bhagavad Gita into English and wrote Anasakti Yoga in 1929 while staying at **Kausani** (Uttarakhand). {29}
- **Songs from Prison**: Gandhi's English translations of Sanskrit hymns and lyrics from the Upanishads and other scriptures, made in Yeravada Prison in 1932. {114}
- Memoir **Bapu: My Mother** was written by Manubehn Gandhi. {4}
- The book 'Satyagrah' is by Vinoba Bhave, not Gokhale. {20}
## Tilak, Aurobindo and Lajpat Rai
- **Gita Rahasya** (Srimad Bhagavad Gita Rahasya): Bal Gangadhar Tilak's commentary on the Gita, in Marathi, written in Mandalay jail. {6,7,8}
- Bal Gangadhar Tilak lived 1856-1920.
- **Indian Unrest** was written by Valentine Chirol. {23}
- Chirol called Tilak 'The Father of Unrest in India'; Tilak sued him for defamation.
- Aurobindo Ghosh was jailed in 1908, where he studied spiritual texts; in 1910 he retired from politics and lived at Pondicherry (Puducherry).
- Aurobindo's works: **The Life Divine**, The Ideal of Human Unity, Essays on the Gita, The Synthesis of Yoga. {9}
- **New Lamps for Old** was written by Aurobindo Ghosh. {21}
- **The Foundation of Indian Culture** was written by Sri Aurobindo Ghosh. {65}
- **Unhappy India** was written by Lala Lajpat Rai; in it he compared Indian society with contemporary USA and Britain. {12,30}
- Printed wording: Unhappy India is by Lala Lajpat Rai, 'not Bal Gangadhar Tilak who chaired the Special Session of Indian National Congress (1920)'.

## Tagore
- **Amar Sonar Bangla** was written and composed by Rabindranath Tagore in 1905 during the Partition of Bengal (Swadeshi Movement). {111,182}
- Its tune was inspired by Gagan Harkara's song 'Ami Kothay Pabo Tare'.
- Bangladesh officially adopted the first ten lines of Amar Sonar Bangla as its National Anthem in 1972.
- Syed Ali Ahsan translated Amar Sonar Bangla into English.
- Tagore also composed **Jana Gana Mana**; he is the only poet whose compositions are national anthems of two countries. {112}
- In February 1919 Tagore translated the National Anthem from Bengali to English at **Madanapalle** (Annamayya district), Andhra Pradesh. {54}
- Bengali Gitanjali was published on 14 August 1910 (31 Shravan 1317).
- English edition of Gitanjali was first published in November 1912. {52}
- Tagore won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913; first Indian to receive it. {53}
- 'Gitanjali: Song Offerings' was the English book chiefly linked with the Nobel; it was Tagore's own English selection / recasting, not a direct translation of the Bengali Gitanjali alone.
- Tagore lived 1861-1941; Gitanjali is his work.

## World books and authors
- **Das Kapital** was written by German socialist Karl Marx, 19th-century economist and philosopher. {70}
- In Das Kapital Marx criticised capitalism (its dynamism and tendency to self-destruction) and gave his socialist principles, called Marxism.
- Marx's stated purpose: to lay bare 'the economic law of motion of modern society'.
- First part of Das Kapital came out in Marx's lifetime; second and third parts were edited and published after his death by his friend Friedrich Engels.
- **Hans Christian Andersen** wrote fairy tales: The Steadfast Tin Soldier, The Snow Queen, The Little Mermaid, Thumbelina. {146}
- **Silent Spring** was written by Rachel Carson, famous American biologist; it questions the use of pesticides. {151}
- **David Copperfield**: novel by Charles Dickens, published serially 1849-50 and as a book in 1850; semi-autobiographical. {180}
- J.K. Rowling, British author, created Harry Potter; **Cornelius Fudge** is the Minister for Magic in it. {159}
- **Elizabeth Hawley**, American writer, was known for writings on Himalayan expeditions, though she never did mountaineering. {201}
- **The Audacity of Hope** (2006) is Barack Obama's second book. {205}
- Obama's first book: 'Dreams From My Father'; third: 'Of Thee I Sing: A Letter to My Daughters' (2010).
- **The Challenge of World Poverty** was written by Gunnar Myrdal. {206}
- Karl Gunnar Myrdal: Swedish economist and sociologist; Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences 1974, shared with Friedrich von Hayek.
- 'In the Line of Fire' is by Pervez Musharraf, former President of Pakistan; its actual (ghost) writer is believed to be **Humayun Gohar**, son of Pakistani bureaucrat Altaf Gohar. {209}
- **Living History** was written by Hillary Clinton, former US Secretary of State and wife of former US President Bill Clinton. {216}
- **Spare**: memoir of Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex. {217}
- 'Spare' set a Guinness World Record as the fastest selling non-fiction book, on its release date 10 January 2023.
