@ 38 | Sikh Sect
## The ten Gurus
- **Guru Nanak** was the founder of Sikhism and the first Guru of the Sikhs.
- Sikhism has 10 Gurus: Nanak, Angad, Amar Das, Ramdas, Arjan Dev, Hargobind, Har Rai, Har Krishan, Tegh Bahadur, Gobind Singh.
- Guru Nanak died on 22 September 1539 at the age of 70.
- Nanak appointed **Bhai Lehna** as his successor; he was later known as **Guru Angad**. {1,2,3}
## Guru Angad and Guru Amardas
- Guru Angad, the second Guru, started (is the progenitor of) the **Gurumukhi** script. {4}
- 'Gurumukhi' comes from the Punjabi word 'Gurumukha' = from the mouth of the Guru.
- Guru Amardas divided the religious empire into 22 parts (branches) called **Manji**.
- Each Manji was under a Sikh; a local Sikh preacher was appointed at each place.
## Guru Ramdas and Amritsar
- Fourth Guru **Ramdas** established the city of Amritsar. {5}
- Amritsar district Gazetteer: Ramdas got from Akbar a land grant of about 500 bighas where Amritsar now stands. {6}
- Ramdas dug the holy tank and began building a temple in its midst.
- Some sources say Akbar gifted the land to Bibi Bhani, wife of Guru Ramdas.
- Some other sources say Ramdas bought the land from the zamindars of the villages.
- Ramdas died in 1581 A.D.; his son and successor Guru Arjan completed the temple.
- Guru Ram Das organised a network of Sikhs to collect offerings, called **Masand**.
- **Dasvandh** = practice by which Sikhs pay one-tenth of their income to the Guru.
## Guru Arjan Dev and Adi Granth
- **Guru Arjan Dev**, fifth Guru (1581-1606 A.D.), compiled the Adi Granth or Guru Granth Sahib in 1604 A.D. {7,9}
- Adi Granth has preachings of six Sikh Gurus and many Hindu/Muslim saints like Kabir, Baba Farid, Namdev and Raidas.
- Among the earliest compositions in the Adi Granth is that of Vaishnav poet Jaydev; the last added were of Guru Tegh Bahadur.
- Arjan Dev built **Sri Harmandir Sahib** in the middle of the Amritsar pond (lake).
- Arjan Dev founded two cities: Tarn Taran and Kartarpur (in Jalandhar Doab).
- Prince Khusrau revolted against Jahangir in 1606 A.D. and fled from Agra towards Punjab/Afghanistan.
- Khusrau met Guru Arjan Dev at Tarn Taran; the Guru gave blessings, shelter and some financial help. {8}
- Jahangir was angry, fined Arjan Dev and accused him of conspiracy.
- After he refused the fine, Arjan Dev was imprisoned and put to death by torture in 1606 AD: the first great martyrdom.
- Wrong pairs: Guru Amar Das – Miri and Piri; Guru Ramdas – Dal Khalsa; Guru Gobind Singh – Manji. Dal Khalsa was established by Kapur Singh.
## Guru Tegh Bahadur
- Guru Arjan Dev and Guru Tegh Bahadur were sentenced to death by Jahangir and Aurangzeb respectively. {10}
- **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (1664-1675 A.D.) was the ninth of ten Gurus, revered as the ninth Nanak.
- By his son Guru Gobind Singh's records, he resisted persecution and promised to protect Kashmiri Hindus.
- Aurangzeb called him to Delhi and offered him to abandon his faith and convert to Islam; he refused.
- Tegh Bahadur and his associates were arrested; Aurangzeb had him executed in public at Chandni Chowk, Delhi, in November 1675. {11}
## Guru Gobind Singh and Khalsa
- **Guru Gobind Singh** was the tenth and last Guru (last prophet) of the Sikhs. {19}
- Guru Gobind Singh was born in 1666 A.D. in **Patna** (Bihar). {13}
- His greatness: he formed the military order of the Sikhs, turning them into the military sect 'Khalsa Panth'. {15}
- Guru Gobind Singh founded the Khalsa Panth and called the whole Sikh community 'Khalsa'. {16,18,20}
- Khalsa Panth was set up on 13 April 1699, Baisakhi day, at Anandpur Sahib: more than 300 years ago. {17}
- He ordered each Sikh to take the surname Singh (male) or Kaur (female).
- He ordered the 5 K's: Kesh, Kangha, Kachha, Kripan and Kada.
- Guru Gobind Singh was assassinated at **Nanded**, Maharashtra, by an Afghan noble in 1708 A.D.
- Nanded Gurudwara (Huzur Sahib, 'Takhat Sachkhand Sri Hazur Abchalnagar Sahib') stands at his cremation site. {14}
## Banda Bahadur and Hem Kund
- Banda Bahadur's other (original) name was **Lachman Dev** or Lakshan Dev; this name was given to him by Guru Gobind Singh. {21}
- A famous Sikh Gurudwara is at **Hem Kund** (Chamoli, Uttarakhand). {12}
