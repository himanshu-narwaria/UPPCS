@ 59 | Morley-Minto Reforms
## The Act of 1909
- In 1905 Lord Minto replaced Lord Curzon as Viceroy of India; John Morley was then Secretary of State for India.
- The reforms begun by these two are the **Morley-Minto Reforms**, passed by the British Parliament as the **Indian Councils Act, 1909**. {2}
- The Act brought a limited increase in the involvement of Indians in the governance of British India.
## Separate electorate
- The biggest fault of the Act was **communal representation**: a separate electorate for Muslims. {3}
- Muslim members of the councils were elected only by Muslim voters, not by the general electorate.
- Separate constituencies were marked for Muslims.
- For the first time Muslims were recognised as a completely separate community in India; India's unity was demolished in one stroke.
- Reservation of seats and separate electorates for Muslims sowed the seeds of discord that later divided the country. {4}
- It set off the "cancer" of "Hindu-Muslim disharmony", which ended in the partition into India and Pakistan.
- The principle of communal representation was retained and pushed further by the Government of India Act, 1919.
- Gandhiji said: "The Morley-Minto Reforms have been our undoing. Had it not been for the separate electorates then established then we should have settled our differences by now."
- Rajendra Prasad considered **Lord Minto** the father of Pakistan, as the 1909 reforms introduced communal electorates. {5}
## Events of 1911
- The annulment of the 1905 Partition of Bengal and the shift of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi were both announced at the Delhi Durbar on 12 December 1911. {1}
- Administrative reorganisation (formation of 'Bihar and Orissa') became effective in 1912.
- The formal and gradual transfer of offices to Delhi was completed in 1912.
- The Lucknow Pact between the Congress and the Muslim League took place in 1916, not 1911.
