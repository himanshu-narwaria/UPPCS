@ 56 | Partition of Bengal and Swadeshi Movement
## Partition of Bengal: dates and people
- The resolution on the partition of Bengal was signed on 19 July 1905; BPSC took 19 July as the declaration date. {6}
- The British Government (Lord Curzon) announced the partition on 20 July 1905.
- Bengal was partitioned in the tenure of Viceroy **Lord Curzon** (1899-1905). {7,9}
- The partition formally came into force on **16 October 1905**. {4}
- Sir Andrew Henderson Leith Fraser, an ICS officer, was Lieutenant Governor of Bengal from 1903 to 1908; he played an important role in planning the partition. {10}
- Bengal was split into East and West: East Bengal merged with Assam to form a new province, 'Eastern Bengal and Assam'.
- Eastern Bengal and Assam included Rajshahi, Chittagong and Dhaka; Dacca (now Dhaka) was its capital.
- The second part included the Bengal province, Orissa and Bihar.
- Curzon drew the line so that the western part was a Hindu-majority area and the eastern part a Muslim-majority area.
- Among Permanent Settlement, Subsidiary Alliance, annexation policy and Partition of Bengal, the partition (July 1905) came last. {1}
- Permanent Settlement of Bengal: Lord Cornwallis, 1793; Subsidiary Alliance: Lord Wellesley.
- Doctrine of Lapse (annexation policy): Lord Dalhousie, Governor-General 1848-56.
## Reason for partition
- The British called the partition an administrative necessity; Curzon called it "a mere readjustment of administrative boundaries".
- The real reason was to weaken the growth of nationalism in Bengal. {17}
- Bengal was then considered "the nerve-centre" of Indian nationalism; Curzon decided to divide the politically advanced communities into separate provinces.
- One main objective was to divide the unity of the people of Bengal.
- Curzon: "Calcutta is the centre from which the Congress Party is manipulated throughout the whole of Bengal and indeed the whole of India ..... The whole of their activity is directed to creating an agency so powerful that they may one day be able to force a weak government to give them what they desire."
## Start of the anti-partition and Swadeshi movement
- **Surendranath Banerjee** led the agitation against the partition of Bengal (1905). {11}
- Surendranath Banerjee said the partition announcement "fell on us like a bomb (shell)". {12}
- **Krishna Kumar Mitra**, editor of 'Sanjivani', in a special edition of 13 July 1905 first suggested the boycott of British goods. {14}
- In his opinion the manufacturing of British goods should be abolished.
- He asked people to boycott British goods, mourn the partition and break all links with British officers and offices.
- Krishna Kumar Mitra was a freedom fighter, journalist and Brahmo Samaj leader; 'Sanjibani' was his nationalist Bengali weekly, in which he advocated freedom of the press.
- A public gathering at Bagerhat (then in Khulna district, Bengal Presidency) on 16 July 1905 supported his suggestion.
- On **7 August 1905** a historic meeting at the Town Hall, Calcutta began the anti-partition movement, one of the first mass protests. {2,3}
- The Swadeshi Movement was formally proclaimed at this meeting and the historic **Boycott resolution** was passed; boycott of British goods became national policy. {15}
- The announcement of the partition of Bengal was the immediate cause of the Swadeshi Movement, which began as a reaction to it. {5,21,23,24}
- Swadeshi and Boycott were adopted as methods of struggle for the first time during the anti-partition agitation. {19,20}
- It was an economic strategy aimed at the British Empire.
- 7 August is celebrated as **National Handloom Day** every year in memory of the Swadeshi Movement launched on 7 August 1905. {22}
- 16 October 1905 was observed as a day of mourning in the whole of Bengal; hearths in the houses were kept unlit.
- On Rabindranath Tagore's suggestion the day was celebrated as 'Rakhi Diwas'; people tied rakhis on each other's wrists as a symbol of brotherhood.
- In Calcutta most offices were closed and a strike was observed in some jute mills and railway workshops.
- **Vande Mataram** became overnight the national song of Bengal and later the theme song (rally cry, anthem) of the national movement during the Swadeshi Movement. {32}
## Leaders and spread
- Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Aurobindo Ghosh, Bipin Chandra Pal and Lala Lajpat Rai were the prominent leaders of the Swadeshi idea.
- They wanted a country-wide Swadeshi movement; the Moderates wanted it limited to Bengal and preferred constitutional methods.
- The Swadeshi Movement was not limited to Bengal; it spread across the country. {34}
| Region | Leader of Swadeshi Movement |
| Maharashtra (Bombay and Pune) | Bal Gangadhar Tilak |
| Punjab and Uttar Pradesh | Ajit Singh and Lala Lajpat Rai |
| Delhi | Syed Haider Raza {30} |
| Madras Presidency | V.O. Chidambaram Pillai {29} |
- **Ashwini Kumar Dutt** founded the **Swadesh Bandhav Samiti** to organise the masses for the movement.
- Ashwini Kumar Dutta, not Liyakat Hussain, led the Muslim peasants of Barisal in their agitation. {33}
- In 1889 the scheme of national education was formulated by Satish Chandra Mukherjee.
- The Bengal National College was founded in 1906 with Aurobindo as Principal.
- Tagore preached the cult of Atmasakti; its main plank was social and economic regeneration of the villages.
## Participation
- Women took an active part for the first time in any movement: they came out of their houses, joined rallies and sat in protest.
- Farmers and Muslims showed limited participation and stayed apart from the Swadeshi and Boycott movements. {31}
- Only some specific Muslims were influenced; the movement did not influence the farmers of Bengal, Barisal being an exception.
- The movement affected only the high and middle classes of society.
- It is also said to have spread to all classes and groups, including women, children, school and college students; this made the British reverse the partition order.
- People opposed British offices and institutes and boycotted foreign goods.
## Programme and results
- Programmes against the partition: Boycott, Swadeshi and National education (not Non-cooperation). {18}
- Swadeshi was complementary to Boycott: with the boycott of foreign goods, the use of indigenous goods grew.
- The movement helped set up Swadeshi industries: textile mills, banks, chemical works and insurance companies; Swadeshi stores were opened.
- National education and Indian languages were encouraged.
- The movement revived indigenous artisan crafts and industries; the **National Council of Education** was set up as part of it. {35}
## Annulment of partition
- At the **Delhi Durbar** on 12 December 1911 King George V annulled the partition of Bengal. {8,25,26,27,28}
- King George V and Queen Mary were presented to Indian dignitaries and princes as Emperor and Empress of India at the Durbar.
- The King also declared the transfer of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi.
- Bengal was reorganised as a new province; Orissa and Bihar were separated from Bengal.
## Tagore, Nevinson and art
- **Rabindranath Tagore** criticised parts of the Swadeshi movement (aggressive and exclusive nationalism, sole reliance on boycott) and pleaded for better relations between East and West. {13}
- He believed the West had made some mistake in understanding the East, but the East need not do the same with the West.
- Tagore denounced European imperialism and at times fully supported Indian nationalists, yet shunned the Swadeshi movement.
- He denounced it in his September 1925 essay **"The Cult of the Chakra"** (an allusion to elements of Gandhism and the Non-cooperation Movement).
- He called British control of India a "political symptom of our social disease" and urged steady and purposeful education instead of blind revolution.
- British journalist **Henry Woodd (H.W.) Nevinson** was associated with the Swadeshi Movement. {36}
- His brief: to "discover the causes of the present discontent and to report, without prejudice, the opinions of leading Indians as well as officials".
- He travelled across India for four months, reporting for the Manchester Guardian, Glasgow Herald and Daily Chronicle; he compiled the reports into the book **'The New Spirit in India'**.
- **Abanindranath Tagore** painted the famous image of **Bharat Mata** in 1905 during the Swadeshi Movement. {16}
- Bharat Mata is shown as a goddess with four hands in a saffron sari; the image became a symbol of nationalism and unity.
- Abanindranath Tagore and his brother Gaganendranath Tagore founded the **Indian Society of Oriental Art** in 1907, initiated by E.B. Havell. {37}
- Its aim: to revive precious Indian art and culture and build new consciousness among the masses.
- Abanindranath was the first Indian artist to gain international recognition and the principal artist of the society; his elder brother Gaganendranath was also an artist.
- He was a major exponent of Swadeshi values in Indian art and gave new life to the Indian School of Art.
