@ 51 | Political Organisations Established Before Congress
## Raja Ram Mohan Roy and the first bodies
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** was the first Indian to struggle for political reforms; guide of social reform and political movements in modern India. {11}
- Roy wrote about human rights and the growth of democratic values.
- Bipin Chandra Pal called Ram Mohan Roy the first Indian political reformer; some historians give this credit to Surendra Nath Banerjee.
- **Zamindari Association** (Landholders Society), Calcutta, 1838 = first political organisation in India. {1}
- It began organised political activity and constitutional agitation for redress of grievances.
- Its aim was to safeguard the rights and interests of zamindars (landlords); founded by Dwarkanath Tagore and fellow zamindars.
- **Bengal British India Society** was founded in 1843 by the efforts of George Thompson, whom Dwarkanath Tagore brought from England to organise the political movement. {2}
- George Thompson was president of the Bengal British India Society.
- **British Indian Association** was started in October 1851 at Calcutta; **Radhakant Deb** was its first president. {29}
- British Indian Association founder members: Rajendralal Mitra, Radhakant Deb (President), Debendranath Tagore (General Secretary) and Harish Chandra Mukherjee.
- **Hindu Mela** was founded in 1867 by Nabagopal Mitra, inspired by Rajnarayan Basu.
- Hindu Mela aimed to promote nationalist feeling and the use of indigenous goods among the masses.

## Poona Sarvajanik Sabha
- Poona Sarvajanik Sabha was one of the strongest political organisations of 19th century India.
- M.G. Ranade joined in November 1871 and gave it fresh vigour and fine leadership.
- In **1875** the **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** sent a petition to the House of Commons demanding India's direct representation in the British Parliament. {3}
## Indian Association and Surendranath Banerjee
- **Indian Association** of Calcutta was the most important pre-Congress nationalist organisation, with an all-India character. {7}
- Indian Association preferred the middle class over landlords; its purpose was to include everyone, not only middle-class people.
- Surendranath Banerjee passed the Civil Services exam in 1869 but was disqualified over a dispute about his exact age.
- He was reappointed after a court judgment, but was dismissed from the Indian Civil Service in **1874** for a minor judicial error. {8,9}
- He was a founding member of the Indian Association and the Indian National Conference.
- He published 'The Bengalee' to promote mass consciousness; he was Congress President twice.
- **Indian National Conference**, organised by Surendranath Banerjee, merged with the Indian National Congress in 1886. {10}
- The two merged because their objective, ideology and methods were alike; this helped Indian integration and the Congress.
- Indian National Liberal Federation is linked with Surendranath Banerjee; it held a liberal, pro-British view in politics.
- India League's purpose: promote nationalism and political education in India.
## Associations in England
- Dadabhai Naoroji founded the **East India Association** in 1866 in London to discuss Indian questions and influence British public opinion. {19}
- Bristol Indian Association was set up in September 1870; renamed 'The Indian Association' in October 1870; renamed National Indian Association in 1871.
- It was founded in England by Mary Carpenter, Keshav Chandra Sen and Elizabeth Adelaide Manning.
- This 'Indian Association' worked for women's education, so it was not a political organisation. {18}
## Bombay and other bodies
- Pherozeshah Mehta, K.T. Telang and Badruddin Tyabji are known as the **Trimurti of Bombay**.
- Pherozeshah Mehta became president of the Bombay Presidency Association; he urged Indians to take Western education and culture to uplift India.
- Also printed in one matching list: Bombay Presidency Association against Dadabhai Naoroji, and Poona Sarvajanik Sabha against Mahadev Govind Ranade.
- United Indian Patriotic Association = political body to build close relations between the Muslim community and the British Government.
- Servants of India Society was established by Gopal Krishna Gokhale in 1905.
