@ 34 | Mughal Administration
## Administrative units
- Akbar divided the empire into units for easy administration.
- Mughal district = **Sarkar**; each Suba (province) was divided into several Sarkars. {1,2}
- Sarkar was further divided into **Pargana** (sub-district) or **Mahal**; several villages made one Pargana.
- Chief officers of each Sarkar: Faujdar, Amalguzar, Qazi, Kotwal, Bitikchi and Khajandar.
- **Chakla** = territorial unit between Subah and Pargana, but not identical to Sarkar; used especially in Shah Jahan's rule. {18}
## Central officers
- **Mir Bakshi** = head of the military department (army) in the Mughal period. {3}
- Mir Bakshi was appointed by the leading nobles of the court.
- Mir Bakshi fixed monthly salary of soldiers by a signed certificate called **Sarkhat**.
- Provincial Bakshis worked under the control of Mir Bakshi.
- Mir Bakshi was in charge of intelligence gathering and recommended military appointments and promotions to the emperor.
- Mir Bakshi's duty: supervising land revenue officials, besides salaries of the military department. {4}
- Head of land revenue administration = **Diwan/Wazir**.
- Sir Jadunath Sarkar called Mir Bakshi Vetanadhikari (**Pay Master**); the Pay Master's duty was of Diwan-i-Tan.
- Mughal Empire was originally a military state; growth of its central government system depended on military power. {5}
- It is debated whether the Mughal Empire was a military state, but most historians accept Mansabdars had military duties.
- The Mughals had a large army which helped expand and strengthen the empire.
- **Muhtasib** = officer in charge (censor) of public morals; acted as police-cum-judge for canon law among Muslims. {8}
- Prostitutes were expelled from the city and settled at a place named **Shaitanpura**.
- Mint officer in the Mughal period was called **Daroga**.

## Manucci
- **Niccolao Manucci**, an Italian traveller, was appointed physician (doctor) in the Mughal army. {6}
- Manucci first joined Dara Shikoh's army as an artilleryman; took up medicine after Dara Shikoh's death in 1659.
## Ahadi and Dakhili troopers
- Besides soldiers of Mansabdars, there were two other types of cavalry: **Ahadi** and **Dakhili**.
- Ahadis = gentlemen-troopers: served singly, attached to no chief, recruited directly by the emperor as their immediate colonel; did not attach themselves to Mirzas. {7}
- Ahadis had their own muster-master.
- Ahadis were part of Akbar's standing army of a few thousand, had several horses and were known as excellent archers.
- Ahadis were single men with no following of mounted retainers.
- Dakhili soldiers were also recruited by the Emperor but kept in the service of Mansabdars.
## Mansabdari system
- **Akbar** introduced the Mansabdari system; it became the main basis of Mughal military and civil administration (official nobility of the state). {19}
- Mansabdari was introduced mainly to give clean and better administration. {9}
- 'Mansab' is an Arabic word meaning rank, position or status.
- Mansabdari ranked government officials and fixed their civil and military duties and remuneration.
- Mir Bakshi was responsible for smooth working of the Mansabdari system; most Mansabdars were recruited on his recommendation. {10}
- The Emperor himself appointed and promoted Mansabdars; he could raise, lower or remove a mansab and dismiss them at will.
- Mansabdari existed in the Mughal period; Mansabdars were selected on qualification (true, but not the reason for its existence). {20}
- Not all Mansabdars were military officers; high civil officers were also Mansabdars and graded accordingly; system was common to military and civil departments. {21}
- A Mansabdar could be asked to do any civil or military service.
- Abul Fazl mentioned 66 grades of Mansabdars, but only **33** categories were in practice.
- Lowest Mansabdar commanded 10 soldiers; the highest 10,000 soldiers.
- Mansabdars were paid in cash according to their grades.
- Salary due to the soldiers was added to the Mansabdar's personal salary.
- Mansabdari system was not hereditary.
- 'Zat' and 'Mansab' designations were granted and there were three classes of Mansabdars; Mansabdars were not hereditary officials and their salary was not paid by the Diwan office. {12}
- From his salary a Mansabdar met personal expenses and kept a fixed quota of horses, elephants, camels, mules and carts.
- Akbar introduced the dual system of **Zat** and **Sawar** in the 40th year (1591-95).
- Sawar rank could not exceed Zat rank (the normal rule). {11}
- Exception: for a Mansabdar serving in difficult terrain amid rebels, the state raised Sawar rank without changing Zat rank.
- **Mashrut** (conditional rank) = increase of Sawar rank for a temporary period.
## Jagirdars and Zamindars
- Under Akbar all territory was divided into two: **Khalisa** and **Jagir**.
- Khalisa revenue went to the imperial treasury; Jagir revenue was assigned to Jagirdars in lieu of cash salary according to rank.
- Jagirdars used the revenue for their salary and to meet their military obligation.
- Judicial and police functions were performed locally by Zamindars, not by Jagirdars; Jagirs were not hereditary while Zamindari was hereditary. {13}
- Jagir-holders were shifted from one jagir to another; transfers stopped them from developing local roots.
- Jagirs were transferable and could be seized too.
- Zamindars were present in practically every part of the empire and held the most significant position in the agrarian structure.
- Zamindar comes from two Persian words: Zamin (land) and dar (holder).
## Land revenue and grants
- **Land revenue** was the principal source of state income in Mughal India. {14}
- Central feature of the Mughal agrarian system: taking the peasant's surplus produce (above subsistence level) as land revenue.
- **Maal** in Mughal administrative vocabulary = land revenue. {15}
- **Madad-i-Maash** = revenue-free land grant to scholars (learned persons) and religious persons; also called **Suyurghal**. {17}
- Madad-i-Maash was given as charity to pious or worthy persons.
- Nobles gave Madad-i-Maash to support saints, religious institutions, religious places and upkeep of Mashaikhs; it showed composite culture and harmony.
- **Sadr (Sadr-us-Sudur)** inspected all donated lands and watched that donations were not misused.
- Donated lands were not transferable; the grantee had a right of inheritance over them.
- **Batai (Bantai)** = system of calculating revenue by crop sharing; real produce was divided between state and peasants. {29}
- Sher Shah Suri used 3 systems for calculating revenue: Nasq/Muktai/Kankut; Nakadi or Jabti; Galla Bakshi or Bantai.
- Bantai was of 3 types: Khet Bantai, Lank Bantai, Raasi Bantai.
- In Akbar's reign, land revenue in the Deccan was paid on the number of ploughs (per plough). {30}
- In North India under Akbar, Diwan Todarmal set up a systematic land revenue method; none such existed in the Deccan.
- The Deccan plough fact is given in J.N. Sarkar's book 'History of Aurangzeb', page 190.
## Tobacco
- The Portuguese brought tobacco to India; it soon became very popular among common people.
- **Jahangir** saw its harmful effects and banned tobacco by an order in 1617 A.D. {16}
## Coins
- Babur introduced silver and copper coins; his silver coin was called **Shahrukh**. {27}
- Akbar, like Sher Shah, tried to regulate the state currency; chief copper coin of Akbar's time was the Dam (true, but not the reason). {24}
- Under Akbar and his successors, gold coins, silver rupees and copper Dams circulated widely for trade.
- Three basic Mughal coins: **Mohur** of gold, **Rupee (Rupiya)** of silver, **Dam** of copper. {26}
- Dam was a copper coin largely created by Sher Shah (part of his tri-metallic system) and continued under the Mughals.
- **Akbar** issued coins with figures of Ram and Sita and the Devanagari legend 'Ram Siya'. {25}
- Jahangir put his own figure on his coins and Nur Jahan's name with his own on some coins.
- Some Jahangir coins showed him holding a cup of alcohol in hand.
- **Taza sikka** = coin newly minted in the current or previous year.
- **Chalani** (current) = coins issued and circulating in the reigning emperor's time.
- **Khazana** = coins minted in earlier reigns. {28}
## Canals
- **Shahab (Shihab) Nahr** was built by restoring Firuz Shah's canal Rajabwah. {31}
- In Akbar's time first Shihabuddin Khan and later Nuruddin Muhammad Tar reconstructed the canal.
- Shihabuddin Khan, Akbar's Governor of Delhi, repaired it for irrigation and renamed it Shahab Canal.
