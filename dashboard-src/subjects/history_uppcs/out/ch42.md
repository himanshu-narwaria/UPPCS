@ 42 | East India Company and Nawabs of Bengal
## Murshid Quli Khan and Alivardi Khan
- **Murshid Quli Khan** was the last Governor (Subedar) of Bengal appointed independently by the Mughal Emperor. {1,2}
- Mughal Emperor Farrukh Siyar promoted Murshid Quli Khan as Governor of Bengal in 1717 A.D.
- After his death his son-in-law Shujauddin became Governor; Mughal Emperor Muhammad Shah 'Rangeela' only ratified it.
- Murshid Quli Khan shifted his capital from Dhaka to Maksudabad and named it Murshidabad.
- Murshid Quli Khan started the Izara system in land settlement.
- **Alivardi Khan** (1740-1756 A.D.) compared Europeans with honeybees: left undisturbed they give honey; if disturbed they bite you to death.
## Siraj-ud-Daula and Black Hole
- After Alivardi Khan's death his grandson **Siraj-ud-Daula** became Nawab of Bengal.
- Black Hole tragedy took place in his reign on 20 June 1756 A.D.
- J.Z. Holwell, one of the 23 survivors: 146 English prisoners were put in a cell 18 feet long and 14 feet 10 inches wide.
- 123 of them died of suffocation; only 23 survived.
- Contemporary Muslim historian Ghulam Hussain does not mention this incident in his book 'Siyar-ul-Mutakherin'.
## Battle of Plassey
- Battle of Plassey, 23 June **1757** A.D.: Robert Clive's Company troops defeated Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula of Bengal. {5,6}
- Plassey (now Palasi) battlefield is on the banks of the Bhagirathi river in Nadia district, **West Bengal**. {9}
- Part of the Nawab's army was under three traitors: Mir Jafar, Yar Latif Khan and Rai Durlabh.
- Siraj fled from the battlefield to Murshidabad; this led to his defeat.
- Plassey began British supremacy in India and founded the British empire: the Company's first decisive military success. {3,4}
- Plassey gave instant military and commercial gains to the British.
- Plassey founded the British empire in India, but Buxar (1764 AD) actually established British superiority.
- Plassey set the ground for British political rule over Bihar, Bengal and Orissa, leading states of agriculture and handicrafts.
- After Plassey, Bengal was in British control; Clive placed Mir Jafar on the throne.
- In exchange Mir Jafar ceded the 24 Parganas, south of Calcutta, to the British and gave 2,34,000 pounds to Clive as personal gift.
## Robert Clive
- **Robert Clive** is known as the founder of the British (English) Empire in India; he sowed the seeds of British rule. {7}
- Clive was Governor (of Bengal) twice: 1758-60 A.D. and 1765-67 A.D.
- Clive signed (forced) the Treaty of Allahabad with Nawab of Awadh Shuja-ud-Daula and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II.
- The White Mutiny broke out among English soldiers during Clive's governorship.
- Plunder of Bengal's resources by the Company began in Clive's governorship; he set up a 'Predatory State' in Bengal.
- Clive was a statesman in the mask of a soldier; William Pitt called him **"Heaven-Born General"**. {8}
- K.M. Panikkar: from 1757 to 1773 AD the Company made Bengal a 'State of Robbers'.
## Mir Qasim
- **Mir Qasim** (1760-63 A.D.) was the ablest of Alivardi Khan's successors.
- He had proved his ability as Faujdar of Poornia and Rangpur.
- Mir Qasim shifted his capital from Murshidabad to **Munger**. {10}
- Reason: to stay away from Calcutta's constant interference and the intrigues of Murshidabad.
- He organised his army on the European model.
- He set up an arms factory at Munger to make tanks and matchlock guns.
- To improve Bengal's finances he fined embezzling officers heavily and introduced some new taxes.
## Battle of Buxar
- Battle of Buxar, 22/23 October 1764 AD: British defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim, Nawab of Awadh Shuja-ud-Daula and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II.
- The English army at Buxar was led by Major Hector Munro; Henry Vansittart was then Governor of Bengal.
- Buxar was the most decisive battle for establishing British supremacy in India. {11,12,13}
- During Buxar the Nawab of Bengal was **Mir Jafar**. {15}
- During Buxar Delhi was ruled by **Shah Alam II**. {14}
- Buxar made the English a great power in northern India and contenders for supremacy over the whole country.
- The area up to Allahabad came under the British and the door of Delhi was opened; Buxar changed the course of Indian history.
- Buxar confirmed the result of Plassey and set British supremacy over Banaras and Allahabad (Prayagraj) across the Ganges.
- Buxar completed what Plassey had begun and proved the British had no strong competitor.
- Sir James Stephen: "The battle of Buxar deserves more credit than the battle of Plassey as the origin of British power in India."
- Plassey was won by treachery; Buxar showed the superiority of English arms and military skill.
- Plassey made the English the sole controllers of Bengal; Buxar raised the Company's political prestige all over India.
- Also printed: Buxar is regarded as the first decisive military success of the English Company in India.
## Treaty of Allahabad and Diwani
- The **Treaty of Allahabad** (August 1765 A.D.) was signed after the Battle of Buxar (1764 A.D.). {16}
- By it Shah Alam II agreed to live at Allahabad under the Company's protection.
- Nawab Shuja-ud-Daula surrendered Allahabad and Kora to Emperor Shah Alam II.
- Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II**, by a farman of 12 August 1765, granted the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the East India Company. {17,18,20}
- Diwani = fiscal revenue collection authority.
- **Robert Clive** was Governor of Bengal when Diwani was granted. {19}
- In return the Company paid the emperor Rs 26 lakh a year as pension.
- Najm-ud-Daula, son of Mir Jafar, was accepted as Nawab of Bengal.
- The farman legalised the Company's political authority in Bengal; British rule then expanded all over India including Delhi.
- Clive started the **Dual Government** system in Bengal.
- The Company was neither willing nor able to collect revenue itself, so it appointed two deputy Diwans.
- Deputy Diwans: **Mohammad Raza Khan** for Bengal (Murshidabad) and Raja Shitab Rai for Bihar. {21}
- So all Diwani and Nizamat work was done by Indians but authorised by the Company.
## Shah Alam II and after
- Rohilla Sardar Ghulam Qadir took Delhi in July 1788; in August 1788 he ousted Shah Alam II and blinded him.
- In 1803, in Shah Alam II's time, Delhi fell completely to the East India Company.
- Shah Alam II and his successors Akbar II (1806-37 AD) and Bahadur Shah II (1837-57 AD) were mere pensioners of the Company.
## Khasi tribe
- The British came to Sylhet (then in Bengal Presidency) in 1765 AD after getting the Diwani of Bengal.
- The first hill tribe the British met after the Diwani grant of 1765 was the **Khasi**. {22}
- After the Burma War, David Scott tried to link Assam (Brahmaputra Valley) with Sylhet by roads through Jaintia and Garo hill areas.
- The Khasis of these areas resisted strongly (revolted) under **Tirot Sing**.
## Hindu officers of Oudh
- Raja Tikait Rai was 'Artha Mantri' (Diwan) of Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula of Oudh; certain Hindu castes usually held high posts under the Nawabs of Oudh. {23}
- Examples: Raja Tikait Rai and Raja Jhaulal under Asaf-ud-Daula.
- Nawab Safdar Jung appointed Raja Naval Rai as 'Bakshi' and 'Deputy Governor of Bengal' (as printed).
## Battles of the 18th-19th century
- Order of battles: Ambur (1749) → Plassey (1757) → Wandiwash (1760) → Buxar (1764). {24}
- Battle of Ambur (August 1749 A.D.), near Vellore: armies of Muzaffar Jung, Chanda Sahib and the French defeated and killed Anwar-ud-din.
- After Ambur, Muzaffar Jung became Subedar of the Deccan; Dupleix became Governor of all Mughal territories south of the river Krishna.
- The Nizam gave some districts of the Northern Circars to the French; a French army under Bussy was stationed in Hyderabad.
- **Battle of Wandiwash** (January 1760 A.D.): the British (East India Company) defeated the French. {26,27,28}
- At Wandiwash Sir Eyre Coote led the British; the defeated French commander was **Count de Lally**. {25}
- With Wandiwash the power of the French in India came to an end.
- Battle of Chillianwala, 13 January 1849 A.D.: Lord Gough led the British, Sher Singh the Sikh army; it ended undecided.
- Lord Dalhousie was Governor-General at the time of Chillianwala.
- Battle of Khurda/Kharda (1795 A.D.): Marathas defeated the Nizam.
## Maratha resistance
- The **Marathas** opposed the British most vigorously in India. {29}
- Marathas built their power on the ruins of the Mughal Empire; the British also used these conditions.
- Marathas were suppressed after three Anglo-Maratha Wars.
- The British lost the first Anglo-Maratha war badly; it ended with the Treaty of Salbai.
- Lord Wellesley trapped the Peshwa in the Subsidiary Alliance in 1802.
- Lord Hastings set up British supremacy after destroying the remaining Maratha strength in 1818.
