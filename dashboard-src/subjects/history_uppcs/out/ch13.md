@ 13 | South India (Chola, Chalukya, Pallava and Sangam Period)
## Sangam assemblies and Agastya
- In Sangam texts the terms **Kon, Ko and Mannan** were used for the **King**. {76}
- **Sangam** = 'Conference of Poets' or 'Forum of Scholars'; the literature written there, the oldest in South India, made of several anthologies of poems, is 'Sangam Literature'.
- Three Sangams were held under the patronage of the **Pandya** kings.
| Sangam | Place | President |
| First | Madurai | Saint Agastya (one table adds Tolkappiyar) |
| Second | Kapadapuram | Saint Agastya |
| Third | Madurai | Nakkirar {77} |
- Sage **Agastya** is credited with Aryanising South India; called **"Father of Tamil Literature"**. {78}
- Agastya was born in Kashi; believed to have gone south from Kashi at the gods' request and settled there.
## Sangam literature and Tamil texts
- **Tolkappiyam** = treatise on (Tamil) **grammar and poetry**; the surviving (residual) text of the 2nd Sangam era and the oldest available text of Tamil literature. {47,48,49}
- Its author **Tolkappiyar** was one of the 12 disciples of Saint Agastya; it is written in formula (sutra) style.
- Tolkappiyam has three parts of nine sections, dealing with **Ezhuttu** (letter), **Col** (word) and **Porul** (subject matter).
- **Silappadikaram** was written by **Ilango Adigal**; it tells the tragic story of **Kannagi** and her husband **Kovalan**. {50}
- Ilango Adigal: second son of Chera king **Nedun-ceraladan** (another passage: grandson of Chola king Karikal).
| Tamil text | Subject |
| Thirukural | Philosophy {51} |
| Tolkappiyam | Grammar |
| Silapadikaram | Love story |
| Manimekalai | Merchant story |
- **Kural**, a Tamil text by poet **Tiruvalluvar**, is called the ethical Bible of Tamil and the **Laghuveda** of Tamil literature; also known as **'Muppal'**. {62,63}
- By hearsay, Thiruvalluvar was an incarnation (advent) of Brahma.
- **Ramavataram** (Tamil Ramayanam) was written by **Kamban**. {65}
- **Thirumurai** = collection of writings of Saivite saints, seen as a Fifth Veda: twelve-volume compendium of Tamil hymns to Shiva, 6th to 11th century CE, by various poets. {52}
- Thirumurai, with the Vedas and Saiva agamas, forms the base of **Saiva Siddhantha** philosophy in South India.
- **Alvars** = 12 saint-poets devoted to Vishnu; **Nayanars** = 63 saint-poets devoted to Shiva; both of the South Indian (Tamil) Bhakti tradition. {53}
- Sangam literature is among the oldest Tamil literature; its poetry shows the social, economic and religious conditions of South India; the Varna classification was known to Sangam poets. {54}
- Sangam poems also show material culture, the warrior ethic and magical forces in nature.
- **'Vattakirutal'** = Tamil ritual of fasting unto death, common in the Sangam period: a king defeated in battle killed himself by starvation. {64}
- **Siddhas (Sittars)** of the Tamil region were monotheistic and condemned idolatry; **Lingayats** of the Kannada region questioned rebirth and rejected caste hierarchy. {66}
- Siddhas are traced to the Sangam period, but the word Siddha is not in Sangam literature; linked with Shaivism, Siddhanta philosophy, Tantra, science and technology in South India.
- Lingayats challenged the caste idea of certain Brahmin groups, encouraged widow remarriage and disapproved of pre-puberty marriage.
- **Halbi** = eastern Indo-Aryan (Aryan family) language of Bastar, Chhattisgarh; transitional between Oriya and Marathi; written in Oriya and Devanagari scripts. {46}
## Sangam kingdoms: Chola, Chera, Pandya
- Sangam literature describes the rise and growth of the **Chola, Chera and Pandya** rulers; the Kadamba or any other dynasty is not mentioned. {60}
- The three ruling dynasties of the Sangam Age were Chera, Chola and Pandya; the **Pallavas** were not rulers of this age. {61}
- **Chera** state lay in present Kerala (south-west) and partly Tamil Nadu; capital **Vanchi**, also named **Karur/Karuvur**; some sources say Thondi; Puducherry was not its capital. {82,83}
- **Udiyanjiral** belongs to the Chera dynasty; first historical king of the Sangam period; legend says he fed all fighters of the Mahabharata war. {72}
- India's trade with the Roman empire began with the **Chera** kingdom in the Sangam period. {92}
- A **Pandya** king of South India sent an ambassador to Rome in 26 BC. {91}
- Pandya dynasty had the longest reign in South India (about 200 BC to 1378 AD); capitals: **Korkai** (early) and **Madurai** (later).
- **Vaigai (Vengi)** river was the lifeline of the Pandya state; the Vengi river region was famous for fertility. {73}
- The Vaigai rises in the Varushanadu hills near Madurai, Tamil Nadu (another passage: Kandan Manikanur near Madurai).
- Pandya state lay south of the Cauvery: modern Madurai and Tirunelveli districts and some parts of Travancore (Kerala); capital Madura.
- **Uraiyur**, on the Cauvery in Tiruchirappalli (Tamil Nadu), was a key Sangam town and the major centre of **cotton** trade. {71}
- Uraiyur was also called **Koli** and **Varanam**; the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea calls it **Argaru**; it was the Chola capital in the Sangam era.
## Ports and Roman trade
- 'Periplus of the Erythraean Sea', the famous book of an unknown Greek sailor, lists the ports.
- Main ports of the western coast: Naura, **Tondi**, **Mushiri** and Nelisanda. {74}
- **Korkai, Poompuhar and Muchiri** were ports. {75}
- **Poompuhar** (Tamil Nadu) = prosperous ancient port city, also called Kaveri Poompattinam / Kaveripattinam; capital of the early Chola kings.
- **Korkai** = Pandya port city near the Bay of Bengal; shows developed trade with ancient Rome.
- **Muziris/Muchiri** = port city in Kerala, one of the world's oldest ports; mentioned in Sangam literature.
- **Arikamedu** (Pondicherry, south-eastern coast) excavations show brisk Indo-Roman trade in the early Christian centuries; a Roman settlement / trading station. {55,56,58}
- At Arikamedu an intaglio with a man's picture, identified as **Augustus Caesar**, was found, with beads and gems; many Roman objects came from here.
- **Dubreuil** called Arikamedu "a true Roman city"; the Periplus calls it **Poduke**. {57}
- **Amphora jar** = ancient tall jar with a long narrow neck and two handles; used in ancient Rome for wine or oil. {59}
- **'Yavanapriya'** (dear to Yavanas/Greeks) in Sanskrit = **black pepper**, a favourite of the costliest Roman cookery. {45}
- Pepper was the first spice used in Europe, with huge social and economic value; Greeks knew black pepper by the 4th century BC, as a rare, costly item.
- South Indian merchant guilds: **Manigramam, Nanadesi, Valanjiyar**; their aim was to encourage trade and business. {68}
- **Nanadesis and Manigrama** = traders' guilds, among the biggest trading bodies of the early medieval period; they traded with foreign countries. {37}
- **Sreni** = association of traders, merchants and artisans; **Nagaram** = local association of merchants.
- **Tagara** was an important trade centre on the route between Kalyan and Vengi. {38}
- **Motupalli** was the famous trading seaport of the Kakatiyas; Marco Polo entered the Kakatiya kingdom here and wrote of Andhra Desh's prosperity and power. {90}
## Chola rulers
- The medieval Chola empire was founded by **Vijayalaya** (850-871 AD) in the 9th century, after the gap following the fall of the Sangam Cholas. {1,2}
- One passage says the Chola dynasty was set up in South India by Parantak I.
- **Parantaka I** defeated the Madura/Pandya king Rajasimha and took the title **"Madurai-Kond"**.
- **Battle of Takkolam** (949 AD): Chola prince **Rajaditya**, son of Parantaka I, against western Gangas, Banas and Vaidunbas led by Rashtrakuta king **Krishna III**; Cholas lost and Rajaditya died on the field. {69}
- Chola golden era began with the accession of **Rajaraja I** (985 AD); he was the first Chola king to create a navy. {30}
- Rajaraja I destroyed the Chera fleet at Trivandrum (**Kandalur Salai**) in the 10th century and captured the Pandya king. {5}
- This feat is named in his inscriptions as "Kandalur salai kalam-arutta"; the Thanjavur inscription also records his defeat of the Chera navy at Kandalloor.
- Larger Leiden Copper Plates and Tiruvalangadu inscription say he captured Pandya king **Amarabhujanga** (or his chief general) and overran Madurai.
- After these wins Rajaraja took titles such as **"Mummudi Cholan"** (three-crowned Chola) and **"Pandya-kula shani"**.
- **Rajaraja I** invaded Sri Lanka, destroyed Anuradhapura and took only the northern part; the south stayed free. {31}
- **Rajendra I** took the southern part too, so conquered all Sri Lanka (Ceylon), and brought king **Mahinda V** of Sinhaldweep to the Chola kingdom as prisoner. {32}
- **Rajendra Chola I**, son and successor of Rajaraja I, is counted among India's greatest rulers and generals.
- Rajendra I defeated Pala ruler **Mahipala**, took the title **Gangaikonda Chola** and founded the new capital **Gangaikonda Cholapuram**. {28}
- The Chola navy was the strongest in the area; under Rajendra I the Bay of Bengal became a **'Chola lake'**. {27}
- **Chola Gangam (Ponneri)** = artificial lake built by Rajendra Chola I to mark his victory over rulers of the Ganga basin, incl. Bengal kings and the Kalinga dynasty. {29}
- Cholas beat Pandya and Chera rulers and dominated peninsular India; Rajendra I sent an expedition against the **Sailendra** empire of South-East Asia and took some areas, as trade with China was being hindered. {17}
- Tamil inscriptions: Rajendra I led a successful naval expedition in 1025 CE against **Kadaram** (Kedah) and **Srivijaya**, a maritime empire controlling the Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, Java and nearby islands. {33}
- The Cholas were famous for naval power: strong standing army and navy; conquests of Ceylon and naval raids on Srivijaya, Sinhala and Maldives. {25}
- In **Kulottunga I's** reign (1077 AD) a Chola goodwill (Buddhist) delegation of **72 merchants** was sent to China. {24}
- Ceylon king **Vijayabahu** declared Sinhala independent in Kulottunga I's time; Kulottunga showed no hostility and married his daughter to Sinhala prince **Virapperumal**. {34}
- Chola emperors usually chose their **'Yuvraj'** (heir) in their own lifetime; other empires also show emperors choosing the crown prince.
- Around 1250 AD the Hoysala and Pandya kingdoms defeated Chola king **Rajendra III**; the Chola dynasty ended about 1279 AD.
- **Malik Kafur** conquered South India about 1310 AD: Yadav, Hoysala, Kakatiya and Pandya.
- Chola territory stretched from the Tungabhadra to the Krishna river banks; it covered the **Coromandel coast and some parts of the Deccan**: Uraiyur, Kaveripattinam, Thanjavur. {6}
- Chola capital = **Tanjore**; later Gangaikondacholapuram too. {7}
- We know far more about the Cholas than earlier dynasties because they had inscriptions on temple walls telling their victories. {35}
- Rajaraja I began the practice of recording his ancestors' victories in inscriptions; Rajendra I and later kings followed.

## Chola administration
- The special feature of Chola rule was **autonomy of village administration** (local self-government, village assemblies); very systematic and well developed. {8,9,10,11,12,13,14}
- **Uttaramerur inscription** details the working of the executive committee of the Gram Sabha; each village had its own committee, the **'Sabha'**, usually independent of central command. {15}
- **Variyam** = working committee supervising the activities of Chola villages.
- **Thotta Variyam** = garden (horticulture) committee. {16}
- **Samvatsara Variyam** = annual committee; **Eri Variyam** = tanks (lakes) and water supply; **Pon Variyam** = gold/finance committee.
- Chola empire was divided into six provinces called **'Mandalam'**.
- A Mandalam was divided into **'Kottam'** or **'Valanadu'** (like commissionerates); each Kottam had many **'Nadu'** (districts).
- Assembly of a Nadu = **'Nattar'**; village association = **'Kurram'**; smallest unit = Gram Sabha.
- **Eripatti** = land whose revenue was kept for upkeep of the village tank; **Ghatikas** = colleges generally attached to temples. {36}
- **Taniyur** = a very large village run as a single unit (not a village given to Brahmins).
## Chola art and temples
- **Brihadeeswara temple** (Thanjavur), a fine Dravidian temple of Chola style, was built in **Rajaraja I's** reign; counted the largest and tallest temple in India. {4}
- Its huge **Nandi**, cut from a single rock, outside the sanctum, is believed the tallest (biggest) Nandi idol in India; idols of 'Dwarpals' stand around the main shrine. {3}
- Chola artists (**Sthapati**) were skilled carvers in stone and metal; they made more bronze than stone sculptures.
- **Bronze Nataraja (Shiva)** idols of the Cholas, found in large numbers, are among the best sculptures of the world. {19,20,21}
- Chola Nataraja images show Shiva with **four arms**. {18}
- Nataraja shows dancing Shiva as creator, preserver and destroyer; the dance is set in a halo.
- Nataraja's hands: upper right holds the **damru** (first sound of creation); upper left holds **agni** (fire that destroys the universe); lower right makes **abhayamudra**; front left points to the raised left foot = refuge for the troubled soul.
- Nataraja wears a man's earring in the right ear and a woman's in the left, a symbol of **Ardhanarishwara**. {23}
- **Dakshinamurti** idol shows Shiva as a teacher (Guru) giving knowledge to devotees; it is installed facing south. {22}
- Mighty gateways of South Indian temples are called **Gopurams**. {96}
- Dravidian style flourished under the Pandyas and Cholas; Pandya temples were small with many ramparts and grand, richly worked Gopurams; the special feature of Chola architecture is the Gopuram, not the temple.
- **Meenakshi temple** is at **Madurai**, the Pandya capital, where the 1st and 3rd Sangams met under Agastya and Nakkirar. {93,94}
- Meenakshi temple was first built by **Kulasekara Pandya**; the lotus-shaped city surrounds it fully.

## Pallavas
| Pallava ruler | Reign |
| Simhavishnu | 575-600 AD |
| Mahendravarman I | 600-630 AD |
| Narasimhavarman I | 630-668 AD |
| Parmeshwaravarman I | about 670-700 AD |
| Nandivarman II | 731-795 AD {84} |
- **Simhavishnu** took the title **Avanisimha**; he defeated the kings of Chola, Pandya, Singhal and Kalmra.
- **Mahendravarman I** took the titles **'Mattavilasa', 'Vichitrachitta' and 'Gunabhara'**; a great patron of art, literature and craft, and a scholar. {85}
- 'Vichitrachitta' and 'Gunabhara' occur in his inscription on the rock of the **Mandagapattu** rock-cut Trimurti temple.
- Mahendravarman I wrote the Sanskrit farce (comic play) **'Mattavilasa Prahasana'**, which gave him the title 'Mattavilasa'.
- **Narasimhavarman I** took the title **'Mahamalla'** (great wrestler); the famous Panch Ratha temples were built in his reign.
- Narasimhavarman I captured and destroyed the Chalukya capital Vatapi and took the title **Vatapikonda**; in 642 AD he sent two naval expeditions to Ceylon to help a Sri Lankan prince. {44}
- **Parmeshwaravarman I** took the titles Lokaditya, Ekmalla, Ranajaya, Atyantakama, Ugradanda, Gunabhajana.
## Chalukyas and Kadambas
- **Vatapi**, now **Badami** in Bagalkot district (Karnataka), was the Chalukya capital in the 6th-8th century (one passage: second capital, 6th-7th century). {41}
| Dynasty | Capital |
| Chalukyas | Badami {79} |
| Pallava | Kanchipuram |
| Harsha (with sister Rajyashri) | Kannauj |
| Pandya | Madurai |
| Chola | Tanjore (Thanjavur) |
| Hoysala | Dwarasamudra |
- **Pulakesin I** was the real founder of the Chalukya dynasty of Vatapi.
- **Pulakesin II** (610-642 AD) was the most able and powerful (greatest) Chalukya ruler; the Aihole inscription tells his achievements. {39}
- **Aihole inscription**, written by **Ravikirti** in Sanskrit language and southern Brahmi, names the poet **Kalidasa**: Ravikirti claims fame like Kalidasa and Bharavi. {42,43}
- Chalukyas did not rule North India; their rule centred on the Deccan and South India, capital Badami (Vatapi). {87}
- Pulakesin II of Badami defeated Harshavardhana in 618 AD; among smaller Chalukya branches the Chalukyas of Kalyani were most prominent.
- Chalukya administration gave women high posts. {40}
- **Vijaya Bhattarika**, queen of Chandraditya (brother of Vikramaditya I), issued two copper plates in her name, was a fine poetess, and ran the administration after her husband's death.
- Vijayaditya (another passage: Vikramaditya) granted a village to a Jain monastery on the advice of his younger sister **Kumkumdevi**.
- Chinese traveller **Ma-twa-lin (Matwalin)** wrote on China-India relations in the Chalukya period. {86}
- Chalukya king **Kumarapala** sent his minister to stop cruelty to animals in the Kashi region (Jain tradition). {88}
- Kumarapala reigned 29 years; a Jain and disciple of Jain scholar **Hemachandra**; several medieval Jain scholars wrote chronicles of him.
- Capital of the **Kadamba** rulers = **Banavasi (Vanavasi)**, also called Vaijayanti. {89}
- Kadamba dynasty was founded by **Mayurasharman**; Pulakesin II annexed the Kadamba state.
