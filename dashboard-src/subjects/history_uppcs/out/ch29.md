@ 29 | Humayun and Sher Shah
## Humayun: family and early campaigns
- Humayun = eldest son of Babur; born in Kabul in 1508 AD; his mother Maham Begum was related to the Shia sect.
- **Kamran, Askari and Hindal** were sons of Babur and half-brothers of Humayun; Usman was not his brother. {1}
- Gulrukh Begum = mother of Kamran and Askari; Dildar Begam = mother of Hindal.
- After Babur's death Humayun took the Mughal throne; he wanted firm control over northern India by subduing the Rajput states.
- In the 1530s Humayun sent his brother Kamran Mirza against Rao Jaitsi (Rao Jait Singh), independent ruler of Bikaner.
- By Beethu Souza's text **Rao-Jaitsi-Ro-Chhanda**, Rao Jaitsi defeated Kamran in battle. {3}
- Humayun invaded Kalinjar in 1531 AD and annexed Raisina Fort in 1535 AD.
- Humayun first invaded **Chunar Fort in 1532**; besieged it for four months, then Sher Khan accepted his supremacy. {11}
- **Sher Khan** offered Humayun to surrender Bihar and pay 10 lakh dinars yearly tribute if allowed to keep Bengal; Humayun declined. {4}
- After the fall of the Delhi Sultanate, **Humayun** was the first ruler to issue a gold coin; after him Sher Shah also issued one. {10}
! First gold coin after the Sultanate: UKPSC accepted Akbar as the answer, which is wrong; Humayun is correct.
## Humayun's battles
- Humayun's four major battles in order: Devara (Daurah) 1532 → Chausa 1539 → Kannauj 1540 → Sirhind 1555; Battle of Samugarh came later, in 1658. {2,12}
- Daurah (Devara), 1532 AD: Humayun defeated Afghan rebels (Mahmud Lodi) on the banks of the Gomti.
- Battle of Chausa, 26 June 1539 AD: Sher Shah defeated Humayun; a water fetcher named Nizam saved Humayun's life.
- The Chausa victory raised the prestige and power of Sher Shah (Sher Khan).
- **Battle of Kannauj**, 17 May 1540 AD: Sher Shah again defeated Humayun; Humayun then fled from place to place. {5}
- It is also called the Battle of Bilgram; Bilgram is an ancient town, now a municipal board in Hardoi district, U.P.
- Humayun occupied Delhi after the Battle of Sirhind on 22 June 1555 AD.
- Battle of Samugarh (1658) was fought between Dara Shikoh and his two younger brothers Aurangzeb and Murad Baksh.
- **Haji Begum** built the tomb of her husband Humayun at Delhi (near Deen Panah) in 1565–1572 AD. {13}
- Humayun's Tomb = first double-dome mausoleum of Mughal architecture; a garden surrounds it.
- Its architects: Mirak Mirza Ghiyas, a Persian, and his son Sayyid Muhammad.
## Sher Shah: rise
- Sher Shah was the son of an Afghan Jagirdar of Sasaram, Bihar; original name Farid Khan; given the title 'Sher' after he killed a tiger.
- Farid left home in 1494 AD and was educated at **Jaunpur**, then famous as 'Siraj-i-Hind' and a centre of education; Sher Shah was highly educated. {6,7}
- Sher Khan took the title **'Hazrat-e-Ala'** after defeating Nusrat Shah, ruler of Bengal. {8}
- After Chausa (June 26, 1539) he took the royal title 'Sher Shah', had the Khutba read and coins struck in his name.
- Sher Shah = founder of the Sur Empire.
## Sher Shah: reforms
- Sher Shah made revenue, administrative, military and currency reforms. {9}
- Sher Shah is considered the antecedent of Akbar as an administrative imperialist.
- Sher Shah = brave warrior and architect of a brilliant administrative system; his qualities as a ruler were more remarkable than his battlefield victories. {17}
- His brief reign of five years saw wise and salutary changes in every branch of administration.
- Revenue: he held that satisfying the peasants was essential for the empire's stability.
- His land revenue was based on Ryotwari, settled directly with cultivators; he used an improved, systematic method of measuring land and assessing revenue.
- Sher Shah introduced **'Patta' and 'Qabuliyat'** to help peasants. {30}
- Patta was given to the peasant by the central authority and stated the government's share; the peasant accepted it through Qabuliyat.
- Administration: he learnt it managing his father's manor; knew the Mughals' military composition, administration and finance.
- He headed the central government; administration was fully centralised before the victory over Bengal.
- He divided the whole kingdom into 47 Sarkars (districts), as per Dr. Kanungo.
- Bengal got a different system: divided into 19 Sarkars (as per Satish Chandra), each under a military official, the Shiqdar.
- A non-military official 'Amir-i-Bangala' was appointed to assist, to end the threat of revolt; Qazi Fajilat held this post.
- Military: inspired by Alauddin Khalji's reforms; recruited and paid soldiers directly.
- Each soldier's chehra (face) was recorded and his horse branded with the imperial sign, to stop fraud.
- Sher Shah built **Sadak-e-Azam** (Grand Trunk Road), one of Asia's oldest and longest roads, from Sonargaon (Bangladesh) to Kabul (Afghanistan). {31}
## Sher Shah: currency
- Sher Shah's reign was an era of experiment in coin history; V.A. Smith: "It is the basis of the existing British currency".
- His reformed currency circulated in the Mughal period and became the basis of British currency.
- Sher Shah introduced pure gold, silver and copper coins and abolished mixed-metal (beaten) coins; he issued the pure **silver 'Rupiya'**. {14,16}
- Silver rupee weighed 178 grains, of which 173 grains were pure silver.
- Copper 'Daam' weighed 308 grains (20 gm); one explanation prints 380 grains.
- Exchange rate of Rupiya to Daam = **1:64**. {15}
- Coins carried Sher Shah's name, title and mint place in the Arabic language and Devanagari script.
## Sher Shah: Marwar, death and successors
- Battle of Sammel: Maldeo Rathore lost to Sher Shah; his generals **Jaita and Kumpa** of Marwar fought hard with a few thousand men. {21}
- Their valour caused heavy losses to Sher Shah's larger army.
- Impressed by Rajput chivalry at Marwar, Sher Shah said: **"I would have lost the empire just for a handful of millet"** (bajra). {20}
- Sher Shah died on 22 May 1545 AD at **Kalinjar**, during the siege of the Rajput fort; it was his last campaign. {18,19}
- Kirat Singh was king of Kalinjar then.
- When all tactics failed he ordered the fort walls blown up with gunpowder and was seriously wounded by the explosion.
- **Islam Shah Suri**, second son of Sher Shah, was his successor and the second ruler of the Sur Dynasty. {32}
- Islam Shah ruled all North India for 8 years; divided his troops into units of 200, 250 and 500; Sikandar Shah continued this practice. {33}
## Sher Shah: buildings
- Sher Shah's tomb is at **Sasaram** (Rohtas district, Bihar); he was buried there. {22,23,24}
- The tomb is an imposing brick structure partly veneered with stone, standing in the middle of a tank; one of the noblest specimens of Afghan architecture in India.
- Sasaram tomb has an octagonal plan; begun in Sher Shah's reign (1540–45), completed August 16, 1545 in the reign of his son Islam Shah.
- Sher Shah built the Sasaram tomb and the Qila-e-Kuhna Mosque (not the Tomb at Sikandra or Jama Masjid). {29}
- Akbar's tomb at Sikandra (Agra, U.P.) was begun in Akbar's reign and completed in 1612 AD under Jahangir.
- Jama Masjid in Delhi was built under the patronage of Shah Jahan.
- Sher Shah's short reign = a revolutionary era in Indian architecture.
- **Qila-i-Kuhna** mosque, inside Purana Qila, Delhi, was built by Sher Shah in 1541 AD; famous for elegant architecture. {25,27}
- **Purana Qila** of Delhi, one of Delhi's oldest forts, was built in its present form by Sher Shah Suri (built by Sher Shah and Humayun). {26,28}
- Purana Qila = inner citadel of Din-panah founded by Humayun (1533); Sher Shah renamed it Shergarh and added structures.
- Sher Shah built the Qila-i-Kuhna mosque and Sher Mandal in Purana Qila.
