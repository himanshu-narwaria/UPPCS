@ 32 | Shah Jahan
## Family and accession
- Shahzada Khurram (Shah Jahan) was born in 1592 AD in Lahore; son of Jahangir.
- His mother = Jagat Gosain, daughter of Mota Raja Uday Singh, ruler of Marwar.
- In 1612 AD Khurram married **Arjumand Banu Begum**, daughter of Asaf Khan; she is famous as Mumtaz Mahal. {8}
- Shah Jahan took the throne with the title 'Abul Muzaffer Shihabuddin Muhammad Sahib Kiran-i-Sani'; crowned in Agra.
- Shah Jahan shifted the capital from Agra to **Shahjahanabad (Delhi)**. {12}
- Shah Jahan abolished the Persian (Iranian) court custom of **'Sijda'**, started by Balban. {26}
## Deccan, Qandahar and Balkh
- Ahmadnagar was included in the Mughal Empire in 1600 AD.
- Hussain Nizam Shah III, ruler of Ahmadnagar, was imprisoned in Gwalior fort.
- Nizam Shahi sardar Shahaji Bhonsle continued the struggle in the name of a child, Murtaza Nizamshah III.
- In 1636 AD Shahaji was surrounded by the Mughals in Chunar Fort; he handed over several forts and Murtaza Nizamshah III.
- Murtaza Nizamshah III was imprisoned in Gwalior fort; Shahaji took service under Bijapur State.
- **Aurangzeb** was governor of the Deccan twice under Shah Jahan: 1636–44 AD and 1653–1658 AD. {27}
- He held the post till he won the war of succession and became emperor.
- Map of Shah Jahan's empire: Golconda was not part of it; Aurangzeb captured Golconda in 1687. {1}
- **Qandahar** was the root of dispute between the Shah of Iran and the Mughals: strategic and economic grounds, and a prestige issue. {2}
- The expansion of both dynasties depended mostly on suzerainty over Qandahar.
- Loss of Qandahar was a big **strategic** blow; it exposed the Mughal kingdom to outside attacks. {3}
- Qandahar finally slipped from Mughal hands in Shah Jahan's period, weakening them on the North-West frontier.
- Motive of Shah Jahan's Balkh and Badakhshan campaign: remove the Persian representative and **secure a friendly ruler** in Balkh and Badakhshan, which border Kabul. {6}
- It was not to conquer Samarqand and Ferghana, the Mughal homelands.
- This would help control the rebel Afghan tribes near Ghazni and Khyber; it became a buffer state between Mughals and Persians.
- **Kohinoor** diamond was found in the mines of Golconda; **Mir Jumla** presented it to Shah Jahan. {25}
- Mir Jumla = Persian noble, reputed diamond dealer, minister of Abdullah Qutub Shah, Sultan of Golconda (1626–1672 AD).
- The Sultan, angry at Mir Jumla for accepting Shah Jahan's mansab, seized all his property.
- Shah Jahan sent Aurangzeb to attack Golconda; the matter was settled by a treaty.
- Mir Jumla was later called to Agra and given the title 'Muazzam Khan'.
## Literature and the golden age
- Shah Jahan's period is called the Golden Age of the Mughals for growth in art, literature and education.
- **Dr. A.L. Srivastava**, in 'Mughalkaleen Bharat' (1526–1803 AD), called Shah Jahan's regime the golden period of medieval Indian history. {24}
- This is true only on the basis of art and especially architecture; J.N. Sarkar and V.A. Smith do not accept it, but R.S. Sharma does.
- Elphinstone wrote that Shah Jahan's period was the most prosperous in Indian history.
- Two Persian styles prevailed: Indo-Persian (Indian style dominant) and Iranian-Persian (native Persian).
- Abul Fazl (Akbar's courtier) was the best promoter of the Indo-Persian style.
- Indo-Persian scholars: Abdul Hamid Lahori, Muhammad Waris and Chandrabhan Brahmin.
- Iranian-Persian scholars: Ameeni Qazwini and Jalaluddin Tabatabai; the Iranian poetry style was dominant then.
- Shah Jahan appointed the Persian-style poet **Kaleem** (Abu Jalih) as court poet (poet laureate). {7}
- Other Persian poets: Qudsi, Mir Muhammad Yahya (Kashi), Salim, Masih, Rafi, Farrukh, Munir, Shoda, Chandrabhan Brahmin, Hajiq, Dileri.
- **Kavindracharya** (Kavindra Acharya Saraswati of Banaras, scholar of Sanskrit and Hindi) was a dependent poet of Shah Jahan, who patronised him; led a delegation of Banaras pandits and got the pilgrim tax on Banaras (Kashi) and Allahabad abolished. {4,5}
- His language was a perfect mix of Awadhi and Braj; 'Kavindra Kalplata' was written in praise of Shah Jahan.
## Dara Shikoh
- Dara Shikoh, eldest of Shah Jahan's four sons, was the most educated scholar and writer.
- Among the given choices, Dara Shikoh was the first Muslim to study Hindu scriptures. {23}
- He translated Hindu texts like the Upanishads, Yoga Vashisht and Bhagwat Gita into Persian.
- Upanishads were translated into Persian in Shah Jahan's reign by Dara Shikoh as **Sirr-i-Akbar**; about 52 Upanishads. {16,19,20}
- **Majma-ul-Bahrain** is Dara Shikoh's original work and masterpiece. {21}
- Shah Jahan gave Dara the title **'Shah Buland Iqbal'**. {17,18}
- Lane-Poole compared Dara with Akbar for his tolerance and generosity.
- Dara was handed to Aurangzeb by a Baluchi chieftain, Malik Jeevan.
- A special judicial committee named by Aurangzeb declared Dara a heretic and ordered death; he was killed in 1659 AD.
- Dara's head was buried in the Taj Mahal complex, Agra; his body in the Tomb of Humayun complex, **Delhi**. {22}
! Many history books do not mention Dara's burial details; Delhi is the appropriate answer, as Dara's tomb is in the Tomb of Humayun complex.
## Buildings
- Shah Jahan gathered designers, engineers and architects from India, Persia and Central Asia to build the Taj Mahal.
- **Taj Mahal** = best example of Mughal architecture, blending Indian (Hindu), Persian (Iranian) and Islamic styles. {9}
- **Jama Masjid of Delhi** was built by Shah Jahan. {10,11}
- Shah Jahan's buildings: Diwan-e-Aam, Diwan-e-Khas, Sheesh Mahal, Moti Masjid (Agra), Khas Mahal, Musamman Burj, Nagina Masjid, Jama Masjid (Delhi), Taj Mahal, Red Fort.
- Like Akbar's Fatehpur Sikri, Shah Jahan founded the city of Shahjahanabad in Delhi and filled it with magnificent buildings.
- **Red Fort** (Lal Qila), Delhi, was built by Shah Jahan; the main building of Shahjahanabad. {14,15}
- Red Fort = irregular octagon with two long sides on west and east; western gate = Lahori Darwaza, southern gate = Delhi Darwaza.
