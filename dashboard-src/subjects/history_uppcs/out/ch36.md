## Baburnama
- **Tuzuk-i-Baburi**, Babur's autobiography, is also called Baburnama; written in the Turkish language.
- Baburnama was translated (from Persian) into English by **John Leyden and William Erskine**. {1}
## Gulbadan Begum and Maham Anaga
- **Gulbadan Begum** was the daughter of Babur; born 1523 A.D., died 1603 A.D. {2}
- Gulbadan Begum wrote historical records in the Mughal period: her book **Humayun Nama** records many historical events. {3}
- She wrote that she penned the history of Babur and Humayun on Akbar's orders; Akbar greatly respected her.
- Humayun Nama describes the battle between Humayun and Kamran.
- **Maham Anaga** built the Khair-ul-Manzil mosque (Mughal architecture), opposite the Purana Qila, Delhi. {7}
- Khair-ul-Manzil later served as a Madarsa; today it is also known as **Madarsa-e-Begum**. {6}
## Translations
- Hitopadesha = collection of Sanskrit fables in prose and verse.
- Hitopadesha was translated into Persian by **Taj-ul-Din Mufti al-Maliki** (Tajul Mali) as 'Mufarrih-al-Qubal'. {8}
- Panchatantra translations: Nasrullah Munshi Shirazi → 'Kalila-wa-Dimnah'; Mulla Husain Waiz Kashifi → **Anwar-i-Suhaili**. {19}
- Abul Fazl translated the Sanskrit Panchatantra into Persian as **Ayar-i-Danish**.
- Akbar set up a translation department under the supervision of poet laureate Faizi.
- Akbar's translation department translated works of Sanskrit, Arabic, Turkish, Greek and many other languages.
- **Yogavasistha** was translated into Persian by Nizamuddin Panipati in the reign of Akbar. {20}
## Akbarnama and Ain-i-Akbari
- Akbarnama was written by Abul Fazl, one of Akbar's nine gems, from 1590 to 1596 A.D. (seven years). {21}
! Some books say Akbarnama took more than 7 years (12 or 13 years); so UPPSC did not evaluate this question.
- Akbarnama has three volumes: first = ancestors of Akbar; second = Akbar's own reign; third = **Ain-i-Akbari**. {23}
- Ain-i-Akbari, in Persian by court historian Abul Fazl, gives Akbar's administration, court, army, revenue and geography.
## Shah Jahan's historians
- **Abdul Hamid Lahori** was an official historian of Shah Jahan's reign. {15}
- Lahori wrote Padshahnama (Badshahnama), the history of Shah Jahan; he joined Shah Jahan's court as a traveller.
- Shahjahannama (Chronicles of Shah Jahan) was written by Inayat Khan in the 17th century.
- Shahjahannama was translated into English by A.R. Fuller in the 19th century A.D.
## Language and script
- **Persian** was the court, official and administrative language of the Mughals. {24,25}
- Urdu was the language of princely courts such as Delhi and Lucknow.
- Akbar was a protector of many languages.
- **Nastaliq** was a Persian script used in medieval India in the Mughal period. {29}
- Aurangzeb was an expert in writing the Nastaliq script.
## Hindi literature
- Akbar's tolerance and balanced attitude let Hindi grow; his reign is called the golden era of Hindi poetry.
- Both Hindus and Muslims had equal rights on Hindi.
- **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khana** was the Muslim scholar with the most significant contribution to Hindi literature. {14}
- Rahim was a master of Arabic, Persian, Turkish, Sanskrit, Hindi and Rajasthani; 'Rahim Satsai' is his famous collection of couplets.
- **Nagri Das** (Servant of Radha) was the pen-name of Raja Sawant Singh (17th-18th century) of Kishangarh state, Ajmer. {30}
- Sawant Singh wrote many verses in praise of Lord Krishna; Kishangarh rulers patronised the Nimbarka sect.
- **Keshavdas** (1555-1617 A.D.) wrote 'Ramachandrika' and 'Rasik-Priya'. {31,32}
- Keshavdas was a Sanskrit scholar and Hindi poet; Rasik Priya is a pioneering work of the Ritikaal of Hindi literature.
- Works of Keshavdas: Ramachandrika, Rasik Priya, Ratan Bavani, Kavipriya, Chhandamala.
- Ram Rasayan, Ganga Lahari and Prabodh Pachasa are works of **Padmakar**, not Keshavdas. {33}
