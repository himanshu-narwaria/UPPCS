@ 19 | Tughluq Dynasty
## Ghiyasuddin Tughluq
- **Ghiyasuddin Tughluq (Ghazi Malik)**, a general of Alauddin Khalji, founded the **Tughluq dynasty** and was its first ruler. {1,2}
- His mother was a Hindu Jat woman; his father was a Qaraunah Turk, a slave of Balban.
- He led several important campaigns in Alauddin's reign and was governor of Multan and Dipalpur.
- He earned the epithet **'Ghazi Malik'** for repeated frontier fights against the Mongols: 20 routs per Barani, 29 victories per Ibn Battuta.
- He took Delhi's throne by defeating (assassinating) Khusrau Shah and became Sultan in 1320 AD.
- He was also called Ghazi Beg Tughluq or Ghazi Tughluq; so his successors were called 'Tughluq'.
- Under him the land tax was fixed at 1/10 or 1/11 of the yield; in famine, tax was taken according to capacity to pay.
- He took steps to improve the condition of farmers and restored the old rights of Khuts and Muqaddams.
- Ghiyasuddin Tughluq is credited as the first ruler of the 14th century to dig **canals** for agriculture. {3}
- Tax-free jagirs were given to government employees without any share in the revenue.
- Harsh punishments of Alauddin's time were abolished, but tax defaulters, those dishonest with government money and thieves were severely punished.
- Dynasty spans: Slave 1206–1290; Khalji 1290–1320; Tughluq 1320–1412; Lodi 1451–1526 — the **Tughluqs ruled longest**. {4}
## Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq: learning, agriculture, capital
- **Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq** (1325–1351 A.D.) = most learned and educated Sultan of Delhi; versed in astronomy, mathematics and medicine. {5}
- He set up a new department, **Diwan-i-Amir-i-Kohi (Diwan-i-Kohi)**, for the advancement of agriculture; crops were grown by rotation. {6,7,8,9}
- Its work: give more support to farmers and bring more land under cultivation; a tract of about 30 karohs × 30 karohs was chosen.
- He moved the capital from Delhi to **Daulatabad (Devgiri)** — one of his most important experiments. {10,11}
- Reasons given: Devgiri was probably at the centre of the empire (Barani); to punish Delhi's people who wrote undeferential letters to the Sultan (Ibn Battuta).
- He was the first Sultan of Delhi who tried to bring administrative and cultural unity of north and south India — probably the main reason.
- Dr. K.A. Nizami: Qutbuddin Mubarak Khalji named Devgiri Qutbabad; Muhammad Tughluq named it Daulatabad.
- Barani's five major policies of Muhammad Tughluq: tax increase in Doab; Devgiri (Daulatabad) as capital; token currency; planned Khurasan expedition; Qarachil expedition.
## Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq: coins and token currency
- **Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq** was the first to introduce **token currency** in India. {12,13}
- Per Barani, lack of silver in the treasury and the policy of expanding the empire forced the token currency.
- Token currency was tried unsuccessfully in Iran under Gaykhatu and successfully under Kublai Khan; Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq was inspired by them.
- Modern historians note a worldwide shortage of silver, severe in India too.
- His new gold coins were called **'Dinar'** by Ibn Battuta; his token currency was in copper or bronze, to promote military power, not gold tokens for trade with West Asia and North Africa. {14}
- The token currency failed because the state had no control over minting; many fraud mints came up and Lagan was paid in forged coins. {15}
- The token coin's value was equal to the silver coin.
- His coins carried 'Al Sultan Zilli Allah' (Sultan is the shadow of God) and 'God is the supporter of Sultan'.
## Ibn Battuta
- **Ibn Battuta** (1304–77 AD), African traveller of **Moroccan** origin, resident of the Tanzir region of Morocco. {17}
- He came to India during 1333–42 AD in the reign of **Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq**. {16,18}
- Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq made him Qazi of Delhi; in 1342 AD he was sent to China as the Sultan's ambassador.
- His travel book = **Kitab-ul-Rihla**.
- A detailed description of the Sultanate **postal system** comes from Ibn Battuta's travelogue. {19}
## Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq: society and death
- Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq was the first Sultan of Delhi to join Hindu festivals, mainly **Holi** (Isami accuses him of this). {21}
- He appointed non-Turks and Indian Muslims to government posts; Barani harshly criticized him for promoting low-born people like gardener, weaver, barber, cook.
- While he was at Daulatabad, Taghi led a revolt in Gujarat; Taghi was defeated and fled to Sindh.
- Chasing Taghi to Sindh, the Sultan fell sick on the way and died on March 20, 1351 near Thatta.
- On Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq's death **Badayuni** wrote in "Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh": "The king was freed from his people, and the people from their king". {22,23}
## Firuz Shah Tughluq: welfare
- **Firuz Tughluq**, cousin of Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq, ruled after him (1351–1388 A.D.).
- **Firuz Shah Tughluq** opened an employment office: jobs were given after checking each person's qualifications and merit, to solve unemployment. {24,25}
- He set up **'Diwan-i-Khairat'**: money help to poor Muslims, orphans, women and widows, and marriages of poor Muslim girls. {26}
- Firuz donated property to saints and religious people.
- Firuz Tughluq = first Indian ruler to arrange **Hajj** at state expense. {39}
- **'Dar-ul-Shafa/Shifa'** = charitable (free) hospital set up by Firuz, with skilled Hakims. {40}
- Firuz was very fond of slaves: about **1,80,000 slaves**; he set up a separate department **'Diwan-i-Bandagan'** for them. {27,28}
- He attended to slaves' education; each slave got 10 to 100 Tankas and sometimes estates; this hobby proved harmful to the state.
- Balban set up 'Diwan-i-Arz' for a large standing army against the Mongol threat; his honest, hardworking commander Imad-ul-Mulk, freed from the Wazir's financial control, deserves credit for the army's good arrangement.
- The branding system of horses was introduced by Alauddin, not Balban.
- Firuz set up a **'Translation Department'** so that Hindus and Muslims could exchange ideas better; some Sanskrit texts were put into Persian. {37}
- In the Nagarkot expedition Firuz collected about 300 Sanskrit manuscripts from the Jawalamukhi temple library; **Azizuddin Khan** translated them into Persian. {38}
- Firuz levied **Jizya** (religious tax on non-Muslims) on **Brahmins**, who had been exempt till then. {33}
## Firuz Shah Tughluq: public works
- **Firuz Shah Tughluq** established the **Public Works Department**. {29}
- He is said to have built 200 new cities; prominent: Fatehabad, Hisar, Firozpur, Jaunpur, Firozabad.
- Per Firishta, Firuz built 40 mosques, 30 schools, 20 palaces, 100 inns, 200 towns, 100 hospitals, 5 tombs (Makabara), 100 public baths, 10 pillars and 150 bridges.
- In his Bengal campaign he renamed Ikdala as Azadpur and Pandua as Firuzabad.
- His chief architect = **Malik Ghazi Shahana**.
- Each building's plan with cost estimate was put before the 'Diwan-i-Wajarat' before money was released.
- Firuz Tughluq built the **largest network of canals** in India. {30,31}
- His five canals: Sutlej to Ghaggar (96 miles); Yamuna to Hisar (150 miles); Sirmor hill to Hansi; Ghaggar to Firozabad; Yamuna to Firozabad.
- He dug 150 wells for irrigation and travellers; per Firishta he built 50 dams and 30 lakes.
- Firuz = first Sultan of Delhi to levy the irrigation tax **'Haqq-i-Sharb'**, after taking the Ulema's opinion; 1/10 of produce from users of royal canals. {32}
- Firuz planted 1200 new gardens near Delhi, restored 30 old orchards of Alauddin's time and took steps to improve the **quality of fruits**. {34}
- Firuz focused on maintaining and rebuilding the buildings of his predecessors.
- **Firuz Shah Tughluq** brought two **Ashoka pillars** to Delhi from Meerut and Topra (now in Yamuna Nagar district, Haryana). {35,36}
- The Topra pillar was re-set near the palace and mosque of Firozabad; the Meerut pillar near the present Bara Hindu Rao Hospital, Delhi, by the mound 'Kashke-Shikar'.
## End of the Tughluqs and Timur
- **Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah** (1394–1412 AD) = last ruler of the Tughluq dynasty. {41}
- In his reign Khwaja Jahan founded the independent state of Jaunpur.
- Nusrat Shah, another grandson (also called son) of Firuz, challenged Nasiruddin; the dynasty split: Nasiruddin in Delhi, Nusrat Shah in Firozabad.
- Famous saying on Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah: 'The sultanate of Shahanshah extends from Delhi to Palam'.
- **Timur**, Turco-Mongol general of Central Asia, invaded India in **1398 AD**, in the reign of Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah. {43}
- Timur's stated aims: fight and destroy the Kafirs and plunder wealth.
- Before leaving India, Timur appointed Khizr Khan Subedar of Multan, Dipalpur and Lahore.
- Timur's invasion destroyed Delhi and began the fall of the Tughluqs; the dynasty ended with Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah's death in 1412 AD.
- Delhi's warlords elected Daulat Khan Sultan in 1413 AD; Khizr Khan defeated him.
- In 1414 AD Khizr Khan took Delhi and founded the **Sayyid dynasty**, which ruled after Timur's invasion; he did not take the title Sultan. {44}
- Order: Rukn-ud-din (1236 A.D.) → Mubarak Khan (1316–1320) → Firuz Shah Tughluq (1351–1388) → Alam Shah (1445–1451). {45}
- Rukn-ud-din became ruler in 1236 AD after Iltutmish, though Iltutmish had named Raziyya as successor.
- Mubarak Khan became Sultan as Mubarak Khalji in 1316; Alam Shah was of the Sayyid dynasty.
## Kashmir
- **Yousuf Shah Chak**, last Muslim ruler of the Kashmir valley, was exiled to Bihar by Akbar; he died in Puri in 1592 AD and was buried in **Nalanda**. {20}
