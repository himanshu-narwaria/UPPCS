@ 48 | Development of Education in Modern India
## Early British attitude and oriental learning
- In the early phase of their rule, the British showed no interest in the advancement of learning in India.
- Some Britishers took interest in spreading education through personal effort and for political gain.
- **Warren Hastings** = Governor-General of Bengal 1774–85; a prolific scholar and staunch supporter of oriental learning.
- Warren Hastings encouraged the study of Sanskrit, Persian and Arabic.
- **Calcutta Madarsa** = first Madarsa set up by the British in India; founded by Warren Hastings in 1780 for Muslim law and related subjects. {1,38}
- Mulla Majduddin = first principal (Najin) of the Calcutta Madarsa.
- Calcutta Madarsa taught Farsi, Arabic and Muslim law; its graduates worked as interpreters in the British Raj.
- **Sanskrit College**, Benaras (Varanasi) = founded 1791 by **Jonathan Duncan**, the Resident at Benaras, for Hindu law, philosophy and literature. {3}
- Sanskrit College at Benaras was not founded by William Jones.
- Charles Wilkins, Nathaniel Halhed and Jonathan Duncan contributed to oriental studies under Warren Hastings' patronage and support.
- Oriental education got a foothold by the efforts of Wilkins, Jones, Colebrook and William Carey.
- Halhed published a Bengali grammar, 'A Grammar of the Bengal Language', in 1778.
- **Sir Charles Wilkins** = first Englishman to translate the Bhagavad Gita into English; Warren Hastings wrote its preface. {6}
## Asiatic Society
- Sir William Jones was appointed Judge of the Supreme Court at Calcutta during Warren Hastings' tenure (also called Justice of Calcutta High Court).
- **Asiatic Society of Bengal** = founded at Calcutta in 1784 by **Sir William Jones**, who became its first President. {2}
- The Society was set up with Warren Hastings' cooperation; Hastings declined its Presidentship in favour of Sir William Jones, as he was himself a scholar and Orientalist. {4}
! On Hastings declining the Presidentship, the Commission's answer held that the reason does not explain the assertion; the book holds that the reason (Hastings was a scholar and Orientalist) correctly explains it.
- The Society tried to throw light on India's past through its publication **Asiatick Researches**.
- At the Society, research on oriental education and culture began; British civilians were taught Indian languages, laws, customs, religion and geography.
- Sir William Jones was the first to translate Kalidasa's **Abhigyan Shakuntalam** into English, in 1789. {7}
- **Raja Rammohan Roy** was offered membership of La Societe Asiatique (Asiatic Society of Paris), which was established in 1822. {5}
- Dadabhai Naoroji = Liberal Party MP in the UK House of Commons 1892–1895; first Asian to be a British MP.
- Raja Ram Mohan Roy founded the Brahmo Sabha movement in 1828, which gave rise to Brahmo Samaj, a Bengali socio-religious reform movement.
- Swami Vivekananda represented India at the Parliament of the World's Religions, United States, 1893; founded Ramakrishna Mission in 1897.
## Fort William College
- **Fort William College**, Calcutta (Kolkata) = founded on 10 July 1800 (not 10 June) by Governor-General Lord (Richard) Wellesley; not by Arthur Wellesley.
- Its purpose = to train and teach British civilians (civil servants) for administration in India, in Indian languages: Persian and Hindustani. {39,40,41}
## Reason for modern education and Charter Act 1813
- Main reason the British spread modern education = need for educated Indians in minor administrative posts, to reduce expenditure on administration. {8}
- **Charter Act 1813** = first to allot not less than Rs. 1 lakh per year for education of Indians. {9}
- Charter Act 1813 = Act of the UK Parliament that renewed the East India Company's charter and continued Company rule in India.
- Charter Act 1813 ended the Company's commercial monopoly except the tea trade and trade with China.
## Orientalist–Anglicist controversy and Macaulay
- In 1823 the Governor-General in Council appointed the **General Committee of Public Instruction**, responsible for granting the one lakh rupees for education.
- English education was introduced by three things: Charter Act 1813, General Committee of Public Instruction 1823, and the Orientalist–Anglicist controversy. {27}
- The Committee had 10 members with Lord Macaulay as President.
- Attitudes shifted towards English education mainly due to the missionaries and the political influence of the English language.
- The controversy over the medium of instruction went on for 12 years.
- Committee was split: 5 members were Anglicists (English medium) and 5 were Orientalists (classical languages), so no decision by vote.
- William Bentinck appointed **Macaulay**, Law Member of the Governor-General's Executive Council, as head of the Committee of Public Instruction to report on the medium dispute.
- Macaulay advocated English education: European literature and science through English medium. {21}
- **Macaulay's Minute** (issued 2 February 1835) laid the foundation of the modern education system in India. {24,25}
- Macaulay held Indian learning inferior to European: "A single shelf of a good European library was worth the whole literature of India and Arabia."
- Macaulay suggested English as the language of higher education with Western literature as the subject of study.
- Macaulay's view: the Indian public mind might expand under the English system and be educated into a capacity for better government.
- English education was introduced under **Lord William Bentinck**, who accepted Macaulay's proposal on 7 March 1835; English became the medium of Indian education. {28}
- Resolution of 7 March 1835: the great object of the British Government = promotion of European literature and science; all education funds best spent on English education alone.
- The Government Resolution of 1835 made English the official and literary language of India.
- Bentinck's Resolution opened a new chapter in Indian education; Western education then began to spread rapidly.
- **Downward Filtration Theory** was related to education in colonial India. {22}
- Filtration theory: education is first given to the higher class and slowly percolates down to the lower classes and general public.
- **Macaulay** propounded the Filtration Theory: the Company should educate only an elite group, which would later educate the masses. {23}
- Lord Thomas Babington Macaulay = British politician and historian; Law Member in the Supreme Council of India; head of the Law Commission 1834–1838.
- Macaulay prepared the manuscript of the Indian Penal Code.
- Macaulay had an important role in making English the official language and medium of education, and European literature, philosophy and science the goal of Indian education.
- Macaulay on the East India Company: "The Company is an anomaly, but it is part of a system where everything is an anomaly." {26}
## Wood's Despatch 1854
- At the 1853 renewal of the Charter Act, a systematic pattern of education was felt necessary for India.
- **Wood's Despatch** was issued on 19 July 1854 by Sir Charles Wood, President of the Board of Control; it was related to education. {12}
- Wood's Despatch = "Magna Carta of English Education in India"; first comprehensive plan for the spread of education in India. {13}
- It introduced the grants-in-aid system and recommended universities; it did not recommend English at all levels. {10}
- It recommended English as medium for higher studies and stressed government support for female education and vocational training. {11}
- Recommendation: an education department in every province.
- Recommendation: universities on the model of London University in big cities: Bombay, Calcutta and Madras.
- Recommendation: at least one Government school in every district.
- Recommendation: affiliated private schools to be given grants.
- Recommendation: Indian natives should also be trained in their mother tongue.
- Recommendation: a systematic method of education from primary level to university level.
- First three universities (Calcutta, Madras, Bombay) were set up in **1857** on the model of London University. {29}
## Education commissions and plans
| Commission / Plan | Year | Appointed by / Head | Main points |
| Hunter Commission (first Indian Education Commission) | 1882 | Lord Ripon; headed by W.W. Hunter | Reviewed educational development after 1854 {15} |
| University Commission | 1902 | Lord Curzon; chairman Sir Thomas Raley | Indian members: Syed Hussain Bilgrami and Justice Gurudas Banerjee; led to University Act 1904 {19} |
| Sadler Commission | 1917 | British Government; chairman Dr. M.E. Sadler, Vice-Chancellor of Leeds University | Studied problems of Calcutta University {18} |
| Sargent Plan | 1944 | Report on educational reforms | Make Indian education more vocational and scientific {20} |
- Hunter Commission stressed reform and development of **primary education**. {14}
- Hunter Commission recommended vocational training in secondary education and pointed out inadequate facilities for female education.
- Hunter Commission's recommendations were only partially implemented.
- Hunter Commission on Education is dated 1882–83; Hartog Committee is dated 1929.
- **Sadler Commission** was related to education. {17}
- Sadler Commission's two Indian members: Dr. (Sir) Ashutosh Mukherjee and Dr. Ziauddin Ahmad.
- Sadler Commission was to inquire into Calcutta University and make wider recommendations on secondary, intermediate and university education.
- Sadler Commission: improving secondary education is a necessary condition for improving university education.
- Sadler Commission strongly criticised the University Act 1904 and found no proper coordination between colleges and universities.
- Sargent Plan: universal, compulsory and free primary (basic) education for all children aged 6–14.
- Sargent Plan stages: junior basic (6–11) and senior basic (11–14).
## National education movement
- **Rangpur National School** = set up on 8 November 1905; first milestone in national education.
- Rangpur National School aimed to give indigenous education, general and technical, to Indians.
- Citizens of Rangpur held a conference on 16 November 1905.
- On 16 November 1905 a session in Calcutta decided to set up the National Council of Education, Bengal, for science, national literature and technology.
- **National Council of Education (NCE), Bengal** was formed on 15 August 1906. {16}
- Also printed: Satish Chandra Mukherjee and others constituted the NCE, Bengal on 11 March 1906; Bengal National College was inaugurated later on 14 August 1906.

- Justice Ranade was not a founder of the Deccan Education Society; he was one of its five patrons.

## Aligarh
- M.A.O. College began in 1875 as Madarsatul Uloom Musalmanan-i-Hind; renamed Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College in 1877; became Aligarh Muslim University in 1920.
- **Theodore Beck**, Principal of M.A.O. College, encouraged Muslim separatist tendencies in the last decades of the 19th century. {33}
- **Aligarh Movement**, led by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan in the late 19th century, aimed to promote Western scientific (modern) education among Indian Muslims. {37}
- Aligarh Movement stressed scientific and Western learning while preserving Islamic values.
- Aligarh Movement sought socio-political upliftment and a progressive Muslim identity; it influenced the later demand for separate representation.
## Banaras Hindu University
- **Madan Mohan Malaviya** vigorously advocated religious education in Indian universities. {42}
- Malaviya = educationist and politician; founded BHU in 1916; Vice-Chancellor of BHU 1919–1939.
- Malaviya published magazines and newspapers in English and Hindi: Hindustan, Indian Union (also printed India Union), Abhyudaya, Maryada and The Leader.
- Central Hindu School later became the centre from which BHU developed.
- BHU was established in 1916 with the cooperation of Annie Besant and the efforts of Madan Mohan Malaviya.
- Central Hindu School was included in the new university in 1917; at present it is a constituent institute of BHU.
- **Lord Hardinge II**, the Viceroy, laid the foundation stone of BHU on 4 February 1916, Basant Panchami day. {45}
- Among AMU, Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar University Lucknow, BHU and Allahabad University, BHU was the first Central University. {46}
