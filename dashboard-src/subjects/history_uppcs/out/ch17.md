@ 17 | Delhi Sultanate: Slave Dynasty
## The Slave (Mamluk) dynasty
- Delhi Sultans from 1206 AD to 1290 AD are popularly called the sultans of the **Slave dynasty**; founder **Qutbuddin Aybak**. {1}
- Slave dynasty = first of five unrelated dynasties of the Sultanate period; Aibak and all his successors were slaves to their predecessors.
- They were all Turks but of separate houses/lines, so 'Mamluk Sultans of Delhi' is the more appropriate name.
## Qutbuddin Aybak (1206–1210)
- **Qutbuddin Aybak** (1206–1210 AD) was the first ruler of the Slave Dynasty. {2}
- He belonged to a Turk tribe called Aybak; born in Turkistan; ugly in appearance but intelligent and impressive in behaviour.
- In childhood he was sold as a slave to the Qazi of Nishapur, Fakhruddin Abdul Aziz Kufi, who gave him education and military training.
- He recited the Quran very melodiously, so he was famous as **Quran Khwan** (one who recites the Quran).
- After the Qazi's death his sons sold Aybak; he was brought from Nishapur to Ghazni, where Muhammad Ghori bought him.
- He was well-versed in Islamic theology, horse riding and swordsmanship; he won Ghori's confidence by talent, passion and honesty.
- He was first made commander of a troop, then promoted to **Amir-i-Akhur** (master of the royal stable).
- He came to India with Ghori and gave active support in battles; Ghori made him governor (viceroy) of his Indian empire.
- Ghori died in 1206 A.D. on his way to Ghazni; having no son, his empire was inherited by his slaves.
- Ghori's nephew Ghiyasuddin Mahmud took the throne of Ghor in 1206 A.D.; Taj-ud-Din Yalduz, Governor of Kirman, took the throne of Ghazni.
- Aybak, Ghori's general in charge of Lahore and the north-Indian possessions, seems to have been Ghori's own choice to succeed him in India.
- Aybak was invited by the Amirs of Lahore to assume power; his coronation was in June 1206 AD, three months after Ghori's death.
- Aybak's capital was **Lahore**. {8}
- He kept the capital at Lahore to frustrate Yalduz's ambition of annexing Punjab; he always stayed in Lahore and never got to move to Delhi.
- Nasiruddin Qabacha of Multan refused to accept Aybak as ruler of the Delhi Sultanate.
- Aybak strengthened his position by matrimonial alliances with influential rival Turkish chiefs.
- Aybak never took the title 'Sultan'; he was content with the titles **'Malik'** and **'Sipahasalaar'**.
- In 1208 AD he got a letter of liberation from Ghori's successor Ghiyasuddin Mahmud, was freed from slavery and ruled as an independent sovereign.
- For his generosity Aybak was called **'Lakh Bakhsh'** (giver of lakhs). {3}
- Aybak = first Muslim ruler who settled and ruled in India, unlike earlier Muslims who only raided and plundered.
- He patronized scholars Hasan Nizami and Fakhra-i-Mudabbir; both dedicated their works to him.
- His four-year reign was mostly spent on revolts of nobles like Taj-ud-din Yalduz, Nasir-ud-din Qabacha and a few Hindu chiefs, yet he set up a firm administration.
- Aibak built two mosques: **'Quwwat-ul-Islam'** in **Delhi** and **'Adhai Din Ka Jhopra'** in **Ajmer** (1192 AD). {4,5}
- Aybak started the **Qutub Minar** in Delhi; **Iltutmish** completed it. {7}
- Its fourth (topmost) storey was damaged by lightning; Firuz Shah Tughluq rebuilt the fourth floor and built the fifth; **Ghiyasuddin Tughluq** contributed nothing. {6}
- Aybak died in 1210 AD after falling from his horse while playing **Chaugan** (a game like modern polo). {9}
- He was buried in Lahore near the Anarkali Bazaar.
## Iltutmish (1211–1236)
- **Iltutmish** (1211–36 AD) = real founder of the Delhi Sultanate; first Sultan to shift the capital from Lahore to **Delhi** and to introduce regular coins. {10,11,12,13}
- He was the first to use pure Arabic coins: silver **Tanka** (175 grain; another passage: 175 gm) and copper **Jital**, the two basic coins of the Sultanate.
- He started the tradition of inscribing the name of the Tankshal (mint) on coins.
- Iltutmish was a Turk of the **'Ilbari'** tribe (Eurasian Steppe of Turkestan).
- Before becoming Sultan he was governor of Budaun, given for his services in the campaign against the Khokhars in 1205–06 AD.
- He was with Ghori and Aybak in the Khokhar campaign; impressed by his courage and skill, Ghori freed him from slavery.
- Iltutmish is called **'Slave of a slave'**: he was the slave of Aybak, himself a slave of Ghori; he later married Aybak's daughter. {16}
- After Aybak's death, Amirali Ismail invited Iltutmish for coronation in Delhi with the approval of Turkish nobles; he declared himself Sultan in 1211 A.D.
- He took control of the Delhi Sultanate as independent ruler after Aram Shah's death.
- Rivals of Iltutmish: **Tajuddin Yalduz** in Ghazni, **Nasiruddin Qabacha** in Sindh, **Ali Mardan** in Lakhnauti; Malik Altunia was not a rival. {17}
- Iltutmish got approval as Sultan from the Khalifa, not from any Ghor ruler.
- In **1229 AD** the Caliph of Baghdad, Al-Muntasir Billah, gave him a 'Khilat' and the title **'Sultan-e-Azam'** (Sultan the Great); he became the first legally independent Sultan of Delhi. {14}
- The Khilat made Iltutmish a legitimate Sultan and the Delhi Sultanate an independent state; in theory it became part of **Dar-ul-Islam** under the Caliph. {15}
- Iltutmish then took the title 'Amir-ul-Mommini' (Commander of the Faithful) and had the Khalifa's name put on his coins.
- He kept Delhi safe from the Mongols and from Yalduz and Qabacha; broke Rajput powers in the north; made his rule hereditary; issued coins in his name; made Delhi a beautiful capital.
- **Genghis Khan** reached the Indus during Iltutmish's reign, chasing Jalaluddin Mengubirni of Khwarazm; Mongols were first seen on the Indus then. {19,20,21,22,24}
- Genghis Khan stayed near the Indus for 3 months but did not cross into India.
- Mengubirni sought refuge; Iltutmish prudently refused asylum to avoid Genghis Khan's invasion.
- Mengubirni stayed some time in the north-west; this led to Mongol attacks like the siege of Multan around 1224; his departure (~1223–24) did not end frontier troubles at once.
- Original name of Genghis Khan = **Temujin (Temuchin)**; a Mongolian ruler important in Mongol imperial expansion. {23}
- Taimur began his conquests in 1370 A.D.; Muhammad bin Tughluq ruled 1325–1351 A.D., so Taimur did not cross the Indus in his reign.
- Iranian ambassador Abdur Razzaq came to Vijayanagara in the reign of Deva Raya II.
- Vasco da Gama reached the coast of Kerala in 1498 AD, when Narsa Nayaka's son Timma ruled Vijayanagara.
- In 1225 A.D. Iltutmish defeated Hisamuddin Awaz (Sultan Ghiyas-ud-din Iwaz) in the Rajmahal hills near Teliyagarhi and annexed Bihar Sharif and Barh.
- Awaz accepted subordination; Iltutmish appointed **Malik-Jani** as the new (first) Subedar of Bihar. {25}
- Per Ibn Battuta, Iltutmish set up two marble lion statues before his palace with bells round their necks that anyone could ring for justice.
- Iltutmish appointed officials Qazi and Amir-i-dad in all cities.
- Dr. R. P. Tripathi: the history of Muslim sovereignty in India begins with Iltutmish.
- Dr. Ishwari Prasad: Iltutmish was undoubtedly the founder of the Slave Dynasty.
## Raziyya Sultan (1236–1240)
- **Raziyya Sultan** (1236–40 AD) = first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate. {18}
- Turkish nobles were reluctant to accept a woman as ruler, especially when she challenged their power.
- She broke the prevailing gender norms of Islam for the first time in India.
- She wanted power in the Sultan's hands, not divided among Chiefs (Sardars) or Iqtadars; this provoked the Turkish slave-nobles (Chahalgani).
- **Chahalgani** (40 nobles) played an important role in overthrowing Raziyya.
- **Turkish** nobles led by Malik Altunia, governor of Bhatinda, rebelled and removed her. {26}
## Balban (1266–1287)
- Full name of Balban = **Ghiyasuddin Balban**; ninth Sultan of the Mamluk dynasty; ruled 1266–1287 AD. {27}
- His actual name was Baha-ud-din; also known as Ulugh Khan; an Ilbari Turk like Iltutmish; founded a new line, the Balban Dynasty.
- Mongols caught Balban in childhood and sold him at Ghazni to Khwaja Jamaluddin of Basra.
- He was brought to Delhi in 1232 AD; Iltutmish bought him in 1233 AD after the Gwalior victory and gave him the title Khasdaar.
- Under Raziyya he rose to **Amir-i-Shikar**.
- He joined the Turkish warlords' conspiracy against Raziyya; Bahramshah became Sultan and Balban got the title 'Amir-i-Akhur'.
- By the grace of Badruddin Rumi he got the Jagir of Rewari.
- He helped Alauddin Masudshah become Sultan and got the fief of Hansi.
- In 1249 AD Balban married his daughter to Sultan Nasiruddin; he got the title **'Ulugh Khan'** and the post 'Nayab-i-mamlikat'.
- He was Wazir and heir of the last Shamsi Sultan, Nasiruddin Mahmud; he sat on Delhi's throne in 1266 AD.
- He reduced the power of the treacherous nobility and raised the stature of the Sultan.
- Despite few military achievements, he was the most powerful Sultan between Iltutmish and Alauddin Khalji.
- **Balban** adopted the policy of **'blood and iron'**. {28,29}
- Two features of his rule: divine right of the Sultan, and the Sultan must be autocratic.
- His political ideology was inspired by the popular heroes of Persia; the king = God's representative on earth, **'Niyabat-i-Khudai'**.
- He said the Sultan's place comes after the Paigambar; the Sultan gets inspiration and strength from God, so public and Sardars cannot criticize him.
- Balban took the title **'Zil-i-Ilahi' / 'Zille Allah'** = Shadow of God; he was the first to use it. {32,37}
- Balban = first Sultan of Delhi to set out a **theory of kingship**, similar to the divine right of kings. {33}
- He told his son Bughra Khan: "The post of Sultan is a living symbol of autocracy."
- Balban brought Iranian court traditions: **'Sijada'** (prostration before the Sultan) and **'Paibos'** (kissing the Sultan's feet).
- Balban started celebrating the Persian festival **'Nawruz'** in his court every year. {34,35}
- Balban made his government firm and centralized all authority; he also built strong forts on the north-west border against the Mongols — both true, but the second does not explain the first. {30,31}
- The **Iqtadari system** was introduced by Iltutmish, not Balban. {36}
- Balban abolished the 'Turkan-e-Chahalgani' set up by Iltutmish.
- The only revolt of Balban's reign: **Tughril Khan**, Subedar of Bengal, in 1279 A.D.; Balban crushed it and hanged the rebels. {39}
- Tughril's rebellion was in protest against the appointment of Amin Khan in Bengal; Tughril fled fearing Balban's Bengal campaign.
- Balban set up the military department **'Diwan-i-Arz'**.
- He made Imad ul-Mulk, a very honest and hardworking man, his military minister (Arz-i-Mamalik), free from the Wazir's financial control.
- Balban called himself **'Helper of a Caliph'** in his inscription on the walls of the mosque at Garhmukteshwar. {38}
