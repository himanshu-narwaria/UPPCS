@ 30 | Akbar
## Birth, coronation and Bairam Khan
- Akbar was born on 15 October 1542 AD at Amarkot, in the palace of Raja Veersaal.
- Humayun died in a fatal accident, falling from the stairs of the Dinpanah library.
- Akbar was crowned at **Kalanaur** (now in Gurdaspur district, Punjab) on 14 February 1556 AD, on news of Humayun's death; he was under 14 years old. {1}
- Shah Abdul Mali refused to join Akbar's coronation; Humayun used to call him 'Farjand' (son).
- **Bairam Khan** was Akbar's guardian and regent; Akbar appointed him Wazir and gave him the title 'Khan-i-Khanan'. {2}
- Akbar personally killed **Adham Khan**, son of Maham Anga, in 1562 AD, as he had murdered Akbar's prime minister Atgah Khan. {13}
## Rajput policy and conquests
- Akbar's first matrimonial alliance with Rajputs was with the **Kachhwaha** house (of Amber/Amer). {10,11}
- In 1562 Akbar went on pilgrimage to the Dargah of Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti at Ajmer; his route lay through Amber.
- Its ruler Raja Bihari Mal (Bharmal), to safeguard his position, submitted to Akbar at Sanganer and offered his daughter Princess Harkhabai (Jodha) in marriage.
- Akbar married her in 1562 on his return from Ajmer; his first marriage with a Rajput girl.
- Raja Bharmal = first Rajput king to accept Akbar's supremacy of his own accord.
- Salim (later Jahangir), heir to the throne, was born of this marriage on August 30, 1569 AD at Fatehpur Sikri.
- Order: Mughal invasion of Malwa (1561) → Akbar marries princess of Amber (1562) → Karrani's conquest of Orissa (1568) → Battle of Tukaroi (1575). {22}
- **Durgavati** was custodian of the Hindu kingdom of Gondwana for her minor son Vir Narayan.
- Akbar sent about 50,000 cavalry with heavy artillery against Gondwana (1564 AD); Durgavati fought bravely but lost the battle between Garh and **Mandla** (modern Mandla district, M.P.). {16}
- Surrounded by the enemy, Durgavati committed suicide.
- Rao **Chandrasena of Marwar** fought the Mughal army at Bhadrajun (1565 AD in the chapter notes; 1571 AD in an answer explanation); surrounded, he fled to Siwana.
- Chandrasena annexed Sojat in 1579 AD; Akbar besieged him again, he fled to the hills and died in January 1581 AD.
- Like Maharana Pratap, Rao Chandrasena kept up his struggle for independence against the Mughals and did not surrender. {15}
- Akbar annexed Gujarat in 1572–73 AD.
- Daud Khan succeeded his father Sulaiman Karrani in 1572 AD; declared himself independent and invaded the Mughal fort of Patna.
- Akbar ordered Munim Khan to crush the revolt; Munim Khan defeated Daud and annexed Bihar in 1574 AD; Daud fled to Bengal.
- On 12 July 1576 Todarmal defeated Daud; thus Akbar merged Bengal and Bihar in his empire in **1576 AD**. {83}
- Map of Akbar's empire at his death (1605): Gondwana = independent state; Lahore = part of the empire. {9}
- Akbar's empire stretched from Bengal to Afghanistan in the north and from Kashmir to the Godavari in the south.
## Battle of Haldighati and Mewar
- Akbar's main aim in the Battle of Haldighati was to **subdue Rana Pratap** and make him accept Mughal suzerainty. {3}
- In April 1576 AD Akbar deputed Man Singh with about 5000 soldiers against Maharana Pratap.
- Man Singh went via Mandalgarh to Gogundagarh, fourteen miles from Haldighati; Rana Pratap came down from the hills to face him.
- **Battle of Haldighati** was fought in June **1576 AD**; Rana was defeated and took refuge in the Aravalli Hills. {4,5}
- **Hakim Khan Sur** was the commander (commander-in-chief besides Maharana Pratap) of Rana Pratap's army at Haldighati. {6,7,8}
- Hakim Khan Sur = Pathan adventurer of the Sur Dynasty, a descendant of Sher Shah Suri; led about 5,000 Afghans in the battle.
- **Mewar** did not accept Akbar's supremacy willingly; Rana Pratap kept up his struggle for a long time. {14}
- After Rana Pratap's death his son Rana Amar Singh signed a treaty with Jahangir in 1615 AD.
## Salim's revolt and Abul Fazl's death
- When Prince Salim began acting as an independent king at Allahabad (Prayagraj) and rebelled, Akbar called his friend Abul Fazl from the Deccan in 1602 AD.
- Vir Singh Bundela killed Abul Fazl on his way back from the Deccan, in a plot by **Prince Salim**. {17}
## Religious policy
- Akbar patronised the Chishti sect, showing his faith in Sufism; he sought the company of saints and talked with zeal and humility.
- He often visited the Mausoleum of Khwaja **Muinuddin Chishti** at Ajmer and the shrine of the contemporary Sufi Shaikh Salim Chishti at Sikri. {12}
- Akbar abolished the **pilgrimage tax** in 1563 AD and Jizya in 1564 AD under his liberal religious policy. {20}
- Akbar was the first Muslim ruler to see he could not consolidate Mughal rule without the confidence and active support of the Hindu majority.
- He was the first medieval monarch to raise religious tolerance to the pinnacle of secularism; he tried to separate religion from politics.
- Religion had been a divisive force in Indian society since the advent of Islam, which most people had refused to embrace.
- His measures for religious harmony: freedom of worship, marriage alliances with Hindus, high civil and military posts to Hindus, translation of Hindu scriptures, Ibadat Khana, infallibility decree, freedom to build temples, land grants, regard for Hindu sentiments, reforms in Hindu society.
- Akbar had **not forsaken Islam**; administrative unity, unity in law, cultural unity and religious policy make him a nationalist. {18}
- Akbar promulgated **Sulh-i-Kul** (peace with all, universal harmony) in place of the Islamic concept; it reflects political generosity, religious tolerance and a liberal cultural attitude. {48,49}
- Through Sulh-i-Kul he tried to build Hindu and Muslim communities into national unity.
- Akbar's reign is known for annexation of regions, provincial administrative system and judicial administration, not religious intolerance; he is world famous for religious liberalism. {25}
- Akbar built the **Ibadat Khana** at Fatehpur Sikri in 1575 AD for philosophical and theological discussions with Ulemas, Sayyids and Shaikhs; a hall where he held discussions with scholars. {43,44}
- He opened the Ibadat Khana to all schools of religion in 1578 AD.
- These discussions led to the **Mahzarnama** in 1579 AD, prepared by Shaikh Mubarak, father of Abul Fazl.
- Mahzarnama ended the predominance of the bigoted orthodox and allowed the generous spirit Akbar wished to encourage.
- Smith and Wolseley Haig called the Mahzarnama an infallibility decree; after it Akbar was titled Sultan-i-Adil or Imam-i-Adil.
- In 1580 AD an Ulema, Mulla Muhammad Yazdi of **Jaunpur**, issued a 'Fatwa' against Akbar and asked all Muslims to rebel. {50}
- Akbar launched **Tauhid-i-Ilahi / Din-i-Ilahi** in 1582 AD, for reconciliation between all religious schools. {39,40,41}
- Din-i-Ilahi = syncretic creed meant to merge the best elements of all religions; its 'prophet' was Abul Fazl.
- Among Hindus only **Birbal** accepted Din-i-Ilahi; Raja Bhagawan Das and Man Singh bluntly refused.
- Din-i-Ilahi was not a religion but an ideology based on Sufi pantheism. {42}
- Jahangir's contemporary Mohsin Fani first described Din-i-Ilahi as an independent religion, in 'Dabistan-i-Mazahib'.
- Smith: "Din-i-Ilahi is a monument of Akbar's foolishness, not of his wisdom".
- Order of events: abolition of Jizya (1564) → Ibadat Khana (1575) → Mahzar (1579) → Din-i-Ilahi (1582). {81}
- Goa Church authorities sent priests to Akbar in 1595 AD to satisfy his curiosity about Christianity: the third mission; it met Akbar in Lahore. {82}
- First mission reached Fatehpur Sikri in 1580 AD; second reached Lahore in 1591 AD.
- Third mission was led by Jerome Xavier, nephew of missionary saint Francis Xavier.
- Antonio Monserrate was a member; Akbar hired him as tutor for his son Murad.
- Akbar took part in festivals such as Basant, Holi, Diwali.
- Stanley Lane-Poole: "This assimilation of the Hindu chiefs was the most conspicuous feature of Akbar's reign."
- Dr. R.P. Tripathi: Akbar was both a child and a father of his era.
## Social and educational reforms
- Akbar ordered that a man should marry only one wife, and another only if the first proved barren. {23}
- He banned some social customs to improve society; encouraged widow remarriage and forcefully discouraged Sati.
- Akbar tried to fix the marriage age at 14 for girls and at least 16 for boys; gave girls freedom to marry at their own will, not under parental pressure. {24}
- Causes of Akbar's popularity: Mansabdari system, religious policy, land revenue system and social reforms. {19}
- Akbar was the first Muslim ruler to practise religious harmony and treat Hindus and Muslims alike; child marriage and Sati were abolished in his reign.
- Akbar is credited with establishing, consolidating and centralising Mughal administration.
- His central administration, idea of kingship, provincial administration, revenue system, monetary policy and mansabdari became the foundation for his successors.
- Akbar is called **"an enlightened despot"**, like Queen Elizabeth I of England and Frederick the Great: liberal and tolerant, with complete authority of the empire vested in him; all subjects were equal before the law. {21}
- Akbar introduced **educational reforms** in the curriculum, especially at primary level. {55}
- Subjects added at his command: arithmetic, logic, mensuration, geometry, astronomy, physiognomy, accountancy, public administration and agriculture.
- Akbar had no formal education but patronised men of learning.
## Administration and Mansabdari
- Under Akbar's central system **Mir Bakshi** headed the military department but was not chief commander of the army. {26}
- Bakshi recommended recruitment, proper rank and correct pay of soldiers; inspected mansabdars and troops and reported to the emperor.
- Akbar revived Chehra (descriptive roll of each soldier) and Dagh (branding of horses) in 1573–74 AD.
- Akbar's military system was based on **Mansabdari**; he organised soldiers by it in 1574–75 AD (19th year of reign, 1575 AD). {27}
- Mansab = rank or position; Mansabdar = official holding a rank in the royal army or government office; based on the decimal system.
- Three classes: Mansabdar (10 to below 500), Amir (500 to 2500), Amir-i-Azam (above 2500).
- Mansabdari was introduced to ensure clean administration. {28}
- Mansabdari was borrowed from **Mongolia** (Central Asia): Genghis Khan's army used a decimal troop organisation; yet the system was largely new. {30}
- In Akbar's time mansabdars kept twenty horses for every ten cavalrymen, as horses needed rest on the march and replacement in war. {31}
- 'Diwan' is a Persian word adopted by Muslims in Caliph Umar's time for the treasury department.
- **Muzaffar Khan Turbati** was made Diwan (Todar Mal as assistant) early in Akbar's reign; the Diwan's department grew from his appointment. {29}
- Todar Mal and Khwaja Shah Mansur later served in the finance ministry; Akbar sometimes appointed more than one diwan.
- Diwan = supreme official of financial affairs and revenue.
## Land revenue: Zabti and Dahsala
- **Zabti system** of land revenue was introduced in Akbar's reign: measurement-based assessment; revenue circles (dasturs) with per-bigha cash rates; Ain-i-Dahsala (ten-year average rates). {32}
- Zabti rested on schedules of Dastur-ul-Amal and Zabti Khasre for survey and revenue fixing; most of the area was under it.
- **Todarmal** won fame in land revenue (land revenue reforms). {33,35}
- **Ain-i-Dahsala** was introduced by Akbar in the 24th year of his reign (1580 AD) and instituted by Todarmal; also called Todarmal Bandobast / Bandobast system / Todarmal settlement. {34,38}
- Todarmal was then finance minister; his main assistant was Khwaja Shah Mansoor; Muzaffar Khan Turbati also helped.
- Akbar appointed Raja Todarmal and Muzaffar Khan Turbati as finance minister to re-assess land revenue; the Dahsala (Zabti) system of 1580 was meant to consolidate the Mughal empire.
- Dahsala: average produce of crops and average prices of the last ten years (1571 to 1580 AD) were calculated.
- One-third of the average was state share (Maal); two-thirds was left to cultivators (Kharaj).
- Raja Todarmal, honoured for land revenue reforms, was the connecting link between Sher Shah and Akbar in land revenue measures. {37}
- **Abul Fazl** wrote 'Ain-i-Akbari'. {36}
- Tansen's name was Ram Tanu Pandey; the title 'Tansen' was given by the king of Gwalior/Rewa, not Akbar.
- Jahangir (not Akbar) married the sister of Raja Man Singh of Amber.
- Raja Todarmal headed the revenue department; he was not commander-in-chief of Akbar's army.
## Fatehpur Sikri and other buildings
- Sikri village, about 37 km from Agra, was the home of Sufi sage Shaikh Salim Chishti; Akbar often went there for his blessings.
- Akbar founded Fatehpur Sikri (City of Victory), a fort-cum-palace town in Agra district, U.P.; Mughal capital from 1571 to 1585 AD.
- Akbar's **finest monuments are at Fatehpur Sikri**. {56}
- Sikri buildings: Diwan-i-Aam, Diwan-i-Khas, Treasury, Panch Mahal, Khas Mahal, Kothi of Turki Sultana, Jodha Bai Mahal, Mariyam ki Kothi, Birbal ki Kothi, Swarn Mahal (Sunahala Makaan), Jama Masjid, Buland Darwaza, Tomb of Sheikh Salim Chishti.
- Buildings in Gujarat and Bengal styles are found in the Sikri fort.
- Percy Brown called the palace of Turkish Sultana a 'Pearl of Architecture' (one explanation says he called Sikri so).
- Akbar built **Buland Darwaza** at Sikri as a victory tower for his conquest of **Gujarat** (not for Jahangir's birth), as one class of historians holds. {51,52,53}
- Percy Brown links Buland Darwaza with the southern victory (1601 AD).
- Akbar built the Buland Darwaza and the Jama Masjid at Fatehpur Sikri. {54}
- Jama Masjid of Fatehpur Sikri is called **'Shan-i-Fatehpur'** (also 'Shah-i-Fatehpur' or 'Shan-i-Sikri'). {45}
- **Panch Mahal** (Fatehpur Sikri): five-storey, pyramid-shaped, wholly columnar palace built like a Buddhist monastery; inspired by the multi-storey buildings of Nalanda. {57,58,59}
- Panch Mahal is set asymmetrically on the pattern of a Persian badgir (wind-catcher tower); its ground floor has 84 pillars.
- **Akbari Mahal** is in Agra Fort, not Fatehpur Sikri. {46}
- Agra Fort = earliest example of Akbari style; built by Akbar under his chief architect Kasim Khan.
- Akbar built more than 500 buildings of red sandstone in Agra Fort.
- **Jahangir Mahal** is inside Agra Fort; built by Akbar for Shahzada Salim. {60}
- Jahangir Mahal has intricate Hindu and Islamic motifs; inspired by the Palace of Man Singh of Gwalior.
- Agra Fort, Allahabad Fort and Lahore Fort were built in Akbar's reign; the **Red Fort of Delhi** was built by Shah Jahan. {62}
- Red Fort is of red sandstone; completed in 1648 AD.
- **Humayun's Tomb** in Delhi is a synthesis of Persian and Indian architecture; built under Humayun's wife, designed by Mirak Mirza Ghiyas. {47}
- **Akbar's tomb** is at Sikandra, a small village founded by Sikandar Lodi, on the outskirts of Agra. {61}
- Akbar named Sikandra 'Bahistabad'; he began the tomb, Jahangir completed it in 1613 AD.
- Akbar's tomb has five storeys; its speciality is the domeless square apex.

## Court: translations, scholars, artists
- Akbar set up a translation department under his poet laureate Abul Fazl.
- The Mahabharata was translated into Persian as **Razmnama** (book of wars) on Akbar's order, by Badauni, Naqib Khan and Faizi. {66,67,68,69}
- The Mahabharata translation was carried out under the direction of **Faizi**. {65}
- **Abdul Qadir Badauni** translated the Ramayana into Persian. {70,71}
- Faizi translated 'Leelavati'; Abul Fazl translated 'Kaliyadaman' into Persian.
- Muhammad Husain of Kashmir, famous calligrapher of Akbar's court, was honoured with **'Zari Qalam'**. {72}
- **Hari Vijay Suri**, a Jain monk, stayed some years at Akbar's court and got the title **Jagadguru**. {73,74,75}
- Akbar invited Hari Vijay Suri to explain Jainism; moved by non-violence, Akbar turned vegetarian and banned slaughter of animals and birds.
- Jain scholar Jin Chandra Suri got the title 'Yug Pradhan' from Akbar.
- **Daswant** was Akbar's famous painter; son of a potter, became the first artist of his time; went insane and committed suicide at the height of his glory. {76}
- Painters listed in Abul Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari: Daswant, Basawan, Keshav Lal, Mukund, Miskin, Jagan, Mahesh, Khemkaran, Tara, Sanwal, Harivansh.
- **Abul Fazl** = medieval writer who refers to the discovery of America. {79}
## Akbar and the English
- Akbar (1556–1605 AD) was a contemporary of Queen Elizabeth I of England (1558–1603 AD); he ruled India when the English East India Company was formed in 1600 AD. {77,78}
- **Ralph Fitch** (1583–86 AD) = first English merchant to visit Agra and Fatehpur Sikri (Akbar's court). {80}
- Fitch travelled widely and left a valuable account of India's trade points and township centres of the 16th century.
