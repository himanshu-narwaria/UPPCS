@ 6 | Shaiva, Bhagavata Religion
## Yugas, Astika and Nastika
- Ancient Indian cosmogony: four epochs (yugas) in order = **Krita → Treta → Dvapara → Kali**. {1}
- 6th century BC context: **Astika** = believed in the authenticity of the Vedas; **Nastika** = did not believe in it. {19}
## Bhagavata (Vaishnava) religion: origin
- Vaishnava religion developed from the **Bhagavata** religion.
- Traditional originator of Bhagavata religion = **Lord Krishna (Vasudeva)**, a Vrishni (Satvat) descendant, basically from Mathura. {9}
- **Chhandogya Upanishad** describes Krishna as son of Devaki and disciple of Ghor Angirasa. {10}
- Earliest form of Vaishnavism = worship of Krishna, son of Devaki; first worshippers of Vasudeva Krishna = **Bhagavatas**. {11}
- Krishna's early name (Vasudev) was current in Panini's age; Panini mentions Vasudev worship and Bhagavata Dharma and calls Vasudev's worshippers **'Vasudevak'**.
- Rig Veda mentions Vishnu as God of the sky.
- Three major gods of the later Vedic period: Prajapati, Rudra, Vishnu.
- Vishnu is considered guardian and protector of the people.
- Patanjali described Vasudev as a form of Vishnu; in Vishnu Purana Vasudev is also a name of Vishnu.
- When the Krishna-Vishnu link was joined with Narayana, Vaishnavism became **'Pancharatra Dharma'**.
- The religion was first prevalent in Mathura and nearby areas, then slowly spread to other parts of India.
- Megasthenes (Greek ambassador): people of Sursen (Mathura) worshipped **'Heracles'** = Vasudeva Krishna.
- **Garuda Pillar of Vidisha (Besnagar)** = first stone monument and earliest epigraphic evidence of Bhagavata religion. {15,16}
- The pillar was set up by **Heliodorus**, Greek (Yavana) ambassador from **Taxila (Takshila)**, who accepted the Bhagavata religion. {17}
- Besnagar inscription calls Heliodorus 'Bhagavata' and Vasudeva **'Devadevas'** (God of Gods); it relates only to Vasudeva. {14}
- Bhagavata cult recognises **Navadha (9) Bhakti** — nine forms of bhakti — for Moksha. {13}
- **Balarama** (Haldhar), brother of Krishna, is shown in art holding a plough. {12}
## Vaishnavism under Guptas and later
- Bhagavata/Vaishnava religion reached its peak under the **Guptas**; they made it the state religion. {8}
- Most Gupta rulers held the title **'Parambhagavata'**; Garuda, the mount (Vahana) of Vishnu, was the Gupta state symbol.
- Mehrauli Pillar: Chandra (identified as Chandragupta II) set up the Vishnu flag on Vishnupad hill.
- Bhitari Pillar inscription: Skandagupta installed a statue of Vishnu.
- Amarsimha gives 39 names of Vishnu in his book **Amarkosha**.
- Chalukyas of Eastern Vengi were Vaishnavas; Garuda was their state symbol, like the Guptas.
- Rashtrakuta king **Dantidurg** built the Dashavatara temple at Ellora; it tells the story of Vishnu's ten incarnations.
- **'Dashavatar Charitam'** was composed by Kshemendra.
- **Varaha** = third incarnation of Vishnu; killed the giant-king Hiranyaksha and freed the Earth from him. {18}
- Purana pictures: Varaha balances the earth on his tusk (teeth) and rises from the sea.
- Varaha form: boar head on a human body with four arms holding Shankha, Chakra, Gada, Padma.
## Alvars and Nayanars
- Medieval devotional spirit spread mainly in South India, specially Tamil regions, through two sects: Alvars and Nayanars.
- **Alvars** = devotees of Vishnu; they spread Vaishnavism in South India; number = 12.
- 'Alvar' = one who is immersed in the experience of God, the omnipresent mysterious one.
- Poigai, Pudam and Thirumangai were Alvar saints; Tirugyan was not. {6}
- **Andal** = the only female saint among the 12 Alvars. {7}
- **Nayanars** = devotees of Shiva (Shaivas); they spread Shaivism in South India; number = 63. {4,5}
- Notable Nayanars: Tirugyan, Sundar Murthy, Sambundar, Appar, Manikkavachakar.
- Alvars and Nayanars: personal love and dedication to God is the only way to Moksha; they opposed caste rigidity.
- **Nathpanthis, Siddhas and Yogis** made devotional (Bhakti) religion popular in **Northern India**. {30}
- Nathpanthis, Siddhas, Yogis criticised ritual, conventional religion and the social order, and advocated renouncing the world.
## Gita, Vedanta, epics
- **Bhagavad Gita** gives equal importance to Jnana, Karma and Bhakti as means of liberation. {20}
- Advaita Vedanta: Shankaracharya accepted Brahma as the only truth.
- Vedanta and Vishistadvaita Vedanta give supremacy to Bhakti; Mimamsa mainly propounds Karma.
- Main teaching (central theme) of the Gita = **Nishkama Karma Yoga**; Gita holds it supreme among all yogas. {21}
- Nishkama Karma = to act unselfishly, without thought of personal gain or expecting good in return.
- **Prasthantrayi** of Vedanta = Upanishad, Brahmasutra, Bhagavad Gita; Bhagavata is not in it. {23}
- Upanishad is the prime Prasthan; the other two are based on it.
- Shankaracharya = oldest of the Vedanta Acharyas who founded sects by writing commentaries on the Brahmasutra.
- **Naimisharanya** (Sitapur district, U.P.) = home of 60,000 hermits, where the whole Mahabharata was narrated. {24}
- Sut Goswami narrated the Mahabharata to Saunak and other hermits during a Yagya; earlier Vaisampayana narrated it to King Janamejaya.
- **Valmiki** wrote the Ramayana, the Adi Kavya in Sanskrit, with seven Kands.
- **Kishkindha Kand** (4th Kand): first meeting of Rama and Hanuman, death of Bali, Sugriva becomes king of the Vanaras. {25}
- **Ananga** (incorporeal) = Kamadeva, god of human love/desire; wife Rati. {22}
- Other names of Kamadeva: Kandarpa (inflamer even of a god), Madana (intoxicating), Manmatha (churner of hearts).
## Vaishnava festivals and temples
- **Rathyatra** of Puri (Odisha) honours **Lord Jagannath** (Krishna), with Balram (Balbhadra) and Subhadra; yearly on dwitiya of Shukla Paksha, Ashadha month. {28}
- **Kumbh Mela at Nasik** is held on the bank of the **Godavari**, every twelve years. {29}
- Kumbh is also held at Haridwar (Ganga), Prayag/Prayagraj (confluence of Ganga, Yamuna and extinct Saraswati) and Ujjain (Kshipra/Shipra).

## Shaiva religion
- Religion devoted to Shiva = **Shaiva Dharma**; worshippers = Shaiva.
- Shaiva is the oldest Hindu tradition in India; it goes back to the prehistoric age.
- Indus Valley people worshipped Shiva: a Mohenjodaro seal shows a three-faced Yogi with trishul-like headgear; Marshall linked it to Rudra Shiva.
- Rig Veda calls Shiva **'Rudra'**, famous for ferocity.
- Shatarudriya Mantra of the Vajasaneyi Samhita calls Rudra the God of the universe.
- Atharvaveda calls him Pashupati, Bhav, Sarva, Bhupati.
- Patanjali's Mahabhashya shows Shiva's idol was worshipped in the 2nd century BC.
- Names of Shiva in the Mahabhashya: Rudra, Mahadev, Girish, Bhav, Sarva, Trayambak.
- Kushan coins carry figures of Shiva, Vrisabha and Trishul.
- Udayagiri cave inscription: Virasena, minister of Chandragupta II, built a Shaiva cave on Udayagiri hill.
- In Kumargupta's time Shiva Lingas were set up at Khoh and Karamdanda.
- Gupta period: Shiva temple at Bhumara and Parvati temple at Nachana-Kuthar.
- Kalidas praised Shiva's glory in **Kumarasambhavam**.
- **Kandaria Mahadev** temple of Khajuraho was built by the Chandela rulers.
- The **Kailash temple** of Ellora was built under the Rashtrakutas.
- Chola rulers were exclusive Shiva worshippers; **Rajaraja I** built the Brihadeeswar (Raj Rajeshwar) temple at Tanjore.
- Kulottunga II, a staunch Shaiva, threw the Vishnu statue of the Chidambaram temple into the sea.
- **Ardhanarishvara** = composite androgynous form, half male Shiva (usually the right half) and half female Parvati, split down the middle; shows union of God with his Shakti. {3}
- Ardhanarishvara = synthesis of masculine and feminine energies; Shakti (female principle) is inseparable from Shiva (male principle).
- **Twelve Jyotirlingas**: Somnath, Nageshwar (near Dwarka), Kedarnath (Uttarakhand), Kashi Vishwanath (Varanasi), Vaidyanath (Deogarh, Jharkhand), Mahakaleshwar (Ujjain), Omkareshwar (M.P.), Bhimashankar (Pune), Trayambakeshwar (Nashik), Ghushmeshwar (Maharashtra), Mallikarjuna (Andhra Pradesh), Rameswaram (Tamil Nadu).
## Shaiva sects
- Vaman Purana names four Shaiva sects: Shaiva, Pashupat, Kapalik, Kalamukh.
- **Pashupat** sect arose in the 2nd century BC; Puranas: founded by a Brahmachari, **Lakulish (Lakuli)**, held by followers to be Shiva's incarnation.
- **Kapalik** sect worshipped Bhairav as Shiva's incarnation; followers ate meat, smeared cremation ashes and carried a Narmund in hand.
- **Mattamayura** = a Shaiva sect of ancient India, found in inscriptions of the Chedi dynasty. {2}
- **Basav** is considered founder of the Lingayat sect of Shaivism.
- **Nathpanth** was started by Matsyendra Nath at the end of the 10th century, taking Shiva as Adinath; Baba Gorakhnath promoted it.

## Shakta religion
- People of the **Shakta** sect worshipped goddess Shakti; Shaiva Dharma is closely related to Shakta Dharma.
- Shakta religion is also prehistoric: Mother Goddess worship was very popular in the Indus civilization; her statues were found in excavations.
- Vedic literature tells of goddesses Saraswati, Aditi, Usha, Lakshmi.
- Markandeya Purana has a detailed Goddess Mahatmya.
- **Kalika Purana** (Kali Purana / Sati Purana) = a main upapurana of **Shaktism**. {26}
- Kalika Purana was likely composed in the Assam region; attributed to sage Markandeya.
- Kalika Purana opens with legends of Devi bringing Shiva back from ascetic life to householder life by making him fall in love again.
