## Rulers and invaders in order
- Order of rulers: Jahangir (1605-1627 A.D.) → Muhammad Shah (1719-1748 A.D.) → Ahmad Shah Abdali (Third Panipat, 14 January 1761) → Bahadur Shah II 'Zafar' (1837-1857 A.D.). {5}
- Order of foreign invasions: Genghis Khan → Timur → Nadir Shah → Ahmad Shah Abdali. {6}
- Genghis Khan attacked in the reign of Iltutmish (1211-1236 A.D.).
- Timur invaded in 1398 A.D. in the reign of Nasiruddin Mahmud.
- Nadir Shah invaded in 1739 A.D. in the reign of Muhammad Shah.
- Ahmad Shah Abdali, an Afghan invader: invasions 1748-69 A.D.
- Qutbuddin Aybak was the first ruler of the Delhi Sultanate and founder of the Slave dynasty; Babur did not establish it.
- Shah Jahan did not fight the war of succession; it was fought among his sons (Shah Shuja, Dara Shikoh, Aurangzeb).
- Jahangir was a lover of beauty and nature.

## Hemu and Prithviraj
- **Hemu** = Hem Chandra Vikramaditya. {9}
- Hemu rose to be army chief and Prime Minister of Adil Shah Suri of the Sur dynasty.
- Hemu fought Afghan rebels from Punjab to Bengal and the Mughal forces of Akbar and Humayun in Agra and Delhi; won 22 battles in a row.
- Hemu defeated Tardi Beg (Targi Begh) and took the title Vikramaditya, used earlier by many Hindu kings since Vedic times.
- Hemu lost the Second Battle of Panipat and was beheaded by Bairam Khan, commander of Akbar's army.
- Historian B.N. Luniya called **Prithviraj Chauhan** 'the last sun of Indian glory'. {10}

## Surat, Nauroz, education
- **Surat** was called Babul Mecca (Gate of Mecca), 'Meccai dwar' and 'Meccabari'; Haj pilgrims left for Mecca from Surat. {24,25}
- Mughals adopted the **Nauroz** festival from Persia (Iran), i.e. from the Zoroastrians. {26}
- Nauroz was a national festival celebrated for nineteen days; preparations began long before.
- Abdul Qadir Badauni called Nauroz 'Nauroz-i-Jalali'.
- The Madarsa specialising in Muslim Jurisprudence in the Mughal period was at **Lucknow**. {27}
- Mulla Nizamuddin, born in Sihali (Lucknow), originated the new curriculum 'Dars-e-Nizami'.
- Dars-e-Nizami included Tafsir (exegesis of Quran), Hadith (tradition of Prophet Muhammad) and Fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence).
## Famous queens in order
- Order of queens: Padmini → Durgawati → Tara Bai → Ahilya Bai. {29}
- Padmini: queen of Chittor, wife of Rana Ratan Singh; Khalji defeated and killed Ratan Singh at Chittor and wanted her by force; she committed Jauhar (similar to Sati).
- Rani Durgavati: Hindu warrior queen of Gondwana, regent for her son; defeated by Akbar, she stabbed herself to death.
- Tara Bai Bhonsle: queen of Chhatrapati Rajaram; kept alive resistance to Mughal occupation of Maratha lands after her husband's death.
- Ahilya Bai: Holkar queen of the Maratha-ruled Malwa kingdom; Indore was held by the Holkar dynasty.

- Atala Mosque, Jaunpur was built by Ibrahim Shah Sharqi around 1408 A.D.

## Trade and travel terms
- **Muallim** = navigator, an employee on Indian merchant ships (a worker, not a ship).
- Muallim held a spherical device to read positions of stars and the sun and find the ship's correct position.
- **Banjaras** in medieval India were generally traders. {40}
- Banjaras moved place to place, sometimes with thousands of oxen laden with food grain, salt, ghee and daily goods.
- Banjaras specialised in caravan trading and also in textile embroidery.
- **Sarais** = places giving temporary accommodation to Indian and foreign travellers, pilgrims, merchants and traders. {41}
- Sarais ringed cities and dotted the sub-continent; mostly built on a simple square or rectangular plan.
- Sarais were public places full of people of varied cultures; this led to cross-cultural interaction and syncretic tendencies.
