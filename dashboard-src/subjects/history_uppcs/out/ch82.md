@ 82 | Demand for Pakistan
## Iqbal and the idea of a separate Muslim state
- **Muhammad Iqbal** (poet and political philosopher) first gave the idea of a separate Muslim nation/state (an autonomous Muslim-majority homeland); it first found definite expression in his presidential address at the **Allahabad Session of Muslim League, 1930**. {3,4,9}
- Source cited for this: R.C. Majumdar, "The History and Culture of the Indian People: Struggle for Freedom", page 613.
- Iqbal was inspired by the spirit of Pan-Islam.
- In that address Iqbal declared Hindus and Muslims two separate nations in the religious, social, cultural and historical context.
- Iqbal said: "I would like to see Punjab, the North-West Frontier Province, Sindh and Balochistan amalgamated into a single state."
- Iqbal said: self-government within or without the British Empire, a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim state, is the final destiny of Muslims, at least of North-West India.
## The word Pakistan
- The word **PAKSTAN** (later Pakistan) was first used/coined in **1933** by **Chaudhary Rahmat Ali**, a Cambridge University student and Pakistani nationalist. {1,2}
- He used it in his pamphlet (letter) **"Now or Never; Are we to Live or Perish for Ever?"** {10}
- The name came from five northern units of British India: **Punjab, Afghan Province (North-West Frontier Province), Kashmir, Sindh and Balochistan**.
- The idea of Pakistan gained momentum and led to the independent state of Pakistan in 1947.
## Lahore Session and Pakistan Resolution 1940
- Muslim League session was held at **Lahore** from **22 to 24 March 1940**; there the League formally demanded Pakistan as an independent, sovereign state (division of India). {16}
- **Mohammad Ali Jinnah** presided over the Lahore Session, 1940. {18}
- The Pakistan (Lahore) Resolution was passed on **23 March 1940**. {11,12}
- The session demanded separate independent Muslim states in the North-Western and Eastern zones of India.
- Jinnah's **Two Nation theory** was accepted at the Lahore Session. {15}
- Jinnah led the movement for a separate Pakistan; at Lahore he said he would accept nothing less than a separate Muslim country. {5}
- The Pakistan Resolution was **drafted by Sikandar Hayat Khan**. {14}
- The resolution was presented by **Fazlul Haq** on 23 March 1940 and approved by **Khaliquzzaman**. {13}
- Sir Sikandar Hayat Khan, Fazlul Haq and Khaliquzzaman played an important role in preparing the outline of the Pakistan proposal.
- Resolution text: no constitutional plan is workable or acceptable to Muslims unless contiguous geographical units are demarcated into regions, with necessary territorial readjustments.
- First Pakistan Day was observed on **23 March 1941**; the League also celebrated Pakistan Day on **23 March 1943**. {17}
## Jinnah
- **Sarojini Naidu** called Jinnah the **"Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity"**. {6}
- She said so for his nationalist views and for bringing a compromise between Muslim League and Congress.
- In 1919 Jinnah gave testimony before the Parliament Select Committee and said he spoke as an Indian Nationalist.
- Asked by Major Oksbergi if he wanted to end Hindu-Muslim discrimination at the earliest, Jinnah said he would be the happiest person when that day came.
- Iqbal remarked to Nehru in a meeting: **"You are a patriot, while Jinnah is a politician."** {7}
- Nehru recorded this remark on page 352 of his book **"The Discovery of India"**.
- Jinnah supported the two-nation theory, presided over the Lahore Session 1940, and did not take part in the Non-Cooperation Movement (he did not support it); "he always had dairy" is not true. {8}
- Jinnah raised the slogan **"Divide and Quit"** during the Second World War. {19}
- Meaning: the British should first divide India into Hindustan and Pakistan and then leave India.
