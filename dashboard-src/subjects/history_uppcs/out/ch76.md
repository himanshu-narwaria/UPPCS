@ 76 | Communal Award and Poona Pact (1932)
## Communal Award
- The Second Round Table Conference reached no consensus on separate electorates for depressed classes and other communities.
- His Majesty's Government had declared (endorsed by Parliament) that if communities failed to agree, it would devise and apply a scheme.
- British PM **Ramsay MacDonald** announced the **Communal Award** on 16 August 1932. {1}
- The Award gave each minority some reserved seats in the legislatures, to be filled through separate electorates. {3}
- For the first time (August 1932) a separate electorate was made for the untouchables (depressed classes), now treated as a minority. {2}
- Depressed-class voters would also vote in the general constituency.
- Earlier Muslims and Sikhs were treated as minorities; Europeans, Anglo-Indians and Indian Christians had got separate electorates in 1919.
- Award gave separate electorates to Muslims, Indian Christians, Sikhs, Anglo-Indians, Europeans, depressed classes and others; only for provincial legislatures.
- **Buddhists** were not given separate electorates or reserved seats by the Award. {4}
## Gandhi's fast
- Gandhiji began his first fast unto death on 20 September 1932 against the Communal Award. {5,7}
- He fasted in **Yerwada Jail**. {6}
- The fast ended on 26 September 1932 after the Poona Pact was approved.
- 14 August 1931: **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** told Gandhi in Mumbai, "History tells that Mahatmas, like fleeting phantoms, raise dust, but raise no level." {25}
- Gandhi and Ambedkar were in conflict over the situation of the depressed classes.
## Poona Pact
- **Poona Pact** came after the Communal Award; it is called the pact of Dr. Ambedkar and Gandhiji. {12,14}
- It was signed on 24 September 1932 at 5 o'clock in the evening.
- Poona Pact concerned the depressed classes; its purpose was to give representation to the untouchables. {9,10}
- **M.K. Gandhi** did not sign the Poona Pact. {15}
- Signed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar for the depressed classes and Pt. Madan Mohan Malaviya for the Hindu community; it was between Gandhi's followers and Ambedkar.
- Other signatories: M.R. Jayakar, Devdas Gandhi, Vishwas, Raja Bhoj, P. Baloo, Gavai, Thakkar, Solanki, Tej Bahadur Sapru, G.D. Birla, Rajagopalachari, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Rao Bahadur Srinivasan, M.C. Rajah, C.V. Mehta, Bakhale and Kamath.
- Many people signed the document in Bombay; Rajagopalachari exchanged his pen with Dr. Ambedkar after signing.
- Reserved seats for depressed classes in provincial legislatures: 71 under Communal Award, raised to 148 by Poona Pact (147 in some books). {8}
- Poona Pact accepted joint electorates; 18% of general seats in the Central Legislature were reserved for depressed classes.
- Poona Pact defeated the purpose of the Communal Award; it paved the way for reserved seats for SC and ST in Parliament and State Assemblies. {11}

## Harijan work and organisations
- After the Poona Pact Gandhiji lost interest in Civil Disobedience and took up Harijan uplift as an all-India political and social programme. {17}
- **Mahatma Gandhi** founded the All India Anti-Untouchability League (All India Harijan Sevak Sangh) on 30 September 1932, to remove untouchability. {18,21,22}
- **All India Anti-Untouchability League** was the former name of the **Harijan Sevak Sangh**. {23}
- **Ghanshyam Das (G.D.) Birla** was its first President. {16,19,20}
- Gandhi started the weekly 'Harijan' in 1933; Harijan tours also followed.
- **Babu Jagjivan Ram** founded the All India Depressed Classes League. {24}
- B.R. Ambedkar founded the All India Scheduled Castes Federation in 1942.
- M.C. Rajah founded the All India Depressed Classes Association in 1926.
