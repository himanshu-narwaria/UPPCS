@ 16 | Muslim Invasion of India
## Islam and the names of India
- **Hazrat Muhammad**, propounder of Islam (the "Holy Prophet"), was born about **570 A.D.** in Mecca; he died in 632 A.D. {1}
- **Mecca** is in **Saudi Arabia**; it is a religious pilgrimage place for followers of Islam. {2}
- Rigveda mentions the Bharatas as a tribe/people; the land name Bharat/Bharatavarsa comes in later Sanskrit texts (e.g. Vishnu Purana).
- By tradition the country was named after Dushyanta's son 'Bharat'; some scholars say after Rishabhanath's eldest son 'Bharat'.
- In the Rigveda, Bharata is defined as 'Sapta Sindhu'.
- Most scholars feel the name "Hindu" came from invaders who could not pronounce the name of the Indus River.
- Iranians called the country 'Hindustan'; Greeks called it 'India'.
- The word 'Hindu' for the people of Hind was first used by Iranians/Persians (the exam answer given: the Arabs). {3}
## Arab conquest of Sindh
- First successful Muslim invasion of India: **712 AD**, when Arabs under **Muhammad-Bin-Qasim** conquered Sindh. {4,5}
- First Muslim invaders of India were the **Arabs**; first successful Muslim invader = Muhammad-Bin-Qasim. {6,7}
! Year of the first successful Muslim invasion is disputed among scholars: 711 A.D. per V.D. Mahajan, 712 A.D. per Harishchandra Verma.
- Before 712 AD a campaign under Ubaidullah was sent to India, but he was defeated and killed.
- Sindh was ruled by **Dahir**; Qasim defeated and killed him; Dahir ruled from his capital Arod (near modern Karachi).
- **Muhammad-Bin-Qasim** was an **Arab**, born around 695 A.D.; nephew and son-in-law of Iraq's king Al Hajjaj. {8}
- At the age of seventeen he was sent by Caliph al-Walid to lead an army to Sindh.
- The Arab empire could not spread east of Sindh and Multan and gradually declined.
- Dr. Stanley Lane-Poole: Arab conquest of Sindh "remained only an episode in history of India and Islam… an incomplete victory"; the Turks completed the task.
- The Persian text **'Chachnama'** tells about the Arab conquest of Sindh.
- Chachnama ("Story of Chach") = main source for Sindh's history in the 7th–8th century A.D.; written in Arabic; author not clearly known.
- It is named after Raja Chach of Sindh, whose son Dahir opposed Muhammad Bin Qasim.
- Per Chachnama, **Arod** was the capital of the Indus country in the 6th and 7th century A.D. {9}
- Chachnama was translated into Persian by **Muhammad Ali bin Abu Bakr Kufi**. {10}
- Chachnama covers Sindh's history from 680 to 718 A.D.; Mirza Kalich Beg translated it fully from Persian to English in 1900 A.D.
## Mahmud of Ghazni
- Towards the end of the 9th century, Trans-oxiana, Khurasan and parts of Iran were ruled by the Samanids (Iranian by descent).
- **Alptigin**, a Turkish slave among the Samanid governors, set up an independent kingdom with capital **Ghazni** and founded the **Ghaznavid dynasty**. {11}
- Ghaznavid dynasty is also called the **Yamini dynasty**; it soon took over the Samanid kingdom.
- Turkish conqueror **Mahmud** succeeded his father and became ruler of Ghazni in **998 AD**; built a huge Central Asian empire with capital Ghazni (Afghanistan).
- Mahmud attacked India **17 times** between 1000 and 1027 AD (17 times per Sir Henry Elliot); his aim was to plunder wealth, not to set up permanent Muslim rule. {12}
- Baghdad's Caliph Al Qadir-Billah gave Mahmud the titles **'Yamin-ud-Daula'** and **'Amin-ul-Millah'**.
- Mahmud first invaded the Chandelas in 1019–20 AD; Chandela king **Vidyadhar**, the most powerful ruler of the dynasty, repulsed his attacks. {13,14}
- Chandelas ruled Central India from the 9th to the 13th century; first capital Khajuraho, later shifted to Mahoba.
- Mahmud plundered the **Somnath temple** (1025/26 AD) during the reign of Bhimdev I (1022–63).
- After Mahmud left with the loot, **Bhimadeva I** rebuilt the Somnath temple. {23}
- Somnath temple is in Gujarat on the shore of the Arabian Sea; one of the 12 **Jyotirlingas**; Al-Biruni described it in his travelogue. {24}
- India's first President Dr. Rajendra Prasad (not S. Radhakrishnan) was present at the Pran Pratishtha of the present Somnath temple.
- Mahmud introduced **silver coins** with Arabic legend on the upper side and **Sanskrit** legend (Sharada script) on the other. {25}
- In the middle of the coin was written in Sanskrit: "Avyaktmekam Muhammad Avatar Nripati Mahmud".
## Mahmud's court scholars
- Mahmud patronised Persian learning and respected scholars and artists.
- **Utbi** = court historian of Mahmud; wrote Kitab-ul-Yamini / 'Tarikh-i-Yamini'. {15}
- Other court members: Albiruni, Baihaqi (writer of Tarikh-i-Subuktigin), Persian poets Uzari and Unsuri, Asjadi and Farrukhi.
- **Firdausi** (not Firishta), scholar and court poet of Mahmud Ghaznavi, wrote **Shahnama**; titled 'Homer of the East'. {17}
- **Firishta** (1560–1620 A.D.), full name Muhammad Qasim Hindu Shah Astarabadi Firishta, wrote **Tarikh-i-Firishta** (Gulshan-i-Ibrahimi).
- Firishta lived for some time in Murtza Nizam Shah's court at Ahmadnagar, then went to Bijapur; his book is dedicated to Ibrahim Adil Shah II of Bijapur.
- **Al-Biruni** came to India with Mahmud of Ghazni in the **11th century** and stayed many years; called the father of Indian historical writing. {19,20,21}
- Al-Biruni was the **first Muslim to study the Puranas**. {18}
- Al-Biruni was born in 973 AD in Khwarizm (now Khiva), Central Asia.
- His interests: history, astronomy, geography, logic, medicine, mathematics, religion and theology.
- He studied Sanskrit and used the works of Brahmagupta, Balabhadra and Varahamihira.
- Al-Biruni wrote **'Tahqiq-i-Hind' (Kitab-ul-Hind; 'Reality of Hindustan')**, his most important work, in Arabic; it describes the social and cultural conditions of India.
- Edward Sachau translated it from Arabic into English; Rajnikant Sharma translated it into Hindi.
- Al-Biruni translated Patanjali's Yoga-Sutra into Arabic (Kitab Patanjal).
- Al-Biruni was **not a secular author**: he gave no sympathetic reference to Mahmud's raid on Somnath in 1025/1026. {22}
- True of Al-Biruni: writing influenced by India; a Sanskrit scholar; expert in trigonometry.
## Muhammad Ghori
- **Shihabuddin Muhammad Ghori**, Ghurid ruler of Ghor (present Afghanistan), first invaded India in 1175 AD (Multan); attacked Multan and Uch in 1175–76.
- In 1178 AD Ghori invaded Gujarat; **Mularaja II (or Bhimdev II)**, led by his widowed mother Naika Devi (the real regent), defeated him near Mount Abu — Ghori's first defeat in India. {27}
- After this Ghori never tried to attack India from Gujarat's side.
- 1191 AD: first battle of Tarain — Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Ghori.
- 1192 AD: **second battle of Tarain** — Ghori defeated Prithviraj Chauhan and conquered North India. {26}
- The second battle of Tarain laid the foundation of Muslim power in India; a turning point and the most important battle in Indian history. {31}
- Ghori's alertness and skilled battle techniques were the main factors in the Muslim victory.
- Prithviraj, trying to escape, was caught near Sursati (modern Sirsa, Haryana).
- Hasan Nizami's widely accepted view: Prithviraj's son was appointed to govern Ajmer; Prithviraj was given the death penalty when he tried to conspire for a revolt.
- **Battle of Chandawar (1194 AD)**: Ghori defeated Gahadavala king **Jayachandra** of Kannauj. {28,29,30}
- Chandawar is in present Firozabad district (U.P.), on the bank of the Yamuna near Agra.
- Coins of **Ghori** bear the image of Goddess **Lakshmi**; on some others the Kalma is inscribed in Arabic. {33}
- Soon after Ghori's victories the **Iqta system** was introduced in northern India.
- In 1192 AD Ghori (Muhammad Sam of Ghori) appointed **Qutbuddin Aibak** in charge of his Indian territories and made him administrator of Kuhram and Samana (first Iqta). {34}
- Aibak played a significant role in the second battle of Tarain; he ran northern India as Ghori's representative till 1206 AD and expanded Turkish power.
- Order: Mahmud Ghaznavi (1000–1027) → Muhammad Ghori (1175–1205) → Genghis Khan (1221) → Taimur (1398). {32}
- Genghis Khan threatened India in 1221 A.D. while chasing Jalaluddin Mingburni, son of the fugitive Khwarizm Shah.
- Taimur attacked India in 1398 A.D.
## Bakhtiyar Khalji in Bihar and Bengal
- Ghori and Aibak never thought of conquering Bihar and Bengal; their ordinary slave **Ikhtiyar-ud-din Muhammad-Bin Bakhtiyar Khalji** did it. {35,36,37}
- He conquered Bihar (1193–1203 AD; another passage gives 1193 to 1206 A.D.), captured the capital Odantapuri and destroyed **Nalanda** and Vikramshila. {38}
- He invaded Bengal between 1198 and 1204–05 AD; ruler Lakshmana Sena left without fighting.
- The Turkish army entered and plundered the capital Nadia; the city surrendered in the king's absence.
- Lakshmana Sena took refuge in East Bengal and ruled there for some time.
- Bakhtiyar Khalji did not try to conquer the whole of Bengal; he made **Lakhnauti** his capital.
## Why the Turks succeeded
- Turkish invasions succeeded because North India had no political unity: constant warfare among Indian rulers and many small kingdoms. {39,40}
- A strong kingdom in the north would have deterred the Turks; there was no unified resistance.
- Mahmud of Ghazni and Muhammad Ghori invaded northern India between the 11th and 12th centuries; Ghori's invasion led to the Delhi Sultanate.
- Per Professor Habib, the Turkish victory of the North-Western province gave birth to an 'Urban Revolution' and a 'Rural Revolution'.
