@ 50 | Socio-Religious Movements
## Nature of 19th century reform
- Early 19th century: a new modern vision arose among enlightened sections of Indian society; it shaped events for decades.
- This reawakening is sometimes, not with full justification, called 'Renaissance'; it also gave rise to some undesirable by-products.
- 19th century religious reform movements mainly attracted **intellectuals, urban upper castes and liberal princes**; the poor ordinary class stayed almost outside. {1}
- The movements mostly affected intellectuals and middle-class people.
- Socio-religious movements led to the modernisation of India because rationalism and scientific temper were at their core. {2}
- All reforms (Hindu, Muslim, Sikh, Parsi) aimed to spread modern education and fight social evils.
- Reformers stressed a rational understanding of social and religious ideas and a scientific, humanitarian outlook.
- The **educated Hindu middle class** was the first to be influenced by Western civilisation. {3}
- The new Western-educated class was deeply affected by rationalism, scientism and humanism.
- Reformers, inspired by the Enlightenment, rejected superstition, pilgrimage and idolatry.
- Brahmo Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission and Arya Samaj had no political mission but helped develop the spirit of patriotism. {144}
- All three helped social reform and the development of education.
- Dayanand Saraswati, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar and Raja Ram Mohan Roy were all proficient in Sanskrit. {141}
- Chronology: Tulsidas (16th century) → Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772–1833) → Dayanand Saraswati (1824–1883) → Vivekananda (1863–1902). {64}
## Raja Ram Mohan Roy
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** = Father of Indian Renaissance, Father (Prophet) of Indian Nationalism, Father/Maker of Modern India, First Modern Man, Yugdoot, 'Bridge between past and future'. {4,5,6,7,8,9,10}
- He was the first Indian to start a movement against the evils of Indian society.
- **Atmiya Sabha** (1815, Calcutta) = first society founded by Roy; to spread monotheistic ideals of Hinduism; not founded by Debendranath Tagore. {11,12,114}
- Atmiya Sabha's followers were mostly rich and influential youth.
- Calcutta Unitarian Committee was set up by Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Dwarkanath Tagore and William Adam.
- Mughal Emperor **Akbar II** gave Ram Mohan Roy the title 'Raja' in 1830. {18,19}
- Akbar II sent him as ambassador to the court of British King William IV in 1830 to plead for a rise in his pension and allowances.
- Roy died of meningitis on 27 September 1833 at Stapleton near Bristol, England; his Samadhi (mausoleum) is at **Bristol**. {20}
- Roy was an ardent advocate of Western education through English; he did not protest against it. {25}
- Roy's view: moderate Western education is needed to pull Indians out of darkness and give them partnership in administration.
- Roy's letter of 11 December 1823 to Lord Amherst opposed a Sanskrit school under Hindu pandits.
- In the letter he said the Sanskrit system would keep the country in darkness.
- He asked for instruction in mathematics, natural philosophy, chemistry, anatomy and other useful sciences, taught by Europe-educated men in a well-equipped college.
- Roy opposed idol worship, child marriage and Sati.
- Roy loved and respected the traditional philosophical systems of the East, and wanted Indians to accept rationalism, science, human dignity and equality of men and women. {27}
- Roy held that the purest form of Hinduism is contained in the Upanishads. {135}
- Roy launched the Bengali weekly **Samvad Kaumudi** on 4 December 1821; its official editor was Bhavanicharan Bandopadhyay, not Roy. {29}
- Samvad Kaumudi led the campaign against Sati (widow burning) and backed other progressive values.
- **Samachar Chandrika** (1822) was started by Bhavani Charan Bandyopadhyay to oppose Roy's religious and social ideas. {26}
- Roy strongly opposed the **Jury Act of 1826**. {116}
- Jury Act 1826 brought religious distinction into the judicial system, reflecting British religious superiority.
- Under it only Christians could be jurors in trials of Christians (Indian or European); Christians could also judge Hindus and Muslims.
- Hindus and Muslims could be jurors only in trials of Hindus and Muslims, not of Christians.
## Brahmo Samaj
- **Brahmo Sabha**, later called Brahmo Samaj, was founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy on 20 August 1828 at Calcutta. {14,16,17,112}
- Roy formed it in a house rented from Feringhee Kamal Bose; Tarachand Chakroborty was its first secretary.
- Brahmo Samaj = first Hindu reform movement influenced by modern Western liberal ideas and a new reading of ancient Indian texts.
- Brahmo Samaj was based on **monotheism**: one omnipresent, omniscient God; universal worship. {24,142}
- It opposed idolatry and denied the need for a priestly class; it did not teach that the Vedas are infallible. {28}
- It denied anthropomorphism and sacerdotalism; it had no place for social customs and rituals.
- It stressed humanity without regard to colour and character, and aimed to improve the condition of women.
- **Debendranath Tagore** founded **Tattvabodhini Sabha** in 1839 at Kolkata to spread Roy's ideas. {30}
- Tattvabodhini Sabha promoted rational thinking, study of ancient texts and the essence of the Upanishads; it merged into Brahmo Samaj in 1859.
- Debendranath Tagore = Hindu philosopher and religious reformer.
- Maharishi Debendranath Tagore (1817–1905) joined Brahmo Samaj in 1843 and carried it forward as a theist movement.
- Debendranath Tagore appointed **Keshab Chandra Sen** as Acharya of Brahmo Samaj. {15}
- Keshab Chandra Sen joined Brahmo Samaj in 1857, made it popular and opened branches in Uttar Pradesh and Madras.
- Debendranath stripped Keshab of the title 'Acharya' in 1865 over differences of opinion.
- **Keshab Chandra Sen** founded **Bharatiya Brahmo Samaj** (Brahmo Samaj of India, Neo/Navin Brahmo Samaj) on 11 November 1866. {22}
- **Adi Brahmo Samaj** = the original Samaj under Debendranath Tagore after the 1866 split; Roy did not found it. {23,136}
! Historians B.L. Grover and R.L. Shukla, other books and exam bodies wrongly called Keshab Chandra Sen the founder of 'Adi Brahmo Samaj'; the book says the fact is the opposite.
- Sources cited for this: thebrahmosamaj.net, Gazetteer of India vol. II, and Macmillan's history by Puri, Das and Chopra.
- In 1878 Keshab married his daughter, under 14, to the king of Cooch Behar; the Samaj split again.
- Anand Mohan Bose and Shivnath Shastri then formed **Sadharan Brahmo Samaj**.
- Keshab Chandra Sen founded the 'Tabernacle of New Dispensation' temple and the Indian Reform Association. {21}
- Indian Reform Association was formed on 29 October 1870 with Keshab Chandra Sen as president.
- Keshab Chandra Sen said Brahmovad should be made the religion of the world.
- Young India was published by Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi.
- Keshab's reform programme: against child marriage and polygamy; for widow remarriage and inter-caste marriage.
- Under Keshab Chandra Sen, Brahmo Samaj campaigned for women's education; Vinoba Bhave founded Sarvodaya Samaj to work among refugees. {95}
- Sarvodaya Samaj also aimed to raise Indians' living standard and promote Gandhi's principles.
## Ramakrishna and Vivekananda
- **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa**: Hari, Ram, Allah and Christ are names of the same God; there are many ways to God and salvation.
- **Sharadamani** Mukhopadhyaya (Sharada Devi) = wife of Ramakrishna Paramhansa; married at age five in 1859. {45}
- **Swami Vivekananda** (1863–1902) spread Ramakrishna's message in simple language and emerged as the preacher of **Neo-Hinduism**. {31}
- Vivekananda represented India at the Parliament of the World's Religions, **Chicago, 1893**. {32,34,35,36}
! On the question asking the year of the Chicago speech (options 1863, 1892, 1881, 1894), all options were wrong and M.P.P.S.C. gave bonus marks to all.
- Vivekananda tried to fit Ramakrishna's message to the needs of contemporary Indian society.
- At Chicago he gave a short introduction to Hinduism and spoke on "The meaning of the Hindu religion".
- After Chicago he visited the United States and England and promoted Hinduism.
- Vivekananda said the Hindu religion had become restricted to food only.
- He was displeased at religion's silence on the exploitation of the poor by the wealthy.
- He gave no political messages but raised self-dignity in the new generation; he was a staunch patriot.
- Subhas Chandra Bose: "So far as Bengal is concerned, Vivekananda may be regarded as the spiritual father of the modern nationalist movement."
- Vivekananda propagated **Practical Vedanta**: Vedanta should be practical in every sphere of life. {37}
- His first speech on Practical Vedanta was on 10 November 1896 in London.
- Vivekananda wrote Raja Yoga, Karma Yoga, Bhakti Yoga and Jnana Yoga. {38}
- Vivekananda = 'Patron saint' of India; quote: "Truth can be stated in a thousand different ways, yet each one can be true."
- Vivekananda was a leading thinker of the Indian Renaissance.
- **Ramakrishna Mission** was founded by Swami Vivekananda in **1897** (May 1897), not by Ramakrishna Paramhansa. {39,40,41,42,43,44,113}
- He founded it with his sanyasi associates and lay disciples of Sri Ramakrishna.
- The Mission was officially registered in 1909 under the Societies Registration Act 1860.
- Headquarters: Belur, Howrah (West Bengal).
- Twin ideals: "For one's liberation and the welfare of the world at large".
- The Mission = non-political, non-sectarian spiritual movement doing humanitarian, social and cultural service; inspired by ancient Indian culture.
- Its ideal is Pure Vedanta; main aim = highest spiritual upliftment of the inner self.
- It accepted traditional Hindu practices such as idol worship, rituals and symbols in their original form.
- Second feature: belief in the truth of all religions; all faiths are different ways to the one God.
- It works in education, health, welfare of the poor, relief and rehabilitation, arts and culture, morality, ethics and spirituality, regardless of caste, creed and religion.
- It was the last famous religious movement of the 19th century; aimed at harmony of religions, of East and West, of ancient and modern.
- **Sister Nivedita**: "The British Empire is rotten to the core, corrupt in every direction and tyrannical and mean." {47}
## Dayanand Saraswati and Arya Samaj
- **Dayanand Saraswati** (original name Mulshankar / Mool Shankar Tiwari) was born in 1824 in a rich Brahmin family in Morvi princely state, Kathiawar, Gujarat.
- He wandered for fifteen years (1845–60) in search of knowledge.
- In 1860 he came under Swami Virajananda Saraswati at Mathura; he began preaching in 1864.
- In 1867 he hoisted the **Pakhand Khandini Pataka** on the bank of the Ganga against superstition and bad rituals.
- In November 1869 he held a great Shastrartha with leaders of Hindu orthodoxy at Kashi.
- **Arya Samaj** was founded by Dayanand Saraswati in April **1875** at Bombay. {48,49,50}
- Arya Samaj headquarters shifted to Lahore in 1877, which speeded up the movement.
- Arya Samaj spread rapidly as a reaction against Western influences.
- Aims: re-establish Vedic religion in pure form; integrate India socially, politically and religiously; promote noble ideas true for all mankind.
- Arya Samaj advocated peaceful and progressive human living; Dayanand used it as a platform against the social evils of Hindu society.
- Dayanand gave the slogan **"Go back to the Vedas"**: a call to revive Vedic learning and purity of religion. {51,52}
- He called all beliefs added after the Vedic period false religion; he denied the authenticity of the Puranas, blaming them for idolatry.
- He rejected idol worship, incarnations and tantra-mantra; he worked against child marriage and casteism.
- He popularised the doctrine that the Vedas are infallible; he identified Hinduism with the religion instituted in the Vedas.
- His aim was the oneness of all religions, of society and of the nation.
- He made a logical, scientific and critical analysis of faith.
- Dayanand is called the **"(Martin) Luther of India"** for his religious reforms. {53}
- **Satyarth Prakash** (The Light of Truth), 1875, is Dayanand's masterpiece (magnum opus) and the sacred book of Arya Samaj. {54,55,56,57,58}
- Other works: Rigvedadibhashya Bhumika, Rigveda Bhashya, Advaitmat Khandan, Panch Mahayajya Vidhi and Ved-Viruddh-mat-Khandanam.
- Dayanand was the first to use the word **'Swaraj'** and accepted Hindi as the national language. {62}
- He was the first to urge giving up foreign goods and adopting Swadeshi goods; these ideas were later used in the national movement.
- Dayananda said **"India for the Indians"**; per Annie Besant he was the first to say India is only for Indians; this was his political message. {63}
- He felt Western civilisation's stranglehold was a colonial design to submerge Indian national identity in the British Empire.
- **Shuddhi Movement** was started by Arya Samaj to bring back to Hinduism those who had converted to other religions. {59}
- Arya Samaj gave Hindus self-confidence and self-reliance, and it accepted the superiority of Hindus and rejected White Race superiority; the second does not explain the first. {60}
- Arya Samaj followers played an important role in the growth of knowledge and education.
- Arya Samaj split into two groups over Western education.
- Swami Shraddhanand (Mahatma Munshi Ram Vij) and Lekhraj opposed Western education; they set up 'Gurukul' in 1902.
- Lala Lajpat Rai and Hansraj supported Western education; they set up Dayanand Anglo Vedic College.
- Lala Lajpat Rai was an Arya Samaj member; he did not oppose its appeal to the authority of the Vedas.
## Prarthana Samaj and Ranade
- **Prarthana Samaj** was founded in 1867 at Bombay by **Atmaram Pandurang**, inspired by Keshab Chandra Sen. {67,68}
- It was a religious and social reform movement of **Maharashtra**, much like Brahmo Samaj. {66}
- **M.G. Ranade** joined in 1869 and was its chief guide; R.G. Bhandarkar also joined two years after its founding. {69}
- Aims: oppose the caste system, raise the marriage age of males and females, promote widow remarriage and women's education.
- Ranade = "Father (Precursor) of cultural renaissance in Western India"; his reform movement continued till the end of the 19th century.
- Dhondo Keshav Karve and Vishnu Shastri were Ranade's followers in social reform.
- Ranade and Karve started a widow remarriage movement and founded the 'Hindu Widows Home' to educate widows.
- **Indian National Social Conference** was founded in 1887 by **M.G. Ranade** and Raghunath Rao. {91}
- Its aims: abolish polygamy, child marriage and patriarchy (also printed: oligarchy); it met yearly alongside the Congress for many years.
- Ranade relied on legislation against social ills and worked against child marriage and the Purdah system.
- It was formed because the Congress did not want social reforms in its deliberations, keeping its platform for political affairs only. {94}
- **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** was launched on 2 April 1870 at Pune by G.V. Joshi, S.H. Sathe and S.H. Chiplonkar; Ranade joined in November 1871. {120}
- Poona Sarvajanik Sabha was one of the strongest political organisations of 19th century India.
- **Paramahansa Mandali** = secret socio-religious group founded in Bombay, **Maharashtra**, by Dadoba Pandurang Tarkhadkar. {150}
- Paramahansa Mandali began religious reform in Bombay; it promoted monotheism and reform in Hindu society. {151}

## Jyotiba Phule and anti-caste movements
- **Jyotiba Phule** was born in 1827 in a gardener's family; he ran a powerful non-Brahmin movement.
- Phule wrote **Gulamgiri**. {74,75}
- He also wrote 'Sarvjanik Satyadharm Pustak'; his books criticised the Brahmans' scriptures.
- Main aim of Satya Shodhak Samaj = uplift of backward classes: welfare of Dalits and lower castes. {78}
- It sought to free Shudras from Brahmanical scriptures, make them aware of human rights and end mental and religious slavery.
- Satya Shodhak Samaj was an **anti-caste movement in Maharashtra**, to free Shudras and Ati Shudras from Arya-Brahmin exploitation. {80,81}
- Savitribai headed its women's section and worked for lower-caste girls.
- 'Deenbandhu' helped spread the movement to the remotest parts of Maharashtra.
- The women's movement in India began largely under the inspiration of Jyotiba Phule. {143}
! On the women's movement question, the Bihar PSC considered no option correct and starred it; the book says Ramabai would have been correct if listed.
- Phule believed social evils could end only by uplifting women and Dalits; he opened the first girls' school in India in 1848.
- Ramabai Ranade, wife of M.G. Ranade, also inspired the women's movement; she founded 'Seva Sadan'.
- **Justice Party** (1916, Madras Presidency) called the Congress Brahmin-dominated and claimed communal representation for non-Brahmins like that given to Muslims. {118}
- Justice Party = non-Brahmin body for lower castes and backward classes; demanded social justice and educational opportunities.
- **Self-Respect Movement** was started by E.V. Ramaswami Naicker (Periyar) for equal rights of so-called lower castes in Tamil Nadu. {147}
- All India Anti-Untouchability League (later Harijan Sevak Sangh) was founded by Mahatma Gandhi in 1932. {146}
- All India Kisan Sabha was founded by Swami Sahajanand Saraswati in 1936 at Lucknow; Sahajanand President, N.G. Ranga General Secretary.
- **Bahishkrit Hitkarini Sabha** was formed by B.R. Ambedkar, not by Jyotiba Phule. {137}
- Young Bengal Movement: Henry Vivian Derozio; United Indian Patriotic Association: Syed Ahmad Khan.
- **Neo-Buddhism** = Dalit Bauddha Movement started by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar; it redefined Buddhism and founded the new sect 'Navayan'. {148}
- **Nadars** demanded the right to enter temples in 1899, causing a terrible riot in Tirunelveli district, Tamil Nadu. {133}
- **Tilak**: "If God were to tolerate untouchability, I would not recognise him as God at all." {134}
- Tilak said this at a conference on abolition of untouchability; he held all Indians are children of the same motherland.
- Tilak is called Lokmanya or Betaj Badshah.
- **Hali System** concerned bonded labour. {156}
- **Tarkeswar Movement** (1924, Bengal) was against corrupt priests (Mahant) of the Tarkeswar Shiva temple. {155}
- The Tarkeswar priests were accused of misbehaviour, harassment and unjust money demands on pilgrims.
## Maharashtra reformers and widow remarriage
- **Gopal Hari Deshmukh** (1823–92), reformer of Maharashtra born in Pune, was known as **'Lokahitvadi'**. {85,86}
- At 25 he began writing reform articles in the weekly 'Prabhakar' under the pen name Lokahitvadi.
- He wrote 108 articles in the first two years, known as 'Lokahitawadinchi Shatapatre'.
- He became a member of the Governor-General's Council in 1880.
- He appeared at the Delhi court in hand-woven Khadi as a supporter of national self-reliance.
- **Vishnu Parashuram (Shastri) Pandit** led the widow remarriage campaign in Maharashtra. {87}
- He founded the Widow Remarriage Association (Punarvivahottejak Mandali / Vidhwa Vivah Samaj) in the 1850s (also printed 1850) and launched the widow remarriage movement. {119}
- Vishnu Shastri published a Marathi translation of Vidyasagar's book in Indu Prakash in 1868.
- **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**, Principal of Sanskrit College, Calcutta, fought for widow remarriage using sanction of the Hindu Dharmashastras.
- **Hindu Widows Remarriage Act** (Act XV of 1856) was passed on 25 July 1856 through Vidyasagar's campaign. {97,99}
- The Act gave legal safeguards against loss of certain forms of inheritance for a remarrying Hindu widow.
- Vidyasagar was the first secretary of Bethune College (Hindu Female School, later Bethune Female School). {100}
- J.E.D. Bethune established a girls' school in Calcutta.
- Vidyasagar, asked if God exists: "I have no time to think about God because a lot of work has to be done on this earth." {46}
- **D.K. Karve** of Western India is linked with women's education and widow remarriage. {98}
- Karve was Secretary of the Widow Remarriage Association. {138}
- Karve founded the Hindu Widows Home in 1896 at Pune and the first Women's University in 1916 at Bombay.
- Vishnushastri and D.K. Karve were leaders connected with widow welfare.
- **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** Pantulu founded the Rajahmundry Social Reform Association (printed dates: 1871 and 1878), to encourage widow remarriage. {93}
- Veeresalingam = reformer of Madras Presidency; father of the Telugu Renaissance movement.
- Through the Association he fought the hiring of nautch girls and led a campaign for widow remarriage.

- Roy appealed to William Bentinck to ban Sati; Roy opposed Sati, he was not its supporter.

- A clause in the Charter of 1833 asked for slavery to end at the earliest.
- In 1843 slavery was declared illegal all over India; slaves were freed without compensation to owners.
- Native Marriage Act legalised unorthodox inter-caste marriages and banned polygamy.
- Sharda Act was amended in 1978, then repealed and replaced by the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006.
- At present the marriage age is 18 years for girls and 21 years for boys.
- Religious Disabilities Act was never passed in India; Dalhousie's policy of annexation was presented in 1848.
- **Behramji M. Malabari** = greatest Parsi social reformer of the 19th century; born 1853 in a Parsi family at Baroda, Gujarat. {88}
- Malabari, a Parsi Zoroastrian poet and author, edited the **Indian Spectator** and the **Voice of India**. {106}
- Malabari campaigned against child marriage and for widow remarriage through editorials; he is famous for legally eliminating child marriage.
- Malabari advocated the Age of Consent Act 1891; **Tilak opposed** it. {90}

## Theosophical Society
- Founders: Russian (now Ukrainian) noblewoman Helena Petrovna Blavatsky, American army officer Colonel Henry Steel Olcott, William Quan Judge and others.
- Blavatsky and Olcott moved to India in 1879, where the Society spread rapidly.
- In 1882 they set up the international headquarters at Adyar, a suburb of Madras (Chennai).
- **Annie Besant** was the main reason for the Society's success in India. {110}
- Annie Besant came in contact with the Society in 1882, became a member in 1889 and came to India in 1893.
- She became chairman of the Society after Colonel Olcott's death in 1907.
- She held Hinduism to be the most spiritual and esoteric religion and believed in rebirth.
- Theosophical Society is linked with Annie Besant, not with Mahadev Govind Ranade. {111}
- Mahatma Gandhi was born on 2 October 1869 at Porbandar, not Gandhinagar. {145}
## Muslim movements
- **Deoband Movement** began in **1866** at Deoband, Saharanpur district, U.P., to overthrow British rule and restore Islamic rule. {152}
- Darul Uloom Deoband's foundation was laid on 31 May 1866.
- Founders: Muhammad Qasim Nanautawi, Rashid Ahmed Gangohi, Haji Syed Muhammad Abid, Mahtab Ali, Zulfiqar Ali, Fazlur Rahman Usmani, Haji Fazle Haq and Shaikh Nihal.
- **Dar-ul-Ulum** Nadwatul Ulama was set up at Lucknow in 1898; it is linked with **Maulana Shibli Numani**, a teacher there. {149}
