@ 31 | Jahangir
## Family and accession
- Jahangir (Salim) was born on 30 August 1569 AD.
- His first marriage (1584 AD) was with Man Bai, daughter of Bhagwandas, king of Amber, and sister of Man Singh.
- **Khusrau**, son of Man Bai, was the eldest son of Jahangir. {27}
- Salim's second marriage was with Jagat Gosain, daughter of Uday Singh (the Mota Raja); Shahzada Khurram was her son.
- Jahangir was crowned in Agra fort in November 1605 AD, a week after Akbar's death; took the title **Nuruddin Muhammad Jahangir Badshah Ghazi**.
- Following Akbar's tradition, Jahangir began his rule generously and issued various populist (welfare) orders.
- Jahangir gave high posts to his associates; he also rewarded Raja **Vir Singh Bundela**, the murderer of Abul Fazl. {25}
## Mansab change and Mewar
- **Do-aspa and Sih-aspa** system was introduced by Jahangir. {1}
- Under it mansabdars kept extra soldiers without a rise in rank (zat).
- Do-aspa: mansabdar kept double the horses needed for his 'Sawar' rank; Sih-aspa: three times.
- **Treaty of Chittor**, 1615 AD: between Jahangir (who authorised Prince Khurram) and Rana Amar Singh of Mewar. {2}
- Rana accepted Mughal suzerainty; Jahangir returned Mewar and Chittor, which the Mughals had annexed in Akbar's reign.
## Nur Jahan and the Junta
- Nur Jahan = cultured, educated, intelligent, dominating and ambitious wife of Jahangir.
- **Nur Jahan's Junta** had five members: herself, her mother Asmat Begum, her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg (Itmad-ud-Daula), her brother Asaf Khan and Prince Khurram; Jahangir and Mahabat Khan were not members. {28,29}
- Prince Khurram (Shah Jahan) was son-in-law of her brother Asaf Khan; allied with Nur Jahan from 1611 to 1622.
- When Nur Jahan swung her support to her son-in-law Shaharyar, Khurram left the junta and rebelled.
- Jahangir's reign under Nur Jahan's influence has two parts: 1611–1622 AD (Khurram in the group) and 1622–1627 AD (Khurram out of it).
## Revolts
- Those who rebelled against Jahangir: Khusrau, Khurram and Mahabat Khan (not Asif Khan). {26}
- Khusrau, the eldest son, revolted just after the coronation; suppressed in 1606 AD.
- Shahzada Khurram revolted in 1622 AD; defeated at Bilochpur in March 1623 under Mahabat Khan; conflict ended with submission by 1625 AD.
- Mahabat Khan revolted against Jahangir in 1626 AD.
## English and other visitors
- **William Hawkins** (1608–11 AD) was the first Englishman sent by the East India Company to Jahangir's court. {3}
- Hawkins came to Surat in 1608 AD, reached Jahangir's court at Agra in 1609 AD; stayed at court 3 years; got a mansab of 400.
- Jahangir gave Hawkins the title **'English Khan'** and pressed him to marry a lady from Armenia. {5,6}
- **Sir Thomas Roe** came to India as British ambassador in the reign of Jahangir; led the second delegation to his court. {7,8}
- Roe was the first ambassador sent by British King James I to Jahangir's court.
- Roe arrived at Surat on 18 September **1615** as ambassador of King James I. {9}
- Roe was received by Jahangir at **Ajmer** in January 1616 AD. {11}
- Roe accompanied the emperor to **Mandu**, Ahmedabad and Ajmer; also went hunting with him; stayed one year in Agra. {10}
- Roe (1615–1619 AD) gave a clear picture of Jahangir's rule; he was sent to win favourable facilities for British trade with India.
- Roe stayed in India till 1619 AD; returned to England with Jahangir's decree that the British would be welcomed at the Mughal court.
- Order of arrival: Ralph Fitch (1583, first English traveller to India) → William Hawkins (1608) → Nicholas Dawnton (1614–15) → Sir Thomas Roe (court, January 1616). {12}
- **Francisco Pelsaert** = Dutch merchant who came to India in Jahangir's period. {13,14}
- Pelsaert left a unique account of the Mughal empire: "Jahangir's India: The Remonstrantie of Francisco Pelsaert".
- Peter Mundy = English East India Company factor and traveller; came in the reign of Shah Jahan.
- Niccolo Manucci = Italian traveller who came to India at the time of the succession war.
## Painting and literature
- **Mughal painting reached its zenith under Jahangir**. {18,19}
- Earlier, painting was tied to the contents of manuscripts; Jahangir freed it from this bondage.
- Famous painters of the period: Farrukh Beg, Daulat, Mansur, Abul Hasan.
- Jahangir gave **Abul Hasan** the title **Nadir-ul-Zaman**; he was a portrait artist. {20}
- Jahangir gave **Ustad Mansur** the title **Nadir-ul-Asra**. {21}
- Ustad Mansur specialised in painting plants and animals (greatest painter of birds). {22}
- Famous painters of Jahangir's court: Manohar, Bishandas, Mansur; Basawan was Akbar's court painter. {23}
- Jahangir, an outstanding author and critic, wrote his autobiography **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri** in Persian. {24}
- In it Jahangir recorded both his merits and demerits.
- The memoir has three parts: preface by Muhammad Hadi (written after Jahangir's death, on his early life); the original text by Jahangir; a third part by Muhammad Hadi.
- Babur wrote his autobiography 'Tuzuk-i-Babri' in Turkish.
## Tombs and buildings
- Jahangir died in 1627 AD; buried in a tomb garden at **Shahdara, Lahore** (Pakistan); Babur's tomb is at Kabul (Afghanistan); neither has a mausoleum in India. {15,16,17}
- **Tomb of Itmad-ud-Daula** at Agra was built by Nur Jahan between 1622–1628 AD after her father's death. {30}
- It stands on the left bank of the Yamuna; first tomb in India made entirely of white marble.
- **Pietra dura** = decorating walls with floral designs of semi-precious stones; first used in India in the tomb of Itmad-ud-Daula. {31}
