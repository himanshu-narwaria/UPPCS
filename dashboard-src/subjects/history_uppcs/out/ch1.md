## Periods of history
- **Prehistoric period** = period with no documented evidence of its history.
- **Proto-historic period** = period with evidence of a script that is still not deciphered; it lies between the prehistoric and historic periods.
- **Historic period** = time after which documented description is available.
- Stone Age comes under the Prehistoric period.
- Indus Valley Civilization and Copper Age culture come under the Proto-historic period.
- The historical period starts from around 6th century B.C.
- Three-age system (Stone, Bronze, Iron), from the collection of Copenhagen museum, was coined by **Christian Jurgensen Thomsen**. {2}
- Research on Stone Age civilization in India first started in 1863.
- Stone Age has three parts, based on difference of tools used: Palaeolithic, Mesolithic and Neolithic periods.

## Archaeologists and institutions
- **Robert Bruce Foote**: British geologist and archaeologist (as per Encyclopaedia Britannica); discovered the first Palaeolithic tool in India. {1}
- Robert Bruce Foote is considered the father of Indian pre-history; he was with the Geological Survey of India and documented Stone Age antiquities.
- Alexander Rea, A.H. Longhurst, Robert Sewell, James Burgess and Walter Elliot were associated with archaeological excavations; they worked mainly on South Indian history. {3}
- **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** works under the Ministry of Culture; premier body for archaeological research and protection of the nation's cultural heritage. {48}
- 1871 AD: Archaeological Survey set up as a separate department; Alexander Cunningham was its first Director-General.
- 1901 AD: in Lord Curzon's tenure it was centralized and named Archaeological Survey of India; John Marshall became Director-General in 1902.
- **Alexander Cunningham** (1814-1893 AD), British army officer with the Bengal Engineer Group, is called the 'Father of Indian Archaeology'. {49}
- National Museum of Mankind = **Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Manav Sangrahalaya**, at Bhopal (M.P.); autonomous body under the Ministry of Culture; country's biggest museum showing history of human development. {50,51}
## Palaeolithic period
- Palaeolithic period has three parts on the basis of tools: Lower, Middle and Upper.
| Palaeolithic phase | Tools |
| Lower Palaeolithic | Handaxe, Cleaver, Chopper |
| Middle Palaeolithic | Points, Borer, Scraper |
| Upper Palaeolithic | Burins, Blades |
- Chopper-chopping pebble tools were first found in the Sohan river valley of Punjab (Pakistan).
- Hand-axe tools were first found at Pallavaram and Attirampakkam near Chennai (Tamil Nadu); other tools of this culture: cleaver, scraper.
- Yale-Cambridge expedition led by De Terra did the most important research in Sohan valley in 1935.
- Belan valley research was directed by G.R. Sharma of Allahabad University; nearly 44 Lower Palaeolithic sites were found there.
- A bone-made Mother Goddess statue of this age came from Lohanda Nala in Belan Valley; it is now kept in the Prayagraj museum.
- Palaeolithic people lived a natural life and depended mainly on hunting.
- Palaeolithic people did not know fire, so they ate raw meat; they did not know farming or animal husbandry.
- First evidence of man (first human fossil) in India was found in the Narmada valley of Madhya Pradesh, discovered in 1982 A.D. {11,12}
- Stone Age people of the Vindhyas migrated to the Ganga Valley in the terminal Pleistocene period, because climate change had made the Vindhyan valley dry. {47}

## Mesolithic period
- Mesolithic period became known after A.C.L. Carlleyle discovered rock paintings in the Vindhya region (1867-68 A.D.).
- Excavated evidence shows domestication of animals began in the Mesolithic period. {4}
- Earliest evidence of animal domestication in India: **Adamgarh** (Narmadapuram, M.P.) and **Bagor** (Bhilwara, Rajasthan). {5,21}
- Many implements of bones and horns were found at the Mesolithic sites Mahadaha and Sarai Nahar Rai (Pratapgarh district, U.P.). {6}
- 'Puratattva Vimarsh' by Dr. Jai Narayan Pandey describes pointed objects and ornaments of bone from Sarai Nahar Rai, Damdama and Mahadaha. {7}
- Mesolithic sites from west to east: Mahadaha (Pratapgarh, UP) → Lekhahia (Mirzapur, UP) → Paisra (Bihar) → Birbhanpur (W. Bengal). {8}
- **Damdama** (Mesolithic site, Pratapgarh district, U.P.): 41 human graves; 5 are double burials; one triple burial (three skeletons in one grave). {9}
- A grave with four human skeletons was found at Sarai Nahar Rai.
- Lekhahiya rock shelter 1 (Vindhya region) gave 17 human skeletons as per 'Puratattva Vimarsh' by Dr. J.N. Pandey: the most from any Vindhyan rock shelter. {46}
- Some Lekhahiya skeletons were in relatively pristine condition; most others were warped.
- John R. Lucas of the University of Oregon (USA) says remnants of 27 human skeletons were found in Lekhahiya.
## Bhimbetka
- **Bhimbetka** rock shelters are in Abdullahganj, Raisen district (Madhya Pradesh). {32}
- Bhimbetka is famous for prehistoric rock paintings; it is the best example of rock paintings of the prehistoric era; its rock art is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. {30,31}
- Bhimbetka has 500 pictures in 750 rock shelters: the most pictures from any rock shelter in India. {33}
- Bhimbetka caves show the earliest traces of human life on the Indian subcontinent; pictures include elephant, sambhar and deer.
- Bhimbetka was discovered by **V.S. (Vishnu Shridhar) Wakankar** in 1957 (1957-1958); he first found the prehistoric importance of its rock paintings. {34,35,36}
## Neolithic period and start of farming
- Grains were first produced in the Neolithic Age; people were introduced to agriculture then. {10}
- Koldihwa and Mehrgarh are the two Neolithic sites with clear evidence of rice (rice bran) and wheat respectively.
- Fire was first used in the Neolithic period.
- Eight main food grains of modern human society: barley, wheat, maize, millet, sorghum, mustard, oat and rice.
- These crops first grew as weeds in different areas; humans later grew them from seed at different places and times.
- **Barley** was the first grain cultivated by man, in 8000 B.C., in western Asian countries between the northeast Mediterranean Sea and Iran. {13}
- Wheat was later cultivated in the same area around 8000 B.C.
- Latest research: earliest evidence of agriculture in the Indian subcontinent is from **Lahuradewa**, Sant Kabir Nagar district, Uttar Pradesh. {14}
- Lahuradewa: human activity and start of rice cultivation date to around 8000-7000 BC.
- Before this research, the earliest evidence was wheat at **Mehrgarh** (Balochistan, Pakistan), around 7000 BC. {15,16,17,18}
- Earliest evidence of rice before this research: rice bran from near the bank of river Belan, district Prayagraj, dated 6500 BC.
- Rule for the earliest-agriculture answer: Lahuradewa if it is among the options; if not, Mehrgarh.
- Koldihwa (Belan river valley, U.P.) was long held to be the oldest evidence of paddy cultivation in the world; Lahuradewa now gives the oldest evidence of paddy. {19}
- Oldest cultivated grains barley and paddy come from many excavated sites of U.P.: barley and paddy from Mahagada, paddy from Koldihwa.
- Mehrgarh (Kachhi plains, Balochistan) gave the earliest evidence of settled (permanent) life, around 7000 B.C. {20}
- Oldest dates: Kile Gul Muhammad 4000 B.C.; Kalibangan 3500 B.C.
- Cultural remains from the Stone Age to the Harappan Civilization were found at Mehrgarh, Balochistan (Pakistan). {25}
- **Burzahom**: prehistoric (Neolithic) settlement in a village of the same name in Kashmir valley; famous for pit dwelling and Neolithic tool industry, not for rock-cut shrines. {41}
- Burzahom: bones of dogs and antlered deer were found with human skeletons in many pits. {39,40}
- Burzahom: human and animal skeletons had trepanning (bored hole) marks; humans were buried in pits in a sitting position along with animal bones.
- First excavation at Burzahom: Helmut de Terra and Dr. Thomas Paterson, 1935 A.D.
- **Ash mounds** were found at the Neolithic site Sangana Kallu (Ballari district, Karnataka); also at Piklihal and Utnur. {29}
- Ash mounds are burnt relics of seasonal camps of the Neolithic herdsmen community.
## Megaliths
- **Megaliths** = burials made of large stones, in graveyards away from the habitation area. {28}
- Many megaliths are linked to burial or post-burial rituals, including memorials for those whose remains may or may not be available.
- Megalith types: Chamber tomb, Dolmens, Stone alignment, Stone circle (cromlech), Pit-circles.
- **Mangadu** (Kollam district, Kerala): important excavated Megalithic site; its monuments had 28 hard, compact, uneven laterite blocks. {45}
- Urn-burials at Mangadu show post-cremation fragmentary burials.
## Chalcolithic (Copper) Age
- Copper was the first metal used.
- Copper Age is also called the **Chalcolithic Age**: copper instruments were used along with stone. {22}
- Chalcolithic settlements in India: South-East Rajasthan, Western Madhya Pradesh, Western Maharashtra and South-East India.
- **Ahar civilization** (Chalcolithic) flourished around the Mewar region of Rajasthan; people used copper tools, knew copper smelting, and metal work was a means of their economy. {23}
- Ahar people knew rice.
- Ahar pottery: mostly black-and-red ware with white geometric designs.
- **Ahar-Banas culture** (2100-1500 BC) was contemporary with the Harappan Civilisation. {24}
- Ahar-Banas culture flourished in Mewar (Rajasthan), along the Banas River and its tributaries; main sites: Ahar, Gilund, Balathal.
- **Ganeshwar-Jodhpura culture** was in Rajasthan; hundreds of copper objects were found at Ganeshwar. {44}
- **Navdatoli** is near Khargone district, Madhya Pradesh. {27}
- Navdatoli was excavated by H.D. Sankalia, Professor of Deccan College, Pune. {26}
- Navdatoli was the most extensively excavated Chalcolithic site of the Indian subcontinent; dated between 1500 B.C. and 1300 B.C.
- Navdatoli had round and rectangular houses: round ones about 1-4.5 m in diameter, rectangular ones 5-6 m long (also printed as 5.6 m).
- Navdatoli roofs: probably hay, tree branches and leaves, on massive wooden posts 22 cm in diameter, set at regular intervals around the huts.
- Navdatoli: open spaces between posts had mud-plastered bamboo screens.
- Navdatoli floors: silt clay and river gravel, surface coated with lime.
- Navdatoli huts had one or two-mouthed 'chulhas', storage jars and other earthen vessels.
- **Jorwe culture** people lived in large rectangular houses with wattle and daub walls and thatched roofs.
- Jorwe people (Chalcolithic Maharashtra) believed in life after death; they buried the dead under the house floor, adults lying supine with head to the north (north to south). {38}
- Jorwe children were buried in two urns joined mouth-to-mouth, set horizontally in the pit.
- Jorwe culture sites: Daimabad, Inamgaon, Chandoli, Nevasa.
- **Inamgaon** (Pune district, Maharashtra): post-Harappan agrarian village; one of the most intensively and extensively excavated, well reconstructed Chalcolithic sites of the Deccan plateau.
- **Ochre-Coloured Pottery (OCP)** was discovered and named (christened) by B.B. Lal in 1950-52 at Hastinapur. {37}
- OCP features: leaves ochre colour on fingers when rubbed (looks badly fired at first sight); porous; always worn out at the edges.
- Most OCP evidence is found at Atranjikhera and Hastinapur.
## Other sites
- **Chandraketugarh** is in North 24 Parganas district, West Bengal (not Odisha); it was an important trading port city with huge walls.
- In early historic times Chandraketugarh was linked to the Ganga by the Vidyadhari river; it was an important trade centre and possibly a political centre.
- Chandraketugarh was a major centre of terracotta craft.
- Finds at Chandraketugarh: coins, pottery, seals and sealings, and figurines of ivory, wood and bronze.
- **Salihundam** (Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh): important Buddhist site with Buddhist stupas and a huge monastic complex on a hillock; no rock-cut cave shrines.
- Salihundam excavations gave relic caskets, four stupas, a Chaityagriha, structural temples and sculptures of three phases of Buddhism: Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana.
