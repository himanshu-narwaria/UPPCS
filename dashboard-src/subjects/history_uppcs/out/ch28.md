@ 28 | Mughal Dynasty: Babur
## Origin and early life
- Mughal dynasty was of Turkic-Mongol origin; Babur and the Mughal rulers belonged to the **Chagatai branch of Turks**. {3}
- The name Chagatai came from the second son of Genghis Khan, ruler of central Asia and Turkistan.
- Babur descended from the Turk-Mongol conqueror Timur on his father's side and Genghis Khan on his mother's side.
- Full name: **Zahiruddin Muhammad Babur**; founder of the Mughal empire in India. {20}
- Babur was born on February 14, 1483 AD at Ferghana to Umar Shaikh Mirza and Qutlugh Nigar Khanum; he was the eldest son of Umar Shaikh Mirza.
- After his father's death he took the throne of Ferghana (now in Uzbekistan) in June 1494 AD, aged 11 years and four months.
- Babur's accession began the **Timurid dynasty** in the region; he set up the Mughal dynasty in India after Panipat, 1526. {14,15}
- Babur's life teaches success through patience and resolution; his father died in his childhood, yet he founded the Mughal Empire in India. {23}
- Babur captured Samarkand in 1501 AD; it lasted only eight months.
- **Shaybani Khan** invaded Ferghana in 1501 AD, defeated Babur at the battle of Sar-e-Pul and occupied Samarqand. {4}
- Shaybani Khan used the Uzbek 'Tulughuma' technique to defeat Babur at Sar-e-Pul.
- Babur fled; for three years wandered the hills of Afghanistan and plains of Khurasan (central Asia).
- Babur made several attempts to retake Samarkand but was beaten each time by Shaybani Khan.
- After losing Samarkand and Ferghana, Babur moved to Kabul; after winning **Kabul in 1504** he gave up the ancestral title 'Mirza' and took the title **'Padshah'**. {21}
## Invitation to India
- **Alam Khan** = uncle of Ibrahim Lodi; invited Babur to invade India, claiming his right to the Delhi throne. {1}
- In 1524 AD, during Babur's 4th campaign, there was a bitter feud between Sultan Ibrahim Lodi and Daulat Khan, governor of Punjab.
- Ibrahim Lodi ordered Daulat Khan to his capital; **Daulat Khan** disobeyed and sent his son Dilawar Khan to Babur, asking help to dethrone Ibrahim and enthrone Alam Khan. {2}
- Babur had already got the invitation of Rana Sanga, king of Mewar; he felt sure the chance to conquer India had come.
## First Battle of Panipat
- **First Battle of Panipat**: 21 April 1526 AD, Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi; Ibrahim Lodi was defeated and killed. {6,10,11}
- Ibrahim Lodi became Sultan of Delhi in 1517 AD after his father's death.
- Ibrahim Lodi's army lost though larger in number; the whole area up to Delhi and Agra came under Babur.
- Babur declared himself ruler on April 27, 1526 AD and founded the Mughal empire in India.
- Babur used the **Ottoman system of war** at the first Battle of Panipat (1526). {5}
- Main reasons of victory: artillery (cannons, guns) led by Ali Quli, and the Tulughma and Araba tactics; gunners and horsemen also helped.
- Babur's tactics in North India included gunpowder firearms and field artillery.
- The main reason for Babur's victory at Panipat = his **skilled warfare**; his army was smaller than Ibrahim's. {7}
- **Skilled commandants** were the primary reason for Babur's victory over Ibrahim Lodi. {8}
- Babur on Ibrahim: "an inexperienced young man careless in his movements, who marched without order, halted or retired without method and engaged without foresight."
- Artillery (cannons) was used for the first time in India in the **first Battle of Panipat**. {9}
- Babur himself first mentioned using artillery and guns in the Battle of Bajaur (Pakistan), 1519 AD.
- Ustad Ali Quli led the artillery; Mustafa commanded the battalion of gunners.
- Babur's generosity earned him the title **'Qalandar'**.
- Gunpowder entered India before Babur (used to demolish fort walls); credit for using it in cannons and guns goes to Babur.
- Arch and dome were already introduced in the Sultanate period; Babur did not influence India's architecture.
- In 'Babur Nama' Babur described local architecture as lacking shape and balance.
- Babur ordered a garden built in Agra; also built a brick mosque in Panipat.
- Sher Shah, who defeated Babur's son Humayun, built the Qila-i-Kuhna mosque.
## Khanwa, Chanderi, Ghaghra
- Babur declared **'Jehad'** in the Battle of Khanwa. {16}
- **Battle of Khanwa**: March 16, 1527 AD; Babur defeated Rana Sanga of Mewar and took the title 'Ghazi'. {17,18,19}
- In 1528 AD Babur took Chanderi's fort; Medini Rai was killed in the war.
- Battle of Ghaghra, 1529 AD: Babur beat Mahmud Lodi (Afghan rulers); the last battle Babur fought.
- Babur's battles in order: First Panipat (1526) → Khanwa (1527) → Chanderi (1528, not 1530) → Ghaghra (1529). {12,13}
## Empire and administration
- Babur's empire included Kabul, Punjab and the area of modern Uttar Pradesh; Rajasthan was not included, being under various Rajput rulers. {22}
- He governed by allotting fiefs/jagirs and paraganas, appointing nobles/princes to territorial charges.
- Badakhshan (outside India) was given to Humayun; Kabul, Qandahar and Multan to Kamran.
- Mir Yusuf Ali was made governor of Lahore; Bheera, Lahore, Dipalpur, Sialkot, Sirhind and Hisar-Firoza were given to different officers.
- Babur brought innovative governance in the region from Ballia to Hisar, and Bayana and Gwalior; the empire stretched from Bihar up to Badakhshan.
## Babur's writings
- Babur wrote his autobiography **Tuzuk-i-Babri** (Babur Nama) in **Chagatai Turkish**; Turki was not the official language of the Mughal court, Persian was. {25,26,27}
- Babur Nama has an important place among autobiographies of the world.
- In Babur Nama Babur mentioned two Hindu states: Vijayanagara and **Mewar** (Chittor). {24}
- Babur wrote that when he was at Kabul, Rana Sanga had agreed: if the king reached Delhi from that side, Rana Sanga would invade Agra from his side.
- Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan translated Babur Nama into Persian by Akbar's order.
- Babur's verses are collected in the Turkish 'Diwan' (Diwan-i-Babur).
- Babur wrote 'Mubaiyan', a book of Muslim law, in a new verse style, Masnavi.
- Babur's 'Risal-i-Usaj' (Khat-i-Baburi) is considered the most innovative style.
- Babri Masjid in Ayodhya was built by **Mir Baqi**, Babur's commandant (army chief). {28}
