@ 26 | Provincial Dynasty of North India and Deccan Province
## Jaunpur and the Sharqi dynasty
- **Jaunpur** was founded in the 14th century by Sultan Firuz Shah Tughluq in memory of his cousin Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq, whose real name was Jauna Khan. {1,2}
- Firuz Shah Tughluq built 300 new cities.
- Firuz Shah Tughluq made Malik Sarwar governor of the region; he was a eunuch, notorious as the lover of Firuz Shah's daughter.
- The Sultanate was in disarray from factional fighting for power, so Malik Sarwar declared independence.
- Jaunpur became independent in 1394, in the reign of Sultan Mahmud Shah II; Khwaja Jahan **Malik Sarwar** founded the independent Sharqi kingdom of Jaunpur (not Malwa). {12}
- Malik Sarwar was a slave of Muhammad Shah II; the Sultan gave him the titles Malik-us-Sharq (Lord of the East) and Khwaja-i-Jahan.
- Malik Sarwar and his adopted son Mubarak Shah founded the Sharqi Dynasty (dynasty of the East).
- Jaunpur = independent Islamic kingdom in North India from 1394 AD to 1479 AD (about 85 years), ruled by the Sharqi Dynasty.
- In 1479 AD Bahlul Lodi defeated the last ruler **Hussain Shah Sharqi** and made Jaunpur part of the Delhi Sultanate again. {5}
- Ibrahim Shah Sharqi (1402–1440 AD, as per Gazetteer of Jaunpur) = greatest Sharqi ruler; a lover of knowledge and art; Jaunpur reached its greatest height under him.
- In his reign a new style, the **Sharqi style** (Sharqi art/architecture), emerged; he built monuments in it.
- In Sharqi style the simple type of minarets was removed and signs of Hindu influence began to appear.
- Under the Sharqis Jaunpur was a great centre of education and its cultural fame spread; it was called **Shiraz-i-Hind** (Shiraz of India / Shiraz of the East). {3,4,6}
- Vidyapati beautifully described Jaunpur and Ibrahim Shah in 'Kirtilata Kavya'.
## Kashmir: Zain-ul-Abidin
- Shahi Khan, brother of Ali Shah, took the throne of Kashmir as Zain-ul-Abidin in 1420 AD; greatest ruler of Kashmir.
- Zain-ul-Abidin (1420–70 AD) is called **Akbar of Kashmir** for his religious tolerance and good deeds (public welfare). {7}
- He abolished **Jizya** on Hindus and banned cow slaughter; he was the first ruler to do so. {8,9}
- Mahabharata, Dashavatara and Rajtarangini were translated into Persian in his period.
- The scriptures 'Jain Prakash' and 'Jain-Vilas' were compiled during his rule.
- He built the artificial island **Zaina Lank** in the middle of Wular Lake.
- Sikandar Butshikan built the Jama Masjid in Kashmir in 1400 AD; Zain-ul-Abidin expanded (completed) it.
- Features of the Kashmir Jama Masjid: turret, similarity with Buddhist pagodas, Persian style. {10}
## Rajput and hill states
- Scholars in the court of Kumbha: Muni Sunder Suri, Natha and Tilla Bhatt; Muni Jin Vijay Suri was in the court of Mughal emperor Akbar. {11}
- Correct ruler–state pairs: Rana Hamir – Mewar; Rana Chunda – Marwar; Malik Raja Farooqi – Khandesh.
- Medieval states Champaka (Chamba) and Kuluta (Kullu) were in present Himachal Pradesh; **Durgara** was in Jammu and Kashmir. {13}
- Champaka, Durgara and Kuluta were related to Rajputs.
- These three states rose after the downfall of the Tripartite-struggle states (Pal, Pratihara and Rashtrakuta).
- Gujari Mahal was built by Raja **Man Singh Tomar** between 1486–1516 AD; a unique piece of architecture; now converted into a museum. {39}
## Gujarat
- Zafar Khan declared himself Sultan of Gujarat in 1407 AD with the name 'Sultan Muzaffar Shah'.
- Muzaffar Shah defeated Hoshang Shah of Malwa and occupied Dhar, but later returned his kingdom.
- Fateh Khan took the throne in 1458 AD with the title 'Abul Fateh Mahmood'; famous in history as **Mahmud Begada**.
- Mahmud Begada's main victories: forts of Champaner and Girnar.
- **Bahadur Shah** of Gujarat surrendered Diu to the Portuguese. {26}
- In 1535 AD Bahadur Shah made a treaty with Portuguese governor Nuno De Cunha for help against the Mughal danger.
- In return the Portuguese were allowed to build a fortress in Diu and got a site in the harbour.
- After the Mughal danger receded, the Shah of Gujarat realised his mistake in letting the Portuguese build the fort.
## Bahmani kingdom and its successor states
- Bahmani kingdom arose in Muhammad-bin-Tughluq's reign from the rebellion of Amiran-e-Sadah in the Deccan.
- Zafar Khan (**Hasan Gangu**, 1347–1358 AD), a Sardar, took the title Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah and founded the Bahmani kingdom in **1347 AD** (14th century). {14,15,16,18,19}
- He made **Gulbarga** his (first) capital and named it Hasanabad. {17}
- He divided his empire into four provinces: Gulbarga, Daulatabad, Berar and Bidar; Gulbarga was the most significant.
- He annexed southern Hindu rulers and started a new system of giving powers and Jagirs to his followers.
- Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah abolished Jizya on the Hindus.

- Correct pairs: Baz Bahadur – Malwa; Qutb Shah – Golconda; Sultan Muzaffar Shah – Gujarat.
- **Ibrahim Adil Shah II** of Bijapur: skilful administrator, artist, poet, generous patron of arts; his people called him **Jagatguru** for his generous, tolerant approach. {21}
- He was also called 'Ablababa' or 'Friend of the poor' for helping the poor.
- Under him the dynasty had its greatest period; he extended its frontier south up to Mysore.
- He reverted to the Sunni sect of Islam but stayed tolerant of other religions.
- Ibrahim Adil Shah II wrote the music collection **Kitab-i-Nauras** (Book of Nine Rasas) in the Deccani language. {22}
- He founded a new capital/township, Nauraspur, to give shape to his idea of a musical city.
- In the same era Firishta completed the famous history 'Tarikh-i-Firishta'.
- **Gol Gumbaz** (Bijapur) = mausoleum of Muhammad Adil Shah; one of India's largest historic buildings and one of the world's largest domes. {35}
- Ahmad Nizam Shah founded the city/kingdom of Ahmadnagar.
- Nizam Shahi dynasty ended when Shahjahan conquered and annexed Ahmadnagar (1632) and Hussain Nizam Shah-III was imprisoned for life in Gwalior Fort. {23}
- Shahjahan sent an army under Mahawat Khan to win Daulatabad fort; he bribed Fateh Khan with 10.5 lakhs to take over the fort.
- Ruins of **Golconda** (capital of the Qutb Shahi dynasty) are scattered in Hyderabad. {25}
- Qutb Shahi kings ruled the region in the 16th–17th century; Aurangzeb later ended the dynasty and took Golconda; it was famous for diamonds.
## South Indian dynasties

- Dwarasamudra is now **Halebidu** (Hassan district, Karnataka). {34}
- Hoysala monuments are found at Halebidu and Belur (Karnataka). {33}
- Hoysaleshvara temple at Halebidu is dedicated to Shiva; Chennakeshava temple at Belur is in Hoysala style.
- None of Hoysala, Gahadavala, Kakatiya, Yadava set up their kingdoms in the early 8th century AD. {29}
- Hoysala Empire: powerful, ruled South India between the 10th and 14th centuries.
- Gahadwal dynasty: Rajput dynasty; ruled parts of present Uttar Pradesh and Bihar in the 11th and 12th centuries.
- Kakatiya dynasty ruled the eastern Deccan between the 12th and 14th centuries.
- Yadava dynasty of Devagiri: first feudatories of the Western Chalukyas; ruled from the 12th to the middle of the 14th century.
- **Rudrama Devi** belonged to the Kakatiya dynasty; king Ganapati had no sons, so she became ruler after his death. {31}
- Sovereign Kakatiya empire emerged under Rudradeva (Pratap Rudradev I); then Mahadev, then Ganapati Dev ruled.
- Rudrama = daughter of Kakatiya Ganapati Dev of Warangal; **fourth independent ruler** of the dynasty. {32}
- Rudrama defeated the Pandyas of South Tamil Nadu, Eastern Ganga rulers of Orissa and Seuna rulers of Devagiri.
- Palaiyakkarar or **Polygar** = feudal title of administrative and military governors appointed by the Nayaka rulers of South India, 16th to 18th centuries. {42}
