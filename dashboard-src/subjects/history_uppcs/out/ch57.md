@ 57 | Congress: Benaras, Calcutta, and Surat Session
## Gokhale and the Benaras session (1905)
- **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** presided over the 1905 Benaras session of the Congress. {2,3}
- Gokhale founded the **Servants of India Society** in the same year, 1905.
- The Congress took up the Swaraj call at the Benaras session, 1905; it was fully passed at the Calcutta session, 1906.
- Purpose of the Swaraj resolution: to secure self-rule, boycott British products, revive domestic products and Indian industrial production, and get a better education policy for India.
- Gokhale was born on 9 May 1866 in Ratnagiri (Maharashtra).
- He graduated from Elphinstone College in 1884 and became professor of Political Economy and History at Fergusson College, Poona.
- He was a protege of social reformer Mahadev Govind Ranade and a member of the Deccan Education Society.
- He first joined the Congress in 1889 at the Bombay session.
- He became a member of the Bombay Legislative Council and then of the Imperial Legislative Council.
- He fought for decades for greater political representation and power over public affairs for common Indians.
- Gokhale was the political mentor of Gandhiji, who often called him 'Mahatma'.
- A biographer on Gokhale: graduate at 18; professor and associate editor of the Sudharak at 20; Secretary of the Sarvajanik Sabha and of the Provincial Conference at 25; Secretary of the National Congress at 29; leading witness before an important Royal Commission at 31; provincial legislator at 34; Imperial legislator at 36; Congress President at 39; a patriot whom Mahatma Gandhi regarded as his master. {1}
## Calcutta session (1906)
- In 1906 the Congress was close to splitting over the presidentship of the Calcutta session; the split was avoided by agreeing on **Dadabhai Naoroji**, who presided. {4}
- The extremists were more popular than the moderates and could have taken over the Congress had they elected a leader from among them, but this was not done.
- The moderates chose Naoroji; he was respected by moderates and extremists alike.
- Four resolutions were passed at Calcutta in 1906: Swaraj (self-government), Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education; annulment of the partition of Bengal was not one of them. {20}
- 'Swaraj' was first uttered as the goal of the Indian people at the Calcutta session, December 1906, in Naoroji's presidential address. {7,8}
- Naoroji declared that the Congress goal, 'Self-Government or Swaraj', was the same as that of the United Kingdom.
- The moderates toned the resolution down to self-government as in the self-governing British colonies.
- At the Calcutta session of 1906 the flag of Swaraj for India was unfurled by the session president, Dadabhai Naoroji. {5}
- Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856-1920) said "Swaraj is my birthright, I shall have it"; he is taken as the first leader to use the word 'Swaraj'. {9}
! The word 'Swaraj' was first used by Dayanand Saraswati; Tilak is only the closest answer.
## Dadabhai Naoroji
- Dadabhai Naoroji is known as the **'Grand Old Man of India'**; he was a Parsi intellectual, educationist and early political and social leader. {10,11,12}
- He was the first Indian elected to the British Parliament (House of Commons), in 1892, from Central Finsbury. {15}
- He was elected on the ticket of the Liberal Party, not the Conservative Party. {13}
- He presided over the Congress three times (not four): 1886, 1893 and 1906. {14}
- He was the first Indian appointed professor of Mathematics and Natural Philosophy (Physics) at Elphinstone College, Bombay.
- In 1851 he started the Gujarati newspaper 'Rast Goftar'.
- He wrote the book 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India'.
- He worked as Professor of Gujarati at University College, London, and laid the foundation of women's education in Bombay.
- C.Y. Chintamani (editor, journalist, liberal politician): many intelligent and selfless leaders served the people of India, but in his era no one was like Dadabhai Naoroji.
- Gokhale said: "If there is the divine in man, it is in Dadabhai Naoroji" (also given as "if there is a God somewhere, he is Dadabhai Naoroji").
## Surat split (1907)
- The Congress split into Moderates (Naram Dal) and Extremists (Garam Dal) at the **Surat session**, December 1907, its 23rd session; this was the first split of the Congress. {16,17,18,23,24}
- The dispute was over the presidentship: Moderates wanted **Rash Behari Ghosh**, Extremists wanted Lala Lajpat Rai.
- Rash Behari Ghosh became President of the Surat session. {19,22}
- The Extremists wanted a guarantee that all four Calcutta resolutions would be accepted; the Moderates (liberals) were not in favour of continuing them.
- To pressure the Moderates the Extremists decided to oppose even the duly elected president Rash Behari Ghosh.
- Both sides came prepared for conflict; members fought with each other at the session and the Congress split.
- The Surat split was led by Tilak. {25}
- In 1907 the Extremists (Garam Dal) were led by Lala Lajpat Rai and the Moderates (Naram Dal) by Rash Behari Ghosh.
- Main reason for the split: the Extremists' lack of faith in the capacity of the Moderates to negotiate with the British Government. {26}
- The Extremists wanted to extend the Swadeshi and Boycott Movement; the Moderates did not.
- The process of the split began over the strategies of the Congress movement, the objectives of the Congress and people's participation in the movement. {21}
- The Indian National Congress was formed in 1885.
- Moderates and Extremists reunited at the Congress session of 1916, presided over by Ambika Charan Mazumdar, not S.P. Sinha.
