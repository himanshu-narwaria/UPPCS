@ 21 | Vijayanagara Empire
## Foundation
- **Vijayanagara Empire** was founded in **1336 AD** by the brothers **Harihara I and Bukka I**, on the banks of the Tungabhadra. {1,2,3,4,5,6}
- Their line is called the **Sangama dynasty** after their father Sangama; they were also known as the Sangama brothers.
- Harihara and Bukka were feudatories (warrior chieftains and loyal officials) of the Kakatiyas of Warangal.
- After Warangal fell to the Delhi Sultans they shifted to Kampili and became ministers.
- Muhammad Tughluq conquered Kampili and imprisoned them; it is believed they accepted (were forced to accept) Islam.
- They were later sent south as Tughluq commanders to suppress the Kampili revolt (one passage: they suppressed it; another: they could not succeed).
- Under the influence of Saint **Vidyaranya** they became Hindu again.
- Their Guru Vidyaranya (Madhav Vidyaranya) and his brother **Sayana**, famous commentator of the Vedas, inspired the founding of Vijayanagara.
- **Sayana**, commentator of the Vedic texts, was patronized by and flourished under the **Vijayanagara** rulers. {31,32}
- The empire was founded on the south bank of the Tungabhadra near Anegundi Fort.
- **Harihara I** founded a new city on the southern bank of the Tungabhadra (a tributary of the Krishna) and ruled as agent of a deity to whom all land south of the Krishna was supposed to belong. {8}
- Harihara I was the eldest son of Sangama; he built a fort on the west side of Karnataka; Bukka succeeded him.

- These four dynasties ruled for over three hundred years.
- Capitals of Vijayanagara in order: Anegundi, Vijayanagara, Penukonda, Chandragiri and later Vellore.
## Sangama rulers
- Harihara I ruled 1336–1356 AD.
- **Bukka I** (1356–77 AD) sent a mission to the Emperor of **China** in 1374 AD. {44}
- In 1365 the Vijayanagara army under **Kumara Kampana**, son of Bukka I, began and by about 1370 completed the conquest of the Pandyan country (Madurai region).
- Kumara Kampana merged Madurai into the Vijayanagara Empire.
- His wife **Ganga Devi** described his victories in her book **'Madhura Vijayam'**. {9}
- After Bukka's death in 1377 A.D. his son **Harihara II** took the throne with the title 'Maharajadhiraja'.
- Harihara II won Kanara, Mysore, Trichinapalli and Kanchi; the ruler of Ceylon (Sri Lanka) sent ambassadors, rich yearly presents/tax.
- Harihara II's biggest success: capturing **Belgaum and Goa** from the Bahamani state in the west. {10}
- Harihara II worshipped Virupaksha Shiva.
- **Devaraya I** built a dam on the Tungabhadra around 1410 AD and a 24 km canal-cum-aqueduct from the river to his capital. {11}
- Devaraya I is credited with making the capital one of the largest cities of the 15th century.
- Italian traveller **Nicolo Conti** (Niccolo de Conti) visited Vijayanagara in the time of Deva Raya I. {28}
- Persian ambassador **Abdur Razzaq** came to Vijayanagara in the reign of **Devaraya II** (1422–46 AD), the most august ruler of the Sangama dynasty. {25,26,27}
- Telugu poet **Srinath** (1365–1441 AD) was in the court of **Deva Raya II**; also at the court of Pedakomati Vema Reddy, Reddy ruler of Kondavidu. {45}

## Saluva and Tuluva dynasties
- In 1485 A.D. **Narasimha Saluva**, feudal lord of Chandragiri, removed Praudha Deva Raya, last Sangama ruler, and founded the Saluva dynasty; ruled about five years.
- **Vira Narasimha** dethroned Immadi Narasimha (son of Narasimha Saluva, last Saluva ruler) and founded the Tuluva dynasty.
- After Vira Narasimha his younger brother **Krishnadeva Raya** (1509–1529 AD) ruled, followed by his half brother Achyuta Deva Raya. {12}
- Portuguese writer Farnao Nuniz (Nunes) visited in Achyuta Deva Raya's reign.
- Nuniz: women were dancers, domestic servants and palanquin bearers; also wrestlers, astrologers, accountants and soothsayers. {29}
## Krishnadeva Raya
- Krishnadeva Raya (Tuluva dynasty) is considered the greatest ruler of the Vijayanagara Empire.
! 'Who was enthroned 500 years ago' (2008): the question has an error, as Harihara-I and Kulottunga-I had also completed 500 years; nearest answer Krishnadeva Raya.
- 2008 marked 500 years of Krishnadeva Raya's coronation. {33}
- Kulottunga-I (1070–1120 AD) was the grandson of Chola emperor Rajendra-I.
- Krishnadeva Raya fought the **battle of Golconda** with Sultan **Quli Qutb Shah** of Golconda; the Qutb Shahi army was defeated. {13}
- Sultan Quli Qutb Shah founded the Qutb Shahi dynasty in Golconda; he captured forts at Warangal and Kondaveedu while Krishnadeva Raya was fighting the rulers of Odisha.
- Krishnadeva Raya kept friendly relations with the Portuguese and got guns and Arabian horses from Portuguese merchants.
- On the request of Portuguese governor Afonso de Albuquerque, **Krishnadeva Raya** allowed a fort at **Bhatkal** (Uttara Kannada district, Karnataka). {14}
- His reign = classical era / **golden age of Telugu literature**. {18}
- **Ashtadiggajas** = eight Telugu poets of Krishnadeva Raya's court, the eight pillars of his literary assembly. {15,17}
- Krishnadeva Raya took the title **'Andhra Bhoj'**. {16}
- He was himself an outstanding poet and writer; his **'Amuktamalyada'** is one of the five epics of Telugu.
- **Allasani Peddana**, greatest and foremost of the Ashtadiggajas, was given the title **'Andhra Kavita Pitamah'**. {23}
- Peddana was a master of Telugu and Sanskrit, not Sanskrit and Tamil. {20}
- Poet **Mallanarya** in his court wrote Bhava Chinta Ratna and Veerasaivamrita in **Kannada**. {24}
- Bhava Chinta Ratna: on Shiva worship and philosophy, personal devotion; Veerasaivamrita: theories and religious aspects of the Veera Shaiva tradition.
- Krishnadeva Raya founded the suburban town **Nagalapuram** near Vijayanagara in memory of his mother Nagala Devi. {19,21}
- The **Hazara Rama** temple was built in Krishnadeva Raya's reign. {22}
- He also built the Vitthala (Shree Vijaya Vitthala) temple; work continued under Achyutaraya.
- Portuguese traveller **Domingo Paes** visited Vijayanagara in his time.
- Babur in his autobiography called Krishnadeva Raya the most powerful ruler of India.
## Telugu Kavitrayi
- Telugu translation of the Mahabharata: started by **Nannaya** (11th century), continued by **Tikkana** (13th century) and **Yerranna/Yerrapragada** (14th century). {30}
- These three are the **'Kavitrayi'** (trinity of poets) of Telugu literature.
## Battle of Talikota and the Aravidu dynasty
- **Battle of Talikota, January 1565 AD**: combined forces of Bijapur, Bidar, Ahmadnagar and Golconda defeated Vijayanagara; Barar was not included. {34,35,36,37}
- Per Firishta the battle was at Talikota, but the actual field lay between the villages Rakshasi and Tangadi.
- Talikota is in Karnataka near Vijayapura district; the battle was a watershed that weakened one of the greatest south Indian empires before the Marathas.
- Ruler at Talikota was **Sadashiva Raya**, but real power lay with his minister **Rama Raya**; Hussain Nizamashah killed Rama Raya in the battle.
- **Tirumala**, brother of Rama Raya, dethroned Sadashiva at Penukonda in 1570 AD and founded the **Aravidu dynasty** (1570–1672 AD), the last Vijayanagara dynasty.
- His successor was his eldest son Shri Ranga I; then **Venkata II**, one of the greatest Vijayanagara kings, who made Chandragiri his headquarters (capital).
- Venkata II was a contemporary of Raja **Wodeyar**, who founded the kingdom of Mysore in 1612. {38}
## Revenue and administration
- Main source of state income was **land revenue**, fixed after the land was surveyed and categorized. {39}
- Land tax was normally one-third to one-sixth of the yield, depending on land category and crop quality.
- The tax 'Shist' (Rai-rekha) was the main source of state income; the central revenue department was called **'Athavane'** (Asthvan/Athvan).
- Other income: wealth tax, trade tax, commercial tax, industry tax, social and community tax and fines on crime.
- Land was assessed in four categories: wetland, dry land, orchards and woods; the rate varied with land quality, crop, soil and irrigation method; private workshop owners paid an industry tax. {40}
## Hampi, temples and culture
- The ruins of **Hampi** (Central Karnataka) represent the old capital of the Vijayanagara Empire. {41,42}
- Hampi stands on the southern bank of the **Tungabhadra**; its Group of Monuments (Vijayanagara district, Karnataka) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. {43}
- The Virupaksha Temple at Hampi was built in the Vijayanagara period (one passage prints "7th century CE").
- The **Vijaya Vitthal Temple**, with 56 carved pillars that give out musical notes, is at **Hampi**; built by Krishnadeva Raya (1509–29 AD) and continued under Achyutaraya of the Tuluva dynasty. {47}
- **Kalyana Mandapas**, for celebrating divine weddings, were a notable feature of Vijayanagara temples. {46}
- **Yakshagaan** dance tradition first developed in the Vijayanagara period; a traditional folk dance of coastal Karnataka based on mythological and religious stories. {48}
## Hoysala sites
- **Dwarasamudra**, the Hoysala capital, is now Halebidu in Hassan district, Karnataka; famed for heritage architecture.
- Its ancient Hoysaleshwara temple (12th–13th century) is very famous.
- In the reign of Hoysala ruler Narasimha I, architect Kedroz built it under Ketamal, Chief Officer of the Public Works Department.
- The Chennakeshava temple at Belur was built by Hoysala ruler Vishnuvardhana.
