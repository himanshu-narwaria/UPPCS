@ 18 | Khalji Dynasty
## Jalaluddin Khalji
- **Jalaluddin Khalji** established the Khalji dynasty in 1290 AD.
- He was crowned at the Palace of **Kilokhari (Kilugarhi)**, built by Qaiqubad.
- Dr. A. L. Srivastava: Jalaluddin was the first Turkish Sultan of Delhi who believed in the ideal of benevolent despotism.
- Jalaluddin gave Alauddin the post of 'Amir-i-Tuzuk' and married his daughter to him.
- Alauddin got the governorship of **Kara-Manikpur** for his role in suppressing the revolt of Malik Chhajju.
## Alauddin Khalji: titles and ambitions
- Alauddin was the first Sultan of Delhi to establish state control over religion; he stayed free from the influence of the Ulema class.
- He took the titles **'Yamin-ul-Khilafat'** and **'Nasir-e-Amir-ul-Mominin'**. {3}
- Barani said of Alauddin: "When he attained Kingship, he was quite independent of rulers and orders of Shariat". {4}
- Alauddin took the title **'Sikandar-i-Sani'** (second Alexander) and inscribed it on his coins. {2}
- He wished to conquer the whole world and to start a new religion, but gave up the idea on the advice of his faithful friend, Kotwal **Alla-ul-Mulk**. {1}
- Alauddin set up a strong central government and raised a powerful, enormous army to protect the empire's boundaries. {30}
- Alauddin kept a vast army at the centre and was the first Sultan of Delhi to pay soldiers in cash.
## Four ordinances against rebellion
- Alauddin faced some rebellions early in his reign and issued four ordinances to suppress them.
- First ordinance: gifts, pensions and donated land were banned; officials were ordered to collect more and more taxes.
- Second ordinance: an intelligence (spy) department was set up.
- Third ordinance: intoxicating drinks (alcohol, cannabis) and gambling were banned.
- Fourth ordinance: marital relations and feasts among the nobles and Amirs were banned.

- Jaisalmer (1299 A.D.) → Ranthambor (1301) → Chittor (1303) → Warangal of the Kakatiyas (1309–1310). {13}
- Per Jayasi, **Padmini** was the wife of **Rana Ratan Singh**. {6}
- Queen Padmini committed 'Jauhar' after Ratan Singh's death when Alauddin invaded Chittor, the capital of Mewar.
- **Malik Muhammad Jayasi** wrote 'Padmavat', based on the story of queen Padmini, in 1540 AD.
- Amir Khusrau mentioned the love story of Suleman and Queen Shaiba, comparing Alauddin with Suleman and Padmini with Shaiba; Jayasi probably built Padmavat on this.
## Mongols
- **Zafar Khan**, Alauddin's best and most courageous commander, died fighting the Mongols. {5}
- Mongols were so impressed by Zafar Khan's bravery and the Khalji army's persistence that they backed down by 30 miles.
- Original name of Zafar Khan = Malik Hizabruddin.
- After 1306 AD the **Ravi** river was the boundary between the Delhi Sultanate and the Mongols. {28}
- In 1306 A.D. Mongols under Kabak attacked; Malik Kafur and Ghazi Malik stopped them on the bank of the Ravi.
- In Alauddin's reign the Mongol Qutlugh Khwaja marched up to Delhi and besieged the city, while Alauddin was on the Chittor advance. {29}
- The first Mongol attack on India came in the reign of Iltutmish, not Jalal-ud-din Khalji.
- In Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq's reign Tarmashirin led a Mongol attack; the Sultan foiled it by occupying Kalanaur and Peshawar.
- In Ghiyasuddin Tughluq's reign (1325 AD) a Mongol army under Shir Mughal crossed the Indus but was defeated by Malik Shadi, Subedar of Samana.
## Malik Kafur and the Deccan
- **Malik Kafur** was called **'Hazar-Dinari'**; Alauddin got him during the Gujarat conquest. {7}
- **Nusrat Khan** snatched Kafur in the Gujarat campaign from his master, who had bought him for a thousand dinars, and later presented him to Alauddin. {8}
- Malik Kafur rose as a skilled commander; he led the Sultanate's campaigns of conquest in the South (Deccan). {14}
- Alauddin's Deccan expeditions were for wealth and victory; he did not want to annex or interfere in the southern states. {9}
- Dr. K.S. Lal: "the greed of wealth and lust of pride encouraged Alauddin Khalji to attack the states of South."
- **Ramchandra Dev** was the ruler of Devgiri when Alauddin attacked. {10}
- Defeated in 1296 AD, Ramchandra Dev promised the province of Ellichpur's yearly income to Alauddin, but stopped sending it around 1305–1306 AD.
- So Alauddin sent Malik Kafur against Devgiri in 1307 AD; Ramchandra Dev was defeated and surrendered.
- Malik Kafur looted Devgiri and took Ramchandra Dev and his relatives to Delhi.
- Alauddin treated him well, gave him the title **'Rai Rayan'**; six months later sent him back with one lakh Tankas and Navsari district.
- Malik Kafur also led a campaign against Ramchandra's son Shankar Dev (1313 AD; another passage: 1312 AD).
- After Ramchandra Dev's death his son Shankar Dev ruled Devgiri; Ramchandra Dev was not ruler of Warangal. {31}
- Correct pairs: Devgiri – Shankar Dev; Hoysal – Veer Ballal; Madura – Veer Pandya.
- In 1303 A.D. Alauddin's first attempt on **Warangal** failed: the Kakatiya army defeated his army. {34}
- Kakatiya king Prataprudradev II raised a well-equipped army; Alauddin's army was led by Fakhruddin Juna and Malik Chhajju.
## Revenue system
- Alauddin was the first Sultan to collect revenue by **measuring the land**; the department **"Diwan-i-Mustakharaj"** was set up for it.
- His measurement-based system applied only to Delhi and nearby parts, not the whole empire; he also abolished powers of the old tax officers. {19}
- He took tax collection away from the traditional officers (**Khut, Muqaddam and Chaudhary**) and abolished all their privileges.
- Their lands were taxed too, so little difference remained between Khut (zamindar/landlord) and Balahar (ordinary farmer/agricultural labourer).
- Main aim of his revenue and tax system: a powerful and autocratic state.
- He took back land granted as 'Milq' (property, prize, idrarat pensions) and 'Waqf' (charitable land); so **Khalisa land** grew on a large scale. {15}
- Alauddin fixed **50% of the yield** as land tax (Kharaj), based on measurement; Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq also charged 50%. {16,17,18}
- Alauddin = first Muslim ruler of India to fix revenue on the real income of the land.
- Muhammad-Bin-Tughluq raised revenue to half the produce on Doab farmers (land between Ganga and Yamuna) to meet financial difficulties; his demand was based on estimated production.
- Muhammad-Bin-Tughlaq (1325–51 C.E.) is considered an ill-fated idealist: his ambitious schemes and novel experiments failed as they were far ahead of their time.
- Two new taxes of Alauddin: **'Ghari' (house) tax** on houses and huts, and **'Charai' (grazing) tax** on milch animals. {27}
## Market control
- **Alauddin Khalji** introduced market control / price control (market reforms) in Delhi; it had nothing to do with paying lower wages to artisans. {20,21,22,23}
- He appointed Malik Kabool as Shehena (inspector of the market) with a large detachment of cavalry and infantry and wide authority.
- The Shehena controlled all merchants and reported price changes and the market situation to the king.
- Per Barani, the aim of market reforms was to support a huge army against the Mongols and to check rebellion among Hindus.
- Barani: with normal wages the treasury would be finished within 5–6 years; so he cut soldiers' wages and fixed low prices so soldiers could live comfortably.
- Alauddin's attempt to control market prices was **highly successful**. {24}
- Separate markets in Delhi: grain market (Mandi); clothes and groceries (clothes, sugar, dry fruits, butter, oil); horses, slaves and cattle; miscellaneous commodities.
- Each market was under a superintendent, **Shahna-i-Mandi**, helped by an intelligence officer.
- Alauddin also got daily market reports from barids (intelligence officers) and munhiyans (secret spies).
- Alauddin started state granaries and price-controlled sale of grain — the **'Public Distribution System'** of the Sultanate period. {25,26}
## Later Khaljis
- After Alauddin's death, Qutb-ud-din **Mubarak Khalji** was imprisoned by Malik Kafur; he later got out and took the throne.
- Mubarak Khalji declared himself **Khalifa**; titles 'Al Imam', 'Ul-Imam', 'Khalifatullah' (which he was never fit for); the only Sultan to take the Caliph's title. {33}
- Among Iltutmish, Alauddin Khalji, Muhammad-bin-Tughluq and Balban, none opposed the Khalifa's power, though the last three were practically independent rulers; only Mubarak Khalji did. {32}
- Iltutmish attacked Sultan Ghiyasuddin of Bengal, who also had the Khalifa's recognition — in a way opposing the Khalifa.
- **Nasiruddin Khusrau Shah** (April–September 1320) was a convert to Islam from Hinduism.
- He took the title 'Commander of the Prophet' (Paigamber-ka-Senapati).
- His opponents called him 'enemy of Islam' and raised the slogan 'Islam is in danger'.
