## Vasco da Gama
- **Vasco da Gama** reached the port of Calicut on the western coast on 20 May 1498 A.D. {1,4}
- He discovered a new and alternative sea route from Europe to India.
- **Zamorin** (Samuthiri; ancestral title of Calicut's rulers), then ruler of Calicut, welcomed Vasco da Gama. {2,6}
- One theory: Arab navigator Ahmad Ibn Majid helped him; authentic historical evidence says a Gujarati sailor (route guide) helped him reach Calicut.
- On his first trip he stayed in India about three months (not one year) and returned to Portugal with ships of Indian goods.
- Vasco da Gama revisited India in 1502 A.D. and 1524 A.D. (not 1501).
! Chhattisgarh PSC's model answer key accepted both "Zamorin ruled Calicut in 1498" and "Vasco da Gama revisited India in 1501" as correct.
- His cargo of black pepper gave 60 times more profit than the expedition's cost; this lured other merchants to India.
- His arrival began a new era of trade between the Portuguese and Indians.

## Portuguese in India
- The Portuguese were the first Europeans to come to India for trade (1498). {11}
- The Portuguese came first in 1498 A.D. and left last in 1961 (longest stay); de jure transfer in August 1962.
- Portuguese Governor-General of Goa in 1961: Manuel Antonio Vassalo e Silva (1958-61 A.D.).
- Governor-General of Goa when India became independent in 1947: Jose Ferreira Bossa.
- The Portuguese were the first Europeans to set up sea (maritime) trade centres in India. {15}
- The Portuguese set up their first factory at Calicut in 1500 A.D.
- Portuguese merchants set up commercial firms at the ports of Calicut, Goa, Daman, Diu and Hooghly.
- **Francisco de Almeida** came in 1505 A.D. as the first Portuguese Viceroy in India. {5}
- Almeida gave the **Blue Water Policy** to protect Portuguese trade and establish authority in the Indian Ocean. {3}
- Blue Water Policy: "The Portuguese should be the sole trade power in the Arabian Sea and the Indian Ocean."
- Almeida annexed Diu in 1509 A.D.; the victory made the Portuguese most powerful in the Indian Ocean.
- Battle of Diu, 1509 A.D.: Portuguese defeated the combined forces of the Mamluk sultanate of Egypt, Gujarat sultanate and Zamorin of Calicut.
- The Portuguese set up a monopoly over the Indian Ocean and taxed ships passing through their area.
- **Afonso de Albuquerque** came in 1509 A.D. after Almeida as Governor (Viceroy), 1509-15 A.D.
- Albuquerque is the real founder of Portuguese power in India; he made the Portuguese State a strong regional power. {12}
- Albuquerque captured Goa from the Adil Shahi Sultanate of Bijapur in November 1510 A.D. (not 1499). {13}
- Goa's capture was his first big achievement; it fixed Portuguese monopoly on the south-western naval coast.
- The Portuguese kept Goa as the epicentre of their culture and entity.
- Albuquerque encouraged Portuguese men to marry Indian women, to increase Portuguese presence and form permanent colonies.
- Albuquerque captured Malacca, an important South-East Asian market, in 1511 A.D. and Hormuz, at the mouth of the Persian Gulf, in 1515.
- First Portuguese fort (first European fort in India) was built at **Cochin** in 1503 by Albuquerque on his first visit; named 'Emmanuel Fort'. {14}
- Almeida built Portuguese fortresses at Cannanore and Anjediva in 1505.
- Nuno da Cunha became Governor in 1529 A.D. after Lopo Vaz de Sampaio and stayed till the end of 1538 A.D.

- Cultivation of tobacco and red chillies in India was the contribution of the Portuguese.
## Portuguese in Bengal
- In 1535 A.D. Ghiyasuddin Mahmud Shah, ruler of Bengal, promised the Portuguese forts at Chittagong and Satgaon for their help against Sher Shah Suri's invasion.
- After that Portuguese colonies were set up through Bengal; Chittagong was known as their great port.
- In the 16th century Satgaon declined and was replaced by Hooghly.
- In 1579 A.D., with Akbar's permission, the Portuguese founded their settlement at Hooghly; built forts and warehouses along the river.
- Hooghly town, near Chuchura (Chinsurah), became a major European trading centre.
- In 1599 A.D. the Portuguese reached Bandel (Hooghly district, West Bengal) and built 'Bandel Church' (Basilica of the Holy Rosary).
- Portuguese factories in Bengal: **Hooghly and Bandel**. {16}
- The Portuguese used Hooghly as a base for piracy (plunder) in the Bay of Bengal. {18,19}
- In 1632 A.D. Shah Jahan ordered Bengal Subedar Qasim Khan to destroy Portuguese establishments at Hooghly; thousands of Portuguese were imprisoned.

## The Dutch
- In 1602 A.D. the Dutch States General (parliament) chartered a United East India Company with capital of 6,500,000 Guilders.
- The Dutch were the first, among the given options, to start a joint stock company to trade with India. {23}
- The first joint stock company for India trade was actually the English East India Company (London, 1600 A.D.); the Dutch one came in 1602 A.D.
- The Dutch company had a monopoly to trade and to attack and win territories in India and eastern countries.
- Its eastern centre was Batavia (now Jakarta); it ran the Dutch eastern empire; its partners worked as a private group.
- The Dutch set up their first factory at Masulipatnam on the east coast (Andhra Pradesh) in 1605 A.D.
- Dutch east-coast factories were not on land granted by Gajapati rulers; Gajapati rule had declined by the 16th century.
- First Dutch factory in Bengal: Pipali (1627 A.D.); about 1676 A.D. they left Pipali and set up a new factory at Balasore.
- Dutch trade with Bengal began properly in 1653 A.D. with their establishment at Chinsurah; factory properly set up around 1656-57 A.D.
- Dutch factories in Bengal: **Chinsurah and Hooghly**; severe floods of 1600 A.D. had ruined earlier Hooghly factories. {50}
- The Dutch fort at Chinsurah was called Fort Gustavus.
- By the end of the 17th century Dutch factories were at Qasim Bazar, Patna, Dhaka, Malda and Balasore.
- Dutch trading centres in India: Nagappattinam, Chinsura, Machilipatnam, Surat, Pulicat, Agra, Cochin, Ahmedabad, Patna; Bharuch is also counted among them. {59}

## English East India Company: founding
- A group called Merchant Adventurers formed the English East India Company in 1600 A.D.
- Its name: 'The Governor and Company of Merchants of London Trading into the East Indies'.
- Queen Elizabeth I granted its royal charter in December 1600 to trade with the East Indies.
- **Akbar** (1556-1605 A.D.) was Emperor of India when the Company was formed in London. {28}
- The **Levant Company** (1592) got the first charter: monopoly of trade with the Ottoman Empire (the Levant) and its eastern areas including India. {27}
- The Levant Company traded in silk and spices.
- Elizabeth I's Royal Charter gave the East India Company an exclusive 15-year monopoly of English trade east of the Cape of Good Hope, including India.
- Secret of the Company's success: no nationalism in India; Company army had western training and modern arms; Indian soldiers lacked fidelity and served whoever paid enough. {26}
## English factories
- Captain **William Hawkins** sailed on the Company's ship Hector and reached Surat in 1608 A.D.
- After Portuguese resistance, Hawkins reached Jahangir's court at Agra in April 1609 A.D. but got no permission for a factory.
- Hawkins carried a letter signed by King James I asking favourable treatment for British traders; his mission was both royal embassy and commercial venture.
- Hawkins was sent by the Company with the King's credentials; he came in 1608, not in 1611.
- Hawkins spoke Turkish well; impressed, Jahangir gave him the title 'English Khan' and a mansab of 400. {33}
- Captain Middleton landed at Swally near Surat and got trade permission from the Mughal Governor in 1611 A.D.
- Captain **Thomas Best** defeated the Portuguese in 1612 A.D. in the naval battle of Swally, near modern Surat; it broke Portuguese monopoly. {30}
- The English set up their first temporary factory at Masulipatnam in 1611 A.D., in the reign of **Jahangir**. {29}
- The English set up their first permanent factory at **Surat** in 1613 A.D., with Jahangir's permission. {31,32,36}
- The English were the first European company to set up a factory at Surat; Dutch followed in 1616 A.D., French in 1668 A.D. {35}
- If only European presence is counted, the first factory at Surat was Portuguese in 1611 A.D.; officially and permanently, English in 1613 A.D.
- The English later set up factories at Ahmedabad, Burhanpur, Ajmer and Agra.
- English factories in the first quarter of the 17th century: Surat 1613; Agra, Ahmedabad, Baroda and **Broach** by 1619; Masulipatnam 1611. {34}
- Later English factories: Armagaon 1628; Hariharpur and Balasore (Odisha) 1633.
- The English opened their first factory in South India at Masulipatnam, and their first in Eastern India in Orissa in 1633.
- The English Company set up a factory at Madras on land leased from a representative of the Vijayanagara empire. {25}
- The Company first made **Surat** its Western Presidency; later it was shifted to Bombay. {37}
- Bombay came to the English from the **Portuguese** as dowry: Charles II married Portuguese princess Catherine of Braganza in 1662 A.D. under the marriage treaty of 1661. {38}
- Charles II gave Bombay to the East India Company in 1668 at 10 pounds annual rent.
- **Job Charnock**, chief of Qasim Bazar factory, chose Sutanuti instead of Hooghly for the English trade centre and so founded Calcutta. {20,21}
- India's first Municipal Corporation was set up at **Madras** (former Presidency city) on 29 September 1688 as 'Madras Nagar Nigam (Madras Municipal Corporation)'; at present called Greater Chennai Municipal Corporation. {22}
- **John Child**, British Governor of Bombay, blockaded Bombay and other Mughal ports on the western coast in 1688 and tried to seize Mecca (Hajj) pilgrims.
- Aurangzeb ordered Sidi Yakub to besiege Bombay; the British surrendered and sought peace.
- Aurangzeb made John Child's expulsion from India a condition; Child died (printed as 1590 A.D.) before it was carried out. {40}
- Nawab Saadat Ali Khan II of Awadh handed the Fort of Prayagraj (Allahabad) to the British in 1798 AD.
- None of these was introduced by the English: soil-and-crop based land revenue assessment, mobile cannons, tobacco and red chilli cultivation. {60}
- Land revenue assessment by nature of soil and quality of crops began under Sher Shah Suri and Akbar.
- Mobile cannons in warfare came in the Mughal era; Akbar's ordnance factory made various types of cannons.
## The French
- **Colbert**, finance minister of Louis XIV, founded the French East India Company in 1664 A.D.; he is considered its founder. {49}
- The French East India Company was formed in the reign of **Louis XIV**. {48}
- Its name: Compagnie Francaise Pour le Commerce des Indes Orientales (Compagnie francaise des Indes orientales).
- The French state was the largest shareholder and bore most of the company's expenses.
- In 1667 A.D. Francois Caron led an expedition to India and set up the first French factory at **Surat** in 1668. {47}
- Marcara, a Persian who came with Caron, founded another French factory at Masulipatnam in 1669 A.D. with permission of the Sultan of Golconda.
- Under Dupleix the French occupied Madras in 1746.
- **Dupleix** was the first European to take part in quarrels of Indian princes to gain territory. {46}
- Dupleix first placed European forces in Indian courts at Indian expense; his tactics were later used by the English to conquer India; he sowed the seeds of European rule in India.
## Carnatic Wars
- The Carnatic Wars were fought between the English and the French. {42}
| War | Years | Result |
| First Carnatic War | 1746-48 | Ended by Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle {43} |
| Second Carnatic War | 1749-54 (not 1750-52) | Inconclusive war {44} |
| Third Carnatic War | 1758-63 | Ended by Treaty of Paris |
| First Mysore War | — | The British lost |
- First Carnatic War was an extension of the Austrian War of Succession, which began in Europe in 1740.
- In 1746 the English and French began war in India without the consent of their home nations.
- Immediate cause: English navy under Commodore Barnett seized French ships. {41}
- Carnatic Nawab Anwaruddin sent 10,000 men under Mahfooz Khan against the French (to besiege them in Madras).
- French under Captain Paradise defeated the Nawab's force at **St. Thome** on the Adyar river; the war is memorable for this battle.
- Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (18 October 1748) ended the Austrian Succession War and the First Carnatic War; Madras went back to the English. {61,62}
- **Battle of Ambur**, 1749 A.D. (not 1752): first major battle of the Second Carnatic War. {45}
- Ambur was started by Muzaffar Jang, supported by Joseph Francois Dupleix and led by Chanda Sahib, against Carnatic Nawab Anwar-ud-din Khan.
- They fought Anwar-ud-din because he backed Nasir Jang's claim to be Nizam of Hyderabad.
- Anwar-ud-din was defeated and killed at Ambur; Chanda Sahib took control of the Carnatic.
- The French failed in the Deccan mainly due to weak naval strength, while the English had a strong force. {52}
- Voltaire: in the Austrian Succession War French naval strength fell so much that hardly any battleship was left for the Seven Years War.
- Causes of British success over the French: naval superiority, huge wealth and manpower from the conquest of Bengal, better coordination in the British army. {63}
## Opium and saltpetre
- Europeans got the best saltpetre (shora) and opium from **Bihar**; Bihar was famous for opium production in British rule. {55,56}
- **Opium** was the chief economic base of the triangular trade between India, China and England (18th-19th centuries). {57}
- The East India Company held a monopoly of opium cultivation in Bengal and Bihar and forced farmers to grow it.
- Opium was exported to China, where demand was high despite government bans.
- Opium profits bought Chinese tea, silk and porcelain for England; English manufactured goods went to India.
- This exploitative system led to the Opium Wars between Britain and China.
- Silk and black pepper were also traded, but opium was the chief base.
