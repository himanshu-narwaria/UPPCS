@ 84 | Cripps Mission (1942)
## Background and arrival
- With Japan's power rising, the international community, especially **USA, China and Australia**, pressed Britain for a political settlement with India (to grant freedom).
- **Sir Stafford Cripps** arrived in India in **March 1942** to negotiate with Indian politicians. {1}
- British Prime Minister **Winston Churchill** sent the Cripps Mission, to get India's help and participation in the Second World War. {8}
## Cripps proposals
- After the Second World War India would get **Dominion Status** (an Indian Union with Dominion status), not full independence. {3,5}
- Dominion status meant: linked by common allegiance to the Crown, but not subordinate in domestic or external affairs to any external authority.
- Indians would get the right to frame their own Constitution; an elected **constitution-making body** would be set up after the war. {7}
- Any province not agreeing to the new Constitution could stay out of the proposed Union and retain its present constitutional position.
- Cripps's draft declaration included all four: Dominion status; all provinces and States merged into an Indian Union; any province or State may stay outside the Union; Constitution made by the people of India. {2}
- Not part of the Cripps proposals: a new executive council with equal representation for Hindus and Muslims. {4}
## Reactions and negotiators
- **Mahatma Gandhi** called the proposal a **"post-dated cheque on a falling (crashing) bank"**. {6}
- **Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru and Maulana Azad** were the official Congress negotiators with the Cripps Mission. {9}
## Lord Linlithgow
- **Lord Linlithgow** called Gandhi's movements **"Political Blackmail"**. {10}
- Linlithgow was the longest-serving Viceroy and Governor-General of India: **1936 to 1943**.
- The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi during Linlithgow's tenure.
