The Parliament (1)
*Parliament is the legislative organ of the Union Government.
*Articles 79 to 122 in Part V of the Indian Constitution
deal with the Parliament. *According to Article 79, the
Parliament of India consists of the President and the two
houses - House of the People and the Council of States.
*Lok Sabha is also known as the "House of the People".
*Its maximum strength is 550. At present, it has 543
(States-524, UTs-19) members. *Out of 550 members, at
most 530 members are representative of states, 20 members
are representative of Union territories. If, in the opinion
of the President, there is not enough representation of the
Anglo-Indian Community in the Lok Sabha, then he could
nominate two members of that community to the Lok Sabha.
In this regard, the arrangements related to the Anglo-Indian
Community have not been extended beyond the stipulated
date (25 January, 2020) by 'The Constitution (One Hundred
and Fourth Amendment) Act, 2019' [The Constitution (One
Hundred and Twenty-Sixth Amendment) Bill, 2019]. Thus,
the arrangements for the nomination of two members of the
Anglo-Indian community in the Lok Sabha have ended. *The
allocation of state-wise seats in the Lok Sabha is based on
the Census of 1971. *According to the 84th Constitutional

the year 2026. *In Lok Sabha seats are allotted on the basis of
the population. *The normal tenure of Lok Sabha is 5 years
from the date of its first meeting after the general elections but
it can be dissolved by the President in between. *Under the
Article 83(2) Proviso, Lok Sabha can be extended during
the time of National Emergency for one year at a time. *In
any case of extension, it can't continue beyond the period of
6 months after the emergency has ceased to operate. *The

Lok Sabha are (a) He must be a citizen of India and (b) He must
not be less than 25 years. (c) He must make and subscribe to
an oath (d) He must possess other qualifications prescribed by
the Parliament by law.
*According to Article 93, the Speaker and the Deputy
Speaker are elected by Lok Sabha from among its members.
*Suspension of Lok Sabha (Adjournment/Adjournment Sine
die of Lok Sabha sitting) is called up by the Speaker, whereas
prorogation and dissolution of Loksabha is called up by the
President of India. *According to Article 94 (b), the Speaker
of Lok Sabha addresses his resignation in writing to the Deputy
Speaker and vice-versa.
*The speaker of Lok Sabha is removed from his Post by a
resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of Lok
Sabha and such resolution shall not be moved until and unless a
14-day advance notice is given. *While a resolution of removal
of the Speaker or Deputy Speaker is under consideration,
the Speaker/Deputy Speaker concerned can not preside but both
have the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of the
house. *While the proposal of removal of the Speaker or Deputy
Speaker is in process, the Speaker or Deputy Speaker (as the
case may be) have the right to vote in first instance but not in
the case of equality of votes. *The Speaker of Lok Sabha uses
his casting vote only in case of a tie. *Lok Sabha Secretariat
directly functions under the Speaker of Lok Sabha. *After the
general elections, the Protem Speaker administers the oath to
the newly elected members of Lok Sabha. *As provided by
the Constitution, the Speaker of the last Lok Sabha vacates
his office immediately before the first meeting of the newly
elected Lok Sabha. Therefore, the President appoints a senior
member of the Lok Sabha as the Pro-tem Speaker. *Article
85(1) empowers the President to summon each house of the
Parliament if he thinks fit. *In any case, the interval between

six months. (*Thus as per the Constitution, it is mandatory

Article 100(3), the quorum of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha

33. Consider the following statements :
1. The President of India can summon a session of
the Parliament at such place as he / she thinks fit.
2. The Constitution of India provides for three
sessions of the Parliament in a year, but it is not
mandatory to conduct all three sessions.
3. There is no minimum number of days that the
parliament is required to meet in a year.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only
 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
I.A.S. (Pre) 2020
Ans. (c)
Article 85(1) of the Indian Constitution empowers the
President to summon each House of Parliament to meet at
such time and place as he thinks fit, but six months shall
not intervene between its last sitting in one session and the
date appointed for its first sitting in the next session. So, the
constitution provides for the maximum gap between two
sessions of Parliament but there is no minimum gap. Hence,
statements 1 and 3 are correct, while statement 2 is wrong.
So the correct answer is option (c).
34. Generally how many sessions are there in the Lok
Sabha?
(a) 3 (b) 4
(c) 5 (d) 6
(e) None of the above/More than one of the above
67th B.P.S.C. (Pre) (Re- Exam), 2022
Ans. (a)
Article 85 of the Constitution of India deals with the session,
prorogation and dissolution of the Parliament. Under this, the
President is given the power to call the session of each House
of the Parliament from time to time, provided that the session
of Parliament should be called by the President in such a
way that there should not be a gap of 6 months between the
last meeting of one session and the first meeting of the next
session. In other words, at least two sessions of the Parliament
should be called in a year. By the way, the three sessions of
the Lok Sabha, usually called in a year in India are–
1. Budget Session (February – May)
2. Monsoon Session (July – August)
3. Winter Session (November – December)

88. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha enjoys
(a) The right to vote only in case of a tie
(b) The right to vote like other members of the House
(c) Two votes - one in ordinary course and the other in
case of a tie
(d) No right to vote
70th B.P.S.C Re-Exam 2024
Ans. (a)
According to Article 100(1) of the Constitution, All questions
at any sitting of either House or joint sitting of the Houses
shall be determined by a majority of votes of the members
present and voting, excluding the speaker or person acting
as chairman or speaker.
The Speaker or chairman, or person acting as such, shall
not vote in first instance. But shall exercise a casting vote
in the case of equality of votes (i.e. a tie) and hence decide
the matter.
In this case, the Speaker's vote will count. According to an
additional provision under Article 96(1), if the Speaker's
position is challenged by a motion in the Lok Sabha, they
will continue to remain in their position until the motion is
disposed of. According to Article 96(2), in such cases, if there
is a motion of no confidence against the Speaker, they will not
be allowed to vote during the debate on the motion, as their
presence will not influence the vote. However, during other
matters, the Speaker is entitled to cast their vote in case of a
tie. This right is known as the casting vote. The Speaker may
also delegate this casting vote to other members of the House.

(b) In case of Constitutional Amendment
(c) In case of emergency

Ans. (d)
Article 100 (1) of the Constitution provides a procedure for voting
in the Houses. Any question at any sitting shall be decided by a
majority of votes of the members present and voting. According
to 100(1), the Speaker or person acting as Chairman or Speaker,
shall not vote in the first instance, but shall have and exercise
a ‘’casting vote’’ in the case of an equality of votes. Article
96 gives a different exercise of casting a vote by the Speaker.
According to Article 96(2), the Speaker shall have the right to
cast a vote in the first instance on the question of resolution for
his removal, but not in the case of equality of votes.
