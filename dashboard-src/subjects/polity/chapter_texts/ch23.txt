The Parliament (3)
Conduct of Business and 
Legislative Procedure
*The Parliament of India is the supreme legislative body. 
*India has a parliamentary system of Government based on 
the Westminster model. *The Right to amend the constitution 
is vested in the Parliament under Article 368. *Maximum 
time gap between the two sessions of parliament is 6 months. 
*Lok Sabha speaker, Deputy speaker, Secretary General, Rajya 
Sabha chairman, Deputy chairman etc. are called officials of 
parliament. *There are usually three sessions in a year, i.e. 
Budget session, Monsoon session and Winter session. *If 
any member of Parliament remains absent for 60 days from 
all meetings without informing to the respective house, then 
the House may declare his seat vacant. *Article 105 of the 
Indian Constitution lays down the privileges and immunities 
of  the members of Parliament. *For making International 
treaties effective, the Parliament can make laws in the state list 
without the consent of the state. *Speaker or chairman of the 
House can prohibit any member and allow any other member 
to speak. This is called Yielding the floor. *When a member 
of parliament changes party allegiance, leaving one political 
party and joining another political party in the Parliament, it is 
called Crossing the floor. *Zero Hour is  a unique feature of 
the Parliamentary system of India. It can be a maximum of one 
hour, which is around 12 noon to 1:00 pm in Lok Sabha (its 
duration is not fixed in the Rules). *A money bill can only be 
introduced in Lok Sabha and that too on the recommendation 
of the President of India. It cannot be introduced in Rajya 
Sabha. *Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend a money bill and 
must return it within 14 days, after which, Lok Sabha may 
accept or reject its recommendations. If the money bill is not 
returned within 14 days, it is deemed passed by both Houses in 
the form passed by Lok Sabha. *According to Article 108, the 
President summons both the Houses for a joint sitting to resolve 
the deadlock between the two houses over the passage of a bill. 
*According to Article 118(4) the speaker shall preside over a 
joint sitting of two houses of parliament. *The Joint sitting of 
two Houses of Parliament is called up only for ordinary bills 
or non-money financial bills. The Joint sitting of two houses 
of Parliament is not called for Money Bills and Constitution 
Amendment Bills. *Since 26 January, 1950 joint sitting of two 
Houses has been invoked only for three bills- the first one was 
in 1961 for the Dowry prohibition bill, second one was for 
the Banking service commission (repeal) Bill in 1978 and 
third one in 2002 for the prevention of terrorism Bill. *Any 
ordinary bill can be introduced in any house of parliament. *The 
Speaker has power to decide whether a bill is a money bill 
or not. *To withdraw the funds from the Consolidated Fund 
of India, the government needs to pass appropriation bills. 
*According to Article 116 of the Indian Constitution, when 
an appropriation bill is not passed and the government needs 
funds then Lok Sabha has the power to withdraw funds from 
the consolidated fund of India through a V ote on Account, for a 
fixed period of time. *The Estimates Committee is the largest 
among the Financial committes of Parliament . It consists 
of 30 members (all from the Lok Sabha). There is no Rajya 
Sabha member in the Estimates Committee. *The Secretariat 
of the Indian Parliament is free from the Government. *Indian 
Parliament established control over administration through 
parliamentary committees.

28.  Which one of the following is not a collective privilege 
of the members of Parliament?
 (a) Freedom of debates and proceedings. 
 (b) The Rights to regulate the internal matters of the 
Parliament
 (c) Freedom from attendance as Witness.
 (d) The privileges of excluding strangers from the house.
U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2017 
Ans. (c)
Parliamentary privileges are some of the peculiar rights 
enjoyed by each House collectively as a constituent part 
of parliament and by member of each House individually, 
without which they could not discharge their functions 
efficiently and effectively. Some of the collective privileges 
of the members of Parliament are -
(I)  To prohibit the publication of its debates and proceedings.
(II) To exclude strangers from the House. 
(III) To regulate its own procedure and conduct of its  
business.
(IV)  To punish members as well as outsiders for the breach 
of privileges or contempt of the House.
(V)  Prohibition of arrest and service of legal process 
within the precincts of the House without the permission of 
chairman/speaker.
(VI)  Right of the member to receive immediate information 
of the arrest, detention, conviction, imprisonment or release 
of the member.
Notably, freedom from attendance as a witness is an 
individual privilege of a member of Parliament.

‘Zero Hour’ is an Indian innovation in the field of 
Parliamentary procedure. The emergence of zero hour can be 
traced to the early sixties when many issues of great public 
importance and urgency began to be raised by members 
immediately after Question Hour, sometimes with prior 
permission of the chairman or on other ocassions without

Sabha (from 2014) it is 12:00 noon to 1:00 pm.

Financial bills, in a technical sense, are those bills that 
deal with fiscal matters, that is, revenue or expenditure, 
and are generally of three kinds: 1. Money bills-Article 
110, 2. Financial bills (I)-Article 117 (1), 3. Financial 
bills (II)-Article 117 (3). But the Constitution, under 
Article 109, provides a special procedure in respect of



Money Bills and Article 110 defines the "Money Bills". The 
Rajya Sabha has restricted powers regarding a money bill. 
It cannot reject or amend a money bill. It can only make the 
recommendations and it must return the bill to the Lok Sabha

the two houses is allowed with regard to a money bill under

necessarily) summon a joint sitting of the two Houses 
to resolve the deadlock. Hence, statement 3 is not

The Indian Parliamentary Group is an autonomous body 
formed in the year 1949 in pursuance of a motion adopted by 
the Constituent Assembly (Legislative) on 16th August, 1948. 
Membership of the Indian Parliamentary Group is open to 
all Members of Parliament and ex-Members of Parliament. 
A Member of Parliament can become a Life Member of 
the Group on payment of a life subscription. On ceasing to 
be a Member of Parliament, a life member of the Group is

to foreign countries with a view to develop contacts 
with members of other Parliaments. The Group acts as 
a link between the Parliament of India and the various 
Parliaments of the world by exchanging delegations, 
goodwill missions, correspondence, documents, etc., 
with foreign Parliaments. It also functions as the  
(a) National Group of the Inter-Parliamentary Union and 
(b) Main Branch of the Commonwealth Parliamentary 
Association in India. Thus, statements (ii) and (iii) are
