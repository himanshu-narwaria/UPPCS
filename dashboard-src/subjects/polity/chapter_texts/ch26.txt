The Supreme Court
*An Integrated Judicial system has been established under the
Indian Constitution in which the Supreme Court is at the top.
*Below the Supreme Court there are various High Courts.
*This single system of the Judiciary has been adopted from
the Government of India Act 1935. *The Supreme Court of
India was inaugurated on 28th January 1950. *Articles 124 to
147 in Part V of the Indian Constitution deal with the Supreme
Court. *The Supreme Court of India consists of the Chief
Justice of the India and up to 33 other judges. *Earlier there
were 30 Judges and a Chief Justice. Supreme Court (Number
of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019 increased the number of
Judges including Chief Justice from 31 to 34. The President
of India appoints the judges of the Supreme Court. *The
President appoints a Chief Justice after consultation with the
other judges of the Supreme Court of India and the judges of
the high courts. *The person to be appointed as a Judge of the
Supreme Court must be a citizen of India and he should be,
for at least 5 years, a Judge of a High Court or two or more
such courts in succession; or he has been for at least ten years
an advocate of a High Court or of two or more such courts in
succession; or he must be a distinguished jurist in the opinion of
President. *No minimum age is prescribed for the appointment
of judges of the Supreme Court. *Parliament has the power to
increase the number of Judges in Supreme Court. *A person
appointed as the Judge of the Supreme Court has to make and
subscribe an oath or affirmation before the President. *Judges
can give their resignation in writing to the President. *Judges
of the Supreme Court can be removed by the President after the
recommendation of the Parliament (address by each House).
*Recommendation (Address) of Parliament for the removal of
Judges must be passed by a special majority in each House of the
Parliament. *The recommendation of Parliament is presented
to the President in the same session. *The grounds on which
removal of judges takes place are proved misbehaviour or
incapacity. *These removals can be declared unconstitutional
and illegal in the state of being extraordinary. *The Judges of
the Supreme Court retire at the age of 65 years. *Salary and
other allowances of the Supreme Court Judges are determined
by Parliament. *Under Article 127, the Chief Justice of India
can designate a judge of a High Court as an Ad hoc Judge of the
Supreme Court for a temporary period to complete the quorum
of the Judges in the Supreme Court (with the previous consent
of the President and after consultation with the Chief Justice
of High Court concerned). *The bench hearing a case related
to the interpretation of the Constitution should have at least 5
judges. *According to Article 129, the Supreme Court is a
Court of Record. *It has the power to punish for its contempt.
*Court of Record means a court whose proceedings are kept as a
record and are available as an evidence of fact. Article 131 deals
with the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court. Disputes
between the Government of India and any of the States of the
Union, the government of India and any State or States on one
side and any other State or States on the other side or between
two or more States comes under the original jurisdiction of
the Supreme Court (only if the dispute involues a question
on which the existence or extent of a legal right depends).
*Articles 132 to 136 deal with the Appellate jurisdiction of
the Supreme Court. *The Supreme Court under its extraordinary
powers conferred by Article 136 can interfere to quash the
decision of a quasi - Judicial tribunal when the tribunal has
either exceeded its jurisdiction or has approached the question
referred to in a manner likely to result in injustice. *Article 137
provides that the Supreme Court has the power to review any
judgement pronounced or order made by itself. *The concept
of Judicial review is based on the Procedure established by

law and supremacy of the Constitution. *The last authority to
interpret the Constitution is the Supreme Court. It is called as the
Guardian of the Indian Constitution. *According to Aarticle
143, the President may seek the opinion of the Supreme Court
on any question of law or fact of public importance on which
he thinks it is expedient to obtain such an opinion or on any
dispute arising out of the pre-constitutional treaty, agreement,
covenant, engagement or other similar instruments. In first
case the Supreme Court may tender or refuse to answer but in
second case Supreme Court is bound to tender its opinion. *The
Supreme Court can send any case to any court of the country.
*The source of the Doctrine of Basic Structure of the Indian
Constitution is Judicial interpretation. *Article 141 of Indian
Constitution stipulates that the decision of the Supreme Court
would be binding upon all courts within the territory of India.
*In the Keshvananda Bharati Case (1973), the Supreme Court
propounded the Doctrine of the Basic Structure of the Indian
Constitution.

116.Given below are two statements, one is labelled as
Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Public Interest Litigation in India is
essential for the rule of law.
Reason (R): Public Interest Litigation provides effective
access of Justice to socially and economically weaker
sections.
Select the correct answer from the code given below:
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct
explanation of (A).
(b) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct
explanation of (A).
U.P. P.C.S (Pre) 2025
Ans. (b)
h on TG-@freepdfhall

[[PDF_PAGE:318]]
Public Interest Litigation (PIL) is a judicially developed
process in India. It expanded the scope of locus standi (the
legal right to file a petition), thereby making access to courts
easier even for socially and economically deprived sections.
Hence, the statement in Reason (R)—that PIL provides
effective access to justice to weaker sections—is true.
However, the expression used in Assertion (A)—
“necessary for the rule of law”—claims PIL to be an
essential/indispensable condition, which is not correct.
The rule of law in India is based on institutional and
constitutional arrangements such as the basic structure
of the Constitution, the principle of equality (Article 14),
the right to constitutional remedies (Article 32), the writ
jurisdiction of High Courts (Article 226), judicial review,
etc. These existed even before the evolution of PIL, and the
rule of law can continue to exist even without PIL. Thus,
PIL strengthens and promotes the rule of law, but it is not an
indispensable condition for its existence.
Therefore, (A) is false.
