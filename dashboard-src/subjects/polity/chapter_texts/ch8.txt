
The Preamble
*The Objectives Resolution was moved to the Constituent  
Assembly on 13th December 1946 by Pt. Jawahar Lal Nehru, and 
it was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 January 1947. 
Later, this Resolution became the foundation of the Preamble.  
*The words ‘Socialist’, ‘Secular’, and ‘Integrity’ were 
incorporated in the Preamble by the 42 nd Constitutional 
Amendment Act 1976. 



*The Preamble declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, 
Democratic Republic. It secures for all its citizens,  justice of 
three types – social, economic and political. It is very helpful 
in the legal interpretation of the Indian Constitution. *In the 
words of Sir Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, “The Preamble to our 
Constitution expresses what we had thought or dreamt so long”. 
*The Supreme Court accepted it as a part of the Constitution 
in the Keshavanand Bharti case(1973). *Before this, in the 
Berubari case (1960), the Preamble was not considered a 
part of the Constitution. *In Golaknath vs State of Punjab 
(1967), the preamble was considered as the core soul of the 
Constitution. *In Bommai vs Union of India (1994), the 
Supreme Court again gave the concept that the Preamble is a 
part of the Constitution. 
*It is non – justiciable in nature. *It talks about the freedom of 
faith, belief, worship, expression and thought. *It also ensures 
dignity and equal status and opportunities for all. *It is called 
the soul of the Constitution. 
*Dr. B. R. Ambedkar called the right to Constitutional 
remedies (Art. 32) as the heart and soul of the Constitution. 
*K. M. Munshi called the Preamble the political horoscope. 
*Subhash Kashyap said that, if the Constitution is the body then 
the preamble is the soul; if the Constitution is the building then 
the preamble is the foundation stone. 
*Dr. B.R. Ambedkar said that “the Constitution is a pious 
paper.”

The Indian Constitution starts with the preamble, which 
outlines the main objectives of the Constitution. The 
Preamble describes the nature, objectives, and ideals of the

 (a) T wo (b) Three
 (c) One (d) Four
U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2016
Ans. (b)
There are 3 types of justice mentioned in the Preamble of 
the Indian Constitution. The Preamble aims to ensure Social, 
Economic and Political Justice.

 (a) Social, Religions and Political
 (b) Social, Economic and Cultural

 (d) Social, Economic and Political
 U.P.R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2021 
Ans. (d)

48.  Economic Justice’ as one of the objectives of the Indian 
Constitution has been provided in –
 (a) The Preamble and the Fundamental Rights 
 (b) The Preamble and the Directive Principles of State 
Policy
 (c) The Fundamental Rights and the Directive Principles 
of State Policy
 (d) None of the above
I.A.S. (Pre) 2013
Ans. (b)
The Preamble explicitly provides for Economic Justice as an 
objective of the Indian Constitution. The part of Directive 
Principles of State Policy, on the other hand, contains several 
Articles, which provide for Economic Justice. e.g., Article 
38(1) says “The State shall strive to promote the welfare 
of the people by securing and protecting as effectively as it 
may a social order, in which justice, social, economic and 
political, shall inform all the institutions of the national life.”
49. The Preamble to the Constitution of India is :
 (a) a part of the Constitution but has no legal effect
 (b) not a part of the Constitution and has no legal 
effect either
 (c) a part of the Constitution and has the same legal 
effect as any other part
 (d) a part of the Constitution but has no legal effect 
independently of other parts
I.A.S. (Pre) 2020
Ans. (d)
The Supreme Court of India accepted the Preamble as a Part 
of the Constitution in the Kesavanand Bharti Case (1973). 
The Preamble given in the Constitution is not enforceable 
in a Court of Law. Hence, option (d) is the correct answer.
50.  The Preamble given in the Constitution-
 1. is not enforceable in courts
 2. is important and have utility
 3. describes the aims of governance
 4. helps to give a Judicial meaning to our Constitution
 Code :
 (a)  1 and 2 (b) 2 and 3
 (c) 1,2 and 3 (d) 2,3 and 4
U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2004
Ans. (*)
All the answers are correct. The Preamble given in the 
Constitution is not enforceable in a Court of Law. It is notable 
that the Preamble is Amendable and has been amended only 
once, in 1976, by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment.
51.  Which of the following is correct?
 (a) Social equality is not guaranteed in our Constitution
 (b) Social equality already existed in our country
 (c) Social equality is guaranteed in our Constitution
 (d) None of the above
U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2008
Ans. (c)
Social Equality has been guaranteed in our Constitution under 
‘Right to Equality’ in Articles 14 to 18. The Preamble to the 
Constitution also aims to ensure social equality.
The Governance System
*A Government is an institution or a system made of a group of 
people that takes care of or manages a country or a State. Every 
Government has its own Constitution or a set of fundamental 
principles that it follows to ensure effective governance. *An 
autocracy is a form of Government where the supreme power 
or rule is in the hands of one individual or entity. *Autocracy 
includes an absolute monarchy, where a family or a group of 
families rule a country. *The post of monarch is inherited in 
an absolute monarchy. *Monarchy is one of the oldest forms 
of Government. Now, autocracy also includes dictatorship. 
*Dictatorship is a form of Government in which an individual 
possesses absolute power without any effective limitations. 
*Democracy is the most widely adopted system of Governance
