States and the Union Territories
*The Indian Independence Act of 1947 led to the formation of 
two independent and separate Dominions – India and Pakistan. 
The Princely States were provided three options – to join India, 
to join Pakistan or to remain Independent. *There were more 
than 560 princely States within the Indian territory, out of which 
most joined India, whereas three (Hyderabad, Junagadh and 
Kashmir) refused to join India. *Later on, Hyderabad through 
military Action, Junagadh through a referendum and Kashmir 
through the Instrument of Accession (a legal document) 
executed by Maharaja Hari Singh (ruler of the Jammu & 
Kashmir) were included in the Indian Union. The Indian 
Constitution that came into force in 1950 went a step further, 
recognising four different categories of States and territories in 
the first Schedule. Part A was comprised  of former British India 
provinces, which had a governor and a Legislature. Part B was 
comprised of former Princely States governed by a 'raj pramukh' 
while Part C was a combination of Princely States and chief 
commissioner's provinces governed by a chief commissioner. 
Part D was comprised of the lone territory of the Andaman and 
Nicobar Islands administered by the President through a Chief 
Commissioner/Other authority appointed by him. It was Part C 
states and part D territories that would go on to form the basis 
for the creation of Union Territories. *As per the Article 1(1) 
of the Constitution, “India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of 
States” rather than the Federation of States. *Article 2 provides 
for the admission or establishment of New States. *Article 3 
provides for the power of the Parliament in the formation of 
New States and alteration of areas, boundaries and names of 
existing States. *Parliament can form new States through a 
simple majority. *Amendments made under Articles 2 and 3 
are out of the scope of Article 368. *Presently, India comprises  
28 States and 8 Union Territories. S.K. Dhar Commission 
1948 recommended the reorganization of States on the basis 
of administrative convenience instead of a linguistic basis. *In 
December 1948, the JVP committee was constituted by the 
Congress to scrutinize the recommendations of the S.K. Dhar 
Commission. *This committee included Jawahar Lal Nehru, 
Vallabh Bhai Patel and Pattabhi Sitaramaiya. *The committee, 
in its report presented in April 1949, officially rejected the 
demand for the reorganization of the States on linguistic basis. 
*Although the demise of Potti Sriramulu after the 56-day-long 
hunger strike forced the Indian Government for the formation of 
Andhra Pradesh on linguistic basis in October, 1953. *Andhra 
Pradesh is the first State of India formed on the linguistic basis. 
*A States Reorganisation Commission under the Chairmanship 
of Fazal Ali was constituted for the formation of new States in 
1953. *Two other members of this Commission were – K.M. 
Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru. *Some of the recommendations of 
this commission were implemented in the States Reorganisation 
Act, 1956.

15. Which one of the following pairs is not correctly 
matched?
 (a) Equality before Law – Guaranteed to both citizens 
      and non-citizens
 (b) Equality of opportunity – Guaranteed only to Indian
  in public employment  citizens
 (c) Creating a new State – Power of Parliament
 (d) Altering the name of – Power of a State Legislature
  A State
U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2024
Ans. (d)
According to the Article 3 of the Constitution, Parliament 
may by law form new States, and alter the area, boundaries 
or the name of any existing State. With respect to the 
alteration of area, boundary or name of a state, it is expected, 
from the concerned State Legislature to express its views 



on the issue, under the provision of the Article 3. But the

 (c) (ii), (iii) and (iv) (d) (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv)

Raj. P.C.S. (Pre) 2023
Ans. (c)
After the intensification of demand for the creation of states 
on the linguistic basis, the Government of India appointed the 
States Reorganisation Commission under the chairmanship 
of Fazl Ali (the two members were K.M. Panikkar and H.N. 
Kunzru)to re-examine the whole demand. It submitted its 
report in September 1955 and broadly accepted language as 
the basis of the reorganisation of states but rejected the theory 
of 'one language-one state' and suggested the abolition of the 
four-fold classification of states and territories.
The Government of India, while accepting these 
recommendations with certain modifications, enacted the 
States Reorganisation Act (1956) and the 7th Constitutional 
Amendment Act (1956). The Act abolished the distinctions 
among Part A, Part B and Part C states provided by the 
original Constitution. As a result, 14 states and 6 union 
territories were created on November 1, 1956.
