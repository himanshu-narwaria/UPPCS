National Symbols
*The National Flag (Tricolour) is rectangular in shape and 
consists of three colours. *The ratio of the length of the flag to 
its width is 3:2. *The colour of the top band is Indian saffron 
(Kesariya) (symbol of strength and courage) , the middle 
band is white (symbol of peace and truth) and the bottom is 
Indian green (symbol of fertility, growth and auspiciousness 
of  the land). *In the centre of the white band is a navy blue 
wheel, which represents the Ashoka chakra. It has 24 spokes, 
and its design is that of the wheel, which appears on the abacus 
of the Sarnath lion capital of Ashoka. *It is the symbol of 
Justice- (Wheel of the law/Dharma Chakra). *The National 
flag was designed by Pingali Venkayya. *The present design 
of the national flag was adopted by the constituent assembly 
on 22 July, 1947. *The description of the importance of three 
colours and a wheel (chakra) in our national flag by Dr. S. 
Radhakrishnan is mentioned and reproduced in the Flag code 
of India.
*The state emblem is an adaptation of the Sarnath lion 
capital of Ashoka. *It was adopted by the Government of 
India on 26 January, 1950. *The words 'Satyameva Jayate' 
from Mundaka Upanishad are inscribed at the bottom in the 
Devanagari Script. *The National Anthem of India 'Jana Gana 
Mana.....' was officially adopted by the constituent assembly 
on 24th January, 1950. *It was first sung on 27th December, 
1911 at the Calcutta session of the Indian National Congress 
(President- Pt. Bishan Narayan Dar). *It was authored by 
Rabindra Nath Tagore. The Playing time of the full version is 
approximately 52 seconds. *The song 'Vande Mataram' was 
adopted as the national song by the constituent assembly on 
24 January, 1950. It is taken from the novel "Anand Math" 
authored by Bankimchandra Chatterjee. *It was first sung at 
the 1896 session of the Indian National Congress (President 
-Rahimatullah Sayani).
*The National Calendar based on the Saka era (Chaitra as its 
first month and Falgun as the last) was adopted on 22nd March, 
1957 along with the Gregorian Calendar. Generally, the month 
of Chaitra begins on 22 March, but in the leap years, it begins 
on 21 March.
*The National Flower of India is the Lotus (Nelumbo nucifera 
Gaertn). The National fruit is Mango (Magnifera Indica). 
*The National tree is the Banyan tree (Ficus benghalensis). 
*While the government has clearly stated in the Rajya Sabha 
that no notification has been issued regarding India having a 
National flower.  *The National Animal is the magnificent Tiger 
(Panthera tigris). *The National Bird is the Indian Peacock 
(Pavo cristatus). *The National Aquatic animal is the mammal 
Ganges River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica).
*The river Ganga was declared the national river by the 
government of India in the year 2008. *The Government of 
India declared the Independence Day (15 August), Republic 
Day (26 January) and the Birth Anniversary of Mahatama 
Gandhi (2 October) as National Days.

10.  Consider the following statements in respect of the 
National Flag of India according to the Flag Code of 
India, 2002:
 Statement-I :
 One of the standard sizes of  the National Flag of India 
is 600 mm × 400 mm.
 Statement-II :
 The ratio of the length to the height (width) of the Flag 
shall be 3:2.
 Which one of the following is correct in respect of the 
above statements? 
 (a)  Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and 
Statement-II is the correct explanation for Statement-I 
 (b)  Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct 
and Statement-II is not the correct explanation for 
Statement-I
 (c)  Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
 (d)  Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
I.A.S. (Pre.) 2023
Ans. (d)
The Flag Code of India 2002 allowed the unrestricted 
display of the Tricolour as long as the honour and  
the dignity of the flag was being respected. It 
takes effect from January 26, 2002. For the sake of  
convenience, it is divided into three parts - 1. General 
description of the tricolour, 2. Rules on display of the flag by



public and private bodies and educational institutions, 3. Rules 
for the display of the flag by governments and government 
bodies. According to the provision of the code, the National 
Flag shall be rectangular in shape and the ratio of the length
