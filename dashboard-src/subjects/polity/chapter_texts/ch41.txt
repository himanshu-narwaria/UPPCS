Special Provisions Relating to
Certain Classes
*The special provisions relating to certain classes are contained
in Part XVI of the Constitution from Articles 330 to 342 A. *Part
XVI also includes Articles 330A, 332A and 334A inserted by
the Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act,
2023. *Article 330 provides for the reservation of seats for
SCs and STs in the House of the People. *Under Article 331,
provision was made for the representation of the Anglo-Indian
community in the House of the people. It is noteworthy that
the arrangements of nominating representatives of the AngloIndian Community in the House of the People have also not
been extended beyond the stipulated date (25 January, 2020)
by 'the Constitution (One Hundred and Fourth Amendment)
Act, 2019. *Article 332 provides for the reservation of seats
for SCs and STs in the legislative assemblies of the States.
*Under Article 333, provision was made for the representation
of the Anglo-Indian Community in the Legislative Assemblies
of the States. It is noteworthy that the arrangements of
nominating representatives of the Anglo-Indian Community
in the Legislative Assemblies of the states have also not been
extended beyond the stipulated date (25 January, 2020) by 'the
Constitution (One Hundred and Fourth Amendment) Act, 2019.
*Article 338 provides for provisions relating to the National
Commission for SCs. *At present, Kishor Makwana is the
chairman of this commission. *Article 338A was added by the
89th Constitutional Amendment Act 2003, which provides
for a separate National Commission for STs. *The President
can appoint a commission to investigate the conditions of
Socially and educationally backward classes (Article 340).
*The President can declare any caste, race, tribe, part or groups
of a State/U.T. as a Scheduled Caste after consulting with the
Governor of that State. (Article 341(1)). *The President can
declare any caste of a state/UT as a Scheduled Tribe after
consulting with the Governor of that State [Article 342(1)].
Note : The Constitution (One Hundred and Fourth Amendment)
Act, 2019 provides for extending the period of reservation of
seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes for the next
10 years, whereas the provision for nomination of Anglo-Indian
members has not been made beyond the predetermined date
(25 January, 2020). So, currently this arrangement has ended.
Newly added Articles in Part XVI (added by the 106th
Constitution Amendment Act, 2023) have provisions as
mentioned below.
Article 300 A : As nearly as may be, one-third (including the
number of seats reserved for women belonging to the Scheduled
Castes and the Scheduled Tribes) of the total number of seats
to be filled by direct election to the House of the People shall
be reserved for women.
Article 332 A : As nearly as may be, one-third of the total
number of seats to be filled by direct election to the Legislative
Assembly of every State shall be reserved for women
(including the number of seats reserved for women of SC and
ST categories).
Article 334 A : When the Reservation of Seats for Women
takes effect : The reservation, of one-third of the total seats to
be filled by direct election, for women shall take effect after a
delimitation is undertaken for the figures of the first Census
taken after the commencement of the 106th Constitution
Amendment Act, 2023.
This reservation shall cease to have effect, on the expiration of
a period of fifteen years from such commencement.

Article 332 of the Indian Constitution provides for the
reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and
Scheduled Tribes (STs) in the Legislative Assemblies of
states. The number of reserved seats is determined based on
the proportion of the SC or ST population in relation to the
state’s total population. It ensures that the representation of

total number of seats. Additionally, this provision extends to

(a) Article 340 (b) Article 349
(c) Article 342 (d) Article 338

Ans. (c)
Article 342 of the Indian Constitution empowers the President
to declare certain tribes or tribal communities as Scheduled
Tribes for a state or union territory. This declaration is made
through a public notification after consulting the state’s
Governor. Parliament has the authority to add or remove
tribes from the Scheduled Tribes list through legislation.
Once the President issues a notification under this provision,
it cannot be altered by any subsequent notification.

As per Article 342(1) of the Constitution of India, the
President may with respect to any State or Union Territory,
and where it is a State, after consultation with the Governor
thereof, by a public notification, specify the tribes or tribal
communities or parts of or groups within tribes or tribal
communities as a Scheduled Tribe in relation to that State

342 empowers the Parliament to pass a law to include in or

Article 342(1) mandates to specify the tribes or tribal
communities or part of or groups within tribes or tribal
communities as Scheduled Tribe in relation to that State or
Union Territory as the case may be. Thus the list of Scheduled
Tribes is State/UT specific and a community declared as a
Scheduled Tribe in a State need not be so in another State.
Hence, statement 2 is correct.

community in the Lok Sabha was made under Article 331
of the Constitution. According to this Article, even though
provisions were made in Article 81, if the President was

26. Consider the following organizations/ bodies in India:
1. The National Commission for Backward Classes
2. The National Human Rights Commission
2. The National Law Commission

Commission
How many of the above are constitutional bodies?
(a) Only one (b) Only two
(c) Only three (d) All four
I.A.S. (Pre.) 2023
Ans. (a)
The National Commission for Backward Classes is a
constitutional body under Article 338B of the Indian
Constitution. It was established through the Constitution
102nd Amendment Act, 2018. It has the authority to examine
complaints and welfare measures regarding socially and
educationally backward classes. The Commission consists
of five members including a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson
and three other Members appointed by the President by
warrant under his hand and seal. The National Human
Rights Commission is a statutory body established under the
Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. The Law Commission
of India is an executive body whose chief function is to
recommend legal reforms in the country. The National
Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission was established
in 1988 under the Consumer Protection Act of 1986. It is a
quasi-judicial body and statutory in nature.
