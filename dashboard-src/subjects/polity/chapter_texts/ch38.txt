Constitutional Amendment
*Any constitution, even if it is made wisely, needs amendments 
in time and circumstances.
*Indian Constitution is a mixture of rigidity and flexibility . 
*It contains the provision of Amendments in Article 368 of 
Part-XX of the Indian Constitution.
*The Indian Constitution can be amended in three different 
ways.
1. Simple majority
2. Special majority
3. Special majority and by the ratification of not less 
than one-half of the state legislatures. A Constitutional 
Amendment bill can be introduced in either House of the 
Parliament. *Constitutional Amendment bill needs to be 
passed by both the Houses of the Parliament separately 
with a special majority and there is no provision for 
the joint sitting of the Houses for the Constitutional 
Amendment bill.
*Election of President, Representation of States in the 
Parliament, Supreme and High Court, power of Parliament 
to amend the constitution etc, are the subjects that require 
a special majority of the Parliament and ratification by at 
least half of the state legislatures for the Amendment. *Any 
amendment in the provision of the Constitution by a simple 
majority in both the Houses is not included in the amendment 
under Article 368. *These include the provisions regarding 
admission or establishment of new states, alteration of names, 
areas, boundaries, etc.
*The 1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951 added two new 
Articles i.e. Article 31A and 31B and also the 9th Schedule. 
*42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is known as a 
mini-constitution. *52nd Amendment Act is related to the 
Anti-defection law. *61st Amendment Act, 1988 reduced the 
voting age from 21 years to 18 years. *69th Amendment Act, 
1991 gave Delhi the status of the National Capital Territory of 
Delhi.  *86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 added 
Article 21A, i.e. Right to Education, Provision for Free and 
compulsory education for children from the age of 6 years 
to 14 years. *The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 amended 
Articles 75 and 164 and Schedule 10. *It inserted a new Article 
361B. *This Amendment restricts the size of the Council of 
Ministers, including the Prime Minister to aid and advise the 
President. *That cannot be more than 15% of the total number 
of members of the House of the People at the Union level and 
the total number of members of the state legislative assembly 
at the level of the State. *97th Amendment Act 2011 gave 
constitutional status to the co-operative societies (Part IX B 
inserted by the 97 th Amendment is now operative only for 
multi-State Co-operative Societies). *100th Constitutional 
Amendment Act is related to the India-Bangladesh boundary 
treaty. *101st Amendment Act is related to the GST (Goods 
and Services Tax). The National Commission for Backward 
Classes has been given constitutional status through the 102nd 
Amendment Act. The 103 rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 
2019 deals with providing 10 percent reservation in government 
Jobs and education to the Economically Weaker Sections 
(EWS) of citizens. This EWS category includes citizens other 
than the classes mentioned in clauses (4) and (5) of Article 15 
and clause (4) of Article 16. *The Doctrine of basic structure 
of the Constitution refers to the  features of the Constitution 
which are inviolable and cannot be amended or altered by the 
Parliament. *The doctrine of basic structure was given by 
the Supreme Court in the famous Keshavananda Bharati 
case of 1973. The 105th Constitutional amendment amends 
clauses 1 and 2 of Article 342A and also introduces a new 
clause 3. It also amends Articles 366 (26C) and 338B(9). It 
is designed to clarify that states can maintain the 'state list' of 
OBCs. The Constitution (106 th Amendment) Act, 2023 is the 
Latest amendment in the Indian Constitution. It reserves one-
third of all seats for women in Lok Sabha, State Legislative 



Assemblies, and the Legislative Assembly of the National 
Capital Territory of Delhi, including those reserved for SCs 
and STs. This reservation for women shall come into effect 
after the next delimitation exercise following the first census 
conducted after the Act's commencement, and shall remain 
in effect for 15 years from such commencement.
1. Assertion (A) : Under Article 368, the Parliament can 
amend any part of the Constitution.
 Reason (R) : The Parliament is the Supreme 
Legislative body elected by the People 
of India.
 Code :
 (a) Both (A) and (R) are correct, and (R) is the correct 
explanation of (A).
 (b) Both (A) and (R) are correct, but (R) is not the correct 
explanation of (A).
 (c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
 (d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
 U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2015 
Ans. (a)
Article 368 of the Constitution of India deals with the 
power of Parliament to amend the Constitution. Therefore, 
it says that notwithstanding anything in this Constitution, 
Parliament may in the exercise of its constituent power 
amend by way of addition, variation or repeal, any provision 
of this Constitution in accordance with the procedure laid 
down in this Article. However, the Parliament cannot 
amend those provisions that form the 'basic structure' of 
the Constitution. This was ruled by the Supreme Court in 
the Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973). The Parliament is 
the supreme Legislative Body elected by the People of 
India. Therefore, Parliament has the authority to amend 
the Constitution. Thus, both assertion (A) and Reason (R) 
are individually correct and (R) is the correct explanation 
of (A).

amendment of the following topics requires consent of

 1.  Federal provision of the Constitution
 2.  Rights  of the Supreme Court

 4. Formation of new States or transformation in the

U.P.P.C.S. Lower (Pre) 2004
Ans. (a)



The Indian Constitution is flexible and adaptable. Amendments 
can be made to it as per the procedure given in Article 368 of 
the Constitution as per necessity. Amendments can be made 
to the Indian Constitution in three types -
1. By a simple majority—There are certain provisions in 
the Constitution that can be amended by Parliament through 
a simple majority. Such amendments are not considered 
constitutional amendments under the purposes of Article 368. 
For example—changing the names and boundaries of states 
or creating new states, creating or abolishing Legislative 
Councils in states, amendments involving increasing or 
decreasing the salaries of the President, Governors, Supreme 
Court, and High Court judges are made through a simple 
majority.
2. By a special majority— Under the procedure of Article 
368(2), Parliament, by a special majority in each of its 
Houses, (a majority of the total membership and a two-thirds 
majority of members present and voting), can pass a bill to 
amend most of the articles of the Constitution.
3. By a special majority and approval of the states—  
According to the proviso to Article 368(2), there are certain 
provisions in the Constitution where amendments require the 
special majority of Parliament along with the approval of at 
least half of the state legislatures. For example—the election 
of the President and its procedure, the extent of the executive 
power of the Union and the states, provisions related to the 
Supreme Court and High Courts, representation of states in 
Parliament, the power and procedure of Parliament to amend 
the Constitution, division of legislative powers between the 
Union and the states and entries in the lists described in 
the Seventh Schedule, provisions related to the Goods and 
Services Tax (GST) Council (Article 279A), etc.

13.  Consider the following subjects under the Constitution 
of India :
 I. List I-Union List, in the Seventh Schedule
 II. Extent of the executive power of a State
 III. Conditions of the Governor's office
 For a constitutional amendment with respect to 
which of the above, ratification by the Legislatures 
of not less than one-half of the States is required 
before presenting the bill to the President of India for 
assent?
 (a) I and II only (b) II and III only
 (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
I.A.S. (Pre) 2025
Ans. (a)



The power and procedure to amend the Constitution are 
given in Article 368. According to Article 368(2), after an 
Amendment Bill is passed in each House by (i) a majority 
of the total membership of that House and (ii) a majority of 
not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting, 
it is presented to the President for assent. The first proviso 
to this clause provides that if the amendment relates to 
certain specified provisions, then before it is presented to 
the President, it must be ratified by the Legislatures of not 
less than one-half of the States. In the first proviso to Article 
368(2), ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures 
is required for an amendment that seeks to make changes 
in the following kinds of provisions: (a) Certain specified 
Articles—54, 55, 73, 162, 241 and 279A. (b) Chapter IV 
of Part V , Chapter V of Part VI, or Chapter I of Part XI 
(respectively: the Union Judiciary, the High Courts in the 
States, and the distribution of legislative powers between 
the Union and the States). (c) Any of the Lists in the Seventh 
Schedule. (d) Provisions relating to the representation of 
States in Parliament. (e) Provisions relating to Article 368 
itself.  In clause (c) of the first proviso to Article 368(2), the 
condition of ratification by half of the State Legislatures is 
prescribed for an amendment that changes “any of the Lists 
in the Seventh Schedule.”  In clause (a) of the first proviso 
to Article 368(2), the Articles specifically listed by name 
include Article 162 (and after the 101st Amendment in 2016, 
Article 279A as well)—it is placed there along with Articles 
54, 55, 73, 162 and 241.  Therefore, Subject II also falls 
within the matters for which State ratification is necessary 
for an amendment.  Article 158 is neither included among 
the Articles named in the proviso to Article 368, nor does it 
fall within the mentioned Chapters/Lists— therefore, State 
ratification is not required.  Therefore, only I and II are such 
subjects for which, before presenting the Constitutional 
Amendment Bill to the President, ratification by at least half 
of the State Legislatures is mandatory. Therefore, the correct 
option is (a).

 (a) 1st Amendment  (b) 42nd Amendment

Constitution as part of the Constituent Assembly. The First

77. The Keshavananda Bharati Case is important because- 
 (a) It sets aside the orders of the executive
 (b) The Supreme Court interpreted the basic features of 
the Constitution
 (c) It brought the Union Government in the dock.
 (d)  None of the above
U.P.P.C.S.  (Pre) 2005
Ans. (b)
The Supreme Court laid down the 'Basic Structure Doctrine' 
in the case of  Keshavananda Bharati Vs State of Kerala, 
1973. According to this doctrine, Parliament can amend the 
Constitution including the fundamental rights but some of 
the provisions of the Constitution of India, which form the  
basic structure of the Constitution, are not amendable by the 
Parliament by exercising its amending power under Article 
368.
78. In which case(s) did the Supreme Court of India give 
for the first time the doctrine of ‘basic structure’ of the 
Constitution?
 (a) Golaknath vs. State of Punjab
 (b) Kesavananda Bharati vs. State of Kerala
 (c) Minerva Mills vs. Union of India
 (d) Both (A) and (B)
69th B.P.S.C. (Pre) 2023
Ans. (b)
The Supreme Court of India examined the concept of the 
"Basic structure" of the constitution for the first time in 
Kesavananda Bharti v. State of Kerala in 1973. Significantly, 
the Supreme Court reversed its decision in the Golak Nath 
case in this instance. It declared that Parliament has the 
authority to restrict or eliminate any of the Fundamental 
Rights and confirmed the legality of the 24 th Constitution 
Amendment Act. It also established a new theory regarding 
the "basic structure" or "basic features" of the Constitution. 
It decided that Parliament could not change the "basic 
structure" of the Constitution using its constituent power 
under Article 368. This implies that a Fundamental Right that 
is a component of the Constitution's "basic structure" cannot 
be restricted or eliminated by the Parliament.

The Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) 
Act, 2023 (also known as "Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam')
