The Parliament (2)
*The Upper House of the Parliament is also known as the 
Rajya Sabha. It is also known as the 'Council of States'. 
*The Maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha is fixed at 250. 
According to Article 80, the Rajya Sabha consists of not 
more than 238 members from the States and Union Territories 
(elected indirectly) and 12 other members are nominated by the 
President from amongst the persons having special knowledge 
or practical experience in the field of literature, science, art 
and social service (Present Strength is 245, out of which 12 
are nominated members). *Representatives of each state shall 
be elected by the elected members of the legislative assembly 
of the state in accordance with the system of proportional 
representation by means of the single transferable vote. *Only 3 
Union territories (Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir) 
out of 8 have representatives in Rajya Sabha. *Rajya Sabha is 
a Permanent house. Hence it is not subject to dissolution. *As 
nearly as possible 1/3rd members of the Rajya Sabha retire at 
the end of every second year. *The term of its members is 6 
years. *A Person to be elected as a member of Rajya Sabha 
must fulfil two criteria- (1) he must be a citizen of India and 
(2) he must not be less than 30 years of age. In addition to the 
prior two conditions, he must be an elector for a parliamentary 
constituency in India. *The Vice-President of India is the ex-
officio chairman of the Council of States/Upper House of 
Parliament. *When the Vice-President acts as the President 
he shall not be entitled to get the salary and allowances of the 
Chairman of Rajya Sabha. For this period, he receives salary 
and allowances of the President. *Rajya Sabha elects a deputy 
chairman from amongst its members. *The Deputy Chairman 
gives his resignation in writing to the Chairman. *Rajya Sabha 
has the power to remove the Deputy Chairman from his post, 
by a resolution passed by the absolute majority of the total 
members of Rajya Sabha. Such resolution can be moved only 
after giving at least 14 days' notice. *When the post of Chairman 
is vacant, the deputy chairman will perform the duties of the 
chairman. *While presiding over the Rajya Sabha, the Deputy 
Chairman can't vote in general, he can only exercise a casting 
vote in case of a tie. *When a resolution to remove the Vice-
President from his office is under consideration, he will not be 
presiding as the Chairman and when a resolution of removal 
of the Deputy Chairman is under consideration, he will not be 
presiding as the Deputy Chairman. *The representation of states 
in Rajya Sabha is given in proportion to their population. *For 
a quorum, there must be the presence of one-tenth of the total 
number of members in the Rajya Sabha. *The Representation 
of People Act 1951 (through an amendment in 2003) made 
a provision that any person can contest from anywhere for 
Rajya Sabha even if he is a resident of any other state. Now for 
contesting Rajya Sabha elections, a person must be an elector 
in a parliamentary constituency anywhere in India. *The 4th 
Schedule of the Indian Constitution enumerates the allocation 
of Rajya Sabha seats for states and union territories. *Article 
249 states that if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution with a 2/3 
majority of its members present and voting,  the Parliament can 
make laws  in the national interest regarding the subjects of the 
state list, which are specified in the resolution.  *It is valid for a 
period of 1 year, but it can be extended by passing a resolution. 
*Article 250 empowers the Parliament to legislate with respect 
to any matter in the state list if the proclamation of emergency 
is in operation but, six months after the emergency ceases to 
operate, it automatically expires. *Article 252 empowers the 
Parliament to legislate for two or more states by consent and 
adoption of such legislation by any other state. *According to 
Article 253, Parliament has the power to make any law for 
the whole or part of the territory of India for implementing 
international agreements or conventions with any other country 
or any decision made at any international conference.

27. Consider the following statements :
 1. The Rajya Sabha has no power either to reject or 
to amend a Money Bill.
 2. The Rajya Sabha cannot vote on the Demands for 
Grants.
 3. The Rajya Sabha cannot discuss the Annual 
Financial Statements.
 Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
 (a) 1 only (b)   1 and 2 only
 (c) 2 and 3 only (d)   1, 2 and 3
I.A.S. (Pre) 2015 
Ans. (b)
According to Article 109 of the Indian Constitution, a 
Money Bill shall not be introduced in the Council of States. 
After the Money Bill has been passed by the House of 
People, it shall be transmitted to the Council of States for 
its recommendation and the Council of States shall, within a 
period of 14 days from the date of its receipt of the Bill, return 
the Bill to the House of People with its recommendation and 
the House of People may thereupon either accept or reject all 
or any of the recommendation of the Council of States. So 
the Rajya Sabha has no power either to reject or amend the 
Bill. According to Article 113(2) of the Indian Constitution, 
estimates are submitted in the form of Demands for Grants 
to the House of the People and only the House of the People 
has the power to assent or refuse to assent. Rajya Sabha 
cannot vote on the Demand for Grants. So statements 1 and 
2 are correct, and statement 3 is wrong. The Rajya Sabha can 
discuss the Annual Financial Statement but it has no power 
to vote on the same.

61. Identify the incorrect statement regarding Council of 
States (Rajya Sabha).
 (a) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan is the only Chairman of Rajya 
Sabha for two consecutive terms.
 (b) For the first constitutional amendment, ratification of 
the Rajya Sabha was not taken.
 (c) Currently eight members are elected to Rajya Sabha 
from the Union Territories.
 (d) There have been three occasions, when joint sitting 
of Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha was held.
 (e) Question not attempted
Raj. P.C.S. (Pre) 2023
Ans. (a)
The Council of States, the Upper House of the Indian 
Parliament, was constituted in the year 1952. However, the 
origin of the idea for a Second Chamber can be traced to the 
“Montague- Chelmsford Reform of 1919”. The Constitution 
First Amendment Act was enacted in 1951. At that time, the 
Rajya Sabha did  not exist. Article 80 of the Constitution 
deals with the composition of the Council of States. Clause 
1 of this Article provides that the maximum strength of the 
Rajya Sabha is 250, out of which 12 members are nominated 
by the President and 238 are representatives of the States 
and of the Union territories. The present strength, however, 
is 245 members, of whom 233 are representatives of the 
States and Union territories and 12 are nominated by the 
President. Currently, there are 8 members ( 3 from NCT 
Delhi, 1 from Puducherry and 4 from Jammu and Kashmir) 
elected to the Rajya Sabha. The first Vice-President, Dr. S. 
Radhakrishnan, is not the only Chairman of the Rajya Sabha 
who has been in the office for two consecutive terms from 
13 May, 1952 to 12 May 1962; Mohammad Hamid Ansari 
has also been Vice-President/ Chairman of Rajya Sabha 
for two consecutive terms (2007-2017). The Joint Sitting 
(Article 108) of the Indian Parliament has been called for 
only 3 bills: the Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1962, the Banking 
Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1978 and the Prevention 
of Terrorism Bill, 2002.

 (c) 1952  (d) 1953
U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2017

The Rajya Sabha, or Council of States, is the upper house 
of the Parliament of India. Article 80 of the Constitution 
lays down the maximum strength of Rajya Sabha as 250,
