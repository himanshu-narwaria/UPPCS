The Governor
*The Constitution of India envisages the same pattern of
government in the states as that for the centre, that is, a
parliamentary system. Articles 153 to 167 in Part VI of the
Constitution deal with the Government of the state. *The
Governor is the executive head of the state. Generally, a
person is the governor of only one state and after the 7th
Constitutional Amendment Act of 1956, the same person
can be appointed as the governor of two or more States. *The
Governor is appointed by the President of India (Article 155).
*He holds office for a term of 5 years from the date on which
he enters upon his office. *However, his tenure is subject
to the pleasure of the President [Article 156(1)]. *Further
he can resign at any time by addressing a resignation letter
to the President [Article 156(2)]. *According to Article
157, the qualifications for the post of the Governor are - (i)
He should be a citizen of India, and (ii) age over 35 years.
Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act 2019 reorganised
the state of Jammu & Kashmir into : (i) the Union territory
of Jammu & Kashmir with a legislature, and (ii) the Union
Territory of Ladakh without a legislature. Both the newly
created Union Territories will be administered by the President
through a Lieutenant Governor appointed by him. *All the
executive works of the state is formally done in the name of the
Governor. *The Chief Minister of the National Capital Territory
of Delhi is appointed by the President (Article 239 AA).
*The salary, allowances and other expenditures related to the
Governor are charged upon the Consolidated Fund of each state
[Article 202(3)(a)]. *The Governor has the power to appoint
the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers on the advice
of the Chief Minister [Article 164(1)]. *He is an integral part
of State Legislative Assembly and has the right to address and
send messages and of summoning/proroguing and dissolving
the State Legislative Assembly. *The Governor has the right to
nominate one-sixth of the members of the legislative council.
Governor of a state, if he is of the opinion that the Anglo-Indian
community needs representation in the Legislative Assembly
of the state and is not adequately represented therein, could
nominate one member of that community to the Assembly. It is
noteworthy that the arrangements of nominating representatives
of the Anglo-Indian community in the state Legislative
Assemblies have not been extended beyond the stipulated
date (25 January, 2020) by 'The Constitution (One Hundred
and Fourth Amendment) Act, 2019. Thus, the arrangements
of nomination of one member of the Anglo-Indian Community
in the Legislative Assemblies has ended. *A person who has
the qualification required for the judge of a High Court can be
appointed as the Advocate General by the Governor (Art. 165).
*When a bill is sent to the Governor after it is passed by State
Legislature, he can give his assent to the bill, or return the bill
(if it is not money bill) for reconsideration, or reserve the Bill
for the consideration of the President. *However if the bill is
passed again by the state legislature the governor may give
his assent to the bill or reserve the Bill for the consideration of
the President. *According to Article 201, if a bill is reserved
by the Governor for the consideration of the President, then
the President will declare whether to give assent to the bill
or not. *Article 202 (3) (a) the emoluments and allowances
of the Governor and other expenditure relating to office are
charged on the consolidated fund of the state. *The Governor
can promulgate an ordinance when the state legislature is not
in session (Article 213), and only if the circumstances require
immediate action. *He can grant pardons, reprieves, respites
and remission of punishment or suspend, remit and commute
the sentence of any person convicted of any offence against
any law relating to a matter to which the executive power of
the state extends (Article 161). *Sarojini Naidu was the first
woman to be appointed as the Governor of any state. *She was
the governor of Uttar Pradesh (United Provinces) from 15th
August, 1947 to 2 March, 1949.

There was much debate in the Constituent Assembly regarding
the subject of the appointment or election of Governor in
the States. Krishnamachari, B.G. Kher, J. B. Pant were in
favour of election, while B. R. Ambedkar and Jawaharlal
Nehru were in favour of nominated Governor. Finally, with
a general consensus, the provision of a nominated Governor
was accepted and the original proposal for the provision
of elected Governor was cancelled as- (1) this would have
increased election expenditure. (2) Nomination of governor
would help avoid a clash of political ambition of the Chief
Minister and the Governor as the election would have been
fought on major issues, they would have pursued different
policies. (3) The Centre would not have effective control
over the State otherwise.

Ans. (d)
According to Article 156(1) of the Constitution, a Governor

continues to enjoy the “pleasure of the President,” he or she
can hold the office for a term of five years. A Governor may

28. Consider the following statements :

Governor shall act on the advice of the Council of Ministers
headed by the Chief Minister; however, where the Governor

(c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
I.A.S. (Pre) 2025
Ans. (d)

Article 361 of the Constitution of India grants special
protection from legal accountability to the President and
the Governor. Under Article 361(1), the Governor is not
answerable to any court for acts done in the exercise of the
powers and duties of his office. The same Article 361 under
clause (2) clearly states that, during the Governor’s term
of office (same in the case of the President), no criminal
proceedings of any kind can be instituted, nor can such
proceedings be continued. In this manner, he enjoys complete
criminal immunity during his tenure. The provision regarding
privileges and immunity for members of the State Legislature
is contained in Article 194(2) of the Constitution. According
to it, no member of a State Legislature shall be liable to any
proceedings in any court in respect of anything said or any
vote given by him in the Legislature or any committee thereof.
This immunity is absolute, provided the proceedings relate
to something said or a vote given within the House or any of
its committees. Thus, all three statements are in accordance
with the constitutional provisions; therefore, the correct
answer is option (d).
