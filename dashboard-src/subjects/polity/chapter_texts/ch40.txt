Panchayati Raj and
Community Development
*Panchayati Raj is a system of democratic decentralisation.
Its objective is to bring the development to the grass-root level.
*There are provisions related to Panchayati Raj in Part-IX
from Article 243 to Article 243-O in the Indian-Constitution.
*16 New Articles in Part-IX and the 11th Schedule wear
added in Indian Constitution by the 73rd Constitutional
Amendment Act 1992. Total 29 subjects are mentioned in
the 11th Schedule on which, the State legislature may, by
law, endow the Panchayats with such powers and authority
necessary to enable them to implement schemes for economic
development and social justice. *Panchayati Raj is a subject of
state list. *The state legislature determines provisions related
to the structure of panchayats by law. *The State legislature
makes decisions with respect to all matters relating to elections
to the Panchayat. The State legislatures makes decision by law.
The State Election Commission has superintendence, direction,
control of rolls & conduct of elections. *It is conducted every
5 years. *There is a provision to form a Finance Commission
to review the financial condition of panchayats. *Gram Sabha,
is formed by the people registered in the electoral rolls of
a village. *Article 40 of the Indian Constitution directs the
state to organize village panchayats. *Balwant Rai Mehta
committee recommended the establishment of a three-tier
Panchayati Raj system at the Village level, Block level and
District level. *Balwant Rai Mehta is called the Architect of
the Panchayati Raj system. *The inauguration of Panchayati
Raj system was done by Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru on 2nd October
1959 at Nagaur (Rajasthan). *Panchayati Raj institutions got
constitutional status by the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act.
*Firstly the Laxmi Mal Singhvi Committee in 1986 and then
P.K. Thungan Committee in 1988 recommended Panchayati
Raj Institutions be given constitutional status. *Earlier, Ashok
Mehta Committee, in 1977 proposed that some provisions
of Panchayati Raj can be kept in the Indian Constitution, so
the Government of India should think over it. *Due to the
collapse of the Janta Government, no action could be taken
on the recommendations of the Ashok Mehta Committee at
the Central Level. *So, the recommendations of Laxmi Mal
Singhvi and P.K. Thungan committee are clearly accepted.
Major recommendations of the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
are also accepted in the Part IX of the Constitution. *According
to Article 243-D(3) for seats and under Article 243D(4) for
Chairperson's office, women are given 1/3rd reservation at all
levels of Panchayats.
*The minimum age to become a member of the Panchayat is
21 years. *The 74th Constitutional Amendment Act 1992,
gives constitutional status to the Urban Local Self-government.
*By this Amendment Act, a new part [Part IX A] and the 12th
Schedule were added to the Constitution.
*There are 8 types of local self-government which include
Municipal Corporation, Municipality, Notified Area Committee,
Town Area Committee, Cantonment Board, Township, Major
Port Authority/Port Authority (earlier, Port Trust) and Special
Purpose Agencies.
*The 74th Amendment Act provides three types of municipalities.
*This includes Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council and
a Nagar Panchayat.
