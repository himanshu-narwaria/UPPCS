Election Commission
*Articles 324 to 329 in Part XV of the Constitution deal with 
the provisions related to the electoral system. *According to 
Article 324, there shall be an election commission, which 
is a permanent and independent body. *This commission 
is responsible for superintendence, direction and control of 
the preparation of the electoral rolls for, and the conduct 
of, all  elections to the Parliament, the Legislature of every 
State, the President and the Vice-President. *The election 
commission consists of a Chief Election Commissioner and 
other election commissioners (the number being decided by 
the President from time to time) [Art. 324(2)]. The Chief 
Election Commissioner and other election commissioners are 
appointed by the president . *Till 15 October, 1989 there 
was only a Chief Election Commissioner, but on 16 October, 



1989 two additional election commissioners were appointed 
for the first time. *But these posts were in effect upto the 1st 
of January 1990. *On 1st October 1993, again two additional 
election commissioners were appointed and since then the 
election commission has three members. At present, there 
are two Election Commissioners and one Chief Election 
Commissioner in the Election Commission. They have equal 
power and their salary and perks are the same as those of the 
Judges of the Supreme Court. *Their tenure is for  6 years or 
up to the age of 65 years (whichever is earlier). *The chief 
election commissioner can be removed from his office only 
in a manner and on the grounds prescribed for the removal 
of Judges of the Supreme Court [Article 324(5)]. *Other  
Election Commissioner or the Regional Commissioner can 
be removed by the President on the recommendation of the 
Chief Election Commissioner. *The notifications for the  
elections to the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha are issued by 
the President and for the state legislative assemblies by the 
Governor. *Article 326 mentions the provision of Adult 
Suffrage. The right to vote in India is a Statutory Right. 
*As per the Representation of the People Act 1951, there 
is right to vote and to be elected in India. *The age for the 
right to vote was decreased from 21 years to 18 years by the 
61st Constitutional Amendment Act 1988 which came into 
effect from 28 March 1989. *The elections to the local bodies 
(Panchayats and Municipalities) is being conducted by the State 
Election Commissions. *The Delimitation Commission is a 
commission established by the Government of India. Its main 
task is to redraw the boundaries of various assemblies and lok 
sabha constituencies based on the recent census. Till now, the 
Delimitation commission has been set up for five times (1952, 
1963, 1973 and 2002) and the latest in March 2020. The 
March 2020, Delimitation Commission was constituted via 
notification for delimitation of the UT of Jammu & Kashmir.

Ans. (c)
The right to vote and the right to be elected are legal rights 
in India. The Supreme Court pointed out the difference 
between a legal right and a fundamental right in a number 
of cases. Some significant differences are: (a) A legal right is 
the offshoot of the ordinary law framed by the Legislature, 
whereas fundamental rights are of a transcendental character.

the fundamental rights are placed beyond the reach of the 
State, except as provided by the Constitution.
