Money and Banking

Covers money supply, inflation, price indices, banks, RBI and monetary policy, capital markets and rural credit.

New finance terms and digital money

  • InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts): SEBI-regulated pooled infrastructure funds; income taxable; borrowers under SARFAESI Act 2002.
  • Interest Coverage Ratio: firm's ease of paying interest; higher = better; shows present and emerging risk.
  • Insolvency = state of being unable to pay debts; bankruptcy = legal process to sell or reorganize assets.
  • IBBI: regulator of insolvency; set up 1 Oct 2016; powers from Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

Money supply and currency

  • M3 (Broad Money) = currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI + time deposits = M1 + time deposits.UPPSC
  • Money multiplier = Broad Money (M3) / Reserve Money (M0).UPPSC ×2
  • Black money: illegal income or unrecorded income on which tax not paid.UPPSC ×2
  • Demonetization aims: curb black money, fake notes, terror funds; widen tax base; less cash; more digital. Raising GDP growth not an aim.UPPSC
  • Paper Currency Act 1861 gave Govt monopoly of notes; first Govt paper currency 1862.UPPSC
  • Decimal coinage: 1 April 1957 ("Naya paisa"); "Naya" dropped 1 June 1964.UPPSC ×3
  • RBI can print notes up to Rs 10,000 (Sec 24, RBI Act 1934).UPPSC
  • Note issue: Minimum Reserve System (1956) replaced proportional reserve system.UPPSC ×3
  • Since 1957: minimum Rs 200 cr gold and foreign securities, of which gold at least Rs 115 cr.UPPSC
  • Coins issued by Ministry of Finance (GoI); RBI only distributes them.UPPSC
  • Four mints: Mumbai, Noida (UP), Kolkata, Hyderabad.UPPSC

Basic concepts

  • Balance of Trade (BoT) = exports − imports of goods only (visible items).UPPSC
  • Net exports = exports − imports; positive = trade surplus, negative = trade deficit.UPPSC
  • Balance of Payments (BoP): account of a country's exports, imports and other transactions with world, usually one year.UPPSC
  • BoP = complete record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and rest of world.UPPSC
  • BoP includes visible trade, invisible trade and borrowings.UPPSC
  • Current Account Deficit (CAD): payments exceed receipts from goods, services, transfers, net income; happens when country imports more.UPPSC
  • Invisible export = export of services (tourism, banking, insurance, software).UPPSC ×2
  • Entrepot trade: goods imported, stored, then re-exported without processing or customs duty.UPPSC
  • Tariff: tax on imports; raises import prices → reduces volume of trade.UPPSC
  • Duty drawback: refund of customs/excise duty on inputs used to make export goods.UPPSC
  • Super 301 (Special 301): annual US (USTR) list of foreign trade barriers; means blockade in free trade.UPPSC

Theories and demographic transition

  • Natural growth = crude birth rate − crude death rate; migration not counted.UPPSC
  • Demographic transition (term by Frank W. Notestein): gradual long-term change in pattern of population growth.UPPSC
  • Stable population: birth and death rates constant, low and equal.UPPSC
  • Birth rate = death rate and no migration → stability of population (no change).UPPSC
  • India now: high growth rate with definite signs of slowing down.UPPSC
  • Malthus (1798, "An Essay on the Principle of Population"): population grows in geometric progression, food in arithmetic progression.UPPSC
  • Malthus checks: preventive (moral restraint, late marriage) vs positive (disease, vice, war, misery); advised birth control by moral restraint.UPPSC
  • Population control needed for environment; rapid growth disturbs environmental balance (overuse, waste, pollution).UPPSC
  • Demographic features: density, sex ratio, rural-urban share, literacy, age structure, life expectancy; standard of living is not one.UPPSC
